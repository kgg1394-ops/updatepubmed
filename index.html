Cardiovascular morbidity and mortality are high in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH, previously nonalcoholic steatohepatitis [NASH]). Resmetirom, a selective thyroid hormone receptor-ß agonist, is approved for treatment of patients with noncirrhotic MASH with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis. Resmetirom reduces atherogenic lipids and lipoproteins.

This secondary analysis of the phase 3 MAESTRO-NASH clinical trial evaluated effects of resmetirom over 52 weeks on MASH efficacy parameters, atherogenic lipids, lipoproteins, and safety according to baseline statin use.

Data from the MAESTRO-NASH trial were analyzed. Analyses assessed MASH and lipid responses to treatment and safety, including serial liver enzyme measurements and adverse events by baseline statin use.

At baseline, 49% of MAESTRO-NASH participants were on statin therapy. Efficacy endpoints, including MASH resolution, fibrosis improvement, and liver fat reduction, were achieved regardless of statin use. Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), apolipoprotein B (ApoB), and lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] were reduced irrespective of baseline statin use; the placebo-corrected percentage changes at 24 weeks with resmetirom 80 mg and 100 mg, respectively, in combination with statins or alone were: LDL-C (-12.0, -18.7 [on statin]; -15.3, -14.1 [not on statin]), ApoB (-16.4, -20.7; -17.9; -19.7), and Lp(a) (-31.2, -26.4; -36.3, -32.8). In patients on statins who received 100 mg resmetirom, the number with LDL-C <70 mg/dL at week 52 increased by 45.4%, and the number with Lp(a) <75 nmol/L or <125 nmol/L increased by 36.4% and 38.9%, respectively. No significant statin-related adverse events were observed. All confidence intervals or implied P values are nominal and uncorrected for multiplicity.

Consistently within the baseline statin subgroups, treatment with resmetirom improved multiple MASH endpoints and LDL-C, ApoB, and Lp(a) profiles.

Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors (JAKis) are increasingly used to treat inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), but most pivotal data come from randomized controlled trials. Real-world evidence is needed to clarify their effectiveness and safety in routine clinical practice.

We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of real-world studies reporting outcomes in IBD patients treated with JAKis (tofacitinib, upadacitinib, or filgotinib). PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched from inception to August 11, 2025. Primary outcomes were clinical response and clinical remission; secondary outcomes included steroid-free clinical remission, endoscopic and biochemical responses, and safety outcomes. 63 studies involving 8130 IBD patients were included. In ulcerative colitis (UC), pooled clinical response and remission rates were 55% and 43% for tofacitinib, 77% and 65% for upadacitinib, and 69% and 55% for filgotinib; steroid-free remission with tofacitinib approached 50% at 1 year. In Crohn's disease (CD), upadacitinib achieved clinical response and remission in around two thirds of patients. Across agents, safety profiles were acceptable: mortality was 0%; hospitalization and treatment discontinuation rates were 8% and 28% for tofacitinib, 5% and 12% for upadacitinib, and 4% and 15% for filgotinib; serious adverse events occurred in ≤8% of patients.

Descriptive real-world data show that tofacitinib, upadacitinib, and filgotinib all provide clinically meaningful effectiveness in UC and CD, complementing evidence from randomized trials. Across real-world cohorts, JAKis have generally acceptable safety profiles, with low rates of serious adverse events and no observed drug-related mortality.

Protective ileostomy is commonly performed after low anterior resection for rectal cancer to mitigate the clinical consequences of anastomotic leakage; however, it is associated with stoma-related and reversal-related morbidity. Ghost ileostomy has emerged as a selective alternative to avoid unnecessary stoma maturation.

This systematic review and meta-analysis of comparative studies was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched from inception to March 31, 2026. Six comparative studies involving 395 patients were included. The primary outcome was anastomotic leakage. Secondary outcomes were length of hospital stay, readmission/rehospitalization, and postoperative ileus. Random-effects models were used. The risk of bias was assessed using RoB 2 and ROBINS-I, and the certainty of evidence was evaluated using GRADE.

The available evidence did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in anastomotic leakage between ghost and protective ileostomy (RR 1.27, 95% CI 0.64-2.49; I² = 0%). Length of hospital stay was also not significantly different (MD 0.55 days, 95% CI -1.35 to 2.45; I² = 92%). Ghost ileostomy was associated with lower readmission/rehospitalization (RR 0.24, 95% CI 0.06-0.89; I² = 0%) and postoperative ileus (RR 0.33, 95% CI 0.13-0.82; I² = 0%). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis did not materially alter the interpretation of the heterogeneous hospital-stay outcome. The certainty of evidence was low for anastomotic leakage, readmission, and postoperative ileus and very low for hospital stay.

The available evidence did not identify a statistically significant difference in anastomotic leakage between ghost and protective ileostomy, although imprecision and limited sample size preclude a definitive conclusion of equivalence. Ghost ileostomy may reduce postoperative ileus and readmission in carefully selected patients; however, larger, adequately powered randomized trials are required before routine adoption.

💡 Conclusion

Drug-induced enterocolitis (DIC) consists of a heterogeneous group of gastrointestinal inflammatory disorders with clinical, endoscopic, and histological features that often overlap with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Given the increasing recognition of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced enterocolitis and the lack of standardized management algorithms, the European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) convened a multidisciplinary panel to review DIC, with a particular focus on ICI-induced disease.

Gastroenterologists, histopathologists, and oncologists systematically reviewed the literature using Medline, PubMed, EMBASE, and Scopus. Evidence was synthesized into ECCO Current Practice Positions addressing diagnostic principles, histopathological patterns, therapeutic strategies, and ICI-specific considerations. Statements were refined and approved through structured consensus.

Twenty-three practice positions were agreed upon. DIC may present with diverse histological patterns, including IBD-like/chronic enterocolitis, acute enterocolitis, microscopic colitis, ischemic-type injury, and mixed features, none of which are pathognomonic. Close clinicopathological collaboration is therefore essential. In suspected ICI-induced enterocolitis, assessment should include Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events-based grading, stool testing for pathogens, fecal calprotectin, endoscopy with biopsies, and imaging when indicated. Corticosteroids remain first-line therapy, while mild or microscopic disease may respond to budesonide or beclomethasone. Corticosteroid-refractory or corticosteroid-dependent disease should prompt early escalation to infliximab or vedolizumab. Surgery is reserved for refractory or complicated cases. Rechallenge with ICIs may be considered following multidisciplinary evaluation.

DIC represents a diagnostically complex spectrum of disorders that require integrated multidisciplinary evaluation. These ECCO Current Practice Positions provide a practical framework for diagnostic work-up and treatment, particularly for ICI-induced enterocolitis.

Trastuzumab rezetecan (SHR-A1811) is a novel human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC). Its activity in HER2-positive advanced colorectal cancer (CRC) has been suggested by a phase I cohort (n=37; objective response rate 40.5%), but real-world experience is lacking.

We describe two patients with advanced CRC whose tumors were reported as HER2-positive by immunohistochemistry (IHC) score 2+ but were not confirmed by in situ hybridization (ISH)/fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) before treatment. Case 1 was a 53-year-old man with RAS/BRAF wild-type sigmoid and left colon adenocarcinoma with liver and peritoneal metastases who had received three prior lines of therapy (>15 cycles of cumulative irinotecan exposure and prior anti-EGFR therapy). He received three cycles of SHR-A1811 200 mg plus bevacizumab (starting June 5, 2026) and developed progressive disease-an enlarging left iliopsoas lesion with worsening abdominal pain, and a rise in carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) from 157.0 to 253.0 ng/mL. Biopsy of the progressing left psoas lesion was HER2-negative (IHC 0). Case 2 was an 80-year-old man with KRAS G12S-mutant anal mucinous adenocarcinoma, bilateral lung metastases, and baseline interstitial lung changes. He received three cycles of SHR-A1811 200 mg plus bevacizumab, after which bevacizumab was discontinued for bleeding and replaced by tislelizumab for the fourth cycle; FISH was negative (HER2-low). The lung lesions remained stable without worsening of the interstitial lung changes, although CA72-4 rose from 16.27 to 36.38 U/mL. Neither patient experienced grade ≥3 adverse events attributable to SHR-A1811.

Neither patient achieved an objective response [one progressive disease, one stable disease (SD)]. Confirmatory testing showed the progressing lesion in Case 1 to be HER2-negative (IHC 0) and Case 2 to be FISH-negative (HER2-low). These observations illustrate that an IHC 2+ result alone does not establish HER2 positivity and may not predict benefit from HER2-directed ADC therapy, supporting confirmatory ISH/FISH testing before treatment selection.

Chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection is the leading risk factor for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Lactylation-related genes (LRGs) participate in HCC progression, yet their roles in HBV-associated HCC (HBV-HCC) remain largely unclear. In this study, we screened differentially expressed LRGs (DE-LRGs) from HBV-HCC transcriptomic data using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and built a high-precision random forest prediction model. Collectively, this study establishes an LRG-based predictive model and identifies two molecular subtypes for HBV-HCC, providing reliable and promising biomarker candidates for HBV-HCC risk prediction and individualized immunotherapy.
