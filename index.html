This study aimed to investigate the dose-response relationships between exercise prescription parameters and a range of health outcomes in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC). The following PRISMA guidelines, a systematic search of five databases was conducted. The inclusion criteria were randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of exercise interventions lasting ≥ 4 weeks in patients with CRC. Robust Variance Estimation (RVE) was used to synthesize the overall effects of exercise interventions across six outcome domains, including cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) and muscular fitness (MF). Univariable RVE regression models and MetaForest analyses were employed to examine the moderating effects of two groups of variables: exercise prescription moderators and patient background characteristics. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane tool, and the quality of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE approach. A total of 26 RCTs, involving 1731 CRC patients and 266 effect sizes, were included. CRF (ES = 0.35, p < 0.01) and MF (ES = 0.45, p < 0.01) showed statistically significant improvements. Regression analyses revealed threshold effects for both CRF and MF, with maximal improvements requiring sustained engagement in moderate-to-high intensity exercise. Subgroup analyses indicated that prehabilitation interventions were the most effective, while interventions during active treatment exhibited a decline-rebound trend over time. Based on these findings, high-frequency and moderate-to-high volume interventions with individualized load regulation are recommended during the pre-treatment phase. During active treatment, a more cautious approach is advised, involving reduced exercise volume during peak symptom periods and gradual progression during recovery.

💡 Conclusion
Myocardial infarction (MI) remains a leading cause of death worldwide. Persistent myocardial fibrosis and dysregulated energy metabolism following MI contribute to adverse cardiac remodeling and are associated with poor clinical prognosis. Currently, no guideline-recommended therapy provides effective long-term intervention simultaneously targeting both of these core pathological mechanisms. The Qi-Blood Harmony Formula (QBHF), a classical Chinese herbal prescription, has demonstrated cardioprotective potential on attenuating fibrosis, preserving mitochondrial integrity, and restoring myocardial ATP levels in MI, yet its underlying mechanisms and therapeutic targets remain unclear. Using an integrated multi-omics approach in a mouse MI model, we showed that QBHF reactivated genes related to energy metabolism while suppressing pro-fibrotic pathways, as revealed by bulk RNA-sequencing. Non-targeted metabolomics further confirmed its efficacy in counteracting metabolic dysregulation. Single-nucleus RNA sequencing revealed that QBHF altered cardiac cellular composition characterized by reducing a major profibrotic fibroblast subpopulation and expanding a metabolically robust cardiomyocyte subset. Integrative multi-omics analysis identified the fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO) as a central regulatory hub modulated by QBHF across bulk heart tissue, fibroblasts, and cardiomyocytes. Crucially, heterozygous knockout of FTO in mice abolished QBHF-mediated improvements in cardiac fibrosis, ATP level and heart function. Consistently, FTO knockdown in cardiomyocytes blunted QBHF-induced ATP restoration. Together, these findings demonstrate that QBHF confers post-MI cardioprotection via FTO-driven dual modulation of energy metabolism and fibrosis. Our findings highlight FTO as a promising therapeutic target and provide novel mechanistic insights into traditional Chinese medicine-based interventions for post-MI injury.

The global impact of skin injuries is substantial, compromising the wellbeing of affected individuals. Bacterial cellulose is gaining traction as a prospective dressing material due to its distinct characteristics. Nevertheless, its clinical efficacy and safety warrant detailed investigation. This systematic examination and meta-analysis assessed the healing efficacy and safety parameters of bacterial cellulose used as a dressing in skin injury management.

Comprehensive database searches of CNKI, PubMed, Embase and Web of Science were conducted from database inception to 1 September 2024, in line with PRISMA guidelines. Studies included were randomised controlled trials that compared bacterial cellulose dressings (BCDs) with conventional treatment methods. Key outcome parameters were the healing of wounds, mitigation of pain and incidence of adverse events.

The collective analysis comprised 13 studies with 514 participants, divided equally between BCDs and control groups. A notable variance was observed in the healing duration (mean difference: -6.69 days), favouring the BCD group. Equivocal results emerged for healing cure rates (odds ratio (OR): 1.27), overall adverse reactions (OR: 0.4) and pain mitigation (OR: 2.45), with none achieving statistical significance. BCD was, however, observed to hasten wound size reduction and lessen the need for dressings to be changed.

The findings of this review showed that the use of BCD for wound treatment appeared to bolster the healing process and offer pain relief. Further extensive studies are required to confirm these preliminary observations and guide their clinical application.

Psychosocial interventions are known to improve emotional well-being and quality of life in patients with cancer, but their effects on overall survival (OS) remain unclear. An updated meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) was performed.

A systematic literature search was performed in MEDLINE, EMBASE and Cochrane CENTRAL from database inception until December 2025. Randomized controlled trials comparing OS between patients receiving psychosocial interventions and control groups were included. Study characteristics and summary hazard ratios (SHR) were extracted. If SHRs were not provided, they were derived from Kaplan-Meier curves. A meta-analysis was performed using a random effects model.

Of 20365 studies identified, 24 RCTs involving 4795 patients met inclusion criteria. Follow-up time ranged from 1 to 18 years. Patients treated with psychosocial interventions had better overall survival outcomes than those in the control groups (SHR = 0.87, 95% CI 0.77-0.99; I² = 45%). Subgroup analyses showed improved OS among studies involving patients treated with curative intent (12 studies; SHR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64-0.87; I² = 20.9%), studies involving colorectal primary cancers (2 studies; SHR 0.69, 95% CI 0.48-0.99; I² = 0%), and intervention duration ≥ 12 months (N = 13, SHR = 0.85, CI: 0.72-0.99, I2 = 46.8%).

Adult patients with cancer who were offered psychosocial interventions may have improved OS. Given the moderate heterogeneity in our analysis, an individual patient-level meta-analysis may help identify characteristics of patients who benefit from these interventions.

BackgroundPilonidal disease is a debilitating condition which is caused by an inflammatory process due to the penetration of a hair into the sacrococcygeal region. Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) has been growing in several types of surgical wounds; however, the evidence for its use in excision surgery for pilonidal disease is still small and heterogeneous. Interleukin (IL)-23 inhibition is now an established therapeutic strategy in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), supported by the efficacy of selective IL-23p19 monoclonal antibodies in both Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Advances in peptide engineering have also enabled direct oral targeting of the IL-23 receptor (IL-23R), introducing an alternative approach to inhibition of the same inflammatory pathway. This narrative review examines the biological rationale for targeting the IL-23-IL-23R axis and the clinical evidence establishing selective IL-23p19 inhibition in IBD.
