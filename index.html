▶ERS clinical practice guidelines for the management of patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and lung cancer.

💡 Conclusion
Given the poor prognosis of both conditions and treatment-associated risks, management requires multidisciplinary assessment of competing risks and patient preferences in experienced interstitial lung disease and oncology centres.
Managing patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and concomitant lung cancer is a major clinical challenge due to increased incidence and negative impact on quality of life and mortality. Evidence-based recommendations are lacking. This European Respiratory Society (ERS) clinical practice guideline provides recommendations to balance oncological benefits against treatment-associated toxicity.

An ERS multidisciplinary Task Force of experts and patient representatives produced 11 conditional recommendations across seven PICO (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) questions and two narrative questions; for one PICO question no recommendation was issued. Systematic reviews were performed for each question. Evidence certainty was assessed using the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation) approach (for PICOs) and recommendations were formulated using the Evidence-to-Decision framework.

For early-stage lung cancer, the Task Force suggests surgical resection in medically fit patients. Postoperative risk necessitates systematic preoperative evaluation, perioperative antifibrotic therapy discussion and protective perioperative strategies. For stereotactic radiotherapy, a conditional recommendation for either the intervention or the comparison was issued, balancing potential oncological benefit against radiation pneumonitis. In locally advanced lung cancer, the Task Force suggests not administering concurrent radio-chemotherapy, due to very high acute exacerbation rates. For advanced lung cancer, chemotherapy is suggested in medically fit patients, where survival benefits outweigh increased toxicity risks. For stage IV lung cancer, evidence for immunotherapy was limited, thus a conditional recommendation for either the intervention or the comparison was issued. Evidence for targeted therapy for lung cancer in patients with IPF was insufficient to support a recommendation.

Given the poor prognosis of both conditions and treatment-associated risks, management requires multidisciplinary assessment of competing risks and patient preferences in experienced interstitial lung disease and oncology centres.

💡 Conclusion

Mood and anxiety disorders are common in cancer survivors, and are associated with worse outcomes; however, systematic identification of high-risk patients remains limited, despite screening and intervention guidelines. A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted to identify risk factors for mood and anxiety disorders, including diagnoses and clinically significant symptoms, in adult cancer survivors.

PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, PsycINFO, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched for cohort and case-control studies published from January 2000 to November 2024 that evaluated cancer-related, sociodemographic, lifestyle, and psychosocial risk factors in adults with breast, lung, colorectal, prostate, stomach, liver, or pancreatic cancer. Random-effects models with robust variance estimation pooled relative risks (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for factors assessed in ≥5 studies.

Fifty of the 53 eligible studies were included in the meta-analysis. Consistent risk factors included high tumor stage (vs. low; n = 28; RR, 1.55; 95% CI, 1.21-1.97), more physical comorbidities (vs. fewer; n = 36; RR, 1.57; 95% CI, 1.34-1.84), prior psychiatric history (high vs. low burden; n = 13; RR, 4.85; 95% CI, 2.77-8.50), female sex (vs. male; n = 19; RR, 1.52; 95% CI, 1.32-1.75), underweight body mass index (vs. normal; n = 6; RR, 1.93; 95% CI, 1.55-2.44), unmarried status (vs. married; n = 25; RR, 1.35; 95% CI, 1.14-1.58), and current smoking (vs. never; n = 12; RR, 1.27; 95% CI, 1.12-1.46). Suggestive associations, although less consistent in sensitivity analyses, were observed for high tumor grade, more extensive surgery, low income, low education, and low social support.

Several clinical and social factors were associated with increased risk of mood and anxiety outcomes in adult cancer survivors. These findings may support risk-based psychiatric screening and early intervention in resource-limited oncology settings.

▶Beyond cytotoxic chemotherapy: Aspirin and structured exercise to reduce colorectal cancer recurrence.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Recurrence remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality after curative-intent treatment of stage II-III colorectal cancer (CRC), including among patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy. Recent randomized trials demonstrate that aspirin and structured exercise can further reduce recurrence risk and improve patient outcomes. Two phase 3 randomized trials provide evidence for extending adjuvant strategies beyond cytotoxic chemotherapy in CRC. In ALASCCA, 3 years of low-dose aspirin reduced recurrence by approximately 50% among patients with resected colon or rectal cancer harboring PI3K-pathway alterations, establishing a biomarker-guided approach to adjuvant aspirin therapy. In CHALLENGE, a 3-year structured exercise program initiated after adjuvant chemotherapy improved disease-free and overall survival in patients with resected stage II or III colon cancer. Mechanistically, aspirin may exert antitumor effects through inhibition of cyclooxygenase and prostaglandin signaling, platelet-tumor interactions, and PI3K-related pathways, whereas exercise may act through metabolic signaling, systemic inflammation, and antitumor immunity. Despite distinct anti-tumor mechanisms, both interventions may converge on inflammation, the tumor microenvironment, and immune surveillance. ALASCCA and CHALLENGE trials provide randomized evidence supporting biomarker-directed aspirin and structured exercise as additional adjuvant strategies in patients with CRC. These study findings support testing for somatic PI3K-pathway alterations to identify patients who may benefit from adjuvant aspirin, and support incorporation of structured exercise into survivorship care after CRC resection. Their distinct mechanisms, and potentially complementary biological effects also provide a rationale for evaluating aspirin and exercise in combination.

▶Importance of the Quality of TME in Rectal Cancer Resection-A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: ⚠️ 기존 유지 권장
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Retrospective studies and analyses of RCTs showed that surgical quality of mesorectal excision affects oncological outcomes of rectal cancer patients. Objective of this study was to determine the impact of mesorectal excision quality on local recurrence and overall survival in rectal cancer patients. MEDLINE was searched till April 1, 2024, with MeSH terms: rectal cancer/neoplasm, total mesorectal excision, TME, rectal cancer surgery, macroscopic assessment/evaluation, quality/adequacy/completeness of surgery/mesorectum/resection specimen, prognosis, and outcome for randomized clinical trials, prospective and retrospective cohort studies reporting oncological outcomes and quality of the mesorectal excision. The main outcome and measures were local recurrence and overall survival. TME quality was classified into three grading categories (complete or mesorectal resection (MRR); nearly complete or intramesorectal resection (IMR); incomplete or muscularis propria resection (MPR)). Of 438 records screened and an additional three studies by cross-referencing, 15 articles with 11,285 individual patients were identified. Median age of included patients was 63, 55 (range 21, 0-95, 0). Pooled incidence of good (MRR), intermediate (IMR), and insufficient (MPR) TME quality was 65, 6%, 22, 8%, and 11, 6%, respectively. Insufficient TME quality was associated with locally advanced tumors. No difference in TME quality was observed between patients with and without neoadjuvant therapy. Pooled analysis showed lower odds for LR and higher odds for OS in patients with good TME quality, which is also shown in a subgroup analysis of neoadjuvantly treated patients. In conclusion, this meta-analysis confirms that good TME quality is associated with lower risk for local recurrence and higher probability for overall survival, an association that persisted also in neoadjuvantly treated patients. These findings suggest that neoadjuvant therapy will not compensate for suboptimal surgical quality; however, given the substantial heterogeneity across the included studies and the limited availability of data on contemporary treatment regimens, they should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

▶Cancer Risk in Premature Ovarian Insufficiency: Current Evidence and Clinical Implications.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI), defined as loss of ovarian function before 40 years with elevated gonadotropins and oligo/amenorrhea, is increasingly recognized as a systemic condition with long-term health consequences. While cardiovascular, skeletal, and neurocognitive risks are well established, the relationship between POI and cancer risk remains less clearly understood. This mini-review summarizes current evidence on site-specific malignancy risks in women with POI and evaluates the influence of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) on cancer outcomes. Available epidemiological studies, cohort analyses, and guideline recommendations addressing cancer incidence in POI were reviewed, with emphasis on breast, ovarian, endometrial, colorectal, and autoimmune-associated malignancies. Women with spontaneous POI generally demonstrate a reduced risk of breast, ovarian, and endometrial cancers, largely attributed to lower lifetime estrogen exposure and fewer ovulatory cycles. However, cancer risk may vary by etiology: autoimmune POI is associated with increased thyroid cancer incidence, while genetic subgroups such as fragile X mental retardation 1 premutation carriers may exhibit altered ovarian cancer susceptibility. Importantly, physiological HRT in POI differs fundamentally from postmenopausal hormone therapy and does not appear to increase overall cancer risk when continued until the average age of natural menopause. POI is typically associated with lower risks of hormone-dependent malignancies, though subgroup-specific variations exist. Individualized counseling, appropriate progestin opposition, and further POI-specific cohort studies are essential to refine cancer surveillance strategies.

This is the first trial to trace and inhibit mutant clone expansion as efficacy endpoint by boosting normal tissue fitness, aimed at preventing adenoma formation. Although lithium had no effect on the expansion APC-deficient pre-malignant clones or adenoma burden after six months, aberrant clones were detected in histologically normal mucosa, demonstrating the feasibility of tracing early tumorigenesis in humans. Without prophylactic colectomy, the lifetime colorectal cancer risk in familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) patients approaches 100%. Even after surgery, adenomas frequently develop in the residual intestinal mucosa, underscoring the need for effective chemopreventive agents. Here, we evaluated the chemopreventive effect of lithium on adenoma initiation.

This single-arm, phase II trial enrolled FAP patients aged 18-35 years without history of colectomy. In an 18-month protocol, participants received daily lithium carbonate for 6-12 months. Colonoscopies with biopsies of normal mucosa were conducted at baseline, 6, 12 and 18 months. The primary endpoint was the number of APC-deficient pre-malignant clones over time, assessed by tracing mutant clones via marker gene expression. Secondary endpoints included adenoma burden, safety and tolerability. Investigators were blinded during analyses.

In total, 12 FAP patients underwent baseline colonoscopy, the full analysis set included 11 patients who received at least one study dose. Across all participants, no significant change in clone frequency was observed (χ2 = 5.09, df = 3, p=0.165). Moreover, no significant effect on adenoma burden was detected. Lithium was well tolerated, with only grade 1-2 adverse events, mostly gastrointestinal (nausea and diarrhoea: n=3, 27.27%).

This is the first trial to trace and inhibit mutant clone expansion as efficacy endpoint by boosting normal tissue fitness, aimed at preventing adenoma formation. Although lithium had no effect on the expansion APC-deficient pre-malignant clones or adenoma burden after six months, aberrant clones were detected in histologically normal mucosa, demonstrating the feasibility of tracing early tumorigenesis in humans. Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05402891).

💡 Conclusion

Arginase is overexpressed in many advanced cancers, which can deplete arginine in tumors to promote immunosuppression. Arginase inhibition may enhance the efficacy of standard chemotherapy regimens by increasing arginine levels and modulating arginine-mediated immune function. INCB001158 is a potent and selective arginase inhibitor that was assessed in combination with chemotherapy.

This was an open-label, nonrandomized, phase 1/2 dose-escalation/expansion study in adults with advanced or metastatic solid tumors. INCB001158 was administered orally twice daily (bid) in combination with chemotherapy (mFOLFOX6, gemcitabine/cisplatin, or paclitaxel). Phase 2 tumor-type cohorts were colorectal cancer, biliary tract cancer (BTC), ovarian cancer (OC), gastroesophageal cancer, and endometrial cancer. Primary end points included safety, tolerability, and overall response rate (ORR).

Fifty-four patients were enrolled in phase 1 and 95 in phase 2. No dose-dependent adverse event trends were observed. Recommended phase 2 dose of INCB001158 was 100 mg bid. Two phase 2 expansion cohorts met criteria for Simon 2-stage expansion: patients with BTC treated with INCB001158 100 mg bid plus gemcitabine/cisplatin, and patients with platinum-resistant OC treated with INCB001158 100 mg bid plus paclitaxel. The overall response rate was 24.2% (8/33 patients, all partial responses) in the BTC cohort and 16.7% (1/24 complete response and 3/24 partial responses) in the OC cohort. Pharmacodynamic increase in arginine levels was observed systemically in blood plasma and locally detected by mass-spectrometry images of tumor tissue.

INCB001158 was generally well tolerated and associated with increases in arginine levels and responses in the BTC cohort, suggesting that combination regimens with chemotherapy may provide clinical benefit for some patients.

💡 Conclusion
Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).
Complex perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease are often refractory to the recommended combination of surgery and biological drug therapy. We investigated the efficacy and safety of minimally manipulated, microfragmented, autologous adipose tissue ("MFat"), after surgical debridement of the fistula, in patients with Crohn's disease in whom standard treatments had failed.

This double-blind, randomized controlled trial enrolled 80 adults who all underwent cone-like partial fistulectomy of a complex fistula (one inner orifice and up to three external openings) followed by either MFat injection or suture of the inner orifice and saline injection. MFat was prepared from lipoaspirates using Lipogems medical devices immediately before fistulectomy and transplanted around the inner orifice and along the residual tracts immediately after fistulectomy. The primary efficacy outcome was combined (clinicoradiological) remission at 24 weeks in the per-protocol populations. Safety was assessed in the all-patients-treated populations.

After randomization, 37 patients in each group were treated and 33 patients each were assessed. Combined remission occurred in 19 patients (57.6%) of the intervention group and six control patients (18.2%) (risk ratio 3.17; 95% CI, 1.45-6.91; P = .004; number needed to treat, 3). Clinical remission was found in 26 (78.8%) and 18 (54.5%) patients, respectively (P = .044), while clinical indices improved in both groups. Seven adverse events were reported, without significant differences between groups in safety endpoints.

Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).

▶Apixaban reduces hepatic decompensation in patients with cirrhosis (ApiHep): A randomized controlled trial.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 USG 6개월 간격
💡 Conclusion
Apixaban significantly reduced hepatic decompensation events without increasing hemorrhagic episodes in Child-Pugh B7-C10 cirrhosis (CTRI/2023/09/058018).
Evolving data suggest that anticoagulation is safe in cirrhosis and may improve decompensation-free survival in select patients. This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of the direct oral anticoagulant apixaban in patients with cirrhosis.

In this open-label randomized controlled trial with blinded endpoint assessment, adult patients (18-60y) with cirrhosis (Child-Pugh B7-C10) without splenoportal axis thrombosis were randomized (1:1) to receive apixaban (2.5 mg twice daily) along with standard medical therapy (SMT) or SMT alone for 12 months. The primary outcome was the comparison of the proportion of patients with hepatic decompensation at 1 year. Secondary outcomes included frequency of liver-related events, overall and liver-related mortality, and adverse events (AEs).

One hundred thirty-four patients (mean age 43.4±9.5 y; Child-Pugh B/C:115/19) were randomized (67 Apixaban+SMT and 67 SMT). Patients receiving apixaban experienced significantly lower rates of hepatic decompensation compared with SMT (33.1% vs. 54.3%; absolute risk reduction 21.2% [95% CI: 4.5%-38.2%]; p=0.013). Although rates of ascites (6.4% vs. 17.4%), HE (21.2% vs. 36.0%), and variceal bleeding (7.5% vs. 14.6%) were lower among patients receiving Apixaban+SMT as compared with SMT alone, the differences were not statistically significant. No significant between-group differences in all-cause and liver-related mortality at 1 year were noted. There were no significant between-group differences in AEs (55.2% vs. 53.7%), any bleeding (41.8% vs. 46.3%), and major bleeding (9.0% vs. 11.9%) events.

Apixaban significantly reduced hepatic decompensation events without increasing hemorrhagic episodes in Child-Pugh B7-C10 cirrhosis (CTRI/2023/09/058018).

▶Mediterranean diet, obesity, and liver steatosis: Clinical evidence and implications for gastroenterology practice.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease has become the most common chronic liver disease worldwide, closely linked to obesity, insulin resistance, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. Liver steatosis is now recognized as the hepatic manifestation of systemic metabolic dysfunction rather than an isolated liver condition, with fibrosis progression driving long-term outcomes. As pharmacologic options remain limited, lifestyle therapy is central to management. This narrative review summarizes current evidence on the Mediterranean diet as a therapeutic strategy for obesity-related liver steatosis. Mechanistic and clinical data show that this dietary pattern targets key drivers of hepatic fat accumulation and cardiometabolic risk. Observational studies and randomized clinical trials demonstrate improvements in liver fat and metabolic parameters, often beyond those achieved with weight loss alone. The Mediterranean diet is safe, sustainable, and applicable in gastroenterology practice. Future priorities include standardized adherence metrics, longer outcome-driven trials, and precision nutrition approaches integrating metabolic and gut microbiota profiles.

💡 Conclusion
This consensus statement from the Japanese Respiratory Society summarizes updated recommendations for the diagnosis and treatment of drug-induced lung injury (DLI). DLI includes respiratory disorders caused by prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicines, herbal agents, and supplements. Clinical manifestations range widely from mild and reversible conditions to severe and life-threatening disease. Among these, diffuse alveolar damage (DAD) is particularly important because it is associated with severe respiratory failure, poor treatment response, fibrosis, and high mortality. An accurate diagnosis requires careful exclusion of other causes, especially respiratory infections, heart failure, and progression of the underlying disease. Chest computed tomography imaging has a central role in diagnosis and classification, particularly for distinguishing DAD from non-DAD patterns such as organizing pneumonia, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, and nonspecific interstitial pneumonia. Biomarkers including Krebs von den Lungen-6, surfactant protein-A, and surfactant protein-D may assist in disease evaluation and monitoring. Major causative agents include anticancer drugs, immune checkpoint inhibitors, molecular target drugs, antimicrobial agents, and antirheumatic drugs. Risk factors include older age, pre-existing interstitial lung disease, smoking history, impaired respiratory function, and combination chemotherapy. The cornerstone of management is prompt discontinuation of the suspected drug, followed by glucocorticoid therapy according to disease severity and appropriate respiratory support. Early recognition, multidisciplinary evaluation, and close monitoring are essential for improving patient outcomes.

Current international guidelines for the management of symptomatic chronic pancreatitis (CP) with pancreatic ductal obstruction differ and assign a limited role to advanced endoscopic therapies, including pancreatoscopy-guided intraductal lithotripsy. The aim of this multidisciplinary international survey is to provide an overview of current expert opinion of pancreatologists pertaining to the role of advanced endoscopic treatment in symptomatic CP with ductal obstruction.

An international online survey was conducted among pancreatologists that were systematically selected by being affiliated to member associations for pancreatic diseases and a literature search.

Diagnostic performance for mucinous lesion characterization and advanced neoplasia (high-grade dysplasia and invasive carcinoma) was evaluated. Interobserver agreement was assessed using Fleiss κ and Gwet AC1. Logistic regression and ROC analyses identified imaging predictors of advanced neoplasia.

Sex differences in anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury risk are well known; however, whether patient sex influences outcomes after ACL reconstruction (ACLR) remains unclear.

The purpose of this study was to compare graft failure, knee stability, return to sport (RTS), and functional outcomes between males and females following ACLR.

Systematic review and meta-analysis; Level of evidence, 4.

A preregistered systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024614614) was conducted across PubMed, Embase, and CENTRAL (October 2024). Studies reporting sex-stratified outcomes after ACLR were eligible. Random-effects models were used to pool effect estimates separately for graft failure (retear or revision), abnormal laxity, RTS, and patient-reported outcomes.

In total, 43 studies were included in the meta-analysis, and 1 additional study was included in the systematic review. There were no significant sex differences in the odds of graft failure (odds ratio [OR] = 0.90; 95% CI, 0.70-1.17) or RTS rates (OR = 0.69; 95% CI, 0.46-1.03). Functional outcomes (International Knee Documentation Committee, Knee injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score, Lysholm, and Tegner) also did not differ meaningfully between sexes. Heterogeneity was moderate, and subgroup analyses by graft type, follow-up duration, and physical activity level did not alter findings.

Sex alone does not appear to independently determine graft failure, RTS success, or functional recovery after ACL reconstruction. Instead, postoperative outcomes are more likely driven by modifiable factors such as graft characteristics, rehabilitation quality, neuromuscular recovery, and sport exposure. These findings suggest that individualized functional assessment may be more informative for clinical decision-making than sex alone.
