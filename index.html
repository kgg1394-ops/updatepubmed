Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease and its advanced form, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), are increasingly prevalent among people living with obesity (PwO). Despite increasing prevalence, management of liver health in routine obesity care remains inconsistent. This study evaluated how healthcare professionals (HCPs) conceptualize liver health within the broader obesity treatment framework, perceive and identify MASH risk, and make screening, treatment, and referral decisions for PwO.

This was a cross-sectional survey conducted among U.S. based HCPs including physicians and advanced practice providers (APPs; nurse practitioners and physician associates) practicing in primary care, endocrinology, gastroenterology, or obesity medicine. The survey assessed MASH risk perception, guideline use, confidence in non-invasive fibrosis risk stratification (including FIB-4), post-risk assessment management, treatment approaches, and referral patterns. Analyses were descriptive.

A total of 813 HCPs (physicians or APPs) including primary care providers (PCPs) (n = 516), endocrinologists (n = 104), gastroenterologists (n = 101), and obesity specialists (n = 92) completed the survey. HCPs perceived PwO to be at high risk for developing MASH (67-75%), but more often identified type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, and hypertension as conditions for which PwO are at greater risk than MASH. Self-reported use of formal guideline for MASH fibrosis risk assessment was low (14-23%). Confidence in FIB-4 use was lowest among PCPs (31% "very confident"), and reassessment or secondary testing for low and indeterminate risk patients was infrequent. Management approaches for patients with obesity and MASH varied by specialty.

Despite widespread awareness that PwO face high risks of MASH, confidence in fibrosis risk stratification and reported guideline use were low. As effective pharmacotherapies for MASH continue to emerge, ensuring that the infrastructure for early detection, guideline literacy, diagnostic confidence, and health system support keeps pace with therapeutic innovation will be essential for management of the growing population of people living with obesity and MASH.

Bone and joint diseases, including osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, and genetic skeletal disorders, are increasingly studied in the context of aging and metabolic dysfunction. To investigate these underlying pathways, preclinical research requires precise tools to functionally interrogate specific genes within defined skeletal cell populations. Accurately delivering gain- or loss-of-function agents to bone or cartilage compartments is essential for elucidating molecular mechanisms and validating therapeutic targets. Therefore, achieving spatial precision in skeletal tissue delivery is a critical methodological requirement. Targeted delivery systems serve as specific pharmacological tools that localize bioactive molecules to bone or cartilage to study the function and pathogenic mechanisms of specific molecules. Safety, efficacy, and cost-effectiveness are paramount for clinical translation of the targeted delivery systems discussed herein. However, their effectiveness heavily relies on the choice of targeting moiety. Different tissues, cell types, and experimental objectives require distinct ligands, and inappropriate selection may lead to inefficient targeting or misleading conclusions. Despite their importance, clear guidance for selecting skeletal targeting moieties in preclinical studies remains limited. To address this need, the Translational European-Asian Network (TEA-NET) of Academia Europaea Translational Medicine Working Group initiated and coordinated this guideline. Through a structured literature review and a predefined evidence-based evaluation framework, this practical guide assesses available skeletal-targeted delivery strategies, emphasizing the selection and functional integration of targeting moieties. The resulting recommendations provide practical guidance for choosing targeting ligands in preclinical studies and for designing delivery systems with potential for future clinical translation.

GLP-1RA therapy may reduce the need for surgery in IBD and consistently lower weight. Well-powered RCTs should clarify treatment effect, safety and how best to integrate GLP-1RAs agents into IBD care.
Obesity is increasingly prevalent among individuals with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) induce clinically meaningful weight loss and may exert anti-inflammatory effects in the gut, but their effectiveness in modifying the course of IBD remains uncertain.

We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies that enrolled adults with IBD who were treated with a GLP-1RA, searching PubMed, Scopus and CENTRAL from inception to 30 June 2025. Primary outcomes were IBD-related surgery, hospitalization, oral and intravenous corticosteroid use, escalation to advanced therapy and intestinal obstruction. We performed random-effects meta-analyses and reported pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Risk of bias was assessed with ROBINS-I for observational studies and RoB 2 for randomized trials.

Thirteen studies met the inclusion criteria-12 observational and 1 randomized controlled trial (RCT)-comprising 74,513 patients overall; 3 studies contributed data suitable for meta-analysis. Pooled estimates demonstrated a significantly lower odds of IBD-related surgery among GLP-1RA recipients (OR 0.46, 95%CI 0.34-0.61). Estimates for hospitalization, corticosteroid use, therapy escalation and intestinal obstruction were not statistically significant. Across studies, GLP-1RA treatment consistently achieved weight reduction in patients with IBD, aligning with established efficacy in general obesity care.

GLP-1RA therapy may reduce the need for surgery in IBD and consistently lower weight. Well-powered RCTs should clarify treatment effect, safety and how best to integrate GLP-1RAs agents into IBD care.

💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer is a major global health concern, highlighting the need for early and accurate detection to improve treatment outcomes. Existing screening techniques are often expensive, invasive, or lack sensitivity, especially for asymptomatic individuals. Genetic mutations, like Tumor Suppressor Protein (TP53), K-Ras protein (KRAS) and Adenomatous Polyposis Coli (APC), play crucial roles in Colorectal cancer development, while demographic factors, like age, sex, ethnicity, and family history, significantly influence risk. Incorporating genetic and demographic data might improve early and non-invasive diagnosis. Current diagnostic techniques ineffectively combine genetic and demographic factors, which limits the accuracy of early detection. Hence, there is a need for predictive modules to improve screening and personalized risk assessment. To overcome these limitations, Fractional Bitterling Fish Optimization-based Hierarchical Convolutional Neural Network (FBFO_HCNN) is proposed for diagnosing Colorectal cancer based on genetic mutations and demographic factors. This research involves two types of input data, namely, gene and demographic data. First, the gene selection process is performed using gene expression data with the Fractional Bitterling Fish Optimization (FBFO). Then, augmentation is done using the bootstrapping method, which is considered as output-1. Similarly, demographic data is utilized in the data normalization phase is executed by Peldschus normalization. After that, FBFO is designed for the feature selection process, and the bootstrapping method is employed for the data augmentation process, which provides output-2. Lastly, diagnosis of Colorectal cancer is done by Hierarchical Convolutional Neural Network (HCNN) by considering both outputs, where HCNN is tuned using the FBFO algorithm. Experimental results demonstrate that FBFO_HCNN achieved superior values for Accuracy, True Negative Rate (TNR), True Positive Rate (TPR), and False Omission Rate (FOR) of 96.656%, 96.678%, 95.412%, and 3.796%.

Use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) for diabetes and obesity is increasing, but effects on uncommon or long-latency cancers are uncertain. We synthesised meta-analytic evidence for overall and site-specific cancer risk and assessed methodological quality, overlap, outcome-level certainty, and credibility.

We searched PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from inception to 30 June 2026 for meta-analyses of GLP-1RAs or the dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide and incident cancer. Randomised and observational evidence was considered separately; review-level estimates were not recombined. We assessed methodological confidence with AMSTAR 2, overlap with corrected covered area (CCA), outcome-level certainty with a GRADE-informed algorithm, and association credibility with Ioannidis criteria.

We included 38 meta-analyses from 2012 to 2026. The most comprehensive placebo-controlled randomised evidence (48 trials, 94 245 participants, median follow-up 70 weeks) showed no statistically significant association with thyroid cancer (odds ratio [OR] 1·37, 95% CI 0·82-2·31; 1 fewer to 9 more cases per 10 000 treated), pancreatic cancer (0·84, 0·53-1·35), colorectal cancer (1·11, 0·75-1·65), or breast cancer (0·95, 0·60-1·49), and no overall cancer increase (OR 1·05, 0·98-1·13 across 50 trials). Thyroid and agent-specific haematological signals were inconsistent or based on sparse events; between-review discordance for thyroid and colorectal cancer was largely attributable to comparator restriction, choice of estimator, and handling of zero-event trials rather than to differences in cancer ascertainment. Observational syntheses suggested lower hepatocellular carcinoma risk (hazard ratio 0·60, 95% CI 0·41-0·88), but four reviews had very high overlap (CCA 50·0%). Outcome-level certainty was moderate for five outcomes, low for 11, and very low for five; none was high. No association met class I or II credibility criteria.

▶The Inflammation-Electrical Remodeling Axis in the Infarct Border Zone: Mechanisms and Therapeutic Translation for Post-MI Ventricular Arrhythmias.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Ventricular arrhythmias (VA) and sudden cardiac death (SCD) remain leading causes of mortality after myocardial infarction (MI). The conventional fibrosis-centric paradigm explains chronic reentrant VAs but cannot fully account for electrical instability in acute and subacute post-MI phases, highlighting an unmet need for upstream therapies targeting the arrhythmogenic substrate. Accumulating preclinical mechanistic and observational clinical evidence indicates that the inflammatory microenvironment in the infarct border zone may function as a critical upstream driver of electrical remodeling, mediated by neutrophils, macrophages and key pro-inflammatory cytokines (interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α)), constituting an inflammation-electrical remodeling axis that contributes to VA susceptibility across all post-MI stages. While causal relationships have been established in animal models, direct confirmation in human myocardium remains limited. This narrative review synthesizes preclinical and clinical evidence on this axis. It summarizes its spatiotemporal dynamics, four core cellular and molecular mechanisms and the antiarrhythmic potential of diverse anti-inflammatory interventions. We further outline key evidence gaps and translational directions, including precision risk stratification and cell-type-specific targeted delivery. Based on preclinical and preliminary clinical evidence, targeting the inflammation-electrical remodeling axis represents a promising upstream strategy to reduce post-MI SCD by addressing the root causes of electrical instability, though definitive proof of efficacy in humans is pending, and dedicated randomized controlled trials with arrhythmia endpoints are still required for clinical validation.
