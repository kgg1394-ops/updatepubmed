We aimed to assess adherence to nutritional guidelines and its association with mortality.

💡 Conclusion
Gastrointestinal (GI) cancers have been a largely disappointing arena for immune-checkpoint inhibition, with durable benefit confined to mismatch-repair-deficient (dMMR/MSI-H) disease, PD-L1-enriched gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). A shared feature of many resistant GI tumors is a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-driven, immune-excluded microenvironment, providing a mechanistic rationale for co-targeting angiogenesis and the PD-(L)1 axis. The clinical foundation for this strategy has now been established across GI malignancies: atezolizumab plus bevacizumab became standard of care in HCC (IMbrave150), and the combination of zanzalintinib with atezolizumab delivered the first immune-checkpoint-based overall-survival benefit in microsatellite-stable (MSS) colorectal cancer in the phase 3 STELLAR-303 trial - providing phase 3 support for multikinase (VEGFR/MET/TAM-family)/checkpoint co-targeting in a disease long considered immune-refractory, and supporting the VEGF/checkpoint hypothesis without establishing that the benefit is attributable to VEGF-pathway inhibition specifically. These successes have catalyzed the development of single-molecule VEGF/VEGFR × PD-1/PD-L1 bispecific antibodies, which aim to consolidate and potentially improve upon the co-targeting paradigm through cooperative binding, conditional tumor selectivity, and simplified pharmacology. Ivonescimab (AK112) is the most clinically advanced agent and carries the broadest GI portfolio, spanning colorectal, gastroesophageal, hepatobiliary, and pancreatic programs; pumitamig (BNT327/PM8002) and SSGJ-707 (PF-08634404) anchor parallel registrational efforts. In biliary tract cancer, the randomized phase 3 HARMONi-GI1 trial - presented at ESMO 2026 - showed that ivonescimab plus gemcitabine/cisplatin improved overall survival over durvalumab plus gemcitabine/cisplatin (median 14.9 versus 12.1 months; HR 0.75), the first positive phase 3 result for the class in a GI cancer, albeit from an interim analysis in a China-only population with benefit confined to intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma; the remaining pivotal programs (HARMONi-GI2/GI3, ROSETTA) are ongoing. The biological warrant and cross-tumor signals are compelling, but GI-specific efficacy and safety for the bispecific class remain early, geographically concentrated, and confounded by comparator choice; no bispecific-validated biomarker yet exists. HARMONi-GI1 establishes superiority over checkpoint-plus-chemotherapy in one disease; the trials now needed are those comparing the bispecific with the co-administered anti-VEGF/checkpoint doublet, which alone can determine whether single-molecule co-targeting delivers a genuinely immunological gain or merely a superior anti-angiogenic effect.

Radical resection combined with intestinal autotransplantation (RRCIA) has emerged as a potential curative option for highly selected patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC) and extensive mesenteric vascular involvement. This project aimed to develop expert consensus recommendations on indications, surgical strategies, perioperative management, and long-term care of RRCIA in patients with LAPC.

An international multidisciplinary panel of experts in pancreatic surgery, transplant surgery, medical oncology, radiation oncology, radiology, critical care, and perioperative care was convened. A modified Delphi process was used to develop consensus recommendations. The survey comprised six domains with a total of 20 questions covering patient selection, neoadjuvant therapy, surgical strategies, perioperative management, adjuvant therapy, and follow-up. Consensus was predefined as ≥80% agreement.

Twenty consensus statements were developed. The quality of evidence was low for 17 statements and moderate for 3. Six recommendations were strong and 14 conditional. RRCIA was recommended only for highly selected patients at experienced high-volume centres following adequate neoadjuvant therapy and multidisciplinary evaluation. Surgical strategies should be individualized according to tumour location and vascular involvement. No definitive minimum residual bowel length was established, and preservation of well-perfused bowel and the ileocaecal valve was emphasized. Postoperative management should be tailored, including anticoagulation, nutritional support, selective implementation of enhanced recovery principles, adjuvant therapy, and structured long-term follow-up.

This consensus provides the first international framework to guide the application of RRCIA for LAPC, emphasizing its use in carefully selected patients treated at experienced centres with multidisciplinary expertise.

Pancreatic cancer is often difficult to remove by surgery when it grows around major blood vessels that supply the intestine. In a small number of carefully selected patients, a highly complex operation can be performed in which the affected pancreas and blood vessels are removed, part of the intestine is temporarily removed and preserved, and the intestine is then reimplanted to the body after the blood vessels have been reconstructed. Guidance on when and how to perform this operation remains limited.

The authors aimed to develop international expert recommendations for the safe and appropriate use of this operation in patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer.

A group of 33 experts in pancreatic cancer surgery, transplantation, imaging, cancer treatment, and postoperative care reviewed the available evidence. They then used two rounds of voting to reach agreement on 20 clinical questions.

The experts agreed that this operation should be considered only for carefully selected patients who have responded well to systemic treatment before surgery. It should be performed in experienced centres by multidisciplinary teams. The recommendations also cover the type of operation, reconstruction of blood vessels and the intestine, nutrition, prevention of blood clots, treatment after surgery, and long-term follow-up. This guideline provides a framework to improve consistency and safety in performing this type of operation.

Use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) for diabetes and obesity is increasing, but effects on uncommon or long-latency cancers are uncertain. We synthesised meta-analytic evidence for overall and site-specific cancer risk and assessed methodological quality, overlap, outcome-level certainty, and credibility.

We searched PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from inception to 30 June 2026 for meta-analyses of GLP-1RAs or the dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide and incident cancer. Randomised and observational evidence was considered separately; review-level estimates were not recombined. We assessed methodological confidence with AMSTAR 2, overlap with corrected covered area (CCA), outcome-level certainty with a GRADE-informed algorithm, and association credibility with Ioannidis criteria.

We included 38 meta-analyses from 2012 to 2026. The most comprehensive placebo-controlled randomised evidence (48 trials, 94 245 participants, median follow-up 70 weeks) showed no statistically significant association with thyroid cancer (odds ratio [OR] 1·37, 95% CI 0·82-2·31; 1 fewer to 9 more cases per 10 000 treated), pancreatic cancer (0·84, 0·53-1·35), colorectal cancer (1·11, 0·75-1·65), or breast cancer (0·95, 0·60-1·49), and no overall cancer increase (OR 1·05, 0·98-1·13 across 50 trials). Observational syntheses suggested lower hepatocellular carcinoma risk (hazard ratio 0·60, 95% CI 0·41-0·88), but four reviews had very high overlap (CCA 50·0%). Outcome-level certainty was moderate for five outcomes, low for 11, and very low for five; none was high. No association met class I or II credibility criteria.

Older gastric cancer survivors after radical gastrectomy are vulnerable to frailty progression. Family function may influence frailty by enhancing individual psychological resilience. However, few studies have examined these longitudinal associations. We aimed to evaluate the longitudinal associations among family function, psychological resilience, and frailty.

A total of 412 older gastric cancer survivors who underwent radical gastrectomy were enrolled and assessed at hospital discharge, and again at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively. Family function was assessed using the family adaptability and cohesion evaluation scale II, psychological resilience with the Connor-Davidson resilience scale, and frailty using the Tilburg frailty indicator. The random intercept cross-lagged panel model was used to assess within- and between-person longitudinal associations. Mediation effect analysis was conducted at the within-person level.

Family cohesion was indirectly associated with lower subsequent frailty through higher psychological resilience. The estimated indirect effects from T1 to T3 and from T2 to T4 were -.025 (p = .012) and -.032 (p = .004), respectively. While family adaptability was associated with psychological resilience at the between-person level (B = .707, p = .006), and psychological resilience was negatively associated with frailty (B = -2.458, p = .003), the direct association between family adaptability and frailty was not significant (B = -.097, p = .069).

Greater family cohesion was associated with lower subsequent frailty, with psychological resilience representing a potential pathway. These findings highlight the potential of resilience-focused approaches to enhance the benefits of family support in addressing frailty.

To evaluate the safety and toxicity profile of a single-fraction stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) boost delivered to the tumor bed using GammaPod™ technology in patients with early-stage breast cancer at high risk of local recurrence.

This prospective phase II trial enrolled 70 patients with early-stage breast cancer and high-risk features (age ≤ 50 years, triple-negative phenotype, grade 3 tumors, or positive/close margins). All patients underwent breast-conserving surgery followed by a single-fraction SBRT boost (7.5 Gy) to the tumor bed delivered with GammaPod™ prior to whole-breast irradiation (40.05 Gy in 15 fractions). The primary endpoints were acute toxicity (within 3 months) and moderate/severe late toxicity at 2 years. Toxicity was assessed using CTCAE v5.0. Secondary endpoints included local recurrence (LR) and regional recurrence (RR).

With a median follow-up of 30.9 months, treatment was well tolerated. Acute toxicity was minimal, with dermatitis observed in 17% of patients at 3 months, including only 1.4% grade 2 and 1.4% grade 3 events. Late toxicity was predominantly mild, with fibrosis being the most common event (24% at early follow-up, decreasing to 9.5% at 24 months, all grade 1). No grade ≥3 late toxicities were observed. Grade 2 fibrosis occurred in 7% of patients overall. At 24 months, LR and RR rates were 1.5% and 0%, respectively.

A single-fraction stereotactic boost using GammaPod™ prior to whole-breast irradiation is feasible and associated with a favorable toxicity profile in high-risk early breast cancer patients. These findings support further investigation of this approach as a strategy to improve treatment efficiency while maintaining safety and local control.

Radical resection combined with intestinal autotransplantation (RRCIA) has emerged as a potential curative option for highly selected patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC) and extensive mesenteric vascular involvement. This project aimed to develop expert consensus recommendations on indications, surgical strategies, perioperative management, and long-term care of RRCIA in patients with LAPC.

An international multidisciplinary panel of experts in pancreatic surgery, transplant surgery, medical oncology, radiation oncology, radiology, critical care, and perioperative care was convened. A modified Delphi process was used to develop consensus recommendations. The survey comprised six domains with a total of 20 questions covering patient selection, neoadjuvant therapy, surgical strategies, perioperative management, adjuvant therapy, and follow-up. Consensus was predefined as ≥80% agreement.

Twenty consensus statements were developed. The quality of evidence was low for 17 statements and moderate for 3. Six recommendations were strong and 14 conditional. RRCIA was recommended only for highly selected patients at experienced high-volume centres following adequate neoadjuvant therapy and multidisciplinary evaluation. Surgical strategies should be individualized according to tumour location and vascular involvement. No definitive minimum residual bowel length was established, and preservation of well-perfused bowel and the ileocaecal valve was emphasized. Postoperative management should be tailored, including anticoagulation, nutritional support, selective implementation of enhanced recovery principles, adjuvant therapy, and structured long-term follow-up.

This consensus provides the first international framework to guide the application of RRCIA for LAPC, emphasizing its use in carefully selected patients treated at experienced centres with multidisciplinary expertise.

Pancreatic cancer is often difficult to remove by surgery when it grows around major blood vessels that supply the intestine. In a small number of carefully selected patients, a highly complex operation can be performed in which the affected pancreas and blood vessels are removed, part of the intestine is temporarily removed and preserved, and the intestine is then reimplanted to the body after the blood vessels have been reconstructed. Guidance on when and how to perform this operation remains limited.

The authors aimed to develop international expert recommendations for the safe and appropriate use of this operation in patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer.

A group of 33 experts in pancreatic cancer surgery, transplantation, imaging, cancer treatment, and postoperative care reviewed the available evidence. They then used two rounds of voting to reach agreement on 20 clinical questions.

The experts agreed that this operation should be considered only for carefully selected patients who have responded well to systemic treatment before surgery. It should be performed in experienced centres by multidisciplinary teams. The recommendations also cover the type of operation, reconstruction of blood vessels and the intestine, nutrition, prevention of blood clots, treatment after surgery, and long-term follow-up. This guideline provides a framework to improve consistency and safety in performing this type of operation.

👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Hashimoto's thyroiditis (HT) is markedly female predominant, but evidence for the mechanisms underlying this pattern is heterogeneous. This narrative review examines epidemiology, the clinicopathological spectrum, X-chromosome regulation, sex hormones, thyroid-local T- and B-cell responses, and environmental and life-course modifiers. To prevent disease association from being conflated with an explanation of sex bias, we distinguish (1) human HT studies reporting an explicit sex comparison (2), human HT studies without sex-stratified mechanistic analyses, and (3) non-HT human or experimental evidence used to generate hypotheses. Skewed X-chromosome inactivation has been investigated in female HT case-control studies, whereas sex-dependent downstream TLR7, CD40L and FOXP3 effects within human HT remain incompletely established. Estradiol has potentially opposing actions across immune subsets, progesterone-related tolerogenic findings are not proof of protection in human HT, and HT tissue evidence for B-cell activating factor (BAFF) and its receptor (BAFF-R) does not establish sex-specific therapeutic responsiveness. Clinical translation is therefore more limited than mechanistic plausibility. Established management remains based primarily on thyroid functional status and life-stage context, with levothyroxine as the standard treatment for overt hypothyroidism rather than a sex-specific immune intervention. Large randomized trials in euthyroid TPOAb-positive women have not shown improved live-birth outcomes with routine levothyroxine, while selenium, vitamin D and myo-inositol studies predominantly report biochemical or surrogate outcomes and do not establish sex-specific benefit. Published sex-related differences in TPOAb reference limits are method- and reference-population-dependent and should not be confused with validated HT diagnostic thresholds. Accordingly, sex-aware precision strategies are best viewed as a research framework requiring disease-specific biomarker validation, demonstration of incremental clinical utility, patient-relevant outcomes, and sex-stratified prospective trials before routine implementation.

Acute kidney injury is a frequent and serious complication of acute pancreatitis (AP), contributing to increased mortality and healthcare burden. Major adverse kidney events within 30 days (MAKE30) constitute a patient-centered composite endpoint for assessing kidney outcomes. However, effective tools for identifying patients at high risk of MAKE30 are lacking. This study aimed to develop and externally validate an interpretable machine learning model for predicting MAKE30 in critically ill patients with AP.

Three clinical datasets were used. MIMIC-IV served as the development cohort, and external validation used eICU-CRD and a retrospective cohort from the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (AHSWMU). Feature selection combined least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, the Boruta algorithm, and multivariable logistic regression. Seven machine learning algorithms were used to develop prediction models. Model performance was assessed in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) were used to interpret and visualize the final model.

A total of 1,574 patients with AP from multiple centers were included. Six predictors were selected for model development: serum creatinine, red cell distribution width, albumin, aspartate aminotransferase, vasopressor use, and total bilirubin. The random forest model demonstrated favorable overall performance, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.863 in the internal validation cohort. Performance was consistent across the two independent external validation cohorts, with AUCs of 0.840 in eICU-CRD and 0.848 in AHSWMU. A web-based clinical risk calculator was developed to facilitate model use in clinical practice.

The model developed in this study exhibited favorable performance and robust generalizability in predicting MAKE30 among critically ill patients with AP, providing reliable digital decision support for early risk stratification and individualized intervention strategies.

Use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) for diabetes and obesity is increasing, but effects on uncommon or long-latency cancers are uncertain. We synthesised meta-analytic evidence for overall and site-specific cancer risk and assessed methodological quality, overlap, outcome-level certainty, and credibility.

We searched PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from inception to 30 June 2026 for meta-analyses of GLP-1RAs or the dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide and incident cancer. Randomised and observational evidence was considered separately; review-level estimates were not recombined. We assessed methodological confidence with AMSTAR 2, overlap with corrected covered area (CCA), outcome-level certainty with a GRADE-informed algorithm, and association credibility with Ioannidis criteria.

We included 38 meta-analyses from 2012 to 2026. The most comprehensive placebo-controlled randomised evidence (48 trials, 94 245 participants, median follow-up 70 weeks) showed no statistically significant association with thyroid cancer (odds ratio [OR] 1·37, 95% CI 0·82-2·31; 1 fewer to 9 more cases per 10 000 treated), pancreatic cancer (0·84, 0·53-1·35), colorectal cancer (1·11, 0·75-1·65), or breast cancer (0·95, 0·60-1·49), and no overall cancer increase (OR 1·05, 0·98-1·13 across 50 trials). Thyroid and agent-specific haematological signals were inconsistent or based on sparse events; between-review discordance for thyroid and colorectal cancer was largely attributable to comparator restriction, choice of estimator, and handling of zero-event trials rather than to differences in cancer ascertainment. Observational syntheses suggested lower hepatocellular carcinoma risk (hazard ratio 0·60, 95% CI 0·41-0·88), but four reviews had very high overlap (CCA 50·0%). Outcome-level certainty was moderate for five outcomes, low for 11, and very low for five; none was high. No association met class I or II credibility criteria.

Randomised evidence shows no evidence of a material increase in overall or site-specific cancer risk over currently observed trial durations (median about 70 weeks, maximum about 5 years), and cancer concerns alone do not justify withholding indicated GLP-1RA treatment. Small, rare, long-latency, dose-specific, or agent-specific effects cannot be excluded, and the evidence cannot address exposure beyond approximately five years, well short of the 8-10 years generally required to evaluate cancer incidence. Observational protective associations should not support causal or cancer-prevention claims.
