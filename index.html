💡 Conclusion
Chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection is a leading cause of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma, affecting an estimated 50-57 million people worldwide. Although direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) have revolutionized HCV therapy, achieving >90-95% cure rates, hepatitis B virus (HBV)/HCV coinfection is still present in 1-15% of patients, raising HCC risk. This meta-analysis aims to estimate the pooled incidence of HBV reactivation in adults with chronic HCV infection treated with DAAs and to identify risk factors associated with reactivation. A comprehensive and systematic literature search was conducted across major databases including PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science, according to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. Eight randomized controlled trials and 19 observational cohort studies were included, which evaluated adult patients with chronic hepatitis C infection who were seropositive for hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and received DAA therapy. The analysis of 27 studies encompassing 1707 HBsAg-positive patients revealed a substantial risk of virological HBV reactivation, with a pooled prevalence of 0.29 [95% confidence interval (CI) = 0.15-0.46; I2 = 97%]. Subgroup analysis showed a significantly lower reactivation rate in patients receiving nucleos(t)ide analogue prophylaxis (0.11 [95% CI = 0.05-0.22; I2 = 97%]) than those who did not (0.20 [95% CI = 0.09-0.36; I2 = 92%]). The pooled prevalence of clinical reactivation was 0.04 (95% CI = 0.02-0.07; I2 = 67%), which was also lower in the prophylaxis subgroup (0.03 vs. 0.07). The prevalence of alanine aminotransferase flare and HBV-associated hepatitis was 0.04 (95% CI = 0.02-0.06; I2 = 67%) for both, while the pooled prevalence of HBV DNA reappearance was 0.33 (95% CI = 0.18-0.50; I2 = 94%). HBV-related death was negligible (0.00, 95% CI = 0.00-0.01; I2 = 0%). Significant heterogeneity and publication bias were observed across most outcomes. HBV reactivation remains a clinically relevant problem in chronically HCV-infected patients treated with DAAs, particularly in HBsAg-positive patients or those not receiving HBV prophylaxis. Future research should focus on creating sophisticated risk prediction models of different prophylactic and monitoring strategies for preventing this burden of HBV reactivation with DAA use.

Guidelines recommend MRI-based surveillance for BD-IPMNs, imposing a burden on healthcare systems. Abdominal ultrasound (US) reliably visualizes IPMNs and some worrisome features (WF). We aimed to evaluate the safety and potential benefits of integrating US into surveillance protocols.

Retrospective study of 644 patients with BD-IPMN, stratified by surveillance strategy: US-assisted (≥2 US during follow-up) versus standard surveillance, and MRI-limited (MRI only for significant changes on US) versus MRI-prevalent surveillance.

Both US-assisted and MRI-limited strategies showed no significant differences in clinical outcomes with reference groups (mortality, unresectable cancer, appropriate surgery; all p>0.09), lower loss-to-follow-up (12.0% vs 32.9%, p<0.01; 13.2% vs 28.4%, p<0.01), higher guideline adherence (80.3% vs 68.1%, p<0.01; 79.5% vs 70.8%, p=0.03), and lower costs (€478.3 vs €780.9; €272.7 vs €857.4, p<0.01). In an exploratory analysis, even for cysts <20 mm with no baseline WF, risk of developing a new WF persisted beyond the 5-year threshold (conditional probability of 40% between years 5 and 10).

US-based surveillance was associated with improved compliance, guideline adherence and cost-effectiveness, without detectable safety signals. An MRI-limited approach in well-visualized BD-IPMN <20 mm appears promising for long term surveillance.

▶Precision nutrition in inflammatory bowel disease: from biological determinants to personalized nutritional management.

💡 Conclusion
Diet is a key therapeutic target in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet responses vary widely between patients. We distinguish conventional dietary therapy, applied by clinical indication; stratified nutrition, selected on predefined clinical or biological features such as phenotype or disease location; and precision nutrition, which uses individual-level clinical, biological, microbial, metabolic and digital data to predict response and adapt dietary interventions. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase and Scopus were searched from 2010 to June 2026, combining IBD terms with dietary, microbiome, multi-omics, artificial intelligence (AI) and digital health terms; only English-language articles were included. Priority was given to randomized trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and landmark studies, defined as those first establishing a therapeutic principle or biological association subsequently confirmed, appraised for design, sample size, risk of bias and consistency with the literature. Exclusive enteral nutrition and the Crohn's disease exclusion diet have the strongest randomized support for inducing remission in Crohn's disease; the Mediterranean diet is best supported for maintenance, and the low-FODMAP diet improves persistent gut symptoms without changing inflammatory markers. Phenotype, disease location, microbiota, genetics, immune pathways, and environment underlie this heterogeneity, which multi-omics, AI and digital health may capture in predictive models, though most such evidence comes from non-IBD populations. Preliminary, uncontrolled studies suggest that combining diet with advanced therapies may improve selected outcomes, but synergy has not been demonstrated. Precision nutrition is a promising paradigm for personalized IBD management, but IBD-specific prospective trials, validated multimodal biomarkers and multidisciplinary implementation are required before routine use.

💡 Conclusion
Pediatric and adult colonoscopes demonstrate comparable procedural success, efficiency, and safety in routine adult colonoscopy. The lower pain scores associated with pediatric colonoscopes may offer a patient-centered advantage, though the overall certainty of evidence is limited.
Colonoscopy is widely performed for colorectal cancer screening, yet evidence guiding the choice between pediatric and adult colonoscopes in adult patients remains inconsistent. Pediatric colonoscopes may offer advantages in maneuverability and comfort, but their comparative effectiveness has not been systematically established.

We searched Cochrane, PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through 20 November 2025. We included randomized controlled trials and comparative observational studies comparing pediatric and adult colonoscopes in adult patients undergoing colonoscopy. Risk ratios (RR) or mean differences with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Pooled analyses were conducted using random-effects models. The certainty of evidence was evaluated.

Twelve studies comprising 9385 patients were included. No significant differences were observed between pediatric and adult colonoscopes for cecal intubation rate (RR = 0.99, 95% CI: 0.98-1.00; P = 0.065), terminal ileum intubation (RR = 0.97, 95% CI: 0.88-1.08), cecal intubation duration (mean difference = -0.12 min, 95% CI: -0.84 to 0.60), or adverse events (RR = 1.6, 95% CI: 0.37-7.55). Adult colonoscopes were associated with higher pain scores (mean difference = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.04-1.10; P = 0.037). Most outcomes demonstrated low or very low certainty evidence, primarily because of risk of bias and imprecision.

Pediatric and adult colonoscopes demonstrate comparable procedural success, efficiency, and safety in routine adult colonoscopy. The lower pain scores associated with pediatric colonoscopes may offer a patient-centered advantage, though the overall certainty of evidence is limited.

▶Beyond oncologic cure: optimizing surgical outcomes in older adults with early colorectal cancer.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Early colorectal cancer in older adults requires a balance between oncologic benefit and the risks of postoperative functional decline, loss of independence, and competing mortality. Chronological age alone is insufficient to guide this balance because physiological reserve, functional status, comorbidity, cognition, nutritional status, and life expectancy vary substantially among individuals of the same age. Frailty and sarcopenia provide complementary information regarding vulnerability to surgical stress, but frailty should not be used as an automatic indication for treatment de-escalation. Rather, frailty screening should identify patients who require multidimensional geriatric assessment to characterize clinically relevant vulnerabilities and guide individualized preoperative management. This review proposes an optimization-based framework for individualized surgical decision-making in older adults with early colorectal cancer. Patients with evidence of geriatric vulnerability should undergo comprehensive geriatric assessment, including evaluation of functional independence, physical performance, muscle strength, nutritional status, comorbidity, medication burden, cognition, and social support. When oncologically safe, a time-limited period of individualized optimization, including exercise, nutritional intervention, medical optimization, medication review, and psychosocial support, may be considered before definitive surgical planning. Reassessment should emphasize the direction of change in functional reserve and recovery potential rather than categorical reversal of frailty. Patients with a favorable trajectory after optimization may remain candidates for guideline-concordant oncologic surgery. In contrast, persistently limited reserve should prompt multidisciplinary and shared decision-making that considers tumor-specific oncologic benefit, functional consequences, competing mortality, and patient priorities. This approach reframes frailty assessment from static risk prediction toward an individualized, optimization-based strategy while preserving oncologic principles.

Standard neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) for locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) achieves variable pathological responses. Incorporating immune checkpoint inhibitors into nCRT (inCRT) is a promising intensification strategy, but its efficacy and impact on surgical safety remain poorly defined. This meta-analysis evaluated the oncological benefits and surgical risks of inCRT versus nCRT in both proficient and deficient mismatch repair LARC.

A systematic search of the PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library databases was conducted for randomized clinical trials (RCTs) published from inception up to February 2026. The primary outcomes were pathological complete response (pCR) and grade ≥ III severe adverse events (SAEs) according to the Clavien-Dindo complication grading system. Secondary outcomes included major pathological response (MPR) and postoperative complications. Pooled risk ratios (RRs) were calculated using random-effects or fixed-effects models.

Seven RCTs including 1117 patients were identified. Five trials did not restrict enrolment based on mismatch repair or microsatellite instability status, whereas two trials exclusively enrolled patients with mismatch repair-proficient/microsatellite-stable tumours. Although inCRT significantly increased the rate of pCR (31.9% versus 19.3%; RR 1.70; 95% confidence interval (c.i.) 1.37 to 2.10; P < 0.001) and MPR (55.1% versus 40.3%; RR 1.38; 95% c.i. 1.20 to 1.59; P < 0.001) versus nCRT, it was associated with a significantly higher risk of SAEs (30.1% versus 26.8%; RR 1.24; 95% c.i. 1.03 to 1.49; P = 0.02) and overall postoperative complications (32.8% versus 25.6%; RR 1.27; 95% c.i. 1.02 to 1.58; P = 0.03). Subgroup analysis indicated that the pCR benefit was most pronounced in cohorts receiving short-course radiotherapy and consolidation immunotherapy.

The addition of immunotherapy to neoadjuvant therapy for LARC markedly improves pathological response but increases high-grade toxicity and surgical morbidity. Although inCRT is a potent intensification strategy, stringent patient selection and regimen optimization are required before widespread adoption.

Patient experience is a key quality indicator for colonoscopy, yet standardized assessment tools are lacking in China. The Newcastle ENDOPREM scale has shown promise internationally but requires validation in China.

To translate, adapt, and preliminarily evaluate the psychometric properties of the Chinese version of the Newcastle ENDOPREM (ENDOPREM-CN) to assess colonoscopy patient experience in Chinese patients.

Este consenso multidisciplinar fornece orientações estruturadas e baseadas em evidências sobre o uso de ressecção hepática, transplante hepático e terapias locorregionais no tratamento do CHC. Ao abordar questões clínicas importantes relacionadas à seleção de pacientes, indicações terapêuticas e integração de estratégias curativas e locorregionais, essas recomendações visam apoiar a tomada de decisões consistentes e informadas na prática clínica diária e otimizar os resultados para pacientes elegíveis para tratamento potencialmente curativo.
