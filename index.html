💡 Conclusion
Colonoscopy is central to colorectal cancer prevention because it enables early detection and removal of premalignant lesions. However, lesions can still be missed, and endoscopic differentiation of neoplastic from nonneoplastic polyps remains variable, particularly for diminutive lesions. The potential of artificial intelligence (AI) as a supportive technology for improving both polyp detection and characterization during colonoscopy has therefore been investigated. Since early studies of convolutional neural network-based computer-aided detection (CADe), the focus has shifted from retrospective image-based validation to real-time randomized trials, meta-analyses, and clinical guideline discussions. Accumulating evidence suggests that CADe improves surrogate detection outcomes, including the adenoma detection rate (ADR), polyp detection rate, adenomas per colonoscopy, and adenoma miss rate. Although a higher ADR is associated with a lower risk of postcolonoscopy colorectal cancer, it remains unknown whether the incremental improvement in the ADR achieved by CADe translates into lower postcolonoscopy colorectal cancer incidence or colorectal cancer-related mortality. Recent multicenter data further support the effectiveness of real-time CADe systems in improving detection-related outcomes, while also indicating that benefit varies with lesion characteristics and user experience. In parallel, computer-aided diagnosis (CADx) has been developed to assist the optical characterization of colorectal polyps, particularly for diminutive lesions and potential resect-and-discard or diagnose-and-leave strategies. CADx systems using white-light imaging and structured high-confidence diagnostic rules show promise, but the clinical implementation of CADx remains less mature than that of CADe. This review summarizes current evidence for AI-assisted polyp detection and characterization and discusses limitations and future directions for integrating CADe and CADx into human-centered colonoscopy practices.

💡 Conclusion
Histotripsy should be regarded as an investigational non-thermal ablation technology for HCC. Current data support technical feasibility and preliminary clinical signals, but not superiority, equivalence, or guideline-defined positioning relative to established therapies. Histotripsy is a non-thermal focused-ultrasound ablation technology that produces mechanical tissue fractionation through controlled acoustic cavitation. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), its relevance remains investigational, particularly in settings where conventional thermal ablation may be constrained by heat-sink effects, vascular or biliary proximity, limited hepatic reserve, or the need for repeated local therapy. This review evaluates the evidence without assuming an established clinical role.

We performed a structured narrative review with systematic literature identification and evidence classification. The structured core search covered PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Embase, and Google Scholar from database inception through February 2026; a targeted update through 20 September 2026 was used to identify newly published clinical studies, safety reports, and reviews. Evidence was classified as direct HCC, mixed liver-tumour, non-HCC clinical extrapolation, or preclinical/mechanistic evidence.

Evidence remains early-stage and heterogeneous. #HOPE4LIVER reported 95.5% technical success and 6.8% major complications in mixed primary and metastatic liver-tumour cohorts, with incomplete HCC-specific outcome reporting. The Hong Kong prospective cohort included 19 patients with HCC, all Child-Pugh A, and reported 95% technical success, 82.5% complete response at 1 month, and no grade III or higher complications. Real-world reports also document vascular injury, haemorrhage, and pseudoaneurysm, underscoring that relative resistance of collagen-rich structures should not be interpreted as proven vascular or biliary safety.

Histotripsy should be regarded as an investigational non-thermal ablation technology for HCC. Current data support technical feasibility and preliminary clinical signals, but not superiority, equivalence, or guideline-defined positioning relative to established therapies. HCC-specific prospective cohorts, standardized safety reporting, long-term oncological outcomes, and comparative studies are required.

▶Immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with VEGF/VEGFR-targeted therapy in microsatellite-stable advanced colorectal cancer: clinical advances and the potential for immune-sensitive tumor conversion.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a major cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide, and mismatch repair-proficient/microsatellite-stable (pMMR/MSS) tumors account for the majority of metastatic CRC cases. In contrast to dMMR/MSI-H CRC, pMMR/MSS tumors are typically characterized by low immunogenicity, limited neoantigen presentation, insufficient effector T-cell infiltration, and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which collectively restrict the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)/VEGF receptor (VEGFR) signaling contributes not only to abnormal angiogenesis but also to immune suppression by impairing dendritic-cell maturation, reducing immune-cell trafficking, promoting T-cell dysfunction, and facilitating the accumulation of immunosuppressive cell populations. These mechanisms provide a strong rationale for combining VEGF/VEGFR-targeted therapy with ICIs to normalize tumor vasculature and remodel the immune microenvironment. Clinical evidence for this strategy in advanced pMMR/MSS CRC remains heterogeneous. Treatment activity varies across therapeutic settings, antiangiogenic agents, and combination regimens, while many studies are limited by small sample sizes, single-arm designs, and substantial differences in patient characteristics. Emerging data suggest that metastatic pattern, molecular features, baseline immune contexture, and treatment line may influence clinical activity; however, no biomarker has yet been validated for routine patient selection. Multi-agent strategies may provide broader immune modulation but also increase toxicity, treatment complexity, and cost. This review summarizes the biological rationale, clinical efficacy, safety, and potential predictive factors of VEGF/VEGFR-targeted therapy combined with ICIs in advanced pMMR/MSS CRC. Particular emphasis is placed on the heterogeneity of clinical outcomes and on distinguishing mechanistic immune remodeling from the potential enhancement of tumor immune sensitivity. Future studies should prioritize biomarker-guided patient selection, randomized comparative trials, and comprehensive assessment of efficacy, safety, quality of life, and long-term clinical benefit.

💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer is becoming more common in adults under age 50, but little is known about the symptoms these patients experience. This study is a secondary analysis of baseline data examining fatigue, pain, and emotional distress in 165 adults aged 18-50 with colorectal cancer who were enrolled in a symptom management study. Participants completed questionnaires about their symptoms at the beginning of the study. Researchers used statistical methods to identify groups of patients with similar symptom patterns. Overall, participants reported high levels of fatigue, moderate distress, and lower levels of pain. Two distinct symptom groups emerged. About 60% of participants had lower pain with moderate fatigue and distress, while 40% had moderate pain along with high fatigue and high distress. Symptom patterns differed between men and women and by body mass index. These findings suggest that younger adults with colorectal cancer commonly experience multiple symptoms at the same time, particularly fatigue and emotional distress. Recognizing these symptom patterns may help healthcare providers identify patients who need additional support. The results also highlight the need for treatments that address multiple symptoms together rather than focusing on a single symptom. Because this study included only patients who were already experiencing symptoms and enrolled in a symptom management trial, future research should confirm these findings in larger and more diverse groups of younger adults with colorectal cancer.

Neoplastic polyps are precancerous lesions of colorectal cancer (CRC), yet proteomic studies for stratifying their malignant potential remain limited. In this study, we used the Olink proteomics platform to systematically analyze the expression of inflammation- and oncology-related proteins in colorectal polyp tissues from 94 patients. The results showed that most inflammation- and oncology-related proteins were expressed at higher levels in neoplastic polyps than in non-neoplastic polyps. These proteins may provide valuable molecular evidence for deeper mechanistic studies of the CRC precancerous stage, early biomarker discovery, and prevention-oriented therapeutic development.

Gallbladder carcinoma (GBC) is the most common biliary tract malignancy and is usually diagnosed at an advanced stage. Artificial intelligence (AI), including radiomics, machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), is increasingly applied to its diagnosis, surgical management and prognostication, but how far the reported performance can be trusted is unclear.

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library and Google Scholar were searched to 5 May 2026 using database-specific search strategies. Each primary prediction-model study was characterized separately by design, number of centers and validation type (internal, temporal or external).

Of 31 primary AI studies in GBC, 29 were retrospective, 22 were single-center and 24 reported internal validation only. In independent test or external cohorts, DL and ML models discriminated benign from malignant gallbladder lesions with areas under the curve (AUCs) of approximately 0.80-0.89; a multicenter model distinguishing xanthogranulomatous cholecystitis from GBC achieved an external AUC of 0.88, and automated computed tomography (CT) segmentation an external Dice score of 0.76. The best recurrence model achieved an AUC of 0.76 on internal (random-split) validation; none of the reviewed prognostic models reported a quantified external-validation estimate. Intraoperative AI is technically feasible but has no outcome data, and federated learning has been tested only in simulation.

AI could plausibly assist several steps of GBC care, but the evidence is dominated by retrospective, single-center, internally validated studies from Asia, and metrics from different studies are not directly comparable. Prospective multicenter external validation, shared annotated datasets and standardized reporting are required before clinical adoption.

Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) is associated with high short-term mortality and limited therapeutic options. Therapeutic plasma exchange (PLEX) may improve outcomes by removing inflammatory mediators, but randomized evidence in ACLF is limited. This study evaluated the efficacy of PLEX in alcohol-related ACLF (A-ACLF).

In this randomized controlled trial, 100 patients with A-ACLF were randomized (1 : 1) to PLEX plus standard medical therapy (SMT) or SMT alone. PLEX was performed for up to three sessions. Coprimary outcomes were in-hospital mortality and 30-day survival. Secondary outcomes included hospital stay, ventilator requirement, and longitudinal changes in disease severity scores and laboratory parameters.

All 100 patients (PLEX n = 50; SMT n = 50) completed follow-up. In-hospital mortality was lower with PLEX [24 vs. 52%; P = 0.007], with an absolute risk reduction of 28% (number needed to treat: 3.6). Thirty-day survival was higher with PLEX (72 vs. 36%; P < 0.001), with an adjusted hazard ratio for mortality of 0.43 (P = 0.003). Significant improvements were observed in the model for end-stage liver disease sodium, Asian Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver ACLF Research Consortium, and Child-Turcotte-Pugh scores, and in international normalized ratio, creatinine, lactate, ammonia, and von Willebrand factor (all P < 0.001 for interaction). Hospital stay was shorter with PLEX (19 vs. 24 days; P = 0.023). No major PLEX-related adverse events were observed.

PLEX improves short-term survival and disease severity in A-ACLF and may be a valuable therapeutic option where transplantation is limited.

💡 Conclusion
In 1935, Allen Oldfather Whipple performed the first one-stage pancreatoduodenectomy, establishing the foundation for an operation that has since undergone nearly a century of technical refinement. Advances in surgical technique, perioperative care, training, and technology have progressively improved the safety and reproducibility of the pancreatoduodenectomy. More recently, the introduction of minimally invasive approaches-and particularly robotic surgery-has prompted another potential paradigm shift in the operative management of pancreatic disease. As robotic pancreatoduodenectomy (RPD) has expanded over the past decade, an important question has emerged: has the robotic pancreatoduodenectomy matured sufficiently for widespread adoption or will it remain an operation confined to selected surgeons and specialized centers? A growing body of evidence has addressed the safety, feasibility, and potential benefits of RPD. Although early experience was derived from retrospective and observational studies, more recent randomized controlled trials have provided higher-level evidence regarding its clinical performance. Collectively, these studies have demonstrated a reduced LOS, demonstrated noninferiority in morbidity, and similar oncologic outcomes relative to open pancreatoduodenectomy. However, evidence of safety and efficacy alone does not necessarily establish readiness for broad implementation. The critical question is therefore no longer simply whether RPD can be performed safely, but under what conditions it should be adopted and disseminated. In this review, we evaluate the evolution and current evidence for RPD through the context of the IDEAL (Idea, Development, Exploration, Assessment, and Long-term study) framework, a structured model developed to assess the introduction and evaluation of surgical innovations. Viewed through this framework, the available evidence suggests that RPD has progressed beyond the experimental phase and has entered a stage of evidence-supported clinical implementation. Outcomes remain dependent on surgeon experience, institutional volume, and completion of a substantial learning curve. Accordingly, the transition of RPD into routine clinical practice should be accompanied by structured training, mentorship, outcomes reporting, and appropriate institutional and procedural volume standards. These safeguards will be essential to ensure that expansion of RPD preserves the outcomes achieved in experienced, high-volume centers.
