To map the systemic and oral adverse events (AEs) of semaglutide reported in randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

This scoping review was conducted following the PRISMA-ScR guidelines. Only english-language and RCTs were considered.

A literature search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science from 2019 until 2025.

Of 630 identified records (300 in PubMed, 312 in Scopus, and 18 in Web of Science), 12 studies were included after screening. The review identified systemic AEs of semaglutide, primarily gastrointestinal events like nausea (12 studies) and diarrhea (11 studies). Less frequent effects included pancreatitis (5 studies), hepatobiliary (3 studies) and gallbladder disease (3 studies), dental caries (1 study) and dry mouth (1 study). Subcutaneous administration predominating (7 studies), followed by oral (3) and mixed routes (2), over treatment durations of 21-68 weeks and 4-9 weeks of follow-up. Additionally, systemic events such as nasopharyngitis (5 studies), COVID-19 (5 studies), and metabolic or nutrition-related disorders were reported in all 12 studies. Importantly, none of the included studies directly investigated oral or dental outcomes.

Semaglutide demonstrated an overall favorable safety profile in the included randomized controlled trials, with adverse events predominantly gastrointestinal. However, the RCTs did not provide direct evidence regarding oral health outcomes. Therefore, the available evidence is insufficient to draw conclusions about oral adverse events associated with semaglutide, highlighting the need for randomized clinical trials specifically designed to assess oral health outcomes in patients receiving GLP-1 receptor agonists.

To construct and validate a contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) radiomics-clinical model for predicting transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

A total of 168 HCC patients who underwent CEUS on two vendor platforms (Philips E9 and GE Logiq E95) were split into a training set (n = 118) and an internal validation set (n = 50), with an additional 80 patients forming an external validation cohort. For each patient, 2D regions of interest were manually delineated on the largest cross-sectional slice of each CEUS phase. Radiomic features were extracted and screened by LASSO regression; the twelve retained features were linearly combined into a single Rad-score. The Rad-score and three prespecified clinical risk factors (tumor size, Child-Pugh class, AFP) confirmed by multivariate analysis were combined into a parsimonious logistic regression (LR) model with four predictors in total. Performance was assessed by ROC curve, calibration curve, decision curve analysis (DCA), subgroup analysis, and prospective validation.

The combined model had AUCs of 0.895, 0.891 and 0.846 in three sets, with subgroup AUCs >0.86. Prospective validation showed TACE plus sorafenib improved 6-month response rate from 26.7% to 53.3% for high-risk patients.

The CEUS radiomics-clinical model accurately predicts TACE resistance, guiding individualized HCC treatment.

This model integrates CEUS multi-phase radiomics and clinical factors, outperforming single clinical/radiomic models. Strict multi-step screening and comprehensive validation avoid data leakage, ensuring robust prediction and clinical translatability.

Spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy (SPDP) has emerged as the preferred approach for benign and low-grade malignant distal pancreas tumors. Two primary techniques exist: the Kimura technique (splenic vessel preserving) and the Warshaw technique (splenic vessel resection with short gastric vessel reliance). Despite widespread adoption, comparative evidence remains limited and methodologically inconsistent. The objective of this study is to systematically compare perioperative and clinical outcomes between Kimura and Warshaw techniques in SPDP, analyzing only studies with preoperative technique allocation to eliminate intraoperative selection bias. We hypothesize that the Kimura technique is associated with fewer postoperative adverse events compared with the Warshaw technique.

We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis following PROSPERO registration (CRD420251046633). PubMed, MEDLINE, Scopus, Google Scholar, and Web of Science were searched through November 2025 for English and Spanish studies published within 15 years. Inclusion criteria required preoperative technique selection based on imaging and tumor characteristics, with statistical comparison of outcomes. Studies employing "Kimura-first" or intraoperative decision protocols were excluded. Two reviewers independently extracted the data, with disagreements resolved by a third reviewer. Meta-analyses used random-effects models.

Six studies encompassing 1312 patients (839 Kimura, 473 Warshaw) met inclusion criteria. Compared to the Warshaw technique, the Kimura technique was associated with significantly lower odds of readmission (OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.57-0.76; P = 0.006) and splenic infarct (OR 0.20, 95% CI 0.09-0.47; P = 0.004). Overall complications, major complications, minor complications, reinterventions, and pancreatic fistulas showed no significant differences between techniques.

Although both the Kimura and Warshaw techniques demonstrated no significant difference in most of the postoperative outcomes for SPDP, the Kimura technique offers significantly lower odds of readmission and splenic infarction. Technique selection should be individualized based on patient anatomy, tumor characteristics, and surgeon or institutional expertise to optimize outcomes.
