💡 Conclusion
This clinical practice guideline update provides evidence-informed recommendations to support patients and clinicians in the management of diverticular disease.
We identified areas of controversy in the management of patients with diverticular disease to develop key questions addressing the management of symptomatic uncomplicated diverticular disease (SUDD), uncomplicated diverticulitis, and complicated diverticulitis.

We performed a systematic review to identify randomized trials and cohort studies addressing our key questions. Informed by an updated evidence synthesis, an international, multidisciplinary panel of gastrointestinal surgeons, gastroenterologists, patient partners, and methodologists applied the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence-to-decision framework during panel consensus meetings to derive recommendations for the management of patients with diverticular disease.

The systematic review identified 27 studies representing very low to moderate certainty evidence. The panel made the following recommendations: 1. In patients with SUDD, we suggest rifaximin or mesalamine over standard supportive care (conditional recommendation); 2. In patients with uncomplicated diverticulitis, we suggest no routine antibiotics (conditional recommendation); 3. In patients with persistent symptoms following an episode of recurrent uncomplicated diverticulitis, we suggest elective resection over observation (conditional recommendation); 4. In patients who have recovered from a first episode of uncomplicated diverticulitis, we suggest no routine follow-up colonoscopy (conditional recommendation); 5. In patients who have recovered from a first episode of diverticulitis complicated by an abscess, we suggest elective resection over surveillance (conditional recommendation). The panel judged that a significant proportion of patients would place a high value in reducing the risk of recurrence at 1 year but also acknowledged the lack of evidence addressing prioritized outcomes including 30-day major complications, need for emergency surgery, stoma-free survival at 1 year, and quality of life at 1 year. Given that patient values and preferences may vary, clinicians are encouraged to apply patient-centered decision-making and discuss the alternatives.

This clinical practice guideline update provides evidence-informed recommendations to support patients and clinicians in the management of diverticular disease.

Antiviral therapy with nucleos(t)ide analogues is recommended for patients with viremic hepatitis B virus (HBV)-related cirrhosis regardless of levels of viremia or liver enzymes. However, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is not an antiviral treatment criterion in the U.S. HBV treatment guidelines, though prior studies have reported improved survival with antiviral treatment in HBV-related HCC. To inform guidelines, we estimated antiviral treatment rates and their association with survival and HCC recurrence rates in HBV-related HCC. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies published between 2006 and 2024 that reported antiviral treatment, survival, and/or HCC recurrence rates in adults with HBV-related HCC, following PRISMA guidelines. In this exploratory pilot trial, a modified yoga program was feasible and well tolerated and was associated with improvements in selected patient-reported outcome domains. These findings are hypothesis-generating and warrant evaluation in a larger, adequately powered trial.
Patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) have poor health-related quality of life.

To explore the feasibility and potential impact of a modified yoga program on health-related quality of life in patients with IPF compared with usual care.

We conducted a randomized, controlled, nonblinded, 12-week pilot clinical trial with two arms involving 63 adults with IPF diagnosed according to the 2018 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT clinical practice guideline. The yoga arm attended one-hour virtual group sessions twice weekly for 12 weeks, consisting of seated postures, breathing exercises, and meditation designed for patients with IPF, while the control arm continued usual care.

ments: Health-related quality of life was assessed using seven patient-reported outcome measures and their associated domains.

Sixty of 63 randomized participants completed the week-12 assessment and comprised the analysis cohort; their mean age was 73.4 years (SD, 8.2). Statistically significant improvements favoring yoga were seen in the Living with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (L-IPF) cough domain (-9.29 points, 95% CI -18.37 to -0.20; p=0.045; SMD=-0.36), the L-IPF total score (-7.11, 95% CI -13.15 to -1.06; p=0.022; SMD=-0.38), and the R-scale-PF cough domain (-1.18, 95% CI -2.27 to -0.10; p=0.034; SMD=-0.45).

In this exploratory pilot trial, a modified yoga program was feasible and well tolerated and was associated with improvements in selected patient-reported outcome domains. These findings are hypothesis-generating and warrant evaluation in a larger, adequately powered trial.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Our findings suggest that use of NA-NSAIDs, in particular non-selective NA-NSAIDs, might be associated with a reduced risk of adenomas and CRC, but not advanced serrated polyps.
While substantial evidence supports a protective effect of aspirin against colorectal polyps and cancer, the evidence for non-aspirin non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NA-NSAIDs) remains limited. We studied the association between the use of NA-NSAIDs and the detection of colorectal polyps and cancer among screening participants.

We selected participants in the Bowel Cancer Screening trial in Norway-a randomized trial comparing once-only sigmoidoscopy and the biennial faecal immunochemical test (FIT). Using multivariable logistic regression, we estimated odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the association between NA-NSAID use in the 4 years before the screening invitation and detection of colorectal cancer (CRC), adenomas, advanced adenomas, and advanced serrated polyps. Long-term use was defined as ≥2 years of use.

Of 64,889 participants (24,159 sigmoidoscopy, 40,730 FIT), 35,867 (55.3%) were NA-NSAID users, 314 (0.5%) had CRC, 6,208 (9.6%) had adenomas, and 659 (1.0%) had advanced serrated polyps. NA-NSAID users had lower odds of CRC (OR 0.80; 95% CI 0.64-1.00), adenomas (OR 0.94; 95% CI 0.89-1.00), and advanced adenomas (OR 0.87; 95% CI 0.80-0.95) than those with no NA-NSAID prescription. For non-selective NA-NSAIDs, the results were similar to those for any NA-NSAIDs (CRC: OR 0.79, 95% CI 0.63-0.99; adenomas: OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.88-0.99; advanced adenomas: OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.79-0.95). COX2-selective NA-NSAIDs were not associated with CRC or adenomas. Among the long-term NA-NSAIDs users, the ORs were 0.59 (95% CI 0.27-1.26) for CRC, 0.65 (95% CI 0.54-0.79) for adenomas, and 0.59 (95% CI 0.44-0.80) for advanced adenomas. No association was observed between any NA-NSAID use and advanced serrated polyps. The results were consistent across trial arms and sexes.

Our findings suggest that use of NA-NSAIDs, in particular non-selective NA-NSAIDs, might be associated with a reduced risk of adenomas and CRC, but not advanced serrated polyps.

Socioeconomic and racial or ethnic inequalities affect colorectal cancer (CRC) across the care continuum. Although increasingly documented, whether current interventions effectively reduce these inequalities remains uncertain.

To review socioeconomic and racial or ethnic inequalities in CRC incidence, screening and diagnosis, treatment, surveillance and survival and assess interventions designed to reduce them.

We conducted a narrative review of adult studies published in English between January 2000 and March 2026. MEDLINE, EMBASE, PubMed, and ISI Web of Knowledge were searched for studies addressing socioeconomic or racial and ethnic disparities in CRC, using a saturation approach.

Socioeconomic disadvantage and belonging to some racial or ethnic minority groups were associated with higher CRC incidence within high-income countries, lower screening participation, later diagnosis, less timely or guideline-concordant treatment, poorer surveillance and worse survival. In the USA, five-year survival was 60.6% among Black patients versus 66.7% among White patients, with little improvement in this gap over several decades. Inequalities at successive stages may contribute to cumulative disadvantage. Intervention evidence was markedly uneven across the continuum. Mailed stool-test outreach and patient navigation increased screening among underserved populations, whereas evidence for interventions specifically reducing inequalities in treatment, surveillance, and broader survivorship was sparse.

Improving overall CRC prevention and care cannot be assumed to reduce inequalities. The major evidence gap has shifted from documenting disparities towards demonstrating how to reduce them, particularly beyond screening. Equity should become an explicit outcome of CRC research, implementation, healthcare quality assessment and clinical guidelines.

👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal adenocarcinoma in young adults is an emerging public health concern in Mexico that requires specific strategies targeting prevention and early detection.
The prevalence of colorectal cancer in young adults is increasing worldwide but there is limited data from Latin American countries. In Mexico, the burden and clinical profile of colorectal adenocarcinoma in young patients are not well characterized. Our study aimed to estimate the prevalence of colorectal adenocarcinoma and describe its clinical characteristics, in young adult patients in Mexico.

The PubMed and Web of Science databases were systematically searched from database inception to September 2024. The prevalence of colorectal adenocarcinoma in young adults in Mexico was evaluated, comparing the clinical characteristics of early-onset and late-onset cases. A pooled meta-analysis was carried out, using random effects models. Heterogeneity was assessed through the I² statistic, meta-regression, and subgroup analyses. The risk of bias was evaluated using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklist for Studies Reporting Prevalence Data.

The pooled prevalence of colorectal adenocarcinoma in young adults was 10% (95% CI 6-15 %) in patients ≤40 years of age and 19% (95% CI 18-21 %) in those under 50 years of age. Meta-regression indicated a growing trend in the under 40 age group over time and the predominating clinical factors were male sex (52%), rectal involvement (44%), stage IV disease at diagnosis (32%), and a greater number of poorly differentiated tumors.

Colorectal adenocarcinoma in young adults is an emerging public health concern in Mexico that requires specific strategies targeting prevention and early detection.

▶Dissecting the potential impact of appendectomy on colorectal cancer through metabolites and inflammatory proteins: a Mendelian randomization study.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
This MR investigation provides evidence supporting a potential causal relationship between genetic liability to appendectomy and CRC, with carnitine and IL-13 potentially acting as partial mediators of this effect.
Traditional longitudinal epidemiological investigations have demonstrated an association between appendectomy and the cumulative incidence of colorectal cancer (CRC). However, there is a paucity of evidence for a causal relationship between appendectomy and CRC from alternative methodologies.

We extracted summary statistics for CRC, appendectomy, 1,400 metabolites, and 91 inflammatory proteins from the largest European ancestry genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and the FinnGen study. Using two-sample bidirectional Mendelian randomization (MR), we explored the potential causal relationship between genetic liability to appendectomy and CRC. Moreover, we employed a two-step MR approach to identify metabolites and inflammatory proteins that may mediate the effects of genetic liability to appendectomy on CRC risk and analyzed their potential mediator variables. Furthermore, we used the significant single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with the risk of CRC due to appendectomy, searched for the nearest genes in the FinnGen, and explored the relationship between these genes and CRC prognosis by analyzing transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO).

Two-sample MR analysis revealed evidence supporting a potential causal effect of genetic liability to appendectomy on an increased risk of CRC. Two-step MR analysis identified carnitine and interleukin-13 (IL-13) as key mediators potentially involved in the positive causal relationship between genetic liability to appendectomy and CRC. Survival analysis showed that upregulation of HLX and OSR1 expression is positively correlated with poor prognosis in CRC patients.

This MR investigation provides evidence supporting a potential causal relationship between genetic liability to appendectomy and CRC, with carnitine and IL-13 potentially acting as partial mediators of this effect.

▶Antibody-mediated immune responses and inflammatory bowel disease: insights from genetic correlation and Mendelian randomization.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
This study provides genetic evidence for causal associations between antibody-mediated immune responses to 46 pathogens and IBD in European populations, offering insights into IBD pathogenesis and implications for prevention and treatment strategies.
Observational studies suggest a complex relationship between infectious diseases and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. This study examined the genetic correlations and causal associations between antibody-mediated immune responses (targeting 46 infectious pathogens) and IBD, using linkage disequilibrium score regression (LDSC) and Mendelian randomization (MR) to inform early prevention and personalized treatment.

Data from the UK Biobank (8,735 samples) were used to identify independent SNPs associated with antibody responses. IBD-related data from the GWAS Catalog included 2,515 ulcerative colitis (UC) cases and 482,083 controls, as well as 1,342 Crohn's disease (CD) cases and 455,006 controls. Validation was done using datasets from the International IBD Genetics Consortium (IIBDGC), including 6,968 UC and 5,956 CD cases. Genetic correlations were assessed using LDSC, and causal links were investigated through MR analysis, employing inverse-variance weighting (IVW) and sensitivity analyses.

Genetic correlations were found between antibody responses to specific pathogens and IBD. MR analysis revealed causal effects of antibody responses to four pathogens on UC and protective/risk effects on CD. No horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity was observed, and sensitivity analyses confirmed the consistency of the findings.

This study provides genetic evidence for causal associations between antibody-mediated immune responses to 46 pathogens and IBD in European populations, offering insights into IBD pathogenesis and implications for prevention and treatment strategies.
