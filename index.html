BRAF V600E-mutant metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) represents an aggressive molecular subtype associated with poor prognosis and limited response to conventional chemotherapy. Racial/ethnic disparities in colorectal cancer mortality persist in the United States despite treatment advances. We examined racial/ethnic differences in guideline-concordant care (GCC) receipt and cancer-specific mortality, estimated the proportion of the Black-White disparity mediated through GCC, and tested for heterogeneity in the GCC-survival association across groups.

Adults with a first primary colorectal adenocarcinoma diagnosed 2010-2017 were included (N = 45,267). GCC was operationalized as a composite score (0-4) per NCCN recommendations. Short-course radiotherapy (SCRT)-based regimens, including SCRT-alone and SCRT-based total neoadjuvant therapy (SCRT-based TNT, defined as SCRT followed by consolidation chemotherapy), have emerged as alternative neoadjuvant options to long-course chemoradiotherapy (LCCRT) for locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC). This meta-analysis stratified analyses by the type of SCRT regimen and whether LCCRT arms received preoperative consolidation chemotherapy, to systematically compare their efficacy and safety and inform personalized treatment selection.

A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science identified randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing SCRT-based regimens and LCCRT up to November 2025. Two reviewers independently performed study selection, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment. Pooled analyses were conducted using Review Manager 5.3, with relative risk (RR) or hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Nine randomized trials (reported in 16 publications), involving 3,061 patients, were included. In the primary comparison of SCRT-based TNT versus standard LCCRT, the SCRT-based TNT group achieved a significantly higher pathological complete response rate (RR = 1.56, 95% CI: 1.24-1.97, P = 0.0002), whereas tumor downstaging and locoregional failure showed no significant differences from the LCCRT group. The SCRT-based TNT regimen was associated with a significantly higher incidence of grade ≥ 3 acute toxicity (RR = 1.63, 95% CI: 1.14-2.34, P = 0.008), and showed numerical trends toward improved overall survival (HR = 0.99, 95% CI: 0.76-1.28, P = 0.91) and disease-free survival (HR = 1.05, 95% CI: 0.77-1.43, P = 0.77) that did not reach statistical significance. An exploratory analysis based on a single small trial found no significant differences between SCRT-based TNT and LCCRT-based TNT across all major endpoints. Pooling all SCRT-based strategies obscured clinically meaningful heterogeneity and yielded no significant differences for any primary endpoint.

SCRT-alone offers lower acute toxicity but substantially poorer tumor response, warranting use only in selected patients. The comparison between SCRT-based TNT and LCCRT-based TNT remains inconclusive. SCRT-based regimens represent heterogeneous treatment pathways rather than a uniform substitute for LCCRT, requiring individualized clinical decision-making.

For locally advanced esophageal and rectal cancers, radiotherapy (RT) provides essential local control but is frequently limited by acute and late toxicities that erode survival and quality of life (QoL). Over the last decade, evidence has implicated radiation dose to the heart, lungs, bowel, and pelvic bone marrow in increased postoperative morbidity, cardiopulmonary deaths, and hematologic toxicity, compromising chemotherapy delivery and long-term outcomes. The physical properties of proton beam therapy (PBT), which eliminate exit dose and reduce integral dose, offer a fundamental advantage. Systematic evidence confirms PBT consistently lowers low- to intermediate-dose exposure to organs at risk (OARs). In rectal cancer, PBT reduces pelvic bone marrow V10 by 40-55% and small bowel V15-V30 by 30-50%, a prerequisite for improving the tolerability of intensive regimens. While definitive Phase III survival data are awaited, current evidence supports the selective use of PBT to improve the therapeutic index of radiotherapy, allowing its benefits to be realized with less collateral damage.

To investigate associations between CRC genetic liability and VTE-related outcomes within peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) regulatory contexts. Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a rare, chronic, cholestatic disease that can result in cirrhosis, liver failure, and death. While biochemical markers such as alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and total bilirubin (TB) measure disease activity and progression, definitions of biochemical response vary. This systematic literature review (SLR) identified clinical studies for investigational and approved treatments in patients with PBC with inadequate response or intolerance to ursodeoxycholic acid requiring second-line (2L) or later (2L+) therapies.

Electronic databases were searched on August 7, 2025 (MEDLINE; Embase) or September 30, 2025 (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews; CENTRAL), and conference proceedings (2021-2025), bibliographies, and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched on August 15, 2025, following a pre-specified protocol (PROSPERO ID: CRD42023382262). Interventional and observational studies in the 2L/2L+ setting were prioritized for inclusion, and randomized controlled trials for extraction.

A total of 149 publications on 44 unique studies were included. Phase III trials evaluated elafibranor, seladelpar, budenoside, bezafibrate, and obeticholic acid. Various composite biochemical response endpoints were reported across 34% of studies, most frequently the POISE criteria (18%). Biochemical markers were assessed frequently (ALP: 89%; TB: 71%); however, upper limits of normal and response thresholds varied substantially. Response rates in placebo-controlled trials favored active treatment, although placebo responses were highly variable. Non-invasive fibrosis measures were reported in 16% of studies and showed small short-term changes. Patient-reported outcomes assessed pruritus (52%) and fatigue (32%) using a variety of scales.

The evidence base for 2L/2L+ PBC therapies is extensive but heterogeneous in study designs, patient populations, and treatment assessment scales and outcome definitions, limiting cross-trial comparisons. This SLR provides a robust foundation for contextualizing study outcomes within the broader landscape and supporting clinical decision-making.
