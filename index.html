💡 Conclusion
This study aimed to investigate the dose-response relationships between exercise prescription parameters and a range of health outcomes in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC). The following PRISMA guidelines, a systematic search of five databases was conducted. The inclusion criteria were randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of exercise interventions lasting ≥ 4 weeks in patients with CRC. Robust Variance Estimation (RVE) was used to synthesize the overall effects of exercise interventions across six outcome domains, including cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) and muscular fitness (MF). Univariable RVE regression models and MetaForest analyses were employed to examine the moderating effects of two groups of variables: exercise prescription moderators and patient background characteristics. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane tool, and the quality of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE approach. A total of 26 RCTs, involving 1731 CRC patients and 266 effect sizes, were included. CRF (ES = 0.35, p < 0.01) and MF (ES = 0.45, p < 0.01) showed statistically significant improvements. Regression analyses revealed threshold effects for both CRF and MF, with maximal improvements requiring sustained engagement in moderate-to-high intensity exercise. Subgroup analyses indicated that prehabilitation interventions were the most effective, while interventions during active treatment exhibited a decline-rebound trend over time. Based on these findings, high-frequency and moderate-to-high volume interventions with individualized load regulation are recommended during the pre-treatment phase. During active treatment, a more cautious approach is advised, involving reduced exercise volume during peak symptom periods and gradual progression during recovery.

The global impact of skin injuries is substantial, compromising the wellbeing of affected individuals. Bacterial cellulose is gaining traction as a prospective dressing material due to its distinct characteristics. Nevertheless, its clinical efficacy and safety warrant detailed investigation. This systematic examination and meta-analysis assessed the healing efficacy and safety parameters of bacterial cellulose used as a dressing in skin injury management.

Comprehensive database searches of CNKI, PubMed, Embase and Web of Science were conducted from database inception to 1 September 2024, in line with PRISMA guidelines. Studies included were randomised controlled trials that compared bacterial cellulose dressings (BCDs) with conventional treatment methods. Key outcome parameters were the healing of wounds, mitigation of pain and incidence of adverse events.

The collective analysis comprised 13 studies with 514 participants, divided equally between BCDs and control groups. A notable variance was observed in the healing duration (mean difference: -6.69 days), favouring the BCD group. Equivocal results emerged for healing cure rates (odds ratio (OR): 1.27), overall adverse reactions (OR: 0.4) and pain mitigation (OR: 2.45), with none achieving statistical significance. BCD was, however, observed to hasten wound size reduction and lessen the need for dressings to be changed.

The findings of this review showed that the use of BCD for wound treatment appeared to bolster the healing process and offer pain relief. Further extensive studies are required to confirm these preliminary observations and guide their clinical application.

The Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS) diagnostic algorithm assesses hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) probability of liver observations in high-risk individuals, but complexity may limit its adoption.

To evaluate whether LI-RADS v2018 can be simplified without impairing diagnostic performance by removing threshold growth (TG) and reducing size cut-offs.

Retrospective meta-analysis.

Eight thousand five hundred eighty-two patients (mean age: 59 ± 11 years; 6610 male) at risk for HCC with CT/MRI (986 CT, 6554 MRI, and 1042 CT/MRI).

Field strength: 1.5T and 3.0T; sequences varied by institution per LI-RADS technical requirements.

LI-RADS v2018 using major features only was compared with seven simplifications removing TG and/or applying single size cut-offs (≥ 10, ≥ 15, and ≥ 20 mm) in individual participant data (IPD) of 64 studies (29 institutions, 11 countries) from the LI-RADS Living Systematic Review and IPD Meta-analysis Project. Risk of bias was assessed with QUADAS-2.

Summary receiver operating characteristic curves estimated area under the curve (AUC) across LR-3 to LR-5; LR-5 sensitivity, specificity, and positive predictive value (PPV) were estimated using a one-step bivariate mixed-effects model. Differences were assessed by cluster bootstrap. p < 0.05 indicated significance, uncorrected for multiple comparisons.

Nine thousand four hundred six observations were included. Excluding TG with a 10-mm cut-off showed AUC similar to LI-RADS v2018 (0.80 vs. 0.81, p = 0.44) and significantly higher LR-5 sensitivity (73.8% vs. 71.8%) with similar specificity (72.5% vs. 73.7%, p = 0.13) and PPV (97.8% vs. 97.8%, p = 0.73). Removing TG with 15-mm and 20-mm cut-offs had significantly lower AUC than v2018 (-0.03 [95% CI: -0.041, -0.019]; -0.043 [-0.054, -0.032], respectively) and sensitivity (-13.2% [95% CI: -14.5%, -12.0%]; -30.1% [-31.7%, -28.5%], respectively). Of the 64 studies, 17 had low-risk of bias, and 56 had low applicability concerns.

Simplifying LI-RADS v2018 by removing TG and using a 10-mm size cut-off preserved diagnostic table AUC while improving LR-5 sensitivity without loss of specificity or PPV.

The optimal treatment strategy for patients with borderline resectable pancreatic cancer (BRPC) and locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC) remains undefined.
