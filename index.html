Pancreatic cancer is an aggressive malignancy, largely due to its vague clinical presentation and delayed diagnosis. Knowledge of the risk factors is essential to combat this disease. Increasing interest has been directed toward immune-related disorders and their effect on pancreatic cancer development. Studies have investigated the relationship between asthma and pancreatic cancer; however, findings are inconsistent, with some studies reporting a protective association, while others failed to detect any differences. In light of these inconsistent findings, we conducted a systematic review of the available literature to clarify the link between asthma and pancreatic cancer.

We identified studies published from 1978 through December 2025 that investigated the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer using PubMed, Scopus, and Embase databases, searched on 2 February 2026. Eligible studies were English-language comparative studies evaluating the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer, including case-control and cohort studies. Eligible outcomes were pancreatic cancer incidence or pancreatic cancer mortality. Studies that did not assess both asthma and pancreatic cancer were excluded. Studies were classified by design, assessed for bias and quality using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, and presented as a narrative review. This review was reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) reporting guideline.

A total of 27 studies were included, comprising 14 case-control studies and 13 cohort studies. Overall, the available literature demonstrates discordant findings regarding the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer. While some studies suggest a potential inverse relationship, others report a null or even positive association.

The current evidence on the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer remains inconsistent. The discrepancies across studies likely reflect variations in study design, differences in asthma ascertainment, and inconsistent adjustment for established confounders. Future studies with objective asthma diagnosis and adequate confounder control are warranted to clarify this association.

💡 Conclusion
The diagnosis of colorectal cancer encompasses nine complementary modalities: stool-based screening, blood-based biomarkers, cross-sectional imaging, endoscopic evaluation, histopathology, liquid biopsy, molecular and genetic profiling, microbiome analysis, and nanotechnology-based diagnostics. This narrative review synthesizes 160 references spanning seven task-specific artificial intelligence domains, including computer-aided detection, optical diagnosis, invasion depth prediction, computational pathology and radiogenomics, metastasis forecasting, neoadjuvant treatment response prediction, and surgical workflow analysis. We propose a conceptual multimodal pipeline comprising data ingestion, preprocessing and harmonization, feature representation, multimodal fusion, forecasting, explainability, and validation. A structured evidence grading framework classifies each clinical task by evidence level, consistency, and clinical readiness. We further catalog public datasets, challenge benchmarks, and five reporting standards: TRIPOD-AI, CONSORT-AI, SPIRIT-AI, DECIDE-AI, and CLAIM. Computer-aided detection and optical diagnosis demonstrate the highest maturity with Level 1 evidence from randomized controlled trials, while computational pathology, radiogenomics, and metastasis prediction remain at Level 2 with retrospective validation (internal or external). Microbiome screening, nanotechnology diagnostics, and surgical phase recognition are exploratory. The principal barriers to clinical adoption are domain shift, data scarcity, calibration, prospective validation, and workflow integration. We conclude that clinically useful artificial intelligence requires robust external validation, uncertainty quantification, explainable outputs, standardized reporting, and a structured translational pathway from retrospective evidence through regulatory clearance to post-deployment monitoring.

Early detection is critical for improving survival in colorectal carcinoma (CRC), underscoring the need for reliable early-stage biomarkers. Plasma protein biomarkers may support the detection of early-stage CRC. In this study, we profiled plasma from 24 patients with stages I-III CRC and 10 non-tumor participants, together with paired tumor and distant-normal tissues from the patients with CRC, to identify proteins preferentially elevated in stage I disease. Seven plasma proteins had higher abundance in stage I CRC than in stages II and III CRC and non-tumor participants; three of these proteins, HSPA8, ALDOA, and PDLIM1, were also elevated in stage I tumor tissues. Tissue upregulation was further supported by Western blotting. ELISA assessment included 32 participants with matched proteomic measurements and was subsequently expanded to 108 participants. The HSPA8-score was calculated by dividing the dilution-corrected HSPA8 concentration by the total protein amount in 10 μL of plasma. In the expanded cohort, random forest classification using this score achieved internally cross-validated accuracies of 74.47% for stage I versus stages II-IV CRC and 56.67% for stage I CRC versus non-tumor participants. The corresponding exploratory reference cutoffs were 0.066 and 0.029. These findings identify HSPA8 as a potential plasma protein biomarker for stage I CRC detection.

Early diagnosis of ovarian cancer is a significant clinical problem, and this is mainly because there are no strongly sensitive early-detection biomarkers, and the standard screening parameters like CA-125 blood tests and transvaginal ultrasounds are very insensitive in making a diagnosis. This paper presents a machine learning architecture, which is organized and interpretable based on the Prostate, Lung, Colorectal, and Ovarian (PLCO) Cancer Screening data to enhance the risk stratification of ovarian cancer. The original analytical cohort comprised 78,209 participants, including 518 ovarian cancer cases. To select 20 informative pre-diagnostic predictors in a high dimensional feature space, a comprehensive feature selection pipeline with ANOVA (f classifier), Chi-square, Random Forest importance, and XGBoost importance were used. Several classification algorithms were tested and the boosting based methods proved to have a good predictive value. A stacking ensemble of K-Nearest Neighbors and AdaBoost was the highest performing overall (accuracy: 0.7644), and a test-set AUROC of 0.8399, with additional evaluation using sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, balanced accuracy, MCC, and PR-AUC. SHAP analysis and permutation importance were used to provide model interpretability and consistently regarded questionnaire-based screening variables, age, and CA-125 attributes as the strongest ones. The suggested framework offers a methodologically rigorous and transparent method of assessment of population level ovarian cancer risk, providing a foundation for future research toward a clinically useful ovarian cancer screening decision-support system, pending external validation.

Gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms such as indigestion, bloating and reflux are common and can negatively affect quality of life. Bovine colostrum is a rich source of nutrients and other bioactive constituents that has been shown to be beneficial for gut health.

This 12-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 3-arm study aimed to evaluate the effects of MaolactinGI™, a proprietary bovine colostrum supplement, on upper GI symptoms and gut microbiome composition in healthy adults aged 18 years and older experiencing moderate GI discomfort. High-dose supplementation also preserved microbial richness relative to placebo (Chao1); exploratory within-group analyses further indicated enrichment of SCFA-associated commensal taxa. The intervention was well tolerated and demonstrated an acceptable safety profile.

These findings support the potential of targeted colostrum-derived bioactive protein fractions as a novel nutritional strategy for supporting GI health and microbiome balance.

Silymarin is a widely used hepatoprotective agent; however, its effects on hepatic steatosis and fibrosis remain unclear. We systematically reviewed the effects of silymarin on liver biochemical parameters, fibrosis, steatosis, and cardiometabolic outcomes, as well as its safety, in chronic liver disease.

PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were searched from inception to June 11, 2026. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and eligible comparative clinical studies that evaluated the use of oral silymarin were included. Risk of bias was evaluated using RoB 2 for randomized trials and ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies. Random-effects meta-analyses were performed for alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) in placebo-controlled RCTs of silymarin monotherapy and for liver stiffness measurement (LSM) in placebo-controlled RCTs, and the certainty of evidence was assessed using GRADE.

Forty-seven studies (37 RCTs and 10 non-randomized studies) met the inclusion criteria. Compared with placebo, silymarin monotherapy significantly reduced ALT (six trials; mean difference [MD], -7.25 IU/L; 95% confidence interval [CI], -11.60 to -2.89) and AST (four trials; MD, -5.36 IU/L; 95% CI, -8.45 to -2.27). Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is a systemic disorder in which cardiovascular, hepatic and oncological outcomes shape long-term prognosis. Statins are particularly relevant because their cardiovascular benefit is firmly established, yet they remain underused in MASLD, while their potential liver-related effects remain uncertain. Observational studies consistently associate statin exposure with slower fibrosis progression, fewer major adverse liver outcomes, lower hepatocellular carcinoma incidence and reduced liver-related mortality. We propose outcome convergence as a framework for assessing whether a therapy can improve more than one clinically relevant disease domain while preserving independent causal evaluation of each outcome. Statins should therefore be regarded as a candidate, rather than an established, outcome-convergent therapy.

Alcohol is therefore best viewed as a dose-, pattern-, and host-dependent cardiovascular stressor. Acute or binge drinking, sustained heavy intake, chronic intake with episodic binges, and withdrawal produce overlapping but distinct phenotypes, including hypertension, atrial fibrillation, ventricular arrhythmias, sudden death, alcohol-associated cardiomyopathy, heart failure, stroke, and vascular disease. A systems-cardiology framework is presented in which direct myocardial injury, electrical instability, haemodynamic and autonomic stress, vascular and microvascular dysfunction, and disrupted inter-organ signalling interact. Ethanol and acetaldehyde toxicity, redox imbalance, microbial and inflammatory mediators, neurohumoral activation, and metabolic or electrolyte disturbances can impair endothelial signalling, mitochondrial energetics, excitation-contraction coupling, electrical stability, and repair. Pathway dominance varies across exposure patterns and susceptible hosts; coronary microvascular dysfunction may represent a convergence pathway rather than an obligatory mediator. Cumulative injury can diminish perfusion, contractile, electrical, autonomic, and exercise reserve. Alcohol-related cardiovascular ageing is proposed as a unifying cumulative phenotype encompassing mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative and nitrative stress, inflammageing, senescence, impaired repair, circadian and epigenetic dysregulation, fibrosis, and ventriculo-arterial uncoupling, although direct human evidence remains incomplete. Age, biological sex, genetics, comorbidities, sleep-circadian disruption, nutrition, medications, and polysubstance misuse may modify pathway dominance and reversibility. Structured exposure assessment and alcohol reduction or abstinence, when indicated, are actionable components of cardiovascular prevention and care.

Pancreatic cancer is an aggressive malignancy, largely due to its vague clinical presentation and delayed diagnosis. Knowledge of the risk factors is essential to combat this disease. Increasing interest has been directed toward immune-related disorders and their effect on pancreatic cancer development. Studies have investigated the relationship between asthma and pancreatic cancer; however, findings are inconsistent, with some studies reporting a protective association, while others failed to detect any differences. In light of these inconsistent findings, we conducted a systematic review of the available literature to clarify the link between asthma and pancreatic cancer.

We identified studies published from 1978 through December 2025 that investigated the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer using PubMed, Scopus, and Embase databases, searched on 2 February 2026. Eligible studies were English-language comparative studies evaluating the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer, including case-control and cohort studies. Eligible outcomes were pancreatic cancer incidence or pancreatic cancer mortality. Studies that did not assess both asthma and pancreatic cancer were excluded. Studies were classified by design, assessed for bias and quality using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, and presented as a narrative review. This review was reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) reporting guideline.

A total of 27 studies were included, comprising 14 case-control studies and 13 cohort studies. Overall, the available literature demonstrates discordant findings regarding the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer. While some studies suggest a potential inverse relationship, others report a null or even positive association.

The current evidence on the association between asthma and pancreatic cancer remains inconsistent. The discrepancies across studies likely reflect variations in study design, differences in asthma ascertainment, and inconsistent adjustment for established confounders. Future studies with objective asthma diagnosis and adequate confounder control are warranted to clarify this association.

💡 Conclusion
Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibition reduces cardiovascular events in type 2 diabetes, while experimental studies implicate PCSK9 in immune activation, platelet function and organ-specific metabolism. Whether these mechanisms contribute clinically beyond atherogenic lipoprotein lowering remains uncertain. This critical narrative review integrates an original corpus of 108 appraised publications, with targeted additions during revision. It evaluates whether findings from different compartments and experimental perturbations can be transferred to particular treatments and clinical outcomes. Mechanistic evidence implicates receptor trafficking, intracellular cholesterol, mitochondrial stress and inflammatory signalling. Effects vary by cell type and compartment: local PCSK9 promotes injury in some models but preserves pancreatic, glomerular or cardiac function in others. Global knockout, tissue-specific deletion, extracellular antibodies and hepatic silencing are therefore distinct exposures. Human biomarker and imaging studies provide translational signals but generally cannot isolate direct effects from concomitant lipoprotein changes. Randomised antibody trials establish cardiovascular efficacy and an overall reassuring glycaemic profile, without demonstrating clinically meaningful glucose lowering. PCSK9 has broader immunometabolic functions, but the available evidence does not quantify an additional clinical benefit independent of atherogenic lipoprotein lowering. Tissue-specific mechanisms and causal mediation require further investigation.
