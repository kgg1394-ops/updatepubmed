Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are frequently used without long-term indication. Discontinuation remains challenging because of rebound symptoms. Whether digital guidance tools improve discontinuation is unknown. We evaluated whether an app improves PPI discontinuation.

In this open-label adaptive randomised controlled superiority trial, adults using daily PPI for ≥ 4 weeks without chronic PPI indication, according to the Dutch College of General Practitioners guideline, were recruited from outpatient clinics of Internal Medicine, Gastroenterology, Rheumatology and Nephrology departments at three Dutch hospitals. Patients were excluded if their physician deemed PPI use necessary beyond guideline recommendations, after prior upper gastrointestinal surgery, in case of inability to understand the study, unavailability of a smartphone/tablet, or limited life expectancy. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1), stratified by department and initial indication, to receive information on PPI discontinuation through an app (intervention) or brochure (control). Primary outcome was PPI discontinuation at 2 months follow-up, defined as self-declared intake of ≤ 1 tablet in the preceding 14 days. Sample-size was revised earlier than prespecified based on interim data. The trial is registered as closed to accrual at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05348252).

Between May 15, 2022, and March 31, 2023, 174 patients were randomized (86 intervention, 88 control). Nine and two patients, respectively, did not receive the allocated intervention. Of the 163 participants (mean age 54 y/o [SD 11], 76 males [47%]), 66/77 (86%) discontinued PPIs in the intervention group versus 55/86 (64%) in the control group (RR 1.34, 95% CI 1.11-1.61, p = 0.002). Adverse events occurred in 18/121 (15%) patients who discontinued PPI use and in 6/42 (14%) who did not. Only 4/121 (3%) and 1/42 (2%), respectively, were potentially attributable to PPI discontinuation.

An app-based intervention significantly improves discontinuation of inappropriate PPI use compared with an information brochure and could therefore be considered for PPI deprescribing initiatives.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
This expert-verified inventory shows that CIRT studies open to enrollment remain concentrated by center, disease site, and phase. Future progress may depend more on shared outcome definitions, transparent biological dose reporting, and registry-enabled or multicenter comparative designs than on additional isolated single-institution studies.
Radiotherapy with high linear energy transfer (LET) has shown promise for improving cancer outcomes. Carbon ion radiotherapy (CIRT) combines charged-particle dose conformality with enhanced biological effectiveness. However, clinical development remains constrained by limited center availability, modest trial accrual, and few randomized studies. We reviewed the global landscape of CIRT studies open to enrollment to identify current priorities and gaps.

This Particle Therapy Cooperative Group (PTCOG)-sanctioned project, conducted within the CIRT Guidelines Subcommittee, identified studies through registry review and institutional verification. ClinicalTrials.gov and the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials were searched from December 2025 through February 2026. We included interventional CIRT trials and prospective registries open to new enrollment and excluded studies not open to enrollment, retrospective cohorts, proton-only studies, and preclinical studies.

Thirty-six studies were identified: 35 interventional trials in 6 countries and 1 North American registry-based comparative-effectiveness study. Japan had the largest share, followed by Germany, China, Italy, Austria, and France. Three centers (SPHIC, QST, and HIT) accounted for 21 of 36 studies. The portfolio included 7 phase I, 23 phase II, 5 phase III, and 1 registry study. Pancreatic cancer and sarcoma-related tumors each accounted for 7 studies; head and neck cancers, 5; prostate and breast cancers, 3 each; glioblastoma, lung cancer, and gynecologic malignancies, 2 each; and mucosal melanoma, mixed solid tumors, kidney cancer, esophageal cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma, 1 each. Most studies were conducted at a single institution and emphasized feasibility, toxicity, local control, progression-free survival, or overall survival.

This expert-verified inventory shows that CIRT studies open to enrollment remain concentrated by center, disease site, and phase. Future progress may depend more on shared outcome definitions, transparent biological dose reporting, and registry-enabled or multicenter comparative designs than on additional isolated single-institution studies.

Before standardized screening guidance, anal dysplasia in HIV-positive MSM was identified through multiple diagnostic entry pathways. These findings establish a baseline for evaluating future implementation of guideline-concordant screening in HIV care settings.
Prior to the implementation of standardized screening guidance, multiple diagnostic entry pathways identified anal dysplasia in MSM living with HIV.

To describe diagnostic pathways leading to anal dysplasia identification in men who have sex with men (MSM) living with HIV over a 30-year period, before standardized anal cancer screening guideline implementation.

This longitudinal retrospective cohort study included cisgender men who have sex with men (MSM) living with HIV who received care at an urban HIV clinic from January 1, 1993, through December 31, 2023. A comprehensive manual electronic medical record review identified documented cases of anal dysplasia. Participants were categorized by diagnostic entry pathway: cytology-first, direct diagnostic evaluation without preceding cytology, or incidental detection during unrelated procedures. Demographic and clinical characteristics were described.

Among 347 eligible participants, 47 (13.5%) had documented anal dysplasia. Of these, 25 (53%) were identified through direct diagnostic evaluation, 17 (36%) following anal cytology, and 5 (11%) incidentally. Demographic and HIV-related clinical characteristics did not differ significantly between the cytology-first and direct diagnostic evaluation groups. Among participants in the cytology-first group, 15 (88%) had abnormal cytology results; of these, 9 (60%) completed follow-up evaluation with colorectal surgery, including 7 (78%) within 1 year. Limitations include retrospective design, single-center setting, and incomplete documentation.

Before standardized screening guidance, anal dysplasia in HIV-positive MSM was identified through multiple diagnostic entry pathways. These findings establish a baseline for evaluating future implementation of guideline-concordant screening in HIV care settings.

This study aimed to investigate the associations of two novel inflammatory-nutritional markers, the C-reactive Protein-Albumin-Lymphocyte (CALLY) index and C-reactive protein-to-lymphocyte ratio (CLR), with colorectal polyps, and to develop machine learning models for improved risk prediction.

The study conducted a retrospective analysis of 946 patients who underwent colonoscopy at the Second Qilu Hospital of Shandong University from 2023 to 2024. Clinical, laboratory, and endoscopic data were retrieved. Four machine learning models were developed: Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machine (SVM), eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM). Additionally, a Stacking ensemble model was constructed, integrating RF, XGBoost, and SVM with logistic regression as the meta-learner. Model performance was evaluated using metrics including the Area Under the Curve (AUC), accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and Kappa coefficient.

Both the CALLY index and CLR were significantly associated with the presence of colorectal polyps. The Stacking ensemble model achieved the highest predictive performance (AUC: 0.96), outperforming all individual base models. The RF, SVM, and XGBoost models also attained robust predictive capability (AUC: 0.75-0.76). By comparison, LightGBM exhibited suboptimal performance (AUC = 0.72). Aspirin (Asa) and metformin (Met) have shown preventive efficacy in colorectal cancer (CRC). Preclinical data suggest convergence on NFκB/PI3K-mTOR signalling and potential synergy. The ASAMET phase II trial tested this hypothesis in patients with resected stage I-III CRC.

In a 2 × 2 factorial, multicentre, double-blind study (EudraCT 2015-004824-77; NCT03047837), 51 patients were randomized to placebo, Asa 100 mg qd, Met 850 mg twice daily, or AsaMet for 12 months. Biopsies of normal colonic mucosa and fasting blood samples were obtained at baseline and 12 months. NFκB (primary endpoint), Ki67, EGFR, and pS6K were assessed by immunohistochemistry.

After 12 months, compared with placebo, Met reduced global NFκB by -9.04 (95% CI, -17.55 to -0.54; p = 0.04), whereas Asa alone was inactive (-3.58; 95% CI, -12.15 to 4.99; p = 0.41), and AsaMet showed a non-significant reduction (-8.88; 95% CI, -18.86 to 1.10; p = 0.08). Pooled analyses confirmed a Met-driven effect (Met vs non-Met: -7.29; 95% CI, -13.79 to -0.79; p = 0.03), with no impact of Asa (p = 0.73). Ki67 and pS6K were unchanged globally; however, AsaMet increased right-sided Ki67 (12.48; 95% CI, 0.45 to 24.51; p = 0.04). No treatment modulated pS6K. Toxicity was manageable, and no serious adverse events occurred.

Met reduces NFκB expression in normal colorectal mucosa of CRC survivors, supporting its potential as a low-toxicity preventive agent. Aspirin did not favourably affect mucosal biomarkers. These findings support tissue-based endpoints and further evaluation of metformin for CRC prevention.

German Clinical Trials Register, DRKS00036327 (retrospectively registered, 22.04.2025).
Early mobilization is a key component of enhanced recovery after elective colorectal surgery. This pilot trial evaluated the safety and feasibility of an early supervised postoperative exercise intervention in addition to standard care.

In this single-center randomized controlled pilot trial, 31 patients undergoing elective colorectal surgery were randomized to an intervention group (n = 16) or control group (n = 15). One patient withdrew after randomization, leaving 30 patients for modified intention-to-treat analysis. The intervention consisted of individualized, physiotherapist-supervised exercise sessions on postoperative days 1, 3, and 5. The control group received standard postoperative care according to the institutional ERAS protocol. The originally registered primary endpoint was time to first bowel movement; prior to final analysis, safety and feasibility were designated as the primary outcomes in accordance with the pilot nature of the study. The change was documented transparently. Secondary outcomes included physical performance (Short Physical Performance Battery [SPPB], handgrip strength), time to first bowel movement, patient-reported outcomes, and postoperative complications.

Thirty patients completed the study (retention 96.8%). Adherence to the intervention was 100%, and no intervention-related adverse events occurred. The intervention group showed significantly better preservation of physical performance, reflected by a more favorable change in SPPB score from baseline to postoperative assessment. No significant between-group differences were observed for time to first bowel movement, handgrip strength, postoperative complications, or other secondary outcomes.

Early supervised postoperative exercise after elective colorectal surgery was safe and feasible. Although no differences were found for most secondary outcomes, the intervention improved postoperative physical performance. These exploratory findings warrant confirmation in adequately powered randomized trials.

▶Exercise as anticancer medicine? Evidence, mechanisms and implementation in oncology.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Research over the past two decades has repositioned exercise from a general lifestyle recommendation to a potential anticancer intervention. Pooled analyses of more than 1.4 million participants link regular physical activity with lower incidence of at least 13 tumour types, and prospective cohort data report cancer-specific mortality 24-31% lower among the most active patients with breast, colorectal, prostate and lung cancer, although these estimates derive from observational designs using self-reported activity. In 2025, a phase III trial in stage III colon cancer provided the first randomised evidence that a structured exercise and behaviour-change programme improves disease-free and overall survival after adjuvant chemotherapy; a randomised trial in ovarian cancer subsequently did not show a progression-free survival benefit. Candidate mechanisms include insulin and growth-factor signalling, myokine secretion, immune mobilisation, reduced chronic inflammation and gut microbiome remodelling, although these rest on evidence ranging from cell culture to human trials and are rarely distinguished by level. Despite this evidence, structural, educational and economic barriers prevent integration of exercise into routine oncology care. This Review synthesises the available evidence and identifies the steps required for clinical adoption.

▶The prevalence of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: ⚠️ 기존 유지 권장
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
The epidemiology of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) remains poorly characterized. Existing evidence is fragmented across specific conditions or confined to single regions, and no study has simultaneously assessed the principal nutritional disorders-malnutrition, overweight or obesity, and sarcopenia-in a unified framework. To address this gap, we systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and China Biology Medicine from inception to July 31, 2024 and updated on July 30, 2026. Observational studies reporting prevalence based on standard diagnostic criteria were included. Pooled estimates with 95% confidence interval (CI) and prediction interval (PI) were derived using a random-effects model. Pre-specified subgroup analyses were conducted by sex, age, disease subtype and activity, diagnostic criteria, study setting and gepgraphic variation. Study quality and publication bias were also assessed. After literature screening, 201 studies across 32 countries were included. Underweight affected 11.5% (95% CI: 9.0-14.6%, 95% PI : 1.0-61.7), whereas consensus-based malnutrition [GLIM (Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition), ESPEN (European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism), MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment), SGA/PG-SGA (Subjective Global Assessment or its variant Patient-Generated SGA) affected 27.6% (95% CI: 20.5-36.1%, 95% PI : 3.6-79.7). Notably, this malnutrition estimate was derived almost exclusively from hospital-based settings (over 80%) and predominantly from a single region. Overweight and obesity affected 23.1% (95% CI : 20.6-25.9, 95%PI : 8.1-50.8) and 13.5% (95%CI : 11.7-15.6, 95%PI : 2.9-44.6), respectively. For muscle depletion, low muscle mass defined by skeletal muscle index was identified in 48.3% (95% CI: 41.8-55.0%, 95% PI : 19.7-78.1), whereas consensus-based sarcopenia [EWGSOP (European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People) or AWGS (Asian Working Group on Sarcopenia)] affected 12.6% (95% CI: 6.1-24.2%, 95% PI : 0.9-70.6). Of note, the majority of included studies for malnutrition and sarcopenia were hospital-based. Active disease was associated with significantly higher prevalence of malnutrition. No significant differences were observed between disease subtypes. In conclusion, our findings provide a broad overview of the prevalence of major nutritional disorders in patients with IBD, highlighting that nutritional care in IBD should extend beyond simple weight monitoring. However, given the wide prediction intervals, these pooled estimates should be viewed as averages across highly heterogeneous settings rather than a precise global figure. Future research, particularly population-based studies with standardized diagnostic criteria across diverse regions, is needed to validate our observation.

▶Deploying machine learning and metagenomics for predicting gut microbial drug biotransformation in colorectal cancer.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the leading causes of death globally. Chemotherapy is considered as one of the standard treatment option for CRC. Although effective in many cases, drugs used in chemotherapy are often associated with adverse toxicity. Inter-individual variations in drug response are often attributed to pharmacogenomic associations, and current evidence suggests involvement of gut microbes as a contributor to drug response variations. While several studies have implemented machine learning to predict drug responses mediated by gut microbial enzymes, these predictions mainly rely on substrate information and bacterial genome data. In this study, we have developed CRCGMMeta Predictor, a tool that predicts gut microbial enzymes and species capable of biotransforming drugs. Unlike existing tools, CRCGMMeta Predictor integrates enzyme prediction with species-level abundance in CRC metagenomes using eight geographically diverse metagenomic studies. The tool was trained on 437,079 species-specific enzyme data, incorporating 3457 substrate and protein-encoding features. Multiple machine learning models were evaluated, with the random forest model achieving 99% accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score and ROC-AUC metrics. Validation of the generated model with a blind set achieved 97% accuracy, while 10-fold cross-validation achieved an accuracy of 0.831 ± 0.068. Based on the above results, the random forest model was selected to build the CRCGMMeta Predictor tool. This was further assessed against fluoropyrimidine drugs and its ability to predict enzymes supported by literature evidence and those predicted by existing computational tools. These findings highlight the potential of CRCGMMeta Predictor in identifying microbiome-mediated drug responses.

Summary statistics from genome-wide association studies for insomnia and eleven cancer types were examined. instrumental variables (IVs) were selected from single nucleotide polymorphisms that passed strict quality control for minimal linkage disequilibrium and genome-wide significance. All IVs demonstrated strong predictive power for insomnia, with F-statistics exceeding the conventional threshold of 10. The primary analysis was conducted using the inverse variance weighted method. Sensitivity analyses were performed using MR-Egger regression, weighted median, and weighted mode methods to test robustness, pleiotropy, and heterogeneity. MR analysis suggested potential causal associations between insomnia and increased risk of hepatic bile duct cancer (odds ratio [OR] = 4.458, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.074-18.493, P = .040), reduced risk of hepatic cancer (OR = 0.312, 95% CI: 0.100-0.973, P = .045), and reduced risk of skin cancers (OR = 0.599, 95% CI: 0.439-0.818, P = .001). However, after Bonferroni correction (adjusted threshold P < .0045), these associations did not retain statistical significance. No notable causal influence of these cancers on insomnia was observed. Sensitivity analyses showed no evidence of reverse causality or significant bias. These findings, though not retaining statistical significance after stringent multiple testing correction, provide suggestive evidence that genetically predicted insomnia may influence the risk of certain cancers, potentially through mechanisms involving genetic predisposition and biological pathways that modulate inflammation, immune function, and circadian rhythms. The study highlights the translational value of understanding insomnia's role in cancer risk, suggesting that clinicians should consider this association when managing patients with insomnia. Further research with larger sample sizes is warranted to validate these findings.
