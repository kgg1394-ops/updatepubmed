We evaluated whether adherence to acute kidney injury (AKI) specific process measures (PMs) improves outcomes in hospitalized patients with cirrhosis and AKI.

Expert bodies recommend PMs for the management of AKI in cirrhosis, but the link between PMs and outcomes is unclear.

We performed a retrospective cohort study of patients hospitalized with ascites and AKI at Veterans Administration hospitals. We examined the relationship between PMs [avoidance of diuretics, nonselective beta-blockers (NSBB), and other vasodilators, volume expansion with albumin/crystalloids, infectious work-up, subspecialty consultation, and postdischarge outpatient visit] and patient outcomes. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the most prevalent chronic liver condition and is linked to unhealthy lifestyle patterns. Dietary modification remains the cornerstone of management. Among available nutritional strategies, ketogenic diets (KDs) have gained increasing attention. This systematic review evaluated the impact of KD, versus no dietary intervention or control diets, on hepatic and metabolic outcomes in adults with MASLD. Following PRISMA guidelines, PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science were searched through June 2026. Two reviewers independently screened records, assessed eligibility and extracted data. Risk of bias was evaluated using CASP for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies. Meta-analysis was not feasible due to study heterogeneity. Fourteen studies were included: five RCTs and nine prospective cohorts, involving 776 adults with MASLD and/or hepatic steatosis. KD was associated with improvements in MASLD severity indices, anthropometric measures, lipid profile, glycaemic markers and liver enzymes. Among RCTs, liver fat decreased by -4.77% (PDFF) to a 77% (relative MRI-LFF), with reductions in body weight (up to -12.0 kg) and waist circumference (up to -9.6 cm). Prospective studies showed similar findings, including reductions in controlled attenuation parameter (up to -57 dB/m), intrahepatic triglyceride (-31%) and Fatty Liver Index (-14.03 points). Evidence regarding liver stiffness and fibrosis-related outcomes was more limited and less consistent. KD may improve disease severity, hepatic steatosis, anthropometric measures, glycaemic control, lipid metabolism and liver-related biochemical markers in adults with MASLD. However, methodological heterogeneity precludes outcomes assessment, and limited evidence on fibrosis-related evidence precludes firm conclusions. Larger, longer-standardized RCTs are needed to clarify KD's role in MASLD management.

This systematic review suggests that ketogenic diets may reduce liver fat and improve body weight, blood sugar control, blood fat levels and liver blood tests in adults with metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease. Antibiotics are frequently overused in acute pancreatitis despite guideline recommendations against routine prophylactic therapy. Procalcitonin (PCT)-guided antibiotic stewardship may reduce unnecessary antibiotic exposure without compromising clinical outcomes. This study evaluated the efficacy of a PCT-guided algorithm in patients with acute pancreatitis.

This prospective, single-centre, patient-blinded randomized controlled trial enrolled adults with acute pancreatitis. Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive either PCT-guided antibiotic therapy (PCT arm) or standard guideline-based management (non-PCT arm). This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of liquid biopsy-based molecular diagnostics for colorectal cancer.

Overall, these findings indicate that molecular liquid biopsy may serve as a potential non-invasive adjunct to complement traditional screening strategies, though further validation is required to confirm its clinical utility.

Major low anterior resection syndrome (LARS) affects approximately 60% of rectal cancer survivors, causing urgency and decreased bowel control, reducing quality of life. Bile acid malabsorption (BAM) is an under-recognized contributor whose prevalence and treatment efficacy remain unclear. This proof-of-concept study evaluated the effectiveness of colesevelam for treating LARS and described the prevalence of BAM.

This double-blind randomized placebo-controlled crossover trial was conducted between 2021 and 2024 in New Zealand. Adults aged 18-85 years with major LARS received colesevelam (up to 1.25 g, three times daily) or placebo for 5 weeks, separated by a 6-week washout period. A subset of patients underwent 75-selenium homocholic acid taurine (SeHCAT) testing for BAM. The outcomes of interest included changes in the LARS score, Fecal Incontinence Quality of Life Scale (FIQL), and Short-Form 12 (SF-12) questionnaire, with a composite outcome reflecting clinically important improvements.

Of 80 eligible patients, 49 were randomized. However, 36 patients completed the study after washout and were included in a per-protocol analysis (mean age 71 years; 69% male). Eight of 19 patients (42%) had BAM and exhibited significant improvements in SF-12 physical and mental item scores with colesevelam. Using composite criteria, colesevelam produced a 33% response rate, versus 8% for placebo, with higher rates in BAM-positive (50%) than BAM-negative (11%) patients.

An association has been found between alliin and reduced risks of various cancers; however, evidence for these causal relationships remain limited. This study utilized Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to assess the potential causal relationships between circulating alliin levels and the risks of 22 common cancers. We selected instrumental variables from the latest and largest European genome-wide association study (GWAS) datasets. First, we comprehensively assessed the causal relationships between blood alliin levels and the risks of 22 common cancers through two-sample MR analysis. Subsequently, we repeated the validation of instrumental variables using cancer GWAS statistics from different studies and merged the results by meta-analysis. Two-step MR analysis was used to investigate whether 91 inflammatory proteins mediate the effect of alliin on the risk of cancer. Transanal minimally invasive surgery(TAMIS) enables local excision of selected rectal neoplasms. Robotic TAMIS(R-TAMIS) may overcome technical constraints of conventional laparoscopic TAMIS(L-TAMIS), but whether these capabilities translate into better outcomes remains uncertain. We compared R-TAMIS and L-TAMIS, examining the evidence in the context of robotic procedural maturity. PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Library were searched through 2026 for comparative studies of adults undergoing TAMIS for rectal neoplasm.
