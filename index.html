Combined Bio-Bentall and LVAD implantation may achieve bridge to recovery in carefully selected young patients with end-stage valvular disease, but patient selection remains critical and outcomes are not guaranteed.
End-stage valvular heart disease with severe myocardial fibrosis is traditionally managed with heart transplantation.

A 31-year-old man with bicuspid aortic valve, severe regurgitation, ejection fraction of 16%, and extensive fibrosis (extracellular volume, 39.1%) underwent a Bio-Bentall procedure with left ventricular assist device (LVAD) implantation. After 27 months of support with optimized guideline-directed medical therapy, ejection fraction recovered to 66%, permitting minimally invasive device explantation. Paired histopathology demonstrated collagen volume fraction regression from 40.26% to 0.65%.

This case challenges the dogma that severe fibrosis precludes myocardial recovery. The outcome likely reflects synergistic effects of volume overload correction, sustained mechanical unloading, and aggressive neurohormonal blockade. However, bridge to recovery remains uncommon, requires long-term commitment, and is not reproducible in all comparable patients.

Combined Bio-Bentall and LVAD implantation may achieve bridge to recovery in carefully selected young patients with end-stage valvular disease, but patient selection remains critical and outcomes are not guaranteed.

Drug repurposing builds on pharmacological and safety information already available for an approved drug, but this advantage requires additional studies to establish the biological effect and related mechanisms of action. We conducted a clinically focused review of non-oncology drugs tested as active cancer treatments in interventional studies from 1 January 2021 to 31 July 2026. Randomized trials, phase II-III studies, biomarker-selected cohorts, and studies reporting pharmacokinetics or human target engagement were prioritized. Despite the large number of registered studies, the landscape is still dominated by early-phase, academically sponsored trials. ⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines offer a flexible approach to colorectal cancer (CRC) immunotherapy, with applications ranging from postoperative residual disease to metastatic cancer. This narrative review examines antigen selection, RNA engineering, nanodelivery, and clinical development, drawing on literature available through 21 October 2026. Tumor-associated antigens, shared driver mutations, and personalized neoantigens offer different advantages in population coverage, specificity, and manufacturing. Their activity also depends on RNA chemistry and carrier design, including lipid composition, endosomal release, APC targeting, tissue distribution, stability, and repeated dosing. Early clinical studies have demonstrated antigen-specific T-cell responses in selected patients, while randomized melanoma data provide a benchmark for combining vaccination with checkpoint blockade. The termination of the CRC adjuvant autogene cevumeran monotherapy trial highlights the difficulty of translating immune priming into recurrence prevention. Antigen heterogeneity, impaired HLA presentation, stromal exclusion, and liver-associated immune suppression remain important barriers in CRC. Advances in neoantigen prediction, self-amplifying RNA, and mechanism-based combinations may improve vaccine design, but their clinical value will depend on disease-specific trials that account for manufacturing feasibility and established oncological endpoints. The available evidence remains heterogeneous, with small CRC cohorts and limited randomized outcomes; the non-systematic search also introduces potential selection bias.

▶Machine learning survival models for colorectal cancer: advantages of random survival forests over classification algorithms.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer remains a major cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, motivating the development of accurate prognostic models. Despite advances in machine learning for survival prediction, a common practice in the literature reformulates time-to-event outcomes as binary variables and applies standard classification algorithms, disregarding censoring and the temporal nature of survival data. In this study, we critically evaluate this practice and argue that it is unnecessary and methodologically concerning, given the availability of machine learning methods specifically designed for survival analysis that properly account for censoring while offering comparable interpretability. Using population-based data from colorectal cancer patients in the state of São Paulo - Brazil , we revisit a recent classification-based study that employed a classification XGBoost model and refit the same data using Random Survival Forests (RSF) under multiple splitting rules. Predictive performance was evaluated using Harrell's concordance index and the Integrated Brier Score, and interpretability was assessed using SHAP values derived from a scalar risk quantity. RSF achieved stable performance and identified clinically meaningful predictors of mortality, while classification models systematically underestimated survival probabilities, particularly as censoring increased, a pattern confirmed through simulation studies. These findings reinforce the importance of using survival-specific machine learning methods for censored time-to-event data.

Most prognostic models for colon cancer are not tailored to the Chinese population or implemented clinically. This study aimed to identify independent risk factors for five-year conditional survival and compare conventional logistic regression with machine learning models to develop an optimal predictive model.

This retrospective study included 430 newly diagnosed colon cancer patients from Suzhou Municipal Hospital (2015-2022). Based on public transcriptome datasets including The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx), and Human Protein Atlas (HPA), we profiled the pan-cancer expression landscape of MYSM1 and its clinical relevance in CC. Survival analysis was validated using the TCGA, GSE44001, and our internal 44-case clinical cohort. A series of in vitro and in vivo functional assays were performed to explore the regulatory effects of MYSM1 on CC malignant behaviors and autophagy. ChIP-Seq, transcriptomic profiling, and correlation analysis were applied to screen potential downstream target genes of MYSM1, while functional enrichment analyses, e.g., Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and cellular rescue assays were conducted to verify the MYSM1-inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor type 1 (ITPR1)-autophagy regulatory axis. Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was further performed using the GWAS Catalog datasets to evaluate the genetic association of MYSM1 and ITPR1 with CC susceptibility, combined with validation in paired clinical CC specimens.

MYSM1 is aberrantly expressed across pan-cancer, and profoundly downregulated in CC tissues. High MYSM1 expression is positively correlated with benign clinicopathological characteristics of CC patients, consistent with its tumor-suppressive role. Functional experiments confirmed that MYSM1 restrains CC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro and in vivo, and significantly promotes cellular autophagy. Mechanistically, ITPR1 was identified as a key downstream gene epigenetically activated by MYSM1. ITPR1 functions as an autophagy inducer and is associated with favorable clinical phenotypes in CC, thereby mediating the anti-tumor effects of MYSM1, further forming a MYSM1-ITPR1-autophagy regulatory axis. Both MYSM1 and ITPR1 were downregulated in 44 paired CC tumor tissues, possessing diagnostic and prognostic potential for CC. MR analysis revealed a modest suggestive genetic association between genetically predicted MYSM1 and ITPR1 expression and CC susceptibility.

💡 Conclusion
Based on the theoretical and empirical assessments, SNP- and haplotype-based genomic prediction of complex traits are equivalent assuming short haplotypes, regardless of the population LD level, trait heritability, and epistasis type.
We theoretically and empirically compared single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)- and haplotype-based genomic prediction. We derived and computed the relative prediction accuracy of a quantitative trait loci (QTL) additive value based on two SNPs and a haplotype of size 2 and the relative magnitude of the maximum linkage disequilibrium (LD) values for haplotype and SNP alleles. The empirical assessment was based on two criteria, using a simulated dataset: the efficacy of SNP- and haplotype-based genomic additive relationship matrices to express genotypic similarity for identical by descent (IBD) and identical by state (IBS) alleles, and the additive and epistatic genetic values prediction accuracies over six generations of random matings, assuming seven types of digenic epistasis.

The values of efficacy of the haplotype-based QTL additive value prediction accuracy, relative to the SNP-based, showed that the two approaches are equivalent. The maximum LD value for a haplotype allele was higher than the maximum LD value for a SNP allele in only 10% of the scenarios. The correlations between the additive and the SNP- and haplotype-based genomic additive matrices showed that haplotypes expressed better than individual SNPs the genotypic similarity for IBD alleles. Considering the efficacy of the SNP- and haplotype-based genomic additive matrices to express genotypic similarity for IBS alleles, the correlations showed that SNPs and short haplotypes are also similar. Considering the additive value prediction accuracy over generations, in the high and low LD populations the increase in the haplotype size from 2 to 10 decreased the prediction accuracy. Regarding haplotypes of size 2, the absolute differences in accuracy between the SNP- and haplotype-based approaches were insignificant. Epistasis had a negative impact on the additive value prediction accuracy. Using SNPs and haplotypes of size 2, the predictions accuracies under epistasis were lower than the accuracies under the additive-dominance model, except for duplicate genes with cumulative effects and non-epistatic gene interaction. The decreases were higher for duplicate epistasis.

Based on the theoretical and empirical assessments, SNP- and haplotype-based genomic prediction of complex traits are equivalent assuming short haplotypes, regardless of the population LD level, trait heritability, and epistasis type.

▶Autoantibodies and Arrhythmias: An Underexplored Link in Myocarditis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Myocarditis is an inflammatory disease that may be associated with severe acute and chronic complications, including progression to inflammatory cardiomyopathy, characterized by persistent inflammation, cardiac dysfunction, and adverse ventricular remodeling. Arrhythmias are major determinants of morbidity and mortality across this disease spectrum and have traditionally been attributed to structural substrates such as myocardial edema, necrosis, and fibrosis. However, as the role of the immune system in myocarditis is increasingly recognized, growing attention has been focused on immune-mediated mechanisms capable of directly perturbing cardiac excitability. Circulating cardiac autoantibodies are frequently detected in patients with myocarditis. Beyond their potential diagnostic value, specific autoantibody subsets have been hypothesized to exert pathogenic effects by targeting receptors, ion channels, contractile proteins, and intercalated disk components involved in impulse generation, conduction, repolarization, calcium handling, and myocardial structural integrity. This narrative review evaluates the mechanistic basis of autoimmune arrhythmias, the available preclinical and clinical evidence linking autoantibodies to atrial and ventricular arrhythmias, and the potential role of autoantibody profiling in refining arrhythmic risk stratification. Particular attention is given to distinguishing direct evidence obtained in patients with myocarditis and inflammatory cardiomyopathy from findings derived from other cardiac or systemic diseases and experimental models, whose applicability to myocardial inflammatory disorders remains uncertain. Finally, we critically evaluate whether autoantibody testing may help identify patients more likely to benefit from immunomodulatory or immunosuppressive strategies, while emphasizing the current limitations related to small observational cohorts and the lack of randomized trials evaluating antibody-guided treatment strategies with dedicated arrhythmic endpoints.
