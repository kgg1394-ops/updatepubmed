Patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD; historically termed non-alcoholic fatty liver disease [NAFLD]) are at increased risk of advanced fibrosis. Conventional antidiabetic therapies vary in their hepatic effects and, aside from pioglitazone, provide limited direct hepatic benefit. This systematic review evaluates the efficacy of empagliflozin 10 mg, the guideline-recommended starting dose, on hepatic and metabolic outcomes in this population.

We systematically searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane Central through October 10, 2025, for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing empagliflozin 10 mg versus control (placebo or active comparators). Outcomes included liver fat content, liver stiffness measurement (LSM), controlled attenuation parameter (CAP), liver enzymes, and metabolic parameters. Pooled analyses were performed using mean differences (MDs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and a random-effects model.

Seven RCTs (n = 510) were included. Empagliflozin 10 mg significantly reduced CAP (MD - 19.75 dB/m, 95% CI - 36.51 to - 3.00), indicating improvement in hepatic steatosis, while no significant effect was observed on LSM (MD - 0.50 kPa, 95% CI - 1.53 to 0.54). Statistically significant reductions were observed in gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT; MD - 16.91 IU/L, 95% CI - 22.51 to - 11.31) and, less robustly, in alanine aminotransferase (ALT; MD - 3.35 IU/L, 95% CI - 6.32 to - 0.39), with a borderline reduction in aspartate aminotransferase (AST; MD - 7.33 IU/L, 95% CI - 14.60 to - 0.06). In metabolic outcomes, empagliflozin significantly reduced body weight (MD - 4.31 kg, 95% CI - 5.56 to - 3.06), body mass index (BMI) (MD - 1.42 kg/m², 95% CI - 1.78 to - 1.07), and waist circumference (MD - 3.57 cm, 95% CI - 4.38 to - 2.75). However, no significant changes were observed in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) (MD - 0.24%, 95% CI - 0.72 to 0.24) or fasting plasma glucose.

Empagliflozin 10 mg is associated with improvements in hepatic steatosis (CAP) and body composition in adults with T2DM and MASLD/MASH (NAFLD/NASH), whereas no significant effects were observed on liver stiffness, FIB-4, glycemic control, or the lipid profile. The evidence base is small, of short duration, and includes no histological endpoints; accordingly, no conclusions regarding antifibrotic efficacy can be drawn. Larger, longer-duration RCTs with paired histology are required.

Chronic lung diseases, particularly chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and interstitial lung disease, are major contributors to global morbidity and mortality. Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is highly prevalent in these populations and has been implicated in exacerbations and disease progression. This review examined recent guideline statements for these diseases, as well as observational, physiological, and interventional studies evaluating the association between GERD and chronic lung diseases. Colonoscopy is central to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) management, but repeated procedures may be limited by anxiety, discomfort and recovery burden. Remimazolam may provide faster recovery than conventional benzodiazepines while preserving the practical advantages of moderate sedation. We compared patient-reported satisfaction, recovery, safety and endoscopic performance with remimazolam versus midazolam in patients with IBD undergoing ileocolonoscopy.

In this single-centre, prospective, patient-blinded, non-inferiority study using randomized weekly time-block allocation, 200 adults with IBD undergoing elective ileocolonoscopy received remimazolam plus fentanyl or midazolam plus fentanyl. Patients were unaware of the sedative administered. Satisfaction was assessed using a self-administered four-point Likert scale immediately after the procedure and at 24 hours. The primary outcome was the proportion of patients reporting at least "satisfied" immediately after the procedure. Secondary outcomes included "very satisfied" responses, PADSS-defined discharge readiness at 10 and 20 min, adverse events and endoscopic performance. Bayesian analyses used a prespecified non-inferiority margin of -10 percentage points.

Overall satisfaction was high in both groups, and remimazolam was non-inferior to midazolam: estimated rates were 96.4% versus 97.3%; risk difference -0.9% (95% credible interval [CrI] -5.8% to 3.7%); posterior probability of non-inferiority 99.94%. "Very satisfied" responses were more frequent with remimazolam: 62.2% versus 48.3%; posterior mean difference 15.8% (95% CrI 3.3% to 28.1%); posterior probability of superiority 99.3%. At 24 hours, satisfaction was 95.0% (remimazolam) and 94.0% (midazolam). PADSS-defined discharge readiness favoured remimazolam at 10 min (65.0% vs 27.5%) and 20 min (88.3% vs 64.2%). Hypotension (3.0% vs 2.0%), hypoxaemia (3.0% vs 4.0%) and flumazenil use (3.0% vs 2.0%) occurred (remimazolam vs midazolam); no severe adverse events occurred. Endoscopic performance was comparable.

Remimazolam plus fentanyl was non-inferior to midazolam plus fentanyl for overall satisfaction, with more "very satisfied" responses and earlier PADSS-defined discharge readiness, without compromising safety or endoscopic performance.

Peritoneal surface malignancies (PSM) represent a heterogeneous group of diseases requiring multidisciplinary management and individualized treatment strategies. The PSOGI 2025 Congress held in Barcelona in October 2025 brought together over eight hundred international experts to discuss advances in tumor biology, diagnostic imaging, surgical techniques, perioperative therapies, and emerging technologies. This manuscript summarizes ten key messages with potential implications for clinical practice and future research.

A structured synthesis of the ten main take-home messages presented at PSOGI 2025 was conducted. Scientific presentations, clinical trial findings, expert consensus statements, and emerging research displayed at the Congress were screened and integrated to identify advances and challenges across the continuum of PSM care.

Key findings highlighted the growing role of molecular profiling in appendiceal neoplasms and disease extent in prognostic assessment. Randomized trials reinforced the need for careful patient selection, demonstrating that perioperative chemotherapy may improve disease-free outcomes in colorectal peritoneal metastases without consistently improving overall survival, while CRS/HIPEC did not demonstrate a survival benefit in selected patients with gastric cancer and limited peritoneal disease. Diffusion-weighted MRI may reduce futile surgery, and minimally invasive CRS/HIPEC appears feasible in carefully selected patients, although further validation is needed. Additional priorities included occupational safety, frailty assessment, prehabilitation, patient advocacy, artificial intelligence, and intraperitoneal immunotherapy.

PSOGI 2025 highlighted a shift towards biologically informed decision-making, individualized treatment, and patient-centered care. Multidisciplinary management, standardized assessment, and rigorous patient selection remain essential. Although artificial intelligence and novel immunotherapies offer promising opportunities, prospective validation and international collaboration are required to establish their clinical value and improve survival and quality of life in patients with PSM.

The erector spinae plane (ESP) block has been shown to improve recovery in abdominal procedures, but its efficacy in the IBD population remains unknown. We investigated whether bilateral ESP blocks improve postoperative quality of recovery in this population.

In this prospective, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial, adults with IBD scheduled for elective laparoscopic-assisted abdominal surgery were randomized to receive bilateral ESP blocks with either 0.5% ropivacaine or normal saline before induction of anesthesia. The primary outcome was quality of recovery assessed using the 15-item QoR (QoR-15) scale at postoperative day (POD) 2. Secondary outcomes included QoR-15 at POD 1 and 3, pain scores (VAS), opioid consumption, opioid-related side effects, and hospital length of stay.

▶Causal effect of metabolic syndrome on colorectal cancer: A cross-ancestry genomic SEM and mendelian randomization study.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
This study provides strong evidence for the causal relationship between MetS and CRC by defining MetS as a single genetic entity. Clinically, these findings highlight the importance of early detection and management of MetS to potentially reduce CRC burden.
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a leading cause of cancer death worldwide. Metabolic syndrome (MetS), a prevalent and modifiable condition, is a suggested risk factor for CRC, though observational evidence remains inconsistent. This study investigated whether MetS, as an integrated clinical and genetic construct, exerts a causal effect on CRC.

Genetic association data for this study were primarily sourced from Taiwan Biobank, BioBank Japan Project, UK Biobank and NCBI to ensure robust cross-ancestry representation. Genomic structural equation modeling was applied to derive genetic instruments reflecting the shared genetic basis of MetS. A two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) design was then employed to assess the causal effect of MetS on CRC. The primary analysis was conducted using the inverse-variance weighted method, supplemented by MR-Egger, weighted median, LASSO, and MR-PRESSO to evaluate consistency and account for potential pleiotropy and heterogeneity.

A significant causal relationship is observed between genetically predicted MetS and CRC. Among the methods reaching statistical significance (IVW, LASSO, MR-PRESSO), the estimated odds ratios ranged from 1.45 to 1.55 in East Asians and 1.22 to 1.25 in Europeans; weighted median and MR-Egger estimates were directionally consistent but did not reach significance in either population. Cochran's Q test indicates heterogeneity; however, the MR-Egger intercept suggests no directional pleiotropy. IVW, LASSO, and MR-PRESSO consistently supported a causal effect of MetS on CRC, while weighted median and MR-Egger estimates were directionally consistent but did not reach statistical significance, and the leave-one-out analysis confirmed the robustness of the causal estimates.

This study provides strong evidence for the causal relationship between MetS and CRC by defining MetS as a single genetic entity. Clinically, these findings highlight the importance of early detection and management of MetS to potentially reduce CRC burden.

💡 Conclusion
The effect of albumin in decompensated cirrhosis is indication-specific, with a survival benefit demonstrated only in SBP and hepatic encephalopathy and more frequent pulmonary complications across indications, supporting indication-based use with monitoring for fluid overload.
Intravenous albumin is widely used in decompensated cirrhosis, but its efficacy across clinical indications remains inconsistent. Therefore, we evaluated the effect of albumin on mortality, major complications, and safety separately for each indication.

We searched PubMed (MEDLINE), Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, and Web of Science from inception to December 2025. Primary analyses included only randomized trials in which albumin was the randomized contrast; within each indication, comparisons with standard care or placebo were analyzed separately from comparisons with an alternative plasma expander. Non-randomized studies were analyzed separately. Random-effects risk ratios (RRs) were calculated.

Thirty-two studies were included (27 randomized); 22 randomized trials with 3,582 participants reported mortality. Hepatic progression is common in metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). We evaluated hepatic arterial infusion (HAI) floxuridine (FUDR) added to systemic chemotherapy in selected patients with synchronous liver-dominant metastatic PDAC.

PANCPUMP was a prospective, single-center, single-arm phase II pilot study of 14 patients with synchronous PDAC liver metastases and stable disease or response after at least 2 months of systemic chemotherapy. Patients underwent HAI pump placement and received up to six 28 day cycles of HAI FUDR with systemic therapy. The primary endpoint was 1 year hepatic progression-free survival, with a prespecified target of 40%.

Seven patients (50%) remained free of hepatic progression at 1 year, meeting the primary endpoint. Median time to hepatic progression was 15.7 months when deaths without hepatic progression were censored; median all-site progression-free interval and overall survival were 14.5 and 14.0 months, respectively. Five patients (35.7%) survived at least 24 months. Biochemical hepatotoxicity was frequent; two delayed grade 3 gastroduodenal artery (GDA)/catheter-associated vascular complications occurred. Among 10 patients with paired European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Core Quality-of-Life questionnaire assessments, the mean global health/quality-of-life score was 69.2 at baseline and 70.0 at first follow-up (p = 0.91).

HAI FUDR plus systemic therapy met the prespecified hepatic-control endpoint in a highly selected cohort. These proof-of-concept findings support further study of regional hepatic therapy alongside contemporary systemic treatment.

Pancreatic cancer has a poor prognosis and marked heterogeneity, whereas existing pathomics models generally lack biological interpretability. We aimed to construct the pathomics prognostic score GlyPath-Risk and explore the molecular states associated with the high-risk phenotype captured by the score.

We integrated H&E whole-slide images from TCGA-PAAD and Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (FMUUH), extracted handcrafted and deep-learning features using CellProfiler and an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50, and constructed GlyPath-Risk using a combined LASSO and random survival forest model. Prognostic performance was evaluated in training, internal validation, and FMUUH cohorts. Correlation analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), and xCell immune-infiltration estimation were used to investigate associations among GlyPath-Risk, the glycosylation transcriptional program, and the tumor microenvironment. Single-cell transcriptomics, CellChat cell-cell communication analysis, Geneformer virtual perturbation, and B3GNT3 knockdown experiments were used to generate and evaluate candidate biological hypotheses.

GlyPath-Risk comprised six pathological features and showed C-indices of 0.88, 0.68, and 0.83 in the training, internal validation, and FMUUH cohorts, respectively. Its association with overall survival remained after adjustment for age, sex, and clinical stage in the reported Cox model (multivariable HR = 1.230). The glycosylation gene set showed the strongest mean absolute correlation with pathological features among 20 post-translational modification classes. WGCNA identified six co-expression modules significantly associated with GlyPath-Risk (BH-FDR < 0.05), including a blue module containing B3GNT3. Immune-cell score analyses showed nominal associations with selected immune- and vascular-related scores, but none of 51 candidate scores reached BH-FDR < 0.05. Single-cell analysis suggested differences in predicted outgoing communication from B3GNT3-high and B3GNT3-low tumor cells, while virtual perturbation identified transcriptional states associated with perturbation sensitivity. B3GNT3 knockdown was associated with reduced proliferation and altered macrophage-associated markers in conditioned-medium experiments.

GlyPath-Risk showed prognostic associations across the evaluated cohorts. The score was associated with a B3GNT3-related glycosylation transcriptional program and may be related to immune- and vascular-related transcriptomic features. These findings provide a testable framework for investigating the biological correlates of pathomics scores and nominate B3GNT3 as a candidate molecular anchor requiring independent validation.

Acute kidney injury is a frequent and serious complication of acute pancreatitis (AP), contributing to increased mortality and healthcare burden. Major adverse kidney events within 30 days (MAKE30) constitute a patient-centered composite endpoint for assessing kidney outcomes. However, effective tools for identifying patients at high risk of MAKE30 are lacking. This study aimed to develop and externally validate an interpretable machine learning model for predicting MAKE30 in critically ill patients with AP.

Three clinical datasets were used. MIMIC-IV served as the development cohort, and external validation used eICU-CRD and a retrospective cohort from the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (AHSWMU). Feature selection combined least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, the Boruta algorithm, and multivariable logistic regression. Seven machine learning algorithms were used to develop prediction models. Model performance was assessed in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) were used to interpret and visualize the final model.

A total of 1,574 patients with AP from multiple centers were included. Six predictors were selected for model development: serum creatinine, red cell distribution width, albumin, aspartate aminotransferase, vasopressor use, and total bilirubin. The random forest model demonstrated favorable overall performance, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.863 in the internal validation cohort. Performance was consistent across the two independent external validation cohorts, with AUCs of 0.840 in eICU-CRD and 0.848 in AHSWMU. A web-based clinical risk calculator was developed to facilitate model use in clinical practice.

The model developed in this study exhibited favorable performance and robust generalizability in predicting MAKE30 among critically ill patients with AP, providing reliable digital decision support for early risk stratification and individualized intervention strategies.

The genetic regulation of beta-cell function, glycaemic control and insulin action remains incompletely resolved. We sought to prioritize druggable gene-expression targets for these complementary metabolic traits.

We integrated the Finan druggable genome with eQTLGen and performed cis-eQTL Mendelian randomization across 15 metabolic traits. Exploratory cross-axis annotation and colocalization of outcome-wise false-discovery-rate (FDR)-positive associations were conducted independently. Candidate genes were evaluated using summary-data-based MR (SMR) with heterogeneity testing across six tissues, pancreatic single-cell transcriptomics, cellular stress-expression assays, Cry2-siRNA experiments with protein-normalized insulin secretion, protein networks and pharmacological mapping. Sensitivity analyses examined multiple testing, intervention directions, colocalization priors, conditional signals and donor covariates.

Quality control retained 1808 genes and 35 531 gene-variant instruments. The exploratory cross-axis analysis identified 70 genes. Separately, colocalization of 1015 FDR-positive associations identified 18 genes, of which 10 had high-confidence tissue SMR support. Six were retained as core candidates: inhibition of CRY2, AGER and ITIH3 and activation of MPI, mTOR and ITGB2. Each had at least one favourable association after global Bonferroni correction, although nominal phenotype directions and colocalization-prior robustness varied. Covariate-adjusted single-cell analyses did not establish differential-expression confirmation. Stress-expression changes were compatible with five proposed intervention directions; MPI was not significant. In MIN6 cells exposed to glucolipotoxicity, Cry2-siRNA treatment was associated with higher stimulated insulin secretion (109.90 vs. 67.11 ng/μg protein; adjusted p < 0.01). Pharmacological mapping yielded no high-confidence novel disease-modifying candidate.

The evidence prioritizes six candidates with different strengths and limitations for metabolic-trait research. Cry2-siRNA treatment was associated with improved stimulated secretion in stressed MIN6 cells, supporting further functional investigation. These results do not establish clinical remission, tissue-specific mediation or therapeutic efficacy.
