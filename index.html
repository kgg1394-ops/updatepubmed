▶Cirrhotic Cardiomyopathy and Related Cardiocirculatory Dysfunction: A Systematic Review of Therapeutic Responses and Cardiorenal Outcomes.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 Paracentesis + Albumin 고려

📌 Order: 📌 USG 6개월 간격
💡 Conclusion
Cardiocirculatory dysfunction in cirrhosis encompasses impaired cardiac reserve, diastolic dysfunction, systemic vasodilation, neurohormonal activation, and progressive reliance on compensatory cardiac activity. This systematic review evaluated how pharmacological interventions affecting cardiac function, vascular tone, effective circulating volume, or natriuretic signaling influence cardiovascular, renal, and clinical outcomes in adults with cirrhosis. MEDLINE via PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) were searched for clinical studies published from August 1, 2016, through July 31, 2026. Eligibility was structured according to the Population, Intervention, Comparator, and Outcomes (PICO) framework, and study selection followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 (PRISMA 2020) guidelines. Five studies involving 465 participants were included in a narrative synthesis. Metoprolol did not restore impaired cardiac reserve or improve cardiac morphology in established cirrhotic cardiomyopathy. In refractory ascites, propranolol suppressed sympathetic support of systolic function, reduced renal perfusion pressure, and impaired renal function. Conversely, carvedilol-based targeted heart-rate reduction, with ivabradine added when required, improved left ventricular diastolic function and was associated with fewer episodes of acute kidney injury and hepatic encephalopathy, although mortality was not significantly reduced in the randomized comparison. Ularitide caused clinically important reductions in blood pressure, decreased urine output, and increased adverse reactions, while targeted albumin therapy did not improve cardiovascular biomarkers, renal dysfunction, or survival. The four randomized study results raised some concerns about bias, while the nonrandomized study had moderate risk of bias. These findings indicate that cardiovascular interventions in cirrhosis cannot be characterized as uniformly beneficial or harmful. Their effects may depend on disease severity, cardiac phenotype, and the extent to which sympathetic and hemodynamic compensation is required to preserve systemic and renal perfusion. Prospective, phenotype-stratified trials integrating cardiac, systemic, and renal endpoints are needed before individualized treatment strategies can be recommended.

▶Optimal Exercise Dose for Improving Multidimensional Health Outcomes in Colorectal Cancer Patients: A Systematic Review, Meta-Analysis and Meta-Regression.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
This study aimed to investigate the dose-response relationships between exercise prescription parameters and a range of health outcomes in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC). The following PRISMA guidelines, a systematic search of five databases was conducted. The inclusion criteria were randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of exercise interventions lasting ≥ 4 weeks in patients with CRC. Robust Variance Estimation (RVE) was used to synthesize the overall effects of exercise interventions across six outcome domains, including cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) and muscular fitness (MF). Univariable RVE regression models and MetaForest analyses were employed to examine the moderating effects of two groups of variables: exercise prescription moderators and patient background characteristics. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane tool, and the quality of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE approach. A total of 26 RCTs, involving 1731 CRC patients and 266 effect sizes, were included. CRF (ES = 0.35, p < 0.01) and MF (ES = 0.45, p < 0.01) showed statistically significant improvements. Regression analyses revealed threshold effects for both CRF and MF, with maximal improvements requiring sustained engagement in moderate-to-high intensity exercise. Subgroup analyses indicated that prehabilitation interventions were the most effective, while interventions during active treatment exhibited a decline-rebound trend over time. Based on these findings, high-frequency and moderate-to-high volume interventions with individualized load regulation are recommended during the pre-treatment phase. During active treatment, a more cautious approach is advised, involving reduced exercise volume during peak symptom periods and gradual progression during recovery.

💡 Conclusion
The findings of this review showed that the use of BCD for wound treatment appeared to bolster the healing process and offer pain relief. Further extensive studies are required to confirm these preliminary observations and guide their clinical application.
The global impact of skin injuries is substantial, compromising the wellbeing of affected individuals. Bacterial cellulose is gaining traction as a prospective dressing material due to its distinct characteristics. Nevertheless, its clinical efficacy and safety warrant detailed investigation. This systematic examination and meta-analysis assessed the healing efficacy and safety parameters of bacterial cellulose used as a dressing in skin injury management.

Comprehensive database searches of CNKI, PubMed, Embase and Web of Science were conducted from database inception to 1 September 2024, in line with PRISMA guidelines. Studies included were randomised controlled trials that compared bacterial cellulose dressings (BCDs) with conventional treatment methods. Key outcome parameters were the healing of wounds, mitigation of pain and incidence of adverse events.

The collective analysis comprised 13 studies with 514 participants, divided equally between BCDs and control groups. A notable variance was observed in the healing duration (mean difference: -6.69 days), favouring the BCD group. Equivocal results emerged for healing cure rates (odds ratio (OR): 1.27), overall adverse reactions (OR: 0.4) and pain mitigation (OR: 2.45), with none achieving statistical significance. BCD was, however, observed to hasten wound size reduction and lessen the need for dressings to be changed.

The findings of this review showed that the use of BCD for wound treatment appeared to bolster the healing process and offer pain relief. Further extensive studies are required to confirm these preliminary observations and guide their clinical application.

BackgroundPilonidal disease is a debilitating condition which is caused by an inflammatory process due to the penetration of a hair into the sacrococcygeal region. Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) has been growing in several types of surgical wounds; however, the evidence for its use in excision surgery for pilonidal disease is still small and heterogeneous. We conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis comparing this wound closure approach.MethodsPubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library databases were searched from inception to December 2025 to identify studies comparing excision surgery of sacrococcygeal pilonidal disease with NPWT and this surgical technique without NPWT. Statistical synthesis was performed using a random effects model, with results expressed as risk ratios (RRs) and mean differences (MDs) with their 95% confidence intervals.ResultsFive studies in which 244 patients were reported in our analysis; 123 (50.4%) underwent NPWT and 121 (49.6%) underwent conventional dressings. Complete healing time was significantly shorter in the NPWT group compared with conventional dressing, both RCT analyses (MD: -15.88 days; 95% CI: -30.04 to -1.72), observational studies (MD: -31.39 days; 95% CI: -55.03 to -7.75), and overall analysis (MD: -24.02 days; 95% CI: -40.75 to -7.29). There was no statistical difference in terms of recurrence (RR: 0.71; 95% CI: 0.15-3.36) and postoperative infection (RR: 0.50; 95% CI: 0.20-1.25).ConclusionNegative pressure wound therapy may shorten healing time following open excision for pilonidal disease, but the available evidence remains limited, heterogeneous, and methodologically weak.

💡 Conclusion
Interleukin (IL)-23 inhibition is now an established therapeutic strategy in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), supported by the efficacy of selective IL-23p19 monoclonal antibodies in both Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Advances in peptide engineering have also enabled direct oral targeting of the IL-23 receptor (IL-23R), introducing an alternative approach to inhibition of the same inflammatory pathway. This narrative review examines the biological rationale for targeting the IL-23-IL-23R axis and the clinical evidence establishing selective IL-23p19 inhibition in IBD. Particular attention is given to the clinical development of icotrokinra, an oral macrocyclic peptide IL-23R antagonist. Phase 3 studies in psoriasis have established the efficacy of oral IL-23R antagonism, while the phase 2b ANTHEM-UC study has provided clinical evidence of efficacy in IBD, with improvements in clinical and endoscopic outcomes accompanied by translational evidence of pathway suppression. Ongoing phase 3 programmes in UC and CD will determine whether oral IL-23R antagonism can achieve durable clinical and endoscopic efficacy and establish its place alongside existing IL-23p19 monoclonal antibodies. If successful, this approach could represent a broader shift towards highly selective oral targeting of extracellular inflammatory pathways that have traditionally required biological therapy.

What is already known on this topic: Circulating proteins have been linked to colorectal cancer risk, but few have been validated prospectively and most studies have combined colon and rectal cancer despite their recognised biological differences.What this study adds: Colon and rectal cancers had distinct pre-diagnostic proteomic profiles with no overlapping associations, and the colorectal cancer signal was driven almost entirely by colon cancer. Circulating proteins are promising biomarkers of colorectal cancer risk. Most studies combine colon and rectal cancer despite their biological differences.

To identify proteins associated with colorectal, colon, and rectal cancer risk and triangulate findings using cross-cohort confirmation and Mendelian randomisation (MR).

We profiled 6,402 proteins, using the SomaScan 7K assay in a case-cohort study nested within the European Prospective Investigation into Cancer and Nutrition (EPIC; 658 colon and 319 rectal cancers). Cox models identified proteins associated with cancer risk at a false discovery rate <5%. Endoscopic ultrasound-guided tissue acquisition (EUS-TA) is central to the pathological diagnosis of solid pancreatic masses, but the comparative value of rapid on-site cytologic evaluation (ROSE), macroscopic on-site evaluation (MOSE), and no on-site evaluation (NONE) may vary by needle type and remains uncertain.

We performed a PRISMA-DTA systematic review registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251241446). Potential cohort overlap and data dependency were assessed before quantitative synthesis. Diagnostic 2 × 2 data were synthesized with a bivariate binomial-normal random-effects model. Independent direct comparisons were pooled by random-effects meta-analysis for correct versus incorrect diagnosis, with complementary absolute differences in sensitivity and specificity. An exploratory three-node network meta-analysis (NMA) compared ROSE, MOSE, and NONE.

Thirty-one eligible reports contributed 67 strategy-specific diagnostic datasets. After excluding 21 duplicate, nested, paired, or otherwise dependent datasets, 46 independent datasets representing 4,901 arm-level observations were included in the primary DTA. Pooled sensitivity was 0.94 (95% CI 0.92-0.95) and specificity was 1.00 (0.98-1.00); Deeks' asymmetry test was positive (P <0.001). In direct comparisons, ROSE improved composite diagnostic accuracy versus NONE in FNA studies (OR 5.56, 95% CI 1.76-17.62; k = 4), but not in FNB studies (OR 1.08, 0.42-2.79; k = 4); the FNA-versus-FNB interaction was exploratory (P = 0.012). MOSE versus NONE in FNB was uncertain (OR 1.31, 0.60-2.87; k = 4), as was MOSE versus ROSE (OR 1.53, 0.05-49.13; k = 3). The exploratory NMA did not identify a statistically clear difference for ROSE versus NONE (OR 1.67, 0.94-2.95), MOSE versus NONE (OR 1.89, 0.86-4.20), or MOSE versus ROSE (OR 1.14, 0.48-2.71).

EUS-TA showed high overall diagnostic performance. ROSE may add value in FNA-era workflows, whereas a clear incremental benefit was not detected with FNB. MOSE remains a plausible resource-sparing strategy, but comparative estimates are imprecise. The NMA should be regarded as hypothesis-generating because of sparse comparisons, methodological heterogeneity, small-study effects, and low-to-very-low certainty.

Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) is a chronic cholestatic liver disease characterized by progressive bile duct inflammation and fibrosis. Despite its significant clinical burden, the molecular mechanisms underlying PSC pathogenesis remain poorly understood, limiting therapeutic options. Here, we integrated bioinformatic analyses of transcriptomic datasets with experimental validation in a bile duct ligation mouse model and in vitro cell systems. Differential gene expression, weighted gene co-expression network analysis, and Mendelian randomization were performed to identify key molecular signatures. Single-cell RNA sequencing characterized cellular heterogeneity. The IRE1α-NK cell axis was validated using pharmacological inhibition, siRNA knockdown, and NK cell depletion approaches in bile acid-treated NK cells, co-culture systems, and a mouse model of cholestatic bile duct injury. We identified Endoplasmic Reticulum To Nucleus Signaling 1 (ERN1, also as a key mediator in PSC pathogenesis, with causal relationships to disease risk. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis revealed significant NK cell enrichment in PSC samples. In both the bile duct ligation mouse model and in vitro systems, bile acids induced IRE1α pathway activation in NK cells, leading to enhanced cytotoxicity against cholangiocytes. Pharmacological inhibition or genetic knockdown of IRE1α prevented bile acid-induced NK cell activation and cytotoxicity. In vivo, both IRE1α inhibition and NK cell depletion attenuated liver injury, inflammation, and fibrosis in the mouse model. Our data suggest a novel pathogenic mechanism in PSC involving bile acid-induced IRE1α activation in NK cells, promoting cholangiocyte injury. The IRE1α-NK cell axis represents a promising therapeutic target for PSC treatment.
