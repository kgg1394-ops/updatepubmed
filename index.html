💡 Conclusion
Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), has been inconsistently linked to incident atrial fibrillation (AF) across observational cohorts. We performed an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies, following the Meta-analysis Of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) guidelines, searching PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science through July 25, 2026, for studies comparing incident AF risk in adults with versus without NAFLD/MASLD. Adjusted effect estimates were pooled using a random-effects model, with heterogeneity assessed via the I² statistic and explored through subgroup and univariable meta-regression analyses. Eighteen cohort studies comprising over 15 million participants from Asia, Europe, and North America met inclusion criteria. Pooling all studies demonstrated a significantly increased risk of incident AF among individuals with NAFLD/MASLD compared with those without (pooled risk ratio (RR) 1.23, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.12-1.34, p < 0.001), with substantial statistical heterogeneity (I² = 99.4%). In an exploratory analysis of three studies, advanced disease with hepatic fibrosis was also associated with increased AF risk (RR 1.19, 95% CI 1.11-1.28). In the present meta-analysis, NAFLD/MASLD was associated with a modestly higher risk of incident AF after adjustment for conventional cardiometabolic risk factors, although heterogeneity was very high. These findings support attention to AF risk in this population and the use of standardized diagnostic approaches in future research.

💡 Conclusion
Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a near-universally lethal malignancy, with KRAS mutations driving tumorigenesis in over 90% of cases. For decades considered undruggable, oncogenic KRAS has now been therapeutically engaged across multiple mutational subtypes, a revolution precipitated by allele-specific covalent inhibitors and accelerated by the emergence of RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitors. The pivotal phase 3 RASolute 302 trial of daraxonrasib, a pan-RAS(ON) inhibitor, demonstrated nearly doubled median overall survival (13.2 versus 6.7 months; HR 0.40; P < 0.0001) over standard chemotherapy in previously treated metastatic PDAC. Concurrently, mutation-selective G12D inhibitors (zoldonrasib, VS-7375, INCB161734), G12D-targeted protein degraders (setidegrasib), G12C-selective RAS(ON) compounds (elironrasib), and next-generation MEK inhibitors (atebimetinib) are advancing through clinical evaluation. This review synthesizes the biological basis of KRAS-driven oncogenesis in the pancreas, the mutational landscape and its prognostic implications, the structural and mechanistic evolution of KRAS inhibitor classes, contemporary and emerging clinical data from recent trials, toxicity profiles and their management, and resistance mechanisms. The field stands at an inflection point: daraxonrasib now redefines second-line PDAC management, while first-line combination regimens with daraxonrasib plus chemotherapy have produced an ORR of 58% in early-phase single-arm data, a result now being tested in the randomized RASolute 303 trial. The challenge ahead lies in translating these results into durable remissions through rational combination therapy and in overcoming acquired resistance.

💡 Conclusion
Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis are chronic inflammatory bowel diseases (IBDs) driven by the interleukin-23/Th17 pathway. Guselkumab, a selective interleukin-23p19 monoclonal antibody, has shown promise in phase II/III trials. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to synthesize its efficacy and safety versus placebo in moderate-to-severe IBD. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, and Scopus were searched from inception to February 2026. Guselkumab (intravenous induction, 200-1200 mg) was compared with placebo. Random-effects models pooled outcomes as risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Heterogeneity (I2) and evidence certainty were assessed. Seven randomized controlled trials were included. Guselkumab significantly improved week-12 clinical remission (risk ratio: 2.77, 95% CI: 2.30-3.34; P < 0.00001; I2 = 0%), with consistent benefits in ulcerative colitis (risk ratio: 3.09) and Crohn's disease (risk ratio: 2.62). Clinical response also improved (risk ratio: 2.34, 95% CI: 1.99-2.76; P < 0.0001). In ulcerative colitis, endoscopic improvement reached a risk ratio of 2.57, symptomatic remission risk ratio 2.40, and histologic-endoscopic improvement risk ratio 2.96 (all P < 0.0001, I2 = 0%). In Crohn's disease, endoscopic response reached a risk ratio of 2.46 and PRO-2 remission risk ratio 2.78 (both P < 0.0001). Any adverse events were comparable between groups (risk ratio: 0.99), while serious adverse events (risk ratio: 0.57; P = 0.004) and discontinuations (risk ratio: 0.50; P = 0.001) were significantly reduced versus placebo. Guselkumab delivers consistent clinical and endoscopic benefits in both ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease with a favorable safety profile, positioning it as a valuable new option in IBD management.

After curative surgery for colorectal cancer (CRC), patients undergo surveillance because of the risk of recurrence; however, only a subset develops recurrence amenable to further curative-intent treatment. The primary aim of follow‑up is to identify patients with potentially curable recurrence.

A retrospective analysis was performed of stage I-IV CRC patients who underwent curative-intent treatment and entered postoperative surveillance at Isala between 2014 and 2018. Follow‑up was conducted according to Dutch guideline‑based protocols for 60 months. Patients were categorized as having no recurrence, a potentially curable recurrence, or a non-curable recurrence. Prognostic factors at start of follow-up for potentially curable recurrence were assessed using competing‑risk Cox regression, with non-curable recurrence and death as competing events. For patients with recurrence, mode and time of detection were evaluated.

During a median follow-up of 60 months, recurrence occurred in 22.4% of 1118 patients. The cumulative incidence function (CIF) of a potentially curable recurrence was 8.0%. Independent variables associated with a potentially curable recurrence were age HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.96-0.99), minor postoperative complications HR 1.78 (95% CI 1.16-2.72), stage III CRC HR2.76 (95% CI 1.19-6.38) and stage IV CRC HR 11.02 (95% CI 4.44-27.37). Among patients with a recurrence, CEA ≥ 5 ng/mL was present in 63% of all recurrences, but less often in the potentially curable group (57% vs. 69%, p = 0.057). Median time to detection of a potentially curable recurrence was 16 months (IQR 20). The overall number needed to follow-up (NNFU) to detect one potentially curable recurrence was 9.8, with the lowest NNFU observed in stage IV CRC (2.3) and in rectal cancer (6.9).

Standard follow-up enabled the detection of potentially curable recurrence in 8% of CRC patients. The substantial variation in NNFU across subgroups and the limited sensitivity of CEA underscore the need for more personalized, risk‑adapted surveillance strategies.

Venlafaxine, a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, has been investigated in oncology for several supportive care indications and, more recently, for potential anticancer activity. However, the available evidence remains fragmented across clinical and preclinical studies.

This systematic review aimed to synthesize the available evidence regarding the role of venlafaxine in oncology, including established supportive care applications and emerging anticancer potential.

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science from database inception to 22 August 2026. Original clinical and preclinical studies evaluating venlafaxine in cancer populations or experimental cancer models were eligible. Clinical and preclinical evidence was synthesized separately using a narrative approach. Methodological quality was assessed using Joanna Briggs Institute tools for clinical studies and the SYRCLE Risk of Bias tool for animal studies.

25 studies were included, comprising 23 clinical studies and 2 preclinical studies. The strongest clinical evidence supported venlafaxine for treatment-related hot flashes, particularly among breast cancer survivors receiving endocrine therapy, with the strongest randomized, placebo-controlled trial evidence showing significantly greater reductions with venlafaxine than placebo (P < 0.0001 for higher doses); within-group percentage reductions are also reported but should not be interpreted as treatment effects. Evidence for chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy was inconsistent, with potential benefit for acute oxaliplatin-related neurotoxicity but insufficient support for prevention or routine treatment of chronic CIPN. Evidence for other supportive care outcomes, including oral mucositis-associated pain, fatigue, sleep quality, depressive symptoms, and quality of life, was limited. Venlafaxine was generally well tolerated, although adverse effects varied by dose and comparator. Preclinical studies investigated anticancer activity, but not equivalently: one in melanoma models showed direct antiproliferative/pro-apoptotic effects of venlafaxine via JNK1/2-Nur77 signaling, whereas a colorectal cancer study showed only context-dependent antagonism of norepinephrine-driven tumor progression, with no significant effect of venlafaxine alone. No clinical evidence currently supports venlafaxine as a direct anticancer treatment.

Venlafaxine has an established evidence-based role in supportive oncology for treatment-related hot flashes, whereas its role in CIPN and other supportive care indications remains uncertain. Emerging anticancer findings are limited to preclinical models and should be considered hypothesis-generating. Further randomized clinical trials are needed for supportive care indications, while additional mechanistic and preclinical validation is required before venlafaxine can be considered for clinical evaluation as an anticancer therapy.
