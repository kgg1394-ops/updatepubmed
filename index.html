To explore the relationship between recurrent otitis media (ROM), chronic media otitis with effusion (COM), and laryngopharyngeal reflux disease (LPRD) in pediatric populations.

PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane databases.

Two investigators independently screened papers with PRISMA guidelines considering the following primary outcomes: LPRD prevalence in pediatric ROM and COM, diagnostic methods used, and detection rates of gastroduodenal markers in middle ear effusions. Two random-effects meta-analyses were performed to estimate the pooled prevalence of pepsin/pepsinogen detection in middle ear effusions and to compare pepsin concentrations between COM/ROM patients and controls using standardized mean differences (Hedges' g).

Twenty-eight studies were included (2070 pediatric patients). The cumulated gastroesophageal reflux disease prevalence in COM children was estimated to 50.7%, while 27.6% of children reported pharyngeal reflux events. Fifteen studies measured pepsin in effusions, reporting a pooled detection prevalence of 72.5% (95% CI: 53.7%-88.0%), rising to 85.6% with ELISA-based methods versus 17.9% with enzymatic assays. Alkaline-activated gastroduodenal mediators were assessed in 3 studies, with detection rates ranging from 39.6% to 100% for trypsin and from 32% to 42% for bile salts. The incidence of early-onset colorectal cancer continues to increase among adults younger than 50 years, yet delays in diagnosis remain common.

As an initial step of the Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer Awareness campaign, we evaluated U.S. healthcare providers' knowledge, attitudes, diagnostic practices, and perceived barriers related to the timely recognition and evaluation of early-onset colorectal cancer.

This survey established baseline measures for a national early-onset colorectal cancer awareness campaign.

We conducted a national cross-sectional survey of physicians, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants practicing family medicine, internal medicine, emergency medicine/urgent care, and obstetrics and gynecology.

The survey assessed early-onset colorectal cancer awareness, screening recommendations, evaluation of symptomatic younger adults, perceived barriers to diagnosis, and educational needs. Descriptive statistics summarized responses, and multivariable regression identified factors associated with guideline-concordant screening and diagnostic evaluation.

A total of 277 providers completed the survey. Although 87% were concerned or very concerned about early-onset colorectal cancer and 82% reported increased concern over the preceding five years, providers identified substantial barriers to timely diagnosis. Approximately one-half reported that younger adults commonly experienced symptoms for four months or longer before seeking care. The most frequently cited barriers included patients expecting symptoms to resolve (84%), insurance and financial barriers (79%), reluctance to undergo colonoscopy (77%), and failure to recognize symptoms as serious (65%). Most providers (79%) identified additional guidance on early-onset colorectal cancer symptoms, risk factors, and diagnostic evaluation as the educational resource most likely to improve care.

💡 Conclusion
This document provides resource-sensitive HCC guidelines that prioritise feasibility, maximise benefit, and reduce default reliance on best supportive care alone and are produced for the IHPBA by discussion with the global community of IHPBA.
Current guidelines for the management of patients with HCC are predominantly focused on well-resourced healthcare systems. In 2024, the IHPBA Legacy committee initiated a process to develop global resource-sensitive guidelines for the management of HCC.

An IHPBA global guideline group produced and circulated statements on the management of HCC from diagnosis to treatment, discussed these in online webinars and generated guidelines. This document focuses on the diagnosis and management of patients with HCC in resource-constrained healthcare settings.

Initial diagnosis by ultrasound is supported by cross-sectional imaging. Alfa-feto protein and assessment of liver synthetic function are recommended at diagnosis. Lesion biopsy is not mandated. Liver resection is the recommended treatment of choice for single HCC in patients with preserved liver function and up to 3 lesions ≤3 cm. Radiofrequency and microwave are regarded as equivalent and can be used if resection is not available. Ethanol injection can be used if thermal ablation is not available but should be restricted to small lesions. TACE and TAE can be used for intermediate stage disease. Atezolizumab/bevacizumab combination is recommended as first line systemic therapy with sorafenib and Lenvatinib being recommended if first line is not an option. Advice for the care of the patient with advanced or terminal disease is provided.

This document provides resource-sensitive HCC guidelines that prioritise feasibility, maximise benefit, and reduce default reliance on best supportive care alone and are produced for the IHPBA by discussion with the global community of IHPBA.

To evaluate 2-year cumulative success and survival rates, changes in oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL), and patient satisfaction among edentulous patients who continued to wear complete dentures fabricated by three-dimensional (3D) printing (CAD-3DPs) after a crossover randomized controlled trial (RCT).

Current guidelines for the management of patients with HCC are predominantly focused on well-resourced healthcare systems. In 2024, the IHPBA Legacy committee initiated a process to develop global resource-sensitive guidelines for the management of HCC.

An IHPBA global guideline group produced and circulated statements on the management of HCC from diagnosis to treatment, discussed these in online webinars and generated guidelines. This document focuses on the diagnosis and management of patients with HCC in resource-constrained healthcare settings.

Initial diagnosis by ultrasound is supported by cross-sectional imaging. Alfa-feto protein and assessment of liver synthetic function are recommended at diagnosis. Lesion biopsy is not mandated. Liver resection is the recommended treatment of choice for single HCC in patients with preserved liver function and up to 3 lesions ≤3 cm. Radiofrequency and microwave are regarded as equivalent and can be used if resection is not available. Ethanol injection can be used if thermal ablation is not available but should be restricted to small lesions. TACE and TAE can be used for intermediate stage disease. Atezolizumab/bevacizumab combination is recommended as first line systemic therapy with sorafenib and Lenvatinib being recommended if first line is not an option. Advice for the care of the patient with advanced or terminal disease is provided.

💡 Conclusion
Claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2), a tight junction protein largely confined to normal gastric mucosa, is overexpressed and mislocalized on the surface of gastric, gastroesophageal, pancreatic, and other gastrointestinal cancers. This pattern has made CLDN18.2 a leading target in solid tumor immunotherapy. This review traces its path from a structural junctional component to a clinically relevant antigen and assesses the therapeutic platforms built around it. We discuss monoclonal antibodies, especially zolbetuximab, whose phase III SPOTLIGHT and GLOW trials improved survival in CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative gastric and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma and led to regulatory approval. We then evaluate antibody-drug conjugates, bispecific T-cell engagers, and CAR T-cell therapies, comparing their mechanisms, efficacy, toxicity, and resistance. We also examine the rationale for combining CLDN18.2-directed agents with immune checkpoint inhibitors, although clinical evidence for synergy remains indirect. Major challenges include antigen loss and heterogeneity, autophagy-mediated ADC resistance, on-target gastrointestinal toxicity, cytokine release syndrome, and unstandardized expression thresholds. Because optimal cutoffs differ by modality, patient selection should follow the intended therapeutic class rather than a universal biomarker score. Overall, CLDN18.2-directed therapy is a maturing precision oncology strategy; its impact will depend on randomized data, better biomarkers, and rational combinations.

▶Development and temporal validation of nomograms and machine learning models for predicting overall and cancer-specific survival in primary distal cholangiocarcinoma: a SEER-based study.

💡 Conclusion

Distal cholangiocarcinoma (dCCA) differs from intrahepatic and perihilar cholangiocarcinoma in anatomical origin, surgical management, and molecular profile, yet most existing prognostic tools either pool all biliary tract cancers together or rely on anatomical Tumor Node Metastasis (TNM) staging alone. A dCCA-specific model integrating clinical, treatment, and tumor-burden variables is therefore needed. We aimed to develop and evaluate nomograms and machine learning models for predicting overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) in patients with primary dCCA.

We identified 1,515 patients with primary dCCA diagnosed between 2010 and 2017 in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. Patients diagnosed in 2010-2015 were randomly divided into a training cohort (70%, n=773) and an internal testing cohort (30%, n=331); patients diagnosed in 2016-2017 (n=411) formed a temporal validation cohort with no calendar-year overlap. Independent predictors were selected by univariable and multivariable Cox regression for OS and by Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard modeling for CSS, and were used to construct nomograms predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival. Four machine learning survival models-decision tree, random survival forest, gradient boosting survival analysis, and an XGBoost-based accelerated failure time model (XGBAFT)-were trained for OS prediction and interpreted with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP). Performance was assessed by Harrell's concordance index (C-index), time-dependent area under the curve (AUC), calibration plots, and decision curve analysis, and benchmarked against the eighth-edition American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system.

The development cohort was predominantly male (53.2%) and aged over 65 years (61.6%); 28.9% underwent radical resection of the primary site and 47.6% received chemotherapy, with a median observed survival of 8 months. For OS, the nomogram achieved a C-index of 0.754 and 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUCs of 0.854, 0.868, and 0.871 in the internal testing cohort, and a C-index of 0.742 with AUCs of 0.830, 0.836, and 0.843 in the temporal validation cohort. For CSS, the corresponding C-indices were 0.726 and 0.710, with AUCs of 0.813/0.797/0.760 and 0.792/0.712/0.702. Both nomograms outperformed the eighth-edition TNM system (OS C-index, 0.585; CSS C-index, 0.635) and showed good calibration and net clinical benefit. Among the machine learning models, XGBAFT achieved the highest discrimination (AUCs, 0.861/0.834/0.831 internally and 0.807/0.803/0.789 temporally). SHAP identified chemotherapy, primary-site radical surgery, regional lymph node surgery as the most influential predictors. The nomogram stratified patients into low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups with significantly divergent survival curves (P<0.001).

Within the SEER population, our dCCA-specific nomograms and the XGBAFT model provided accurate, well-calibrated, and interpretable predictions of OS and CSS, outperformed eighth-edition TNM staging, and supported effective risk stratification. Because both cohorts were drawn from SEER, these results constitute internal and temporal validation; prospective multicenter studies in geographically and ethnically diverse cohorts are required before clinical implementation.

💡 Conclusion

It is unclear whether recurrence-focused treatment (RFT) improves survival in all patients with recurrent pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). This study aimed (1) to evaluate post-recurrence survival (PRS) in patients treated with RFT versus best supportive care (BSC)-only, and (2) to assess potential effect modification by prognosis at recurrence.

This observational cohort study included patients with local or distant recurrence after PDAC resection (2014-2022) at all Dutch pancreatic surgery centers and NYU Langone Health. Patients with unknown RFT or survival status were excluded. Prognosis at recurrence was defined using a previously established PRS risk score, stratifying patients into worse (≥174.5) and better (<174.5) prognostic subgroups. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed in the overall cohort, and within subgroups. Median PRS in RFT versus BSC-only patients was estimated and compared using Kaplan-Meier curve analysis and the log-rank test. Multivariable Cox regression with robust standard errors clustered by matched pair was used to adjust for residual imbalance after PSM, with results presented as hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (95%CI).

Overall, 1423 patients were included. After PSM, 344 RFT patients were matched to 344 BSC-only patients. The multivariable analysis included ECOG performance score and country as a random effect. PRS was 10 (95%CI 9-12) months for RFT versus 4 (95%CI 3-4) months for BSC-only (adjusted HR0.38, 95%CI: 0.30-0.48, P < 0.001). In the worse prognosis group, PRS was 10 (95%CI 9-11) versus 3 (95%CI 3-4) months (adjusted HR0.38, 95%CI: 0.30-0.50, P < 0.001). In the better prognosis group, PRS was 18 (95%CI 12-27) months versus 8 (95%CI 4-14) months (adjusted HR0.24, 95%CI: 0.10-0.45, P < 0.001).

Treatment for PDAC recurrence was associated with significantly longer PRS compared with BSC-only, irrespective of prognosis at recurrence. The survival benefit associated with RFT supports its consideration in the management of patients with PDAC recurrence.
