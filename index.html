▶Procalcitonin in Acute Pancreatitis: Diagnostic Utility for Differentiating Infected from Sterile Pancreatic Necrosis.

💡 Conclusion
Acute pancreatitis (AP) is an acute inflammatory disorder of the pancreas that disrupts its normal function and often requires hospitalization because of severe abdominal pain. While most patients recover without major complications, some develop pancreatic necrosis, infected pancreatic necrosis (IPN), persistent organ failure, and other severe complications associated with increased morbidity and mortality. A key clinical challenge is distinguishing infected pancreatic necrosis from sterile pancreatic inflammation, as delayed or inaccurate diagnosis may result in inappropriate antibiotic use and poorer clinical outcomes. This review aimed to evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic utility of procalcitonin (PCT) in differentiating infected pancreatic necrosis from sterile inflammation and to assess its role in supporting antimicrobial decision-making in patients with AP. A structured narrative literature review was conducted using PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science to identify English-language human studies, meta-analyses, randomized controlled trials, and international guidelines published between January 2000 and December 2025. A total of 60 studies were included in the narrative synthesis. The reviewed evidence indicates that PCT may provide greater diagnostic specificity for bacterial infection than conventional inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and leukocyte count in selected clinical settings. Elevated or persistently increasing PCT concentrations have been associated with IPN, sepsis, organ failure, and increased mortality. Furthermore, PCT-guided antibiotic stewardship strategies may reduce unnecessary antibiotic exposure without adversely affecting clinical outcomes in selected settings. Overall, PCT represents a potentially valuable adjunctive biomarker for differentiating IPN from sterile inflammation in patients with AP. When interpreted alongside clinical assessment and imaging findings, PCT may facilitate the assessment of infection risk, prognostic stratification, and antimicrobial stewardship. However, further large-scale prospective multicenter studies are warranted to establish standardized diagnostic thresholds and optimize its integration into routine clinical practice.

This narrative review critically appraises the epidemiological, experimental, and interventional evidence proposed to link chronic intermittent hypoxia (CIH) and sleep fragmentation to hepatic steatosis, steatohepatitis, and fibrosis. We summarize the candidate mechanisms, including mitochondrial oxidative stress, hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha signaling, sympathetically driven lipolysis, and Kupffer and stellate cell activation and then examine the limits of the evidence supporting them: hypoxia-induced hypophagia and the scarcity of pair-fed controls in rodent models, species differences in the physiological response to hypoxia, publication bias, common-cause confounding by age and systemic inflammation, and the rarity of progressive liver disease in lean patients with anatomically driven OSA. Current evidence supports CIH as a context-dependent amplifier of liver injury in patients with pre-existing metabolic dysfunction rather than as an established independent cause of MASH. This interpretation still justifies integrated screening and treatment pathways, while identifying the study designs needed to test causality directly.

▶Thyroid Eye Disease in the Teprotumumab Era: Pathophysiology, Clinical Activity Assessment, and an Updated Therapeutic Landscape.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Thyroid eye disease (TED), the principal extrathyroidal manifestation of Graves' disease, is an autoimmune orbitopathy. The pathophysiology involves aberrant thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor (TSHR) and insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R) signaling on orbital fibroblasts, leading to glycosaminoglycan accumulation, adipogenesis, inflammatory edema, and extraocular-muscle enlargement. The active phase is typically self-limited yet medically modifiable, characterized primarily by inflammation, edema, and tissue expansion, whereas chronic inactive disease is dominated by fibrosis and fixed structural change. This US-oriented review aims to provide updates in the field through 2026: (1) summarizing the molecular pathophysiology linking thyroid autoimmunity to orbital remodeling and current drug targets; (2) discussing activity-versus-severity assessment, with appraisal of the Clinical Activity Score (CAS); and (3) explaining the complementary roles of different structural and functional imaging modalities in the evaluation and management of TED. We then follow the therapeutic ladder, from conservative measures through the employment of glucocorticoids and steroid-sparing immunosuppression to the use of targeted biologics. We give particular attention to IGF-1R inhibition, including the use of teprotumumab and veligrotug, and to post-approval results, including otologic and metabolic adverse-event burden, recurrence, treatment sequencing, and the emerging efficacy of the treatment of chronic and low-activity disease. The CAS's limited inter-observer reliability identifies additional reproducible activity measures as an open need.

▶The Evolution of Oligometastatic Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma: From Anatomic Description to Biologically Informed Patient Selection.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) has historically been managed with systemic therapy, and local therapies have, in general, been avoided in the absence of symptoms directly related to a specific metastasis. Even if a specific site of disease, such as a local recurrence, had the potential to cause symptoms in the future, there was a bias against prophylactic palliation. It was well-established that most patients die of systemic progression. This approach is now under pressure from four directions. Retrospective case series have reported survival durations longer than anticipated in highly selected patients with low-volume, single-organ disease. A randomized phase II trial reported that metastasis-directed therapy prolonged progression-free survival. Biologic data indicate that the site of metastatic relapse is encoded in gene expression profiles already present in the primary tumor. Second-line systemic therapy has become substantially more effective, which lengthens the interval over which control of an untreated metastasis may become clinically important. We trace the oligometastatic concept from the 1995 Hellman and Weichselbaum hypothesis to the 2026 OligoPanc consensus definition of up to three lesions confined to a single organ, examine the criteria proposed for patient selection, summarize the surgical and ablative radiotherapy literature, and consider the trials that will determine whether this is a treatable disease state or a favorable prognostic phenotype. We conclude that induction systemic therapy, followed by response assessment that weighs the absence of disease progression, Carbohydrate Antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) kinetics, and performance status, is a defensible route to local therapy directed at the metastases and the primary tumor, and warrants prospective study.
