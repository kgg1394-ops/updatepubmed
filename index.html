▶Role of Gut Microbiota-derived Metabolites in the Pathogenesis and Progression of Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD): A Systematic Review.

💡 Conclusion
The gut microbiota-derived metabolites, particularly conjugated bile acids and endogenous ethanol, are highly promising and may play a fundamental role in the development and progression of MASLD, including steatosis, steatohepatitis, fibrosis, and HCC. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is closely linked with significant comorbidities and mortality. Evidence suggests that gut microbiota-derived metabolites play a vital role in the initiation and progression of MASLD. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the role of these metabolites in the pathogenesis and progression of MASLD, including steatosis, steatohepatitis, fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

This systematic review followed the Preferred Reporting of Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines. A systematic literature search was conducted across PubMed/Medline, Embase, Web of Science, and Scopus from inception to December 2025. Eligible studies included observational, interventional, animal, and in vitro mechanistic studies. Human observational studies were evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa scale, randomized controlled trials using the Cochrane risk of bias-2 (ROB-2) tool, and in vitro studies using a qualitative mechanistic approach. The risk of bias was assessed using the Systematic Review Centre for Laboratory Animal Experimentation (SYRCLE) tool for animal studies. Results were synthesized narratively due to heterogeneity in the data. This study was registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261293786).

A total of 28 studies were included. Alterations in bile acids, ethanol, fatty acids, and other compounds, such as indole derivatives, were associated with fibrosis, steatosis, steatohepatitis, and HCC. Bile acid levels vary with disease stage, while ethanol was reported to be generally high across studies. Different short-chain fatty acids have various roles in MASLD pathogenesis. Indole derivatives are capable of attenuating hepatic steatosis and inflammation. Altering the gut microbiota is also known to play a vital role in reducing the progression of liver cancer.

The gut microbiota-derived metabolites, particularly conjugated bile acids and endogenous ethanol, are highly promising and may play a fundamental role in the development and progression of MASLD, including steatosis, steatohepatitis, fibrosis, and HCC. Further high-quality, well-designed studies in humans are warranted to evaluate their precise role and validate their use in routine clinical practice.

💡 Conclusion
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is characterized by heterogeneous disease presentations and clinical courses. Accurate diagnosis and appropriate disease monitoring play an important role in improving long-term outcomes by preventing misdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment; enabling early detection of disease progression, treatment failure, and disease-related complications; and facilitating timely adjustment of treatment strategies. The Korean Association for the Study of Intestinal Diseases (KASID) developed guidelines for the diagnosis and monitoring of IBD in adults, reflecting the Korean clinical setting, to support clinical decision-making and improve the quality of patient care. To develop these guidelines, existing international clinical practice guidelines and expert consensus statements were comprehensively reviewed, along with updated evidence relevant to each clinical topic. Final recommendations were established through expert consensus using a modified Delphi process. These guidelines address major clinical areas in the diagnosis and monitoring of IBD, including initial diagnosis and baseline assessment, assessment of disease extent and activity, disease monitoring in ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, nutritional assessment, and colorectal cancer surveillance. These guidelines are intended to support individualized clinical judgment and should be applied flexibly, taking into account not only individual patient characteristics and clinical circumstances but also available resources, access to diagnostic and monitoring modalities, regulatory requirements, and reimbursement policies.

The phase III CheckMate 8HW trial met its dual primary endpoints of superior progression-free survival (PFS) with nivolumab plus ipilimumab (NIVO+IPI) versus chemotherapy (first-line [1L]) and versus NIVO (all lines) in patients with centrally confirmed microsatellite instability-high/mismatch repair deficient (MSI-H/dMMR) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). We report prespecified final overall survival (OS) analyses and updated efficacy outcomes.

Immunotherapy-naïve adults with mCRC across different lines and MSI-H/dMMR by local testing were randomized 2:2:1 to NIVO (240 mg) plus IPI (1 mg/kg) Q3W for four doses, then NIVO 480 mg Q4W; NIVO (240 mg) Q2W (six doses), then 480 mg Q4W; or chemotherapy ± targeted therapies. Secondary endpoints include PFS in NIVO+IPI versus NIVO (1L), and OS and objective response rate (ORR) in the centrally confirmed MSI-H/dMMR population.

839 patients across all lines were randomized; efficacy analyses were conducted in those with centrally confirmed MSI-H/dMMR. Median follow-up across all lines and treatment groups was 68.0 months (range 37.6-81.4). NIVO+IPI demonstrated significant OS benefit versus NIVO across all lines (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.46-0.82; P=0.0009) and versus chemotherapy in 1L (HR 0.37; 95% CI 0.24-0.57; P<0.0001]). Clinically meaningful OS benefit was noted with 1L NIVO+IPI versus NIVO (HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.44-0.99; P=0.0432), not meeting the prespecified threshold for significance (P<0.006). PFS continued to favor NIVO+IPI across all three comparisons: NIVO (all lines; HR 0.60), chemotherapy (1L; HR 0.21), and NIVO (1L: HR: 0.68). Grade 3/4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 78 (22%) versus 50 (14%) patients treated with NIVO+IPI versus NIVO across all lines, with no new safety concerns or additional treatment-related deaths from the previous follow-up.

💡 Conclusion

Long-term oncological equivalence of robotic total mesorectal excision (R-TME) and laparoscopic total mesorectal excision (L-TME) remains unproven. The 5-year outcomes of the COLRAR RCT are reported.

Adults with cT1-4a Nx M0 rectal adenocarcinoma within 10 cm of the anal verge were randomized 1 : 1 to L-TME or R-TME at three Korean tertiary centres between July 2011 and February 2016. Pre-specified secondary endpoints were 5-year overall, relapse-free, local recurrence-free, and distant recurrence-free survival. Analyses were on a modified intention-to-treat basis.

Of 295 analysed patients (144 L-TME patients and 151 R-TME patients; median follow-up of 72.6 months), 5-year overall survival (92.6% versus 92.4%; P = 0.838) and relapse-free survival (81.9% versus 83.0%; P = 0.801) were similar. Local recurrence occurred in 4.9% (L-TME) and 1.3% (R-TME); 5-year local recurrence-free and distant recurrence-free survival did not differ (P = 0.076 and P = 0.655). In a post-hoc analysis of 146 patients who received neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, 5-year local recurrence-free survival was 98.6% in R-TME patients versus 90.7% in L-TME patients (P = 0.039).

R-TME and L-TME produced equivalent 5-year oncological outcomes. A post-hoc analysis suggested improved local control with robotic surgery after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, requiring confirmation in prospectively designed studies.

▶Robotic rectal cancer surgery: oncological and functional outcomes, technical pitfalls, and unresolved controversies.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Total mesorectal excision (TME) remains the curative operation for rectal cancer. The male pelvis, obesity, and a narrow anal canal make rectal dissection the most demanding step in abdominal surgery, even for experienced surgeons. Robotic systems were introduced to address these constraints by providing a stable three-dimensional view, wristed instruments, and ergonomic control beyond that of conventional laparoscopy. Two decades after the first robotic TME, randomized trials (ROLARR and REAL), transanal TME trials, and more than a dozen meta-analyses permit a more measured assessment. To make that assessment clear and comparable, we organize it around a prespecified core set of outcome parameters rather than every metric reported in the literature: the primary oncological and surgical-quality outcomes are conversion to open surgery, circumferential resection margin (CRM) involvement, completeness of the mesorectal specimen, and long-term locoregional recurrence and disease-free survival; the primary functional outcomes are bowel, urinary, and sexual function, measured with validated instruments at fixed time points. Overall survival, distal margin, lymph node yield, general morbidity and anastomotic leak, operative time, blood loss, length of stay, quality of life, and cost are treated as secondary outcomes. Against this core set, robotic surgery consistently reduces conversion to open surgery. In the most informative recent trial, it also improved circumferential resection margin quality and three-year locoregional control in high-risk middle and low rectal cancers, without affecting overall survival. Functional benefits, including earlier recovery of urinary function and modest improvements in bowel and sexual outcomes, are plausible but supported by low-certainty evidence. Distinct pitfalls remain, ranging from arm collision and docking errors to distal-plane deviation and anastomotic failure, and the approach is slower and more expensive than laparoscopy. This review summarizes the oncological and functional evidence, relates technical errors to the operative sequence, and examines unresolved controversies: which patients benefit most, how robotics compares with transanal dissection, and whether fluorescence imaging, neuromonitoring, and new competing platforms alter the balance.

Telisotuzumab adizutecan (ABBV-400, Temab-A) is an antibody-drug conjugate targeting c-Met (MET) protein with adizutecan, a novel topoisomerase 1 inhibitor payload. In monotherapy dose escalation, Temab-A demonstrated a manageable safety profile and promising antitumor activity in advanced colorectal cancer (CRC; NCT05029882). Based on these results, a randomized cohort of Temab-A + bevacizumab (Bev) versus trifluridine-tipiracil (FTD-TPI) + Bev (standard-of-care) was added with the hypothesis that addition of Bev would enhance efficacy.

In dose optimization, patients were randomized 1:1:1 to Temab-A 2.0 or 2.4 mg/kg + Bev 7.5 mg/kg Q3W, or FTD-TPI 35 mg/m

Recent evidence has suggested that neoadjuvant chemotherapy may provide clinical benefit for patients with locally advanced colon cancer. Immunotherapy may provide additive or synergistic effects when combined with chemotherapy. This study's objective is to assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of neoadjuvant dostarlimab in combination with capecitabine-oxaliplatin (CAPEOX) versus CAPEOX in patients with T4N0 or stage III mismatch repair proficient/microsatellite stable (pMMR/MSS) resectable colon cancer.

AZUR-4 (NCT06567782) is a multicenter, phase 2, open-label, randomized (3:1 ratio) study of 120 patients across multiple countries. Eligible patients are aged ≥ 18 years with resectable pMMR/MSS colon adenocarcinoma with no prior treatment. Four cycles (21 days) of CAPEOX ± dostarlimab will be administered as neoadjuvant treatment. After completing neoadjuvant treatment, patients will undergo surgery 3 to 6 weeks later, with additional adjuvant chemotherapy administered after surgery at the investigator's discretion; no additional dostarlimab will be administered. Primary endpoints are major pathological response, determined by local assessment, and safety. Secondary endpoints will assess the feasibility and pathological response to the regimen. Exploratory endpoints include biomarker readouts and pathological response rate in biomarker subsets.

▶Reduced-dose alternate-day oral iron supplementation in women with iron deficiency: a randomized controlled trial.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
A reduced-dose alternate-day oral iron supplementation strategy achieved improvements in iron status comparable to standard daily supplementation despite lower cumulative iron exposure. These findings support the potential clinical relevance of physiologically informed low-dose iron supplementation strategies in women with iron deficiency.
Oral iron supplementation is frequently limited by gastrointestinal side effects and reduced absorption associated with hepcidin-mediated regulation. This randomized, parallel-group, active-controlled study evaluated whether a reduced-dose alternate-day oral iron supplementation strategy could improve iron status in women with iron deficiency.

Sixty-six iron-deficient women of reproductive age (18-50 years; serum ferritin < 30 ng/mL) were randomized to receive, for 42 days, either micronized, dispersible ferric pyrophosphate providing 21 mg of elemental iron every 48 h combined with a daily gut microbiota modulator, or sucrosomial ferric pyrophosphate providing 30 mg of elemental iron per day. The primary endpoint was serum ferritin, while secondary endpoints included serum iron, transferrin, transferrin saturation (TSAT), total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), hemoglobin, and red blood cell count.

Serum ferritin increased significantly over time in both groups (p < 0.001), with no significant differences between treatments. Comparable improvements were observed for serum iron, transferrin, TSAT, and TIBC, while hemoglobin remained stable throughout the study. Both interventions were generally well tolerated, with only mild transient gastrointestinal adverse events and no clinically relevant changes in inflammatory markers.

A reduced-dose alternate-day oral iron supplementation strategy achieved improvements in iron status comparable to standard daily supplementation despite lower cumulative iron exposure. Chronic pancreatitis (CP) remains a challenging gastrointestinal disorder with limited treatment options. Qimei Granules, a modified formulation derived from the classical Wumei Pill, were developed to relieve clinical symptoms of CP. This study evaluated the efficacy and safety of Qimei Granules in treating CP patients with Jueyin disease.

This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. A total of 102 diagnosed CP patients with the cold-heat complex syndrome of Jueyin disease were recruited to receive standard Western CP treatment, supplemented with either Qimei Granules or a placebo for six weeks. Primary outcomes included improvement in major symptoms, TCM syndrome scores, and overall clinical efficacy. Secondary outcomes included pancreatic endocrine and exocrine function and nutritional status. Safety was evaluated based on liver and kidney function indicators and adverse event monitoring.

Qimei Granules significantly improved abdominal pain, abdominal distension, and diarrhea in patients with CP (clinical efficacy rate of 74.4% vs. 47.1%, P<0.05). TCM syndrome scores were also significantly improved (P<0.05). Fasting blood glucose and glycosylated hemoglobin levels were significantly reduced after treatment with Qimei Granules (P<0.05). No significant differences were observed in serum amylase, lipase, or hemoglobin levels between the control and intervention groups.

This study demonstrates the clinical efficacy of Qimei Granules in alleviating core symptoms in CP patients with the cold-heat pattern of Jueyin disease. Based on TCM theoretical principles of syndrome differentiation and treatment and supported by the specific components of Qimei Granules that address mixed cold and heat manifestations, this formula is recommended for CP patients with Jueyin disease.
