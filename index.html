To identify the types of cancer and the corresponding screening methods evaluated in randomized controlled trials (RCTs), with a primary focus on situating oral cancer screening within the RCT-based body of evidence.

This review followed the PRISMA-ScR guidelines. PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Scopus, LILACS, ProQuest, and Google Scholar were searched without date restrictions for studies in English, Spanish, or Portuguese. Eligible studies were RCTs primarily evaluating cancer screening methods (clinical, imaging, endoscopic, laboratory, molecular/serological, or self-examination). Data were synthesized as means and frequencies.

Oral cancer ranked last, with only one RCT evaluating a single method, oral visual inspection, the fewest of any cancer type. These findings map the RCT landscape in cancer screening, revealing marked disparities in evidence across cancer types and identifying oral cancer as the most limited. Further RCTs are urgently needed to strengthen the evidence base and support effective oral cancer screening programs.

In experienced centers, ESD appears to be a feasible and promising organ-preserving approach for anal canal lesions, achieving high en bloc and R0 resection rates with low early recurrence. Anal canal lesions (ACLs) represent a significant clinical challenge requiring consideration of sphincter function preservation. Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is a promising minimally invasive technique for en bloc resection, yet its application in the anal canal is technically demanding due to the narrow lumen and somatic innervation. We conducted this meta-analysis to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ESD for ACLs.

Following PRISMA guidelines, we searched PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through February 2026. Eligible studies involved adult patients undergoing ESD for lesions originating in the anal canal. Primary outcomes were en bloc and R0 resection rates. Secondary outcomes included complications and local recurrence. Pooled proportions were calculated using random-effects models.

Four retrospective studies involving 150 patients were included. The pooled en bloc resection rate was 94.1% (95% CI: 89.6%-97.4%). The R0 resection rate was 84.8% (95% CI: 78.7%-90.0%). Safety analysis revealed pooled incidences of anal pain at 18.7% (95% CI: 0.0%-60.0%), delayed bleeding at 6.6% (95% CI: 1.1%-16.1%), and anal stenosis at 1.1% (95% CI: 0.0%-3.7%). The pooled local recurrence rate was 4.7% (95% CI: 0.6%-12.3%). Median follow-up for the studies ranged from 9 to 37 months.

In experienced centers, ESD appears to be a feasible and promising organ-preserving approach for anal canal lesions, achieving high en bloc and R0 resection rates with low early recurrence. Comparative and longer-term data would help define its role relative to surgical excision or repeated ablative therapies.

Large language models may support guideline screening and multidisciplinary tumour board preparation in gastrointestinal oncology, but their performance in postoperative treatment assessment remains insufficiently characterized. To evaluate guideline concordance, tumour board concordance, reproducibility, and discordance patterns of ChatGPT-generated postoperative treatment recommendations in gastrointestinal cancer.

Single-centre exploratory cross-sectional study using retrospectively assembled clinical case vignettes assessed by a large language model (ChatGPT). Thirty adult patients with histologically confirmed pancreatic, gastric, or colorectal adenocarcinoma who underwent curative-intent surgery and subsequent multidisciplinary tumour board (MTB) review at a German tertiary referral centre (January 2025-January 2026) were included. Structured vignettes were entered into ChatGPT via a standardised prompt within a controlled guideline environment; each case underwent three independent runs (run-level) with majority voting to derive the decisive treatment recommendation (case-level). The primary outcome was concordance of ChatGPT recommendations with German guideline-based postoperative treatment recommendations on the case-level. Secondary outcomes included concordance with MTB recommendations, reproducibility, cross-guideline comparison, and qualitative characterisation of discordant recommendations (divided in minor or major deviation).

ChatGPT-generated majority-vote recommendations were fully concordant with German guideline-based recommendations in 21 of 30 cases (70%). Concordance was higher in pancreatic and gastric cancer (8 of 10 cases [80%] each) than in colorectal cancer (5 of 10 cases [50%]). Multidisciplinary tumour board recommendations demonstrated the same overall concordance rate (21 of 30 cases [70%]). Concordance decreased in clinically advanced disease stages. Cross-classification demonstrated complete guideline concordance in both ChatGPT-generated and tumour board recommendations in 17 of 30 cases (56.7%), whereas major LLM discordance despite guideline-adherent tumour board recommendations was uncommon (1 of 30 cases [3.3%]). In exploratory run-level analysis, 23 of 90 outputs (25.6%) were discordant, comprising 14 (15.6%) major and 9 (10.0%) minor deviations. Complete agreement across repeated runs occurred in 61 of 90 case-guideline combinations (67.8%).

ChatGPT-generated treatment recommendations showed relevant but variable concordance with guideline-based and multidisciplinary tumour board recommendations. Discordance clustered in clinically complex treatment situations, supporting a possible role for LLM-based systems in guideline screening and tumour board preparation while requiring expert physician oversight.

The incidence of early-onset colorectal cancer (eoCRC; <50 years) is rising. Previous studies suggested that eoCRC patients have poorer outcomes than older adults. Whether this reflects distinct tumor biology or differences in stage at diagnosis and treatment patterns remains unclear. We compared clinicopathological features, treatment, and survival of eoCRC with average-onset (ao) and older-onset (oo)CRC in a nationwide cohort.

Clinicopathological data of all patients diagnosed with colorectal adenocarcinoma in the Netherlands from 1 January 2016 through 31 December 2022 were retrieved from the Netherlands Cancer Registry (NCR). Patients were categorized as eoCRC, aoCRC (50-69 years), and ooCRC (≥70 years). Multivariable regression and matched analyses were performed to account for established prognostic factors and predictors of treatment response.

Among 80,551 patients with CRC, 3917 (5%) were eoCRC, 34,499 (43%) were aoCRC, and 42,135 (52%) were ooCRC. The five-year post-resection recurrence rate of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains 50-70%, and satellite nodules constitute a well-established independent high-risk factor for early recurrence. The therapeutic benefit of adjuvant transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) in this specific subpopulation has not been rigorously evaluated in large cohorts. We therefore aimed to quantify the impact of postoperative adjuvant TACE and to develop a machine learning-based nomogram for individualized survival prediction in HCC patients with pathologically confirmed satellite nodules following radical hepatectomy.

This retrospective, non-randomized, multi-center cohort enrolled 273 HCC patients with pathologically confirmed satellite nodules who underwent radical hepatectomy between January 2015 and March 2024. Assignment to the adjuvant TACE group versus the radical-resection-only control group was determined by shared decision-making between the treating multidisciplinary team and the patient (or family) at the first postoperative outpatient review, rather than by randomization. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS) were compared by Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank tests. A 1:1 propensity-score-matched sensitivity analysis (caliper 0.2 SD; covariates: sex, age, BCLC stage, cirrhosis, HBV infection, ALBI grade, MVI, Edmondson grade, satellite multiplicity, resection type, and surgical margin) was performed to adjust for residual baseline imbalance. Independent prognostic factors were identified by univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression. A peritumoral delta-radiomics score (peritumoral_delta_radscore), derived from contrast-enhanced CT by three-dimensional volumetric segmentation, was generated. Three machine learning-based Cox models-XGBoost-Cox, Cox-LASSO, and CoxBoost-were constructed and evaluated by time-dependent AUC, decision curve analysis (DCA), calibration, and concordance index (C-index) under ten-fold cross-validation. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values were applied to quantify feature contributions, and a clinical nomogram was constructed.

Adjuvant TACE markedly improved both RFS and OS. In the full cohort, Median RFS was 18.0 months in the TACE group versus 10.6 months in the control group (log-rank P < 0.001), with 1- and 2-year RFS rates of 72.8% vs. 44.6% and 39.2% vs. 8.8%, respectively. Median OS was 36.5 months in the TACE group versus 19.5 months in controls (log-rank P < 0.001), with a 3-year OS of 53.6% vs. 13.5%. After 1:1 propensity-score matching (n = 248; 124 matched pairs), the survival advantage of adjuvant TACE remained statistically significant for both RFS (median 18.0 vs. 9.8 months; log-rank P < 0.001; 1-year RFS 72.6% vs. 42.7%; 2-year RFS 39.5% vs. 9.7%) and OS (median 36.9 vs. 22.9 months; log-rank P < 0.001; 1-year OS 100% vs. 100%; 3-year OS 54.0% vs. 16.1%). Multivariable Cox analysis identified treatment group, female sex, cirrhosis, BCLC stage, and peritumoral_delta_radscore as independent prognostic factors for both RFS and OS. Among the three machine learning models, XGBoost-Cox achieved the highest predictive performance, with time-dependent AUCs of 0.977/0.987/0.984 (1/3/5-year) in the training set (n = 163, events = 155) and 0.900/0.880/0.900 in the independent test set (n = 110, events = 105), consistently outperforming Cox-LASSO and CoxBoost. SHAP analysis identified treatment assignment, sex, BCLC stage C, and peritumoral_delta_radscore as the four dominant contributors. A 4-variable nomogram integrating these features demonstrated excellent calibration and net clinical benefit for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS prediction.

Adjuvant TACE might significantly prolong both RFS and OS in HCC patients with satellite nodules after radical hepatectomy. The XGBoost-Cox nomogram provides accurate individualized 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival prediction and may serve as an evidence-based decision-support tool for this high-risk subpopulation. As a retrospective, non-randomized study, the present findings require confirmation by prospective multicenter validation, ideally incorporating a randomized or propensity-score-stratified design to mitigate residual selection bias.

💡 Conclusion
Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a major manifestation of cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome and remains a leading cause of chronic kidney disease, kidney failure, heart failure, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and premature mortality. Over the past decade, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2is), glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs), and the non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (ns-MRAs), currently represented clinically by finerenone, have transformed the therapeutic landscape of DKD and related CKM phenotypes. Their benefits extend beyond glucose lowering and have been reproduced, to varying degrees, across populations with DKD, heart failure, obesity, and high cardiovascular risk. Although these drug classes act on distinct proximal targets, they appear to converge on several shared downstream mechanisms, including restoration of hemodynamic homeostasis, attenuation of inflammation and oxidative stress, preservation of mitochondrial and cellular metabolic integrity, and suppression of maladaptive fibrosis. At the same time, each class retains mechanistic strengths that may explain their partially distinct clinical profiles, with SGLT2is showing prominent benefits in heart failure and kidney disease progression, GLP-1RAs exerting stronger effects on weight reduction and atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, and ns-MRAs such as finerenone targeting mineralocorticoid receptor-driven inflammatory and fibrotic injury. We further discuss the biological rationale for dual- and triple-pathway combination strategies, the emerging role of phenotype-guided treatment selection, persistent residual risk, and unresolved questions regarding sequencing, safety, and implementation. Upon further disease progression, with multiple new bone lesions showing strong somatostatin receptor expression on 68Ga-DOTANOC PET, lanreotide was initiated, resulting in 17 months of stable disease. Subsequent disease progression with hepatic and suspected pancreatic metastases was treated with four cycles of 177Lu-DOTATATE peptide receptor radionuclide therapy, achieving sustained stable disease on follow-up imaging at six and 16 months. This case highlights the potential value of sequential somatostatin analogue and PRRT for metastatic PPGLs, providing meaningful and durable disease control in the absence of curative options, while emphasizing the need for randomized studies to validate and define the role of these emerging therapies.

Locoregional recurrence remains a major clinical challenge in borderline resectable (BR) and unresectable locally advanced (UR-LA) pancreatic cancer, even after stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT). This phase I trial evaluated the safety, maximum tolerated dose (MTD), and preliminary efficacy of adding prophylactic elective nodal irradiation (ENI) to 5-fraction SBRT.

Patients with BR/UR-LAPC were enrolled in a 3 + 3 dose-escalation design. SBRT was delivered in 5 fractions using either adaptive magnetic resonance image-guided radiation therapy or respiratory breath-hold radiotherapy with a fiducial marker. The ENI dose was fixed at 30 Gy in five fractions, while the primary tumor dose increased from Level 1 (35 Gy) to Level 3 (40 Gy). The primary endpoint was acute dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) (any Grade ≥ 3 non-hematological adverse events within 14 days after the completion of SBRT).

Twelve patients (BR: 4, LA: 8) were enrolled, 11 of whom had previously received systemic chemotherapy. One patient at Level 1 experienced acute Grade 3 anorexia/nausea requiring hospitalization. No DLTs were observed at Levels 2 and 3, and the MTD was not reached. Three patients with preexisting biliary stents developed subacute Grade 3 cholangitis. No Grade ≥ 3 late adverse events occurred. The median overall survival after SBRT was 17.3 months, and the 1-year locoregional control rate was 75%.

Five-fraction SBRT with simultaneous 30 Gy ENI was feasible, and the MTD was not reached within the tested dose levels up to 40 Gy. Further prospective studies are needed to evaluate its long-term safety and treatment outcomes.
