Oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF) is a potentially malignant disorder characterized by debilitating symptoms, including a burning sensation and reduced mouth opening. Although various therapeutic options exist, no consensus exists on the most effective management strategy. This systematic review assesses the efficacy of pentoxifylline over other medical management options for OSMF.

This review was performed in accordance with PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines. We conducted a systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Embase, and Google Scholar for articles published from 2000 up to February 2026. We included studies that compared the efficacy of pentoxifylline with other medical management techniques in OSMF patients. We assessed outcomes including maximal mouth opening, burning sensation, tongue protrusion, and cheek flexibility, and secondary outcomes such as adverse effects of pentoxifylline were recorded. We assessed the risk of bias using the Cochrane Collaboration tool (RoB2) for randomized controlled trials.

This review included 14 studies. Pentoxifylline has some mild, dose-dependent side effects reported, such as gastritis, dyspepsia, vomiting, anxiety, and tremor.

Functioning pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (pan-NETs) cause syndromes that can be life-threatening. Current guidelines recommend syndrome-directed drugs, but the primary evidence for hormonal control has never been mapped separately from the evidence for antitumor activity. We described how much evidence exists, how it is distributed, what outcomes were measured, and how completely the studies were reported across the ten functioning pan-NET syndromes recognized by the European Neuroendocrine Tumor Society (ENETS).

We followed JBI methodology and PRISMA-ScR. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase and Cochrane CENTRAL were searched from 1970 onward. Guideline-reference reconciliation, targeted syndrome-by-drug searches, backward citation checking and a date-sliced PubMed update with a clinical-series arm supplemented database searching. Eligible studies included at least three patients with a pancreatic-primary functioning neuroendocrine tumor and an extractable outcome of medical syndrome control. Evidence was charted by syndrome, therapy, design and reporting domain; formal certainty grading and meta-analysis were not undertaken.

Sixty-five studies formed the primary corpus. Fifty-three were single-cohort retrospective studies or case series, seven were prospective and five were comparative or multicenter retrospective studies. The median eligible sample was 13 patients among 55 studies with an exact denominator; 25/55 enrolled ten or fewer patients. Insulinoma accounted for 33 studies, followed by VIPoma (13), gastrinoma/Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (12) and glucagonoma (11). PTHrP-mediated hypercalcemia was represented by four studies and the combined ectopic ACTH/CRH category by two, both concerning ACTH secretion; no eligible primary study was identified for calcitoninoma or pancreatic carcinoid syndrome, which indicates the absence of eligible evidence rather than evidence of absent clinical experience. Forty-five out of 80 syndrome-by-therapy cells were empty. Biochemical or physiological outcomes were reported in 58/65 studies and clinical outcomes in 44/65, but definitions varied and the outcomes were not pooled; treatment line was reported in 41/65 and tumor grade in 25/65. A separate inventory contained 321 contextual and secondary records.

Future studies should measure hormonal control separately from antitumor activity, adopt a core outcome set and a minimum reporting dataset, and use prospective multicenter networks for the rarest syndromes and for the treatment sequences that remain unstudied.

▶Reappraising Urinary Amylase in Acute Pancreatitis: Methodological and Biological Heterogeneity Underlying Diagnostic Controversy.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common acute abdominal emergency for which serum lipase (LPS) is the preferred enzymatic biomarker. Urinary amylase (U-AMY) continues to attract clinical interest due to the noninvasive nature of urine sampling, its potential for repeatable testing, and its prolonged elevation relative to serum amylase (S-AMY) in some patients. However, the diagnostic performance of U-AMY varies substantially across studies-two systematic reviews published in December 2025 reached opposing conclusions. This review examines how methodological heterogeneity and biological variability may jointly contribute to the inconsistent diagnostic performance of U-AMY across studies. Rather than asking whether U-AMY can replace LPS-a question on which the literature remains divided-we critically examine the sources of heterogeneity that have precluded U-AMY from achieving guideline endorsement: non-standardized thresholds, variable sampling timing, diverse etiologic subgroups, and confounding renal physiology. We propose that U-AMY's value should be understood through a stratified decision framework and that future research should prioritize assay harmonization, prospective evaluation of common versus subgroup-specific diagnostic thresholds, and integration into multi-marker diagnostic models.

Most trials studying advanced therapies for ulcerative colitis (UC) adopted a responder re-randomization design. By contrast, a treat-through design was utilized in the ELEVATE UC 52 trial to assess etrasimod, an oral, once-daily, selective sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor modulator. It is therefore important to contextualize the efficacy of etrasimod given this different study design. We evaluated the efficacy of etrasimod versus placebo at week 52 among induction clinical responders.

In this post hoc analysis of ELEVATE UC 52, selected efficacy endpoints were evaluated at week 52 among all week 12 clinical responders (induction responders), defined as a ≥2-point and ≥30% decrease from baseline in modified Mayo score, with a ≥1-point decrease from baseline in rectal bleeding subscore (or an absolute value ≤1).

In total, 182 patients receiving etrasimod and 52 patients receiving placebo were induction responders. Of these patients, a greater proportion who received etrasimod versus placebo met the criteria for clinical remission (48.9% vs. 19.2%; p < 0.001) and all other endpoints assessed at week 52 (p < 0.05). Greater proportions of induction responders compared with the total trial population treated with etrasimod met the criteria for clinical remission (48.9% vs. 32.5%), endoscopic improvement (56.6% vs. 39.1%), and all other endpoints.

Greater efficacy was observed among etrasimod induction responders for all assessed endpoints at week 52 when compared with the total ELEVATE UC 52 population, contextualizing the efficacy of etrasimod given the treat-through design of the study.

💡 Conclusion
High-dimensional microbial abundance profiles, limited sample sizes, and severe class imbalance make accurate disease prediction from human gut metagenomics challenging. To address these limitations, we propose LiteMicroFormer, a lightweight attention-guided transformer framework for disease-specific binary classification using species-level microbiome profiles and metadata. LiteMicroFormer embeds species values, applies feature-gating attention, extracts microbial representations through a transformer, fuses metadata, and uses attention pooling to capture informative low-complexity patterns. These results demonstrate strong internal predictive performance, while independent cross-cohort generalizability remains to be established. SHAP analysis was performed on learned latent representations to examine their contributions to model predictions, providing model-level explanations without claiming validated species-level biological interpretability.

▶Rebooting immunity: how stem cell therapy is rewriting the future of autoimmune thyroid disease.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Autoimmune thyroid diseases (AITDs), including Hashimoto's thyroiditis and Graves' disease, are the most prevalent organ-specific autoimmune disorders worldwide. Current management remains symptomatic rather than curative, often necessitating lifelong therapy. This narrative review integrates AITD-specific human and preclinical evidence on stem cell-based interventions-including mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs), hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-based approaches, and extracellular vesicles (EVs)-across Hashimoto's thyroiditis, Graves' disease, and Graves' orbitopathy. The review explicitly distinguishes AITD-specific human evidence, AITD-specific preclinical evidence, and evidence extrapolated from other autoimmune or regenerative conditions, while emphasizing translational barriers such as product heterogeneity, potency assessment, patient selection, durability of response, and the limited availability of randomized trials. Preclinical studies show these approaches expand regulatory T cells, suppress pathogenic T helper responses, modulate B cells, and reduce thyroid or orbital fibrosis. Preliminary clinical observations from small, uncontrolled series suggest reductions in autoantibody levels and improved disease activity with acceptable short-term safety, though data remain unconfirmed by randomized trials. Key challenges include inconsistent efficacy, variable product quality, limited durability data, and absent standardized protocols. In conclusion, stem cell therapy may shift AITD treatment from symptom control toward restoring immune tolerance, but rigorous translational research is essential to bridge the gap between experimental promise and clinical application.

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) develops through cooperation between altered hepatocytes, chronic liver injury and a stromal and immune microenvironment. Genomic alterations and signaling abnormalities provide a rationale for treatment development, but pathway activation rarely identifies therapeutic dependence alone. This narrative review connects hepatic stellate cell states, tumor cell plasticity, genomic instability and four selected signaling pathways with antigen presentation, immune exclusion and clinical treatment evidence. Targeted searches were updated through 6 October 2026, retaining foundational work and prioritizing recent studies. Single-cell and spatial analyses reveal that stellate cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts can exert opposing effects according to their state and location. To evaluate the efficacy and safety of adding retention enemas with Chinese botanical drug decoctions to conventional treatment for gastrointestinal dysfunction in severe acute pancreatitis (SAP), and to appraise how the preparations were reported.

Seven English and Chinese databases were searched to 21 January 2026 for randomized controlled trials (RCTs). We assessed risk of bias (RoB 2), data integrity, preparation reporting (ConPhyMP) and certainty of evidence (GRADE), and performed random-effects meta-analyses with Hartung-Knapp confidence intervals.

Twenty-five RCTs (1,963 participants), all from China, were included; one further report was a duplicate publication. Five trials that reproduced the data of earlier trials from other hospitals were excluded from the primary analysis (20 trials, 1,643 participants). No trial was blinded or registered, and none chemically characterised its preparation. The enema was associated with shorter times to first defecation (SMD -2.79, 95% CI -4.37 to -1.20; 7 trials), first flatus (SMD -2.00, -3.64 to -0.35; 6 trials) and recovery of bowel sounds (SMD -2.88, -5.70 to -0.06; 5 trials), and with a higher effectiveness rate (OR 4.29, 2.70 to 6.81; 14 trials), but heterogeneity was very high and prediction intervals included no effect for the recovery times, and small-study effects were evident for the effectiveness rate. Adverse events were reported in six trials (OR 0.64, 0.20-1.99). Certainty was very low for all outcomes except length of stay (low). No clear evidence of a difference between Chengqi-type and other formulas was found, but the subgroup analyses were underpowered and do not establish equivalence.

Very low-certainty evidence suggests that botanical drug retention enemas may hasten the recovery of gastrointestinal function in SAP. Because the trials were small, unblinded, at high risk of bias and partly affected by duplicated data, and the preparations were heterogeneous and uncharacterised, the findings cannot be attributed to any specific preparation, and safety remains uncertain. Rigorous, registered trials using characterised preparations are needed.

▶Reappraising Urinary Amylase in Acute Pancreatitis: Methodological and Biological Heterogeneity Underlying Diagnostic Controversy.

💡 Conclusion
Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a common acute abdominal emergency for which serum lipase (LPS) is the preferred enzymatic biomarker. Urinary amylase (U-AMY) continues to attract clinical interest due to the noninvasive nature of urine sampling, its potential for repeatable testing, and its prolonged elevation relative to serum amylase (S-AMY) in some patients. However, the diagnostic performance of U-AMY varies substantially across studies-two systematic reviews published in December 2025 reached opposing conclusions. This review examines how methodological heterogeneity and biological variability may jointly contribute to the inconsistent diagnostic performance of U-AMY across studies. Rather than asking whether U-AMY can replace LPS-a question on which the literature remains divided-we critically examine the sources of heterogeneity that have precluded U-AMY from achieving guideline endorsement: non-standardized thresholds, variable sampling timing, diverse etiologic subgroups, and confounding renal physiology. We propose that U-AMY's value should be understood through a stratified decision framework and that future research should prioritize assay harmonization, prospective evaluation of common versus subgroup-specific diagnostic thresholds, and integration into multi-marker diagnostic models.

▶From Sequence to Spatial Pharmacology: A Translational Evidence Framework for Peptide Systems in Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), the problem is access, not merely affinity. Yet recent reviews emphasize discovery platforms, theranostic probes or clinical translation; to our knowledge, none appraises, across peptide roles, what the evidence shows about access, exposure and benefit by disease stage, including negative randomized results. This structured critical review introduces a spatial-pharmacology framework that asks of every peptide what job it performs (drug, targeting or penetration ligand, trigger, antigen or reporter), where it must act, which clinical state can reveal benefit, and which unresolved experiment most threatens translation. By classifying evidence maturity (tiers T0-T5) separately from outcome direction and profiling five translational domains, it compares heterogeneous systems without pooling and prevents evidence transfer between roles. We searched PubMed/MEDLINE, ClinicalTrials.gov, publisher records and citation chains through 13 August 2026 and purposively charted decision-informative programs in a source-linked atlas. Three cross-system conclusions emerge. First, early human evidence differs in kind by role (for example, target access for reporters, immunogenicity for vaccines) and is not interchangeable, while many direct, stromal and immune-enabling peptides remain preclinical. Second, evidence maturity and benefit often diverge: randomized evidence is limited, partly conference- or developer-reported, and largely negative or non-confirmatory, whereas supportive signals come mostly from lower evidence tiers. Third, the decisive missing evidence is role-specific: site-of-action exposure for direct, stromal and penetration peptides, setting-appropriate endpoints for vaccines, and effect on clinical decisions for reporters. We propose six minimum development standards; the framework is a planning heuristic, not a validated predictor of benefit.
