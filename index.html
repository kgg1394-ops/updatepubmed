This review critically appraises the evidence underlying guideline recommendations on antibiotic prophylaxis for spontaneous bacterial peritonitis and acute variceal bleeding in cirrhosis, and antimicrobial management in acute liver failure. Current research in CRC screening focuses on traditional screening methods, population-related factors, and emerging technologies, particularly artificial intelligence. Stronger international collaboration and interdisciplinary studies are needed to advance the field.
Colorectal cancer (CRC), is a highly prevalent malignant tumor worldwide, and early screening is key to improving its prognosis. Currently, CRC screening research is growing exponentially, but its core research hotspots, evolutionary trends, and interdisciplinary features are not clear. In this study, we used bibliometric analysis to analyze the distribution of literature, research hotspots, and cutting-edge developments in the field of CRC screening worldwide, and to provide a scientific basis for optimizing screening strategies.

Publications on CRC screening from 2015 to 2024 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. VOSviewer (v1.6.20), RStudio (v4.4.1), and CiteSpace (v6.4.2) were used for bibliometric and visual analyses. The main analyses included publication trends, countries, authors, journals, references, and keywords.

A total of 3245 publications were included in the analysis. Global publications on CRC screening showed a steady upward trend over the past decade, with an annual growth rate of 2.17%. The United States contributed the highest number of publications, and the University of North Carolina ranked as the most productive institution. GASTROENTEROLOGY and GUT were the most frequently cited journals in this field. Lansdorp‑Vogelaar I was among the most prolific authors. The most cited article was "CRC screening for average-risk adults: 2018 guideline update from the American Cancer Society." Keyword analysis showed that the research hotspots in this field included colonoscopy, biomarker, adenoma detection rate, artificial intelligence, etc.

Current research in CRC screening focuses on traditional screening methods, population-related factors, and emerging technologies, particularly artificial intelligence. Stronger international collaboration and interdisciplinary studies are needed to advance the field.

💡 Conclusion

To make informed choices in health-related discrete choice experiments (DCEs), respondents need to understand the choice scenarios, alternatives, attributes and attribute levels presented to them. Respondent populations are often highly diverse in knowledge and experience, leading to varying informational needs that make it challenging to design educational materials that sufficiently inform all respondents without disengaging others. At present, however, structured guidance on developing DCE educational materials is lacking. Drawing on the instructional design literature, this paper investigates whether theory-driven adaptations to educational materials improve respondent comprehension and engagement in health-related DCEs.

Informed by three focus groups on DCE informational needs, we identified four design principles from the instructional design literature relevant to DCE educational materials: whole-task instruction, making inferences explicit, adapting information to respondents' prior knowledge and embedding self-check questions with corrective feedback. Educational materials for three existing DCEs (influenza vaccination, colorectal cancer screening and rheumatoid arthritis prevention) were redesigned to incorporate these principles. Panel respondents (N = 1754) were randomized to receive either the original or the adapted educational materials, and effects on comprehension, engagement, internal validity and choice consistency were assessed.

Adapted materials increased time spent on educational content and were associated with small improvements in selected comprehension outcomes, though effects were often not statistically significant against the co-created comparator. Self-check questions revealed considerable comprehension gaps regardless of condition, underscoring that co-creation and pre-testing alone do not guarantee sufficient respondent comprehension. Educational materials also emerged as a meaningful source of selective dropout in all surveys, predominantly among respondents with lower education levels, posing a risk to sample representativeness that warrants attention in future research.

Adapting DCE educational materials based on educational theory and instructional design principles offers a promising approach to improving respondent engagement and understanding. To enable cumulative learning, we further encourage researchers to report comprehension outcomes and publish educational materials as supplementary materials.

Joint modeling provides a robust statistical framework for simultaneously modeling longitudinal covariate and time-to-event outcome. Apart from the interdependence between longitudinal and survival outcomes there may exist some unobserved random heterogeneity which can be modeled using a joint frailty model (JFM). Unlike existing JFMs, where two survival events are modeled using a frailty, our framework incorporates frailty into the survival sub-model to accommodate an additional source of variation at the survival endpoint. So here we developed a novel JFM and estimated the parameters using maximum-likelihood estimation method. The analyses were performed using R Software and illustrated using colorectal cancer data. The frailty component was introduced in colorectal cancer data to account for the unobserved variability in survival risk beyond the observed clinical characteristics. The highly significant frailty variance confirmed the presence of unobserved heterogeneity in survival outcomes. When compared with the standard Joint model (JM), the JFM demonstrated improved model fit and captured additional unexplained heterogeneity among individuals, leading to more precise hazard estimates. Performance was assessed using bias, root mean squared error and model fit indices. The findings highlight the importance of incorporating the frailty term in joint modeling, particularly in cancer prognostic studies where individual heterogeneity plays a crucial role.

▶The bidirectional causal relationships of insomnia with eleven cancers: A Mendelian randomization study.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Insomnia is closely associated with tumors and is recognized as a risk factor for cancer development. This two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) study investigated the potential causal link between genetically determined insomnia and various cancers, including brain, colorectal, esophageal, gastric, hepatic bile duct, liver, lung, malignant lymphoma, pancreatic, skin, and thyroid cancers. Summary statistics from genome-wide association studies for insomnia and eleven cancer types were examined. instrumental variables (IVs) were selected from single nucleotide polymorphisms that passed strict quality control for minimal linkage disequilibrium and genome-wide significance. All IVs demonstrated strong predictive power for insomnia, with F-statistics exceeding the conventional threshold of 10. The primary analysis was conducted using the inverse variance weighted method. Sensitivity analyses were performed using MR-Egger regression, weighted median, and weighted mode methods to test robustness, pleiotropy, and heterogeneity. MR analysis suggested potential causal associations between insomnia and increased risk of hepatic bile duct cancer (odds ratio [OR] = 4.458, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.074-18.493, P = .040), reduced risk of hepatic cancer (OR = 0.312, 95% CI: 0.100-0.973, P = .045), and reduced risk of skin cancers (OR = 0.599, 95% CI: 0.439-0.818, P = .001). However, after Bonferroni correction (adjusted threshold P < .0045), these associations did not retain statistical significance. No notable causal influence of these cancers on insomnia was observed. Sensitivity analyses showed no evidence of reverse causality or significant bias. These findings, though not retaining statistical significance after stringent multiple testing correction, provide suggestive evidence that genetically predicted insomnia may influence the risk of certain cancers, potentially through mechanisms involving genetic predisposition and biological pathways that modulate inflammation, immune function, and circadian rhythms. The study highlights the translational value of understanding insomnia's role in cancer risk, suggesting that clinicians should consider this association when managing patients with insomnia. Further research with larger sample sizes is warranted to validate these findings.

💡 Conclusion
ITF2357 exerts anti-CRC activity by upregulating miR-1938 to suppress ADAM12, leading to cell cycle arrest. These findings provide new mechanistic insights into the anti-CRC effects of ITF2357 and establish a foundation for exploring its therapeutic potential in CRC.
Histone deacetylase inhibitors (HDACi) have emerged as promising anticancer agents. This study aimed to investigate the antitumor efficacy and molecular mechanism of ITF2357 (Givinostat), a novel HDACi, in colorectal cancer (CRC).

In vitro, CT26 cell viability, proliferation, colony formation, and cell cycle distribution were assessed. In vivo, a subcutaneous CT26 tumor model was established in female BALB/c mice to evaluate the antitumor effect of ITF2357. Multi-omics analysis (miRNA-seq, mRNA-seq, ATAC-seq) was performed to identify key regulatory molecules, followed by qRT-PCR, dual-luciferase reporter, and knockdown assays for mechanistic validation.

ITF2357 significantly inhibited CT26 cell proliferation and induced G0/G1 phase arrest. In vivo, ITF2357 suppressed tumor growth without affecting body weight or liver and kidney tissues, suggesting no obvious systemic toxicity. Multi-omics analysis revealed that ITF2357 upregulated miR-1938, which directly targeted the 3'-UTR of ADAM12. ADAM12 knockdown phenocopied the anti-proliferative and cell cycle arrest effects of ITF2357. Clinically, high ADAM12 expression correlated with poor prognosis in CRC patients.

ITF2357 exerts anti-CRC activity by upregulating miR-1938 to suppress ADAM12, leading to cell cycle arrest. These findings provide new mechanistic insights into the anti-CRC effects of ITF2357 and establish a foundation for exploring its therapeutic potential in CRC.

Complement activation is a key driver of disease progression in IgA nephropathy (IgAN). While previous syntheses were limited to phase II evidence, pivotal phase III data-including the 24-month results of the APPLAUSE-IgAN trial-have recently emerged, necessitating an updated appraisal of complement-targeted therapies.

We conducted a systematic literature search in PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane up to April 24, 2026, for randomized controlled trials of complement inhibitors in adults with biopsy-proven IgA nephropathy. Due to marked mechanistic heterogeneity across agents targeting distinct complement nodes, a quantitative meta-analysis was not performed. Instead, we performed a structured descriptive synthesis, a predefined, domain-based comparative method, focusing on efficacy signals, safety profiles, and methodological disparities across trials.

Four RCTs comprising 584 patients were included, representing factor B and C5 inhibition. The phase III iptacopan trial demonstrated robust proteinuria reduction, eGFR slope attenuation, and a decreased risk of kidney failure, but a higher serious-infection rate and confirmed pneumococcal infections highlighted that alternative-pathway inhibition attenuates rather than abolishes encapsulated-bacteria risk. Phase II trials of ravulizumab, iptacopan, and cemdisiran provided early efficacy signals with no encapsulated-bacterial events reported, though small size and short follow-up limit safety inference. All trials required prophylactic vaccination, and infection-risk obligations differed by target-terminal-pathway inhibitors carrying the strictest REMS requirements. Methodological heterogeneity-particularly regarding biopsy timing and fibrosis exclusion-emerged as a major barrier to cross-trial comparisons. Beyond these RCTs, a dynamic pipeline of ASO, siRNA, and small-molecule inhibitors continues to evolve.

Complement inhibitors show promising but heterogeneous signals across individual trials; however, marked differences in molecular targets, trial design, and eligibility preclude any inference of a uniform class effect. Infection risk mitigation differs by complement target-terminal-pathway inhibitors carry the highest encapsulated-bacterial burden and strictest regulatory constraints, whereas alternative-pathway inhibitors preserve partial vaccine-mediated defence but still require comprehensive vaccination and surveillance. These observations are hypothesis-generating and underscore the need for mechanism-specific phase III programmes with hard renal endpoints and individual patient-level meta-analyses to advance precision complement therapeutics in IgAN.

Further research with larger sample sizes is warranted to validate these findings.
