💡 Conclusion
Minimal residual disease (MRD) is a major contributor to colorectal cancer (CRC) recurrence following curative-intent treatment, yet conventional surveillance methods may fail to detect residual disease at its earliest stages. Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) has emerged as a minimally invasive biomarker for MRD detection and postoperative monitoring. We conducted a structured PubMed-based literature review and included 94 publications evaluating ctDNA in CRC. Across the literature, postoperative ctDNA positivity was consistently associated with increased recurrence risk and poorer disease-free and overall survival, while serial monitoring improved detection sensitivity and enabled assessment of treatment response. ctDNA frequently identified molecular recurrence approximately 2.5-11.5 months before conventional surveillance. Both tumor-informed and tumor-naive strategies were represented, using PCR/ddPCR, targeted next-generation sequencing, whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing, methylation-based assays, and emerging multiomic approaches. Although ctDNA demonstrates strong prognostic value, evidence supporting treatment modification based solely on ctDNA status remains limited. Further prospective randomized trials, assay standardization, and consensus clinical guidelines are required before routine implementation. Overall, ctDNA is a promising tool for recurrence prediction, surveillance, and precision management of CRC.

Management of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is evolving rapidly and decisions regarding locoregional therapy are complex. External beam radiation therapy (EBRT) is a highly effective and safe intervention with an expanding role in HCC, and major guidelines (BCLC 2026, EASL, AASLD, NCCN, ASTRO) now endorse it across defined clinical settings. However, guidelines largely treat EBRT as a single modality, whereas in practice it encompasses multiple delivery techniques and nuanced technical decisions that directly influence safety and efficacy. This review provides radiation oncologists with a practical foundation for selecting patients, planning treatment, and positioning EBRT among contemporary locoregional and systemic therapies.

We synthesized current consensus guidelines, prospective randomized trials, and key contemporary series evaluating EBRT for HCC, drawing on prior systematic reviews updated to include recent studies. We address liver function assessment and patient selection, practical aspects of treatment delivery (motion management, image guidance, target definition, and adaptive planning), particle therapy, comparative evidence against other locoregional therapies, combinations with systemic therapy, management of high-risk scenarios, bridging to transplant, and post-treatment surveillance.

Mature prospective evidence, including randomized comparisons against chemoembolization, thermal ablation, and systemic therapy, demonstrates that EBRT achieves local control equal or superior to standard locoregional therapies with acceptable toxicity. Advances in motion management, image guidance, functional liver assessment, and adaptive planning have expanded the safe application to high-risk scenarios, such as macrovascular invasion, poor liver function, and re-irradiation, and EBRT is an effective bridging therapy.

EBRT delivers excellent local control with acceptable toxicity and is now endorsed by major guidelines as an option in selected settings. As imaging, motion management, adaptive strategies, and functional liver assessment continue to evolve, EBRT can be tailored safely to patients with compromised liver reserve, macrovascular invasion, or transplant pathways. Future studies should refine patient selection and optimize combinations of EBRT with other liver-directed and systemic therapies.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into insulin management through decision-support systems, adaptive bolus calculators, conversational platforms, and automated insulin delivery (AID) technologies. However, the clinical effectiveness and safety of AI-assisted insulin management remain uncertain.

To systematically evaluate evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) investigating AI-assisted insulin management systems in individuals with type 1 or type 2 diabetes.

A systematic literature review was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL were searched for RCTs evaluating AI-enabled insulin management interventions in insulin-treated individuals with diabetes. Eligible studies compared AI-based systems with standard care, clinician-guided insulin titration, conventional bolus calculators, or other AID algorithms. Outcomes included glycemic control, insulin titration, safety, and implementation-related measures. Owing to substantial heterogeneity, findings were synthesized narratively.

We included seven RCTs published between 2020 and 2025. Interventions comprised clinical decision-support systems, adaptive bolus calculators, conversational AI platforms, neural-network-based artificial pancreas algorithms, and exercise-aware AID systems. AI-assisted interventions consistently demonstrated favorable safety profiles, with no increase in severe hypoglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, or serious adverse events. Some systems, particularly fully automated closed-loop platforms and conversational AI-guided insulin titration, showed improvements in time in range, glucose control, adherence, and insulin optimization. User acceptance and adherence emerged as important determinants of effectiveness.

Current randomized evidence suggests that AI-assisted insulin management systems are safe, feasible, and capable of achieving glycemic outcomes comparable to conventional care. Although certain technologies demonstrate promising clinical benefits, the evidence base remains limited by small sample sizes, short follow-up durations, and heterogeneity across interventions. Larger, long-term randomized trials are needed to establish comparative effectiveness, optimize human-AI interaction, and support broader clinical implementation.

To compare early and delayed diverting ileostomy closure after low anterior resection for rectal cancer, focusing on postoperative morbidity and recovery outcomes, and to assess the certainty of available evidence.

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched from inception to 26th April, 2026. Five comparative studies (four randomized trials and one prospective cohort; 512 participants) were included. Random-effects meta-analyses evaluated overall post-closure morbidity as the primary outcome and severe complications, index-to-closure interval, postoperative hospital stay, operative time, and reoperation as secondary outcomes. Certainty was assessed using GRADE.

Overall morbidity occurred in 52/257 patients after early closure and 50/255 after delayed closure (RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.66-1.71; τ²=0.12; I²=40%). Severe complications and reoperation did not differ statistically, with confidence intervals compatible with benefit or harm. The index-to-closure interval was 107.09 days shorter with early closure (95% CI - 153.01 to - 61.17; τ²=2023.39; I²=96%); this process measure was largely inherent to the group definitions. Hospital stay and operative time did not differ statistically.

The available evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in postoperative morbidity but does not establish equivalence. Early closure may be considered in selected patients after postoperative recovery and assessment of anastomotic integrity; however, these findings do not support routine early closure in all patients.

Long-term oncological equivalence of robotic total mesorectal excision (R-TME) and laparoscopic total mesorectal excision (L-TME) remains unproven. The 5-year outcomes of the COLRAR RCT are reported.

Adults with cT1-4a Nx M0 rectal adenocarcinoma within 10 cm of the anal verge were randomized 1 : 1 to L-TME or R-TME at three Korean tertiary centres between July 2011 and February 2016. Pre-specified secondary endpoints were 5-year overall, relapse-free, local recurrence-free, and distant recurrence-free survival. Analyses were on a modified intention-to-treat basis.

Of 295 analysed patients (144 L-TME patients and 151 R-TME patients; median follow-up of 72.6 months), 5-year overall survival (92.6% versus 92.4%; P = 0.838) and relapse-free survival (81.9% versus 83.0%; P = 0.801) were similar. Local recurrence occurred in 4.9% (L-TME) and 1.3% (R-TME); 5-year local recurrence-free and distant recurrence-free survival did not differ (P = 0.076 and P = 0.655). In a post-hoc analysis of 146 patients who received neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, 5-year local recurrence-free survival was 98.6% in R-TME patients versus 90.7% in L-TME patients (P = 0.039).

R-TME and L-TME produced equivalent 5-year oncological outcomes. A post-hoc analysis suggested improved local control with robotic surgery after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, requiring confirmation in prospectively designed studies.

▶Robotic rectal cancer surgery: oncological and functional outcomes, technical pitfalls, and unresolved controversies.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Total mesorectal excision (TME) remains the curative operation for rectal cancer. The male pelvis, obesity, and a narrow anal canal make rectal dissection the most demanding step in abdominal surgery, even for experienced surgeons. Robotic systems were introduced to address these constraints by providing a stable three-dimensional view, wristed instruments, and ergonomic control beyond that of conventional laparoscopy. Two decades after the first robotic TME, randomized trials (ROLARR and REAL), transanal TME trials, and more than a dozen meta-analyses permit a more measured assessment. To make that assessment clear and comparable, we organize it around a prespecified core set of outcome parameters rather than every metric reported in the literature: the primary oncological and surgical-quality outcomes are conversion to open surgery, circumferential resection margin (CRM) involvement, completeness of the mesorectal specimen, and long-term locoregional recurrence and disease-free survival; the primary functional outcomes are bowel, urinary, and sexual function, measured with validated instruments at fixed time points. Overall survival, distal margin, lymph node yield, general morbidity and anastomotic leak, operative time, blood loss, length of stay, quality of life, and cost are treated as secondary outcomes. Against this core set, robotic surgery consistently reduces conversion to open surgery. In the most informative recent trial, it also improved circumferential resection margin quality and three-year locoregional control in high-risk middle and low rectal cancers, without affecting overall survival. Functional benefits, including earlier recovery of urinary function and modest improvements in bowel and sexual outcomes, are plausible but supported by low-certainty evidence. Distinct pitfalls remain, ranging from arm collision and docking errors to distal-plane deviation and anastomotic failure, and the approach is slower and more expensive than laparoscopy. This review summarizes the oncological and functional evidence, relates technical errors to the operative sequence, and examines unresolved controversies: which patients benefit most, how robotics compares with transanal dissection, and whether fluorescence imaging, neuromonitoring, and new competing platforms alter the balance.

💡 Conclusion
We identified fecal miRNA expression profiles with strong diagnostic and prognostic value in PIBD. These results contribute to improved stratification of disease severity and risk, supporting the advancement of precision and personalized approaches in pediatric IBD management.
The identification of novel noninvasive diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for pediatric inflammatory bowel disease (PIBD), including pediatric Crohn's disease (pCD) and ulcerative colitis (pUC), is crucial for improving therapeutic outcomes. Given their involvement in PIBD pathogenesis, microRNAs (miRNAs) represent promising candidates. This study investigates their potential diagnostic value and clinical utility.

The study involved 81 treatment-naive PIBD patients and 30 non-IBD controls from whom stool samples were collected. Fecal microRNA analysis was performed using small RNA next-generation sequencing. Differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) were identified in the exploratory phase (34 PIBD, 20 non-IBD children). Logistic regression was used to establish diagnostic and prognostic miRNA expression signatures. The designed miRNA panels were validated using leave-one-out cross-validation and on an independent cohort (47 PIBD, 10 non-IBD children).

A diagnostic signature of four fecal miRNAs (10392-5p, miR-1246, miR-4695-5p, and miR-5006-5p) distinguished pCD from non-IBD with 79.55% accuracy. Three fecal miRNAs (miR-1290, miR-3960, and miR-5006-5p) differentiated pUC from non-IBD with 78.26% accuracy. For treatment-naïve PIBD patients, panels of five and six miRNAs predicted residual disease activity at 3 months with 100% accuracy for pUC patients and 86.28% accuracy for pCD patients. Additionally, distinct signatures of four and six miRNAs predicted relapse within 12 months with accuracies of 96.87% (pUC) and 88.01% (pCD), respectively.

We identified fecal miRNA expression profiles with strong diagnostic and prognostic value in PIBD. These results contribute to improved stratification of disease severity and risk, supporting the advancement of precision and personalized approaches in pediatric IBD management.

▶Biosimilars in inflammatory bowel disease: from initial uncertainty to clinical normalization.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 Anti-TNF 유지/TDM 고려
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Biosimilars have become an integral part of the treatment of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), evolving from initial uncertainty regarding indication extrapolation and non-medical switching to widespread clinical acceptance. Early concerns focused mainly on the absence of disease-specific evidence and the potential impact of switching on effectiveness, safety, and immunogenicity. Over the past decade, however, extensive real-world evidence and randomized data have consistently supported the use of infliximab and adalimumab biosimilars in both biologic-naïve patients and patients switched from originator products. Recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses further reinforce these findings, showing preserved clinical remission, treatment persistence, inflammatory biomarkers, and immunogenicity after switching. The biosimilar landscape is now expanding to newer therapeutic targets, including ustekinumab, while biosimilar-to-biosimilar and multiple switching is becoming increasingly relevant in routine practice. Nevertheless, scientific interchangeability should not be equated to automatic substitution. Switching decisions should remain clinically supervised and involve gastroenterologists within a multidisciplinary framework, taking into account disease activity, previous response, safety, immunogenicity, and patient preferences. Appropriate communication is also essential to minimize a potential nocebo effect and to avoid unnecessary discontinuation. Biosimilars should therefore be considered fully established biological therapies rather than lower-tier alternatives to originator drugs. Future research should focus less on re-confirming single-switch equivalence and more on unresolved questions, including long-term outcomes after multiple switching, biosimilars of newer therapeutic targets, patient-reported outcomes, treatment persistence, and comparative pharmacoeconomic impact across healthcare systems.

Recent evidence has suggested that neoadjuvant chemotherapy may provide clinical benefit for patients with locally advanced colon cancer. Immunotherapy may provide additive or synergistic effects when combined with chemotherapy. This study's objective is to assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of neoadjuvant dostarlimab in combination with capecitabine-oxaliplatin (CAPEOX) versus CAPEOX in patients with T4N0 or stage III mismatch repair proficient/microsatellite stable (pMMR/MSS) resectable colon cancer.

AZUR-4 (NCT06567782) is a multicenter, phase 2, open-label, randomized (3:1 ratio) study of 120 patients across multiple countries. Eligible patients are aged ≥ 18 years with resectable pMMR/MSS colon adenocarcinoma with no prior treatment. Four cycles (21 days) of CAPEOX ± dostarlimab will be administered as neoadjuvant treatment. After completing neoadjuvant treatment, patients will undergo surgery 3 to 6 weeks later, with additional adjuvant chemotherapy administered after surgery at the investigator's discretion; no additional dostarlimab will be administered. Primary endpoints are major pathological response, determined by local assessment, and safety. Secondary endpoints will assess the feasibility and pathological response to the regimen. Exploratory endpoints include biomarker readouts and pathological response rate in biomarker subsets.

Gastrointestinal cancer, particularly esophageal, gastric, or colon cancer, is a significant cause of morbidity and mortality in Mexico. There are substantial delays in the early identification and timely referral of primary care patients, resulting in more advanced-stage diagnoses and poorer clinical outcomes. Our aim was to issue a position statement from the Asociación Mexicana de Gastroenterología (AMG) to guide primary care physicians in the identification of alarm features, risk stratification, and timely referral of patients with suspected gastrointestinal cancer.

A multidisciplinary group of 20 specialists in the fields of gastroenterology, oncology, endoscopy, epidemiology, and public health, from both public and private institutions, developed the position statement through a structured consensus process.
