Cirrhosis continues to be a major health burden in Indonesia, with high mortality and morbidity related to complications including ascites, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP), and hepatorenal syndrome (HRS). Although international guidelines strongly recommend albumin therapy for these complications, in Indonesia the adoption of international guidelines has been limited by universal healthcare policy and albumin indications defined in the National Formulary. The Indonesia Hepatology Medical Workgroup Meeting (December 2025) was convened to address barriers to the implementation of the newly launched National Cirrhosis Guideline, outline potential solutions, and discuss emerging topics such as long-term albumin (LTA) therapy and recombinant albumin (RA).

Seven hepatology experts from tertiary care centers completed a pre-meeting survey and participated in structured discussions on local barriers to implementation of the National Cirrhosis Guideline, evolution of treatment towards LTA, and clinical considerations for RA.

The workgroup welcomed the National Cirrhosis Guideline for defining clear albumin indications in large-volume paracentesis (LVP), SBP, and HRS that align with international standards and should serve as the primary clinical guidance for management of cirrhosis in Indonesia. Cost-effectiveness evidence supports albumin use for SBP, HRS, and LVP; however, the current real-world management is still suboptimal. The primary barriers to implementation of the National Cirrhosis Guideline were the restricted indications and criteria defined within the National Formulary. The discussion indicated that albumin underutilization, lack of standardized care, and limited adoption of LTA treatment were primarily due to unclear patient selection criteria and dosing guidance. Operating in acute surgical settings is significantly different from elective surgery. Indocyanine green fluorescence-guided surgery (ICG-FGS) is a promising tool, given its history, safety, low cost, and accumulated experience in elective settings for many surgeries, to help transform precision surgery into acute settings (trauma and emergency surgery). The incidence of early-onset colorectal cancer continues to increase among adults younger than 50 years, yet delays in diagnosis remain common.

As an initial step of the Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer Awareness campaign, we evaluated U.S. healthcare providers' knowledge, attitudes, diagnostic practices, and perceived barriers related to the timely recognition and evaluation of early-onset colorectal cancer.
