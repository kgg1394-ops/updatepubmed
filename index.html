Colonoscopy is central to colorectal cancer prevention because it enables early detection and removal of premalignant lesions. However, lesions can still be missed, and endoscopic differentiation of neoplastic from nonneoplastic polyps remains variable, particularly for diminutive lesions. The potential of artificial intelligence (AI) as a supportive technology for improving both polyp detection and characterization during colonoscopy has therefore been investigated. CADx systems using white-light imaging and structured high-confidence diagnostic rules show promise, but the clinical implementation of CADx remains less mature than that of CADe. This review summarizes current evidence for AI-assisted polyp detection and characterization and discusses limitations and future directions for integrating CADe and CADx into human-centered colonoscopy practices.

Residual atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk remains substantial despite optimal conventional lipid-lowering therapy, particularly in patients with persistently elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). Evinacumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting angiopoietin-like protein 3, represents a novel therapeutic approach that lowers LDL-C, triglycerides, apolipoprotein B (ApoB), apolipoprotein C-III (ApoC-III), and non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (non-HDL-C).

To systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of Evinacumab, an ANGPTL3-targeting monoclonal antibody, in patients with dyslipidemia. This review and meta-analysis assesses its effects on lipid parameters, including LDL-C, triglycerides, ApoB, ApoC-III, and non-HDL-C. It also seeks to determine Evinacumab's potential role in reducing residual ASCVD risk across diverse dyslipidemic populations.

A systematic review was conducted according to PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines. Embase, EBSCO, PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, the Cochrane Library, and gray literature sources were searched. Of 1718 identified records, 113 full-text articles were assessed, and 6 studies comprising 9 randomized clinical trials met the predefined eligibility criteria and were included.

Evinacumab demonstrated substantial lipid-lowering efficacy across doses ranging from 5 to 20 mg/kg. LDL-C reductions ranged from approximately 16% to 50% over follow-up periods of 16 to 24 weeks, with consistent efficacy observed in patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, including pediatric populations, and in adults with refractory dyslipidemias. Clinically meaningful reductions were also reported for triglycerides, ApoB, ApoC-III, and non-HDL-C. Triglyceride lowering was particularly pronounced in patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia, supporting its potential role in reducing pancreatitis risk, although responses varied according to the underlying genetic etiology. Evinacumab was generally well tolerated, with no major treatment-related safety concerns. Some studies reported transient anti-drug antibodies, but they did not appear to compromise efficacy or safety.

Evinacumab is an effective lipid-lowering therapy that produces clinically meaningful reductions in LDL-C, triglycerides, and apolipoproteins across difficult-to-treat dyslipidemic populations. Its favorable safety profile supports its use in refractory hypercholesterolemia and severe hypertriglyceridemia. Larger randomized trials with longer follow-up and head-to-head comparisons with other emerging lipid-lowering therapies are warranted to further define its long-term clinical role.

Pancreatic cystic neoplasms (PCN) are common incidental findings and represent the only known precursor lesions of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) that can be detected and monitored using conventional imaging. The different entities of PCNs vary considerably in terms of their potential for malignant transformation. Mucinous cysts are regarded as precancerous lesions, whereas serous PCN are considered benign. The most common type of PCN are intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs), which are subdivided into main-duct and branch-duct IPMNs. Magnetic resonance imaging is regarded as the gold standard for diagnosis and is complemented by endosonography, which offers high local image resolution in cases of discrepant findings or suspected worrisome features (e.g., intramural nodules). Laboratory analysis of cyst fluid can assist in identifying the type of cyst in cases of diagnostic uncertainty. The current Kyoto guideline provides detailed recommendations on surgical management and surveillance intervals for PCNs. Despite these recommendations and improved imaging modalities, the clinical management of predominantly asymptomatic patients remains challenging, and individually tailored monitoring and treatment decisions are often necessary in the context of the patient's age, comorbidities, personal preferences, the costs of long-term surveillance, and the morbidity and mortality associated with pancreatic surgery. The ultimate goal is to avoid overtreatment while ensuring that high-risk lesions are surgically resected in a timely manner.

Protective ileostomy is commonly performed after low anterior resection for rectal cancer to mitigate the clinical consequences of anastomotic leakage; however, it is associated with stoma-related and reversal-related morbidity. Ghost ileostomy has emerged as a selective alternative to avoid unnecessary stoma maturation.

This systematic review and meta-analysis of comparative studies was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched from inception to March 31, 2026. Six comparative studies involving 395 patients were included. The primary outcome was anastomotic leakage. Secondary outcomes were length of hospital stay, readmission/rehospitalization, and postoperative ileus. Random-effects models were used. The risk of bias was assessed using RoB 2 and ROBINS-I, and the certainty of evidence was evaluated using GRADE.

The available evidence did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in anastomotic leakage between ghost and protective ileostomy (RR 1.27, 95% CI 0.64-2.49; I² = 0%). Length of hospital stay was also not significantly different (MD 0.55 days, 95% CI -1.35 to 2.45; I² = 92%). Ghost ileostomy was associated with lower readmission/rehospitalization (RR 0.24, 95% CI 0.06-0.89; I² = 0%) and postoperative ileus (RR 0.33, 95% CI 0.13-0.82; I² = 0%). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis did not materially alter the interpretation of the heterogeneous hospital-stay outcome. The certainty of evidence was low for anastomotic leakage, readmission, and postoperative ileus and very low for hospital stay.

The available evidence did not identify a statistically significant difference in anastomotic leakage between ghost and protective ileostomy, although imprecision and limited sample size preclude a definitive conclusion of equivalence. Ghost ileostomy may reduce postoperative ileus and readmission in carefully selected patients; however, larger, adequately powered randomized trials are required before routine adoption.

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a major cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide, and mismatch repair-proficient/microsatellite-stable (pMMR/MSS) tumors account for the majority of metastatic CRC cases. In contrast to dMMR/MSI-H CRC, pMMR/MSS tumors are typically characterized by low immunogenicity, limited neoantigen presentation, insufficient effector T-cell infiltration, and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which collectively restrict the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)/VEGF receptor (VEGFR) signaling contributes not only to abnormal angiogenesis but also to immune suppression by impairing dendritic-cell maturation, reducing immune-cell trafficking, promoting T-cell dysfunction, and facilitating the accumulation of immunosuppressive cell populations. These mechanisms provide a strong rationale for combining VEGF/VEGFR-targeted therapy with ICIs to normalize tumor vasculature and remodel the immune microenvironment. Clinical evidence for this strategy in advanced pMMR/MSS CRC remains heterogeneous. Treatment activity varies across therapeutic settings, antiangiogenic agents, and combination regimens, while many studies are limited by small sample sizes, single-arm designs, and substantial differences in patient characteristics. Emerging data suggest that metastatic pattern, molecular features, baseline immune contexture, and treatment line may influence clinical activity; however, no biomarker has yet been validated for routine patient selection. Multi-agent strategies may provide broader immune modulation but also increase toxicity, treatment complexity, and cost. This review summarizes the biological rationale, clinical efficacy, safety, and potential predictive factors of VEGF/VEGFR-targeted therapy combined with ICIs in advanced pMMR/MSS CRC. Particular emphasis is placed on the heterogeneity of clinical outcomes and on distinguishing mechanistic immune remodeling from the potential enhancement of tumor immune sensitivity. Future studies should prioritize biomarker-guided patient selection, randomized comparative trials, and comprehensive assessment of efficacy, safety, quality of life, and long-term clinical benefit.

Neoplastic polyps are precancerous lesions of colorectal cancer (CRC), yet proteomic studies for stratifying their malignant potential remain limited. In this study, we used the Olink proteomics platform to systematically analyze the expression of inflammation- and oncology-related proteins in colorectal polyp tissues from 94 patients. The results showed that most inflammation- and oncology-related proteins were expressed at higher levels in neoplastic polyps than in non-neoplastic polyps. We further screened differentially expressed proteins using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, random forest, and LASSO regression. These proteins may provide valuable molecular evidence for deeper mechanistic studies of the CRC precancerous stage, early biomarker discovery, and prevention-oriented therapeutic development.
