Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are frequently used without long-term indication. Discontinuation remains challenging because of rebound symptoms. Whether digital guidance tools improve discontinuation is unknown. We evaluated whether an app improves PPI discontinuation.

In this open-label adaptive randomised controlled superiority trial, adults using daily PPI for ≥ 4 weeks without chronic PPI indication, according to the Dutch College of General Practitioners guideline, were recruited from outpatient clinics of Internal Medicine, Gastroenterology, Rheumatology and Nephrology departments at three Dutch hospitals. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1), stratified by department and initial indication, to receive information on PPI discontinuation through an app (intervention) or brochure (control). Primary outcome was PPI discontinuation at 2 months follow-up, defined as self-declared intake of ≤ 1 tablet in the preceding 14 days. Sample-size was revised earlier than prespecified based on interim data. The trial is registered as closed to accrual at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05348252).

Of the 163 participants (mean age 54 y/o [SD 11], 76 males [47%]), 66/77 (86%) discontinued PPIs in the intervention group versus 55/86 (64%) in the control group (RR 1.34, 95% CI 1.11-1.61, p = 0.002). Adverse events occurred in 18/121 (15%) patients who discontinued PPI use and in 6/42 (14%) who did not. Only 4/121 (3%) and 1/42 (2%), respectively, were potentially attributable to PPI discontinuation.

Radiotherapy with high linear energy transfer (LET) has shown promise for improving cancer outcomes. Carbon ion radiotherapy (CIRT) combines charged-particle dose conformality with enhanced biological effectiveness. However, clinical development remains constrained by limited center availability, modest trial accrual, and few randomized studies. We reviewed the global landscape of CIRT studies open to enrollment to identify current priorities and gaps.

This Particle Therapy Cooperative Group (PTCOG)-sanctioned project, conducted within the CIRT Guidelines Subcommittee, identified studies through registry review and institutional verification. ClinicalTrials.gov and the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials were searched from December 2025 through February 2026. Thirty-six studies were identified: 35 interventional trials in 6 countries and 1 North American registry-based comparative-effectiveness study. Japan had the largest share, followed by Germany, China, Italy, Austria, and France. Three centers (SPHIC, QST, and HIT) accounted for 21 of 36 studies. The portfolio included 7 phase I, 23 phase II, 5 phase III, and 1 registry study. Pancreatic cancer and sarcoma-related tumors each accounted for 7 studies; head and neck cancers, 5; prostate and breast cancers, 3 each; glioblastoma, lung cancer, and gynecologic malignancies, 2 each; and mucosal melanoma, mixed solid tumors, kidney cancer, esophageal cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma, 1 each. Most studies were conducted at a single institution and emphasized feasibility, toxicity, local control, progression-free survival, or overall survival.

This expert-verified inventory shows that CIRT studies open to enrollment remain concentrated by center, disease site, and phase. Future progress may depend more on shared outcome definitions, transparent biological dose reporting, and registry-enabled or multicenter comparative designs than on additional isolated single-institution studies.

Functional dyspepsia (FD) affects over 7% of the global population, impairing quality of life and driving significant healthcare burden. Although genetic contributions to FD pathophysiology have been suspected, large, well-powered genetic studies remain limited. We aimed to identify FD genetic risk factors and implicated biological pathways.

We performed a FD genome-wide association study in 1,168,899 European individuals, including 29,584 FD cases from five large biobanks. We dissected the genetic architecture of FD and its overlap with gastrointestinal and psychiatric disorders and conducted comprehensive gene-prioritization, regulatory, and cell-type enrichment analyzes to identify pathophysiological mechanisms and actionable targets.

We show FD is a complex, highly polygenic trait with a significant genetic component (3.63% heritability) but lower than similar gastrointestinal disorders like irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). Mendelian randomization revealed causal effects of depression and anxiety on FD risk. We observed substantial local genetic correlations with IBS and GERD, including shared genome-wide significant signals at the human leukocyte antigen region, supporting immune-mediated mechanisms. Gene-prioritization analyzes converged on DEAH-Box Helicase 36 (DHX36), involved in immune responses to microbial infections. Integrative expression-quantitative trait loci and colocalization analyzes indicated that higher genetic risk of FD is causally associated with a reduced DHX36 expression in gastrointestinal tissues, potentially triggering increased proinflammatory cytokine production.

This study implicates immune-driven pathways in FD susceptibility, adds insights into the pathophysiological mechanisms of the disease, and reveals DHX36 as a candidate target for future investigation.

Participants were categorized by diagnostic entry pathway: cytology-first, direct diagnostic evaluation without preceding cytology, or incidental detection during unrelated procedures. Limitations include retrospective design, single-center setting, and incomplete documentation.

Before standardized screening guidance, anal dysplasia in HIV-positive MSM was identified through multiple diagnostic entry pathways. These findings establish a baseline for evaluating future implementation of guideline-concordant screening in HIV care settings.
