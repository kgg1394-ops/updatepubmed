Heart failure affects more than 6 million US adults; approximately 4% to 5% progress to advanced disease despite optimal therapy. Heart transplant is a class I guideline-recommended therapy for select individuals with advanced heart failure.

Early referral for heart transplant is guided by the "I NEED HELP" mnemonic, a tool that identifies patients with advanced heart failure based on criteria such as use of inotropes, persistent symptoms (New York Heart Association class III or IV), end-organ dysfunction, left ventricular ejection fraction, defibrillator shocks, hospitalizations for heart failure, persistent edema or escalating use of diuretics, and low blood pressure. Each criterion is associated with higher risk of death or first heart failure hospitalization over a median follow-up of 260 days (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.19 [95% CI, 1.11-1.27] for each additional criterion). Typical absolute contraindications to heart transplant include active systemic infection, malignancy with limited life expectancy, severe and irreversible end-organ dysfunction (eg, cirrhosis, severe pulmonary hypertension), active substance use, major untreated psychiatric illness, inadequate social support, and severe frailty. Approximately 4600 heart transplants are performed annually in the US. The mortality rate was 8.5 deaths/100 patient-years for adults on the transplant waiting list in 2023. Leading causes of early death after transplant include primary graft dysfunction (39.5%), multiorgan failure (18.5%), and infection (13.9%). Among adults, contemporary survival rates after heart transplant are 91.5% at 1 year and 80.3% at 5 years. Lifelong maintenance immunosuppression typically combines a calcineurin inhibitor, antiproliferative agents (typically mycophenolate mofetil or azathioprine) and corticosteroids, with selective use of induction therapy (intensive immunosuppression immediately after transplant). Acute rejection requiring treatment occurs in 20% to 25% of recipients within a year, and may be treated with high-dose corticosteroids, antithymocyte globulin, and B-cell-targeted therapies (such as rituximab or bortezomib). Rejection surveillance with endomyocardial biopsies of the right ventricular septum is performed 5 to 10 times within the first year after transplant. Cardiac allograft vasculopathy (chronic rejection that causes diffuse coronary narrowing) affects 29% of heart transplant recipients within 5 years and 46.8% by 10 years and is the leading cause of late mortality. Cardiac allograft vasculopathy is treated with statins, mechanistic target of rapamycin inhibitors (such as sirolimus or everolimus), and retransplant for severe cases. Infections (62.5% are bacterial, 30.0% are viral, 6.7% are fungal, and 0.8% are parasitic) and malignancy (10.7% within 5 years after heart transplant) are other leading causes of late mortality.

Heart transplant is the definitive therapy for carefully selected patients with advanced heart failure despite receiving optimal, guideline-directed therapy. After heart transplant, lifelong multidisciplinary care is necessary to manage immunosuppression, prevent and treat rejection and cardiac allograft vasculopathy, and monitor for infection and malignancy.

Systemic therapy for biliary tract cancer (BTC) has evolved substantially over the past two decades. Gemcitabine plus cisplatin was established as the global standard first-line regimen, followed by further optimization through alternative doublets, intensified triplet chemotherapy, and the recent introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitor-based combinations. In Japan, pivotal clinical trials have played a valuable role in refining treatment strategies, including the establishment of gemcitabine plus S-1, gemcitabine, cisplatin, plus S-1, and adjuvant S-1 after curative resection. The incidence of early-onset colorectal cancer continues to increase among adults younger than 50 years, yet delays in diagnosis remain common.

As an initial step of the Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer Awareness campaign, we evaluated U.S. healthcare providers' knowledge, attitudes, diagnostic practices, and perceived barriers related to the timely recognition and evaluation of early-onset colorectal cancer.

This survey established baseline measures for a national early-onset colorectal cancer awareness campaign.

We conducted a national cross-sectional survey of physicians, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants practicing family medicine, internal medicine, emergency medicine/urgent care, and obstetrics and gynecology.

The survey assessed early-onset colorectal cancer awareness, screening recommendations, evaluation of symptomatic younger adults, perceived barriers to diagnosis, and educational needs. Descriptive statistics summarized responses, and multivariable regression identified factors associated with guideline-concordant screening and diagnostic evaluation.

A total of 277 providers completed the survey. Although 87% were concerned or very concerned about early-onset colorectal cancer and 82% reported increased concern over the preceding five years, providers identified substantial barriers to timely diagnosis. Approximately one-half reported that younger adults commonly experienced symptoms for four months or longer before seeking care. The most frequently cited barriers included patients expecting symptoms to resolve (84%), insurance and financial barriers (79%), reluctance to undergo colonoscopy (77%), and failure to recognize symptoms as serious (65%). Most providers (79%) identified additional guidance on early-onset colorectal cancer symptoms, risk factors, and diagnostic evaluation as the educational resource most likely to improve care.

Despite widespread awareness of the increasing incidence of early-onset colorectal cancer, patient- and system-level barriers continue to delay diagnosis in younger adults. These findings identify opportunities for targeted education, clinical decision support, and health system interventions to promote timely evaluation of symptomatic patients and improve early detection of early-onset colorectal cancer. Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic, often progressive, type 2 inflammatory disease characterized by eosinophilic infiltration, and esophageal narrowing and dysfunction. This analysis presents 24-week outcomes from the phase 4 REMODEL trial, evaluating long-term esophageal distensibility in adults with EoE.

Adult EoE patients were randomized 2:1 to dupilumab 300 mg once weekly or placebo. Patients were assessed overall and by low (<17 mm) and normal (≥17 mm) baseline distensibility plateau (DP) subgroups. Endpoints at week (W) 24 included: absolute change from baseline in esophageal DP diameter on endoluminal functional lumen imaging probe (primary), and changes from baseline in Endoscopic Reference Scores (EREFS) and EoE histology scoring system (EoEHSS) grade/stage scores. Safety was assessed.

Sixty-nine patients (46 dupilumab, 23 placebo) were randomized. Evaluable patients in the DP subgroups were: 30 low (19 dupilumab, 11 placebo) and 33 normal (22 dupilumab, 11 placebo) baseline DP. At W24 overall, dupilumab improved esophageal DP diameter vs placebo; least squares mean (LSM) (±SE) absolute change from baseline: +1.28 mm (0.29) and -0.01 mm (0.42); LSM difference: +1.30 mm (95% CI: 0.31, 2.29; P < 0.05). No new safety signals were identified.

Dupilumab significantly improved W24 esophageal distensibility outcomes, with greater improvements in the low baseline DP subgroup compared to the overall population. (ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT06101095).

💡 Conclusion
Telemedicine interventions may lead to little difference in readmissions or emergency department visits after colorectal surgery. Telemedicine may improve patient satisfaction versus usual care. Evidence was of low certainty, indicating a need for larger randomized trials and high-quality observational studies with appropriate design and analysis to minimize confounding.
There has been increasing interest in telemedicine follow-up after surgery. This study aims to synthesize the literature regarding the effect of postoperative telemedicine after colorectal surgery on 30-day readmission and emergency department visit rates compared with usual care.

Relevant databases were searched until September 12, 2025. Two authors independently identified trials, extracted data, assessed risk of bias (ROB 2.0 and ROBINS-I), and evidence certainty (GRADE). Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and controlled observational studies were included. Meta-analyses were conducted using random-effects models. Primary outcomes were 30-day readmissions and 30-day emergency department visits. Secondary outcomes included patient satisfaction and compliance with the telemedicine intervention.

Thirteen studies (five RCTs, eight cohort studies) were included. Compared to usual care, telemedicine resulted in no difference in 30-day readmissions (odds ratio [OR]; 0.96; 95% CI: 0.69, 1.35, 11 studies, 7102 patients, low certainty) and 30-day emergency department visits (OR; 1.02; 95% CI: 0.67, 1.57, 8 studies, 6694 patients, low certainty). Evidence certainty was limited by serious imprecision and moderate to high risk of bias. Patient compliance with interventions ranged from 48.6% to 95.3% across studies. Patient satisfaction was higher for telemedicine compared to usual care (standardized mean difference; 0.88; 95% CI: 0.31, 1.45, 3 studies, 217 patients).

Telemedicine interventions may lead to little difference in readmissions or emergency department visits after colorectal surgery. Telemedicine may improve patient satisfaction versus usual care. Evidence was of low certainty, indicating a need for larger randomized trials and high-quality observational studies with appropriate design and analysis to minimize confounding.

To assess photon counting CT (PCCT) spectral parameters in the differentiation of locally recurrent pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PDAC) from benign postsurgical fibrosis after PDAC surgical resection.

Bepirovirsen's approaching approval is an inflection point. qHBsAg should guide personalized, sequential therapy, with partial cure (HBsAg < 100 IU/mL) as a potential intermediate target when functional cure is unlikely. Chronic hepatitis B (CHB) remains a leading cause of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma worldwide, but long-term or even lifelong viral suppression with nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) seldom clears hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), so-called 'functional cure.'
