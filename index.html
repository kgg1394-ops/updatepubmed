💡 Conclusion
This two-step trial will address key therapeutic questions in mPDAC and support translational research.
Treatment of metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (mPDAC) relies on chemotherapy. Although NALIRIFOX (Nal-IRI, oxaliplatin, 5-fluorouracil [5-FU], leucovorin) improves survival, cumulative toxicity may limit duration, and maintenance strategies remain unexplored. Intratumoral microbiota, particularly Gammaproteobacteria expressing cytidine deaminase, may inactivate gemcitabine and contribute to resistance, a process potentially reversible with antibiotics. We aim to assess maintenance 5-FU after NALIRIFOX and microbiota modulation with fluoroquinolones to enhance gemcitabine sensitivity.

PANORAMIX is a multicenter, proof-of-concept, non-comparative phase II trial conducted across 18 French centers with two sequential randomizations. After 8 cycles of NALIRIFOX, patients with mPDAC are randomized (2:1) to either maintenance 5-FU or continuation of NALIRIFOX (Step 1). In Step 2, patients are randomized (1:1) to ciprofloxacin or placebo combined with gemcitabine-based chemotherapy (± paclitaxel). A total of 108 and 92 patients will be enrolled in Steps 1 and 2, respectively. Main inclusion criteria include age >18 years, confirmed mPDAC, ECOG performance status 0-1, and adequate organ function. Primary endpoints are 6-month progression-free survival (Step 1) and 6-month overall survival (Step 2). Secondary endpoints include toxicity and quality of life.

This two-step trial will address key therapeutic questions in mPDAC and support translational research.

Racial/ethnic disparities in colorectal cancer mortality persist in the United States despite treatment advances. We examined racial/ethnic differences in guideline-concordant care (GCC) receipt and cancer-specific mortality, estimated the proportion of the Black-White disparity mediated through GCC, and tested for heterogeneity in the GCC-survival association across groups.

Population-based cohort study using the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Program. Adults with a first primary colorectal adenocarcinoma diagnosed 2010-2017 were included (N = 45,267). GCC was operationalized as a composite score (0-4) per NCCN recommendations. The primary outcome, cancer-specific mortality (CSM), was analyzed using Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard models. Race/ethnicity x GCC interactions were tested by likelihood ratio test; causal mediation decomposed effects into mediated and direct components.

Among 45,267 patients (52% male), CSM occurred in 10,478 (23%) over a median 77 months. GCC receipt differed significantly across groups (all p < 0.001). Non-Hispanic Black patients had higher adjusted CSM than White patients (SHR 1.20, 95% CI 1.11-1.28); Asian/PI patients had lower CSM (SHR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78-0.95). A significant race x GCC interaction was identified (p = 0.0036), with a stronger GCC-CSM association among Black than White patients (interaction HR 0.907, 95% CI 0.847-0.971). GCC differences accounted for only 2.2% (95% CI 0.8-4.5%) of the Black-White disparity.

Racial/ethnic disparities in colorectal cancer mortality were not substantially explained by differences in GCC receipt. A significant race/ethnicity x GCC interaction indicated that Black patients derived greater survival benefit per care component. Equitable care delivery is important but insufficient to eliminate persistent disparities; structural and social determinants must also be addressed.

💡 Conclusion
Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog-directed therapy produces meaningful antitumor activity in previously treated PDAC, greatest for combination regimens and earlier treatment; the evidence remains heterogeneous and largely early-phase, and randomized trials are needed before routine adoption.
Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog mutations drive over 90% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). Direct RAS inhibitors have shown early activity, but the only prior meta-analysis pooled early-phase monotherapy cohorts and predates the first phase 3 randomized trial and newer combination regimens. We estimated the objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and safety of Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog-targeted therapy in locally advanced or metastatic PDAC.

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and SCOPUS were searched from inception through July 2026. Single-arm proportions were pooled using a random-effects generalized linear mixed model; RASolute 302 was analyzed separately. Prespecified subgroups stratified ORR by mechanistic class and line of therapy.

A total of 1008 patients from 16 cohorts across 7 trials were included; 15 single-arm cohorts (507 patients) contributed to pooled estimates. Pooled ORR was 38.9% (95% CI: 29.9%-48.8%; I2=75.7%) and varied significantly by mechanism (P<0.0001): combination regimens showed the highest activity (60.5%), followed by G12D-selective (31.0%), pan-RAS(ON) (26.6%), and G12C-selective monotherapy (25.4%). Response declined with successive lines of therapy (P=0.0102), from 72.5% in treatment-naive patients to 32.5% after ≥3 prior lines. Pooled DCR was 87.6% (95% CI: 82.8%-91.2%). Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 47.3%. In RASolute 302, daraxonrasib nearly doubled median overall survival versus chemotherapy (13.2 vs. 6.6 mo).

Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog-directed therapy produces meaningful antitumor activity in previously treated PDAC, greatest for combination regimens and earlier treatment; the evidence remains heterogeneous and largely early-phase, and randomized trials are needed before routine adoption.

Short-course radiotherapy (SCRT)-based regimens, including SCRT-alone and SCRT-based total neoadjuvant therapy (SCRT-based TNT, defined as SCRT followed by consolidation chemotherapy), have emerged as alternative neoadjuvant options to long-course chemoradiotherapy (LCCRT) for locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC). This meta-analysis stratified analyses by the type of SCRT regimen and whether LCCRT arms received preoperative consolidation chemotherapy, to systematically compare their efficacy and safety and inform personalized treatment selection.

A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science identified randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing SCRT-based regimens and LCCRT up to November 2025. Two reviewers independently performed study selection, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment. Pooled analyses were conducted using Review Manager 5.3, with relative risk (RR) or hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Nine randomized trials (reported in 16 publications), involving 3,061 patients, were included. In the primary comparison of SCRT-based TNT versus standard LCCRT, the SCRT-based TNT group achieved a significantly higher pathological complete response rate (RR = 1.56, 95% CI: 1.24-1.97, P = 0.0002), whereas tumor downstaging and locoregional failure showed no significant differences from the LCCRT group. The SCRT-based TNT regimen was associated with a significantly higher incidence of grade ≥ 3 acute toxicity (RR = 1.63, 95% CI: 1.14-2.34, P = 0.008), and showed numerical trends toward improved overall survival (HR = 0.99, 95% CI: 0.76-1.28, P = 0.91) and disease-free survival (HR = 1.05, 95% CI: 0.77-1.43, P = 0.77) that did not reach statistical significance. SCRT alone was associated with lower pCR (RR = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.03-0.51, P = 0.003) and downstaging rates (RR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.56-0.79, P < 0.00001) but a markedly lower incidence of acute toxicity (RR = 0.12, 95% CI: 0.05-0.29, P < 0.00001). An exploratory analysis based on a single small trial found no significant differences between SCRT-based TNT and LCCRT-based TNT across all major endpoints. Pooling all SCRT-based strategies obscured clinically meaningful heterogeneity and yielded no significant differences for any primary endpoint.

SCRT-based TNT significantly improves pCR compared with standard LCCRT but does not translate into survival benefit, with a numerical increase in locoregional failure. SCRT-alone offers lower acute toxicity but substantially poorer tumor response, warranting use only in selected patients. The comparison between SCRT-based TNT and LCCRT-based TNT remains inconclusive. SCRT-based regimens represent heterogeneous treatment pathways rather than a uniform substitute for LCCRT, requiring individualized clinical decision-making.

▶When toxicity limits cure: Repositioning radiation in esophageal and rectal cancers by proton therapy.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
For locally advanced esophageal and rectal cancers, radiotherapy (RT) provides essential local control but is frequently limited by acute and late toxicities that erode survival and quality of life (QoL). Over the last decade, evidence has implicated radiation dose to the heart, lungs, bowel, and pelvic bone marrow in increased postoperative morbidity, cardiopulmonary deaths, and hematologic toxicity, compromising chemotherapy delivery and long-term outcomes. The physical properties of proton beam therapy (PBT), which eliminate exit dose and reduce integral dose, offer a fundamental advantage. Systematic evidence confirms PBT consistently lowers low- to intermediate-dose exposure to organs at risk (OARs). For esophageal cancer, robust dosimetric data show reductions of approximately 50-65% in mean heart dose and 40-50% in lung V5, translating to reduced total toxicity burden (TTB) and perioperative complications in randomized and real-world studies. In rectal cancer, PBT reduces pelvic bone marrow V10 by 40-55% and small bowel V15-V30 by 30-50%, a prerequisite for improving the tolerability of intensive regimens. While definitive Phase III survival data are awaited, current evidence supports the selective use of PBT to improve the therapeutic index of radiotherapy, allowing its benefits to be realized with less collateral damage.

▶Temporal patterns and influencing factors associated with low anterior resection syndrome after rectal cancer surgery: A scoping review.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
ObjectivesTo map the existing evidence on the evolution of low anterior resection syndrome, including factors associated with stage-specific low anterior resection syndrome outcomes and longitudinal changes in symptoms.MethodsThis scoping review was conducted in accordance with the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews guideline. Seven databases were searched through 31 December 2025, with English databases searched from 1 January 2012 and Chinese databases from 1 January 2013. Eligible studies included adults aged ≥18 years who underwent sphincter-preserving anterior resection for rectal cancer and were diagnosed with low anterior resection syndrome at least twice postoperatively.ResultsTwenty-three studies were included. Major low anterior resection syndrome was most prevalent during the early follow-up period, particularly at approximately 1-3 months. Symptom improvement was most evident between 6 and 18 months, whereas overall symptom patterns tended to stabilize after approximately 18-24 months, with relatively limited changes subsequently up to 36 months. Tumor and anastomotic location, surgical approach, radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy, and stoma-related factors were associated with low anterior resection syndrome at different follow-up stages or with longitudinal symptom changes. However, only a few studies directly examined longitudinal changes or trajectory-related factors.ConclusionLow anterior resection syndrome is a dynamic condition with stage-specific patterns and substantial inter-individual variability. Understanding its temporal evolution and associated factors may help identify high-risk patients, optimize the timing of assessment, and tailor supportive care and rehabilitation across recovery stages. However, heterogeneity in study design, follow-up schedules, and outcome measures limits comparability. Future research should adopt standardized longitudinal designs, harmonized follow-up protocols, larger samples, and more comprehensive assessment tools to strengthen evidence for individualized low anterior resection syndrome management.

📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단

Postoperative nutritional management is critical for gastrointestinal cancer surgery patients, yet discharge advice is often standardized and lacks individualization. Whether an AI-facilitated individualized and quantified strategy improves short-term dietary intake compared with standard generic advice remains unclear; this trial does not isolate the independent effect of AI.

To determine whether AI-facilitated individualized nutritional guidance using structured prompts improves short-term dietary intake adequacy and patient-reported symptoms versus standard discharge advice.

In this single-center, randomized controlled trial, 90 patients undergoing laparoscopic gastric or colorectal cancer surgery were assigned 1:1 to AI-facilitated individualized guidance generated by DeepSeek v3.2 using an ABC-structured prompt plus clinician review or standard discharge dietary advice (standard care group). The intervention combined AI, individualization, quantified targets, written guidance, and clinician review; the trial cannot isolate AI alone. Primary outcome was energy intake adequacy (≥ 90% of target) at 30 days post-discharge. Secondary outcomes included protein intake adequacy, nutritional status, patient-reported outcomes, process measures (expert-rated quality scores, provider time), and 30-day complications. Outcome assessors were blinded to group assignment.

Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between groups. The AI-guided group achieved significantly higher energy adequacy (66.7% vs. 40.0%, OR 3.0, 95% CI 1.3-7.1, P = 0.01) and protein adequacy (62.2% vs. 35.6%, OR 2.9, 95% CI 1.2-6.8, P = 0.01) at 30 days. Expert-rated quality scores were superior in the AI-guided group for completeness (4.6 vs. 3.2), individualization (4.8 vs. 2.8), and actionability (4.5 vs. 3.5) (all P < 0.001), while provider time per patient was reduced (8.2 vs. 12.5 min, P < 0.001). Patient-reported outcomes showed significant improvements in fatigue, nausea/vomiting, appetite loss, and gastrointestinal symptoms (GSRS total score 2.1 vs. 2.5, P = 0.03) in the AI-guided group. No significant differences were observed in weight change, serum nutritional markers, or complication rates.

AI-facilitated individualized nutritional guidance improved short-term dietary intake adequacy and patient-reported symptoms and reduced provider time compared with standard generic advice. These findings do not show that AI itself is superior to similarly individualized non-AI counseling and do not establish longer-term nutritional or clinical benefit. Clinical supervision remains essential.

▶Effects of Psychotherapy on Quality of Life among Patients with Inflammatory Bowel Disease: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis.

⚡ Action: ⚠️ 기존 유지 권장
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Individuals diagnosed with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) have been known to have diminished quality of life (QoL). This present study aimed to examine the impact of psychotherapy on the QoL of patients with IBD. A systematic search for studies was conducted through a total of six databases, namely, MEDLINE, PubMed, Scopus, SAGE, ProQuest (psychology database), and ProQuest (public health database) from their date of inception up to 20 March 2024. The inclusion criteria were studies on patients with IBD, carried out through a randomised controlled trial design, published in the English language, and with full-text content available online. Two researchers independently extracted articles that met the selection criteria and evaluated the quality of each study. A random-effects meta-analysis was conducted, and data were presented in weighted mean difference (WMD) with a 95% CI. Fourteen studies were included in the systematic review, of which three were included in the meta-analysis. Overall, while psychotherapy showed no significant improvements in some QoL domains such as clinical bowel condition and systemic symptoms, notable enhancements were observed in social functioning and overall QoL. The findings underscore the importance of integrating psychotherapeutic approaches into IBD management to address psychological and social challenges, thereby enhancing overall patient well-being. These results have significant implications for clinical mental health counsellors and future research in IBD care.

Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a major complication of colorectal cancer (CRC), but whether CRC-associated thrombotic susceptibility includes cell-specific inherited regulatory components in peripheral blood remains unclear. To investigate associations between CRC genetic liability and VTE-related outcomes within peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) regulatory contexts. We integrated CRC GWAS, FinnGen VTE-related outcomes, and TenK10K PBMC single-cell eQTL data across 28 immune cell types using cell-stratified Mendelian randomization (csMR). Prioritized signals were further examined in single-cell RNA-seq datasets and bulk transcriptomes. Conventional MR showed no clear evidence that CRC liability increased VTE, DVT, or PE risk, and LD Score regression detected no significant genome-wide genetic correlation. In contrast, csMR identified nominal, cell-context-dependent associations. B intermediate cells were prioritized across VTE (OR, 1.16; 95% CI, 1.03-1.32), DVT (OR, 1.30; 95% CI, 1.08-1.57), and PE (OR, 1.26; 95% CI, 1.07-1.49), while CD14 + monocytes and CD4 + central memory T cells received additional support from downstream single-cell analyses. Gene-level analyses prioritized LAMA5, LIMA1, and TRIM28 in these respective cell contexts. In bulk blood, RPS21 showed consistent disease-associated expression patterns and discriminated VTE patients from healthy controls in an external dataset (AUC, 0.865; 95% CI, 0.800-0.923). These findings suggest that genetic overlap between CRC liability and thrombotic phenotypes may be localized to selected PBMC regulatory contexts rather than broadly detectable genome-wide. Among the prioritized signals, B intermediate cells showed the most consistent csMR associations across VTE, DVT, and PE. The cell-gene pairs, together with RPS21, warrant independent genetic, functional, and prospective validation as candidates for studying peripheral immune regulation and blood-based markers of CRC-associated thrombosis.

Endoscopic ultrasound-guided gallbladder drainage (EUS-GBD) and endoscopic transpapillary gallbladder drainage (ET-GBD) are alternatives for high surgical-risk patients with acute cholecystitis (AC). Their comparative efficacy and safety remain uncertain.

Studies comparing EUS-GBD and ET-GBD in high surgical-risk AC patients were pooled using random-effects models, with subgroup analyses by stent type. Primary outcomes were technical and clinical success; secondary outcomes included recurrence of cholecystitis, post-procedure cholangitis, procedural time and adverse events (AEs). Trial sequential analysis (TSA) assessed conclusiveness.

Nine studies (1,479 patients) were included. EUS-GBD achieved higher technical success (odds ratio [OR], 4.34; 95% confidence interval [CI], 2.18-8.65) and clinical success (OR, 2.79; 95% CI, 1.34-5.80), with lower recurrence of cholecystitis (OR, 0.34; 95% CI, 0.18-0.66) and post-procedure cholangitis (OR, 0.22; 95% CI, 0.06-0.87). Overall AE rates were similar (OR, 1.16; 95% CI, 0.75-1.79), although bile leak was more frequent with EUS-GBD (OR, 5.90; 95% CI, 1.01-34.60). Procedural time was shorter with EUS-GBD (weighted mean difference, -13.17minutes; 95% CI, -23.64 to -2.70). Technical success favored EUS-GBD with all stent types, whereas clinical success and recurrence favored EUS-GBD with metal but not plastic stents, although subgroup differences were not significant. TSA supported the technical success and recurrence findings, but clinical success was less firmly established.

EUS-GBD offers superior technical and clinical success and reduces recurrence of cholecystitis and post-procedure cholangitis compared with ET-GBD in high surgical-risk AC patients. Bile leak may be more frequent. Stent-specific findings were exploratory. Prospective trials warranted to confirm these results and to evaluate cost-effectiveness.

Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) remains a challenging biliary disease characterized by persistent inflammation and extensive fibrosis. However, its molecular drivers are not fully understood. Here, we explored the functional significance of NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1) in the progression of PSC.

Wild-type (WT) and Nqo1 knockout (NKO) male mice were fed a diet containing 0.1% (w/w) 3,5-diethoxycarbonyl-1,4-dihydrocollidine (DDC) for 1 or 3 weeks to induce cholestatic liver injury. Liver tissues and plasma samples were collected for biochemical, histological, and molecular analyses. To further investigate the underlying mechanisms, in vitro experiments were performed using HepG2 and LX-2 cell lines as well as primary hepatocytes.

NQO1 expression was markedly upregulated under cholestatic conditions. During the acute phase of DDC-induced injury, NKO mice demonstrated a protective phenotype, characterized by significantly reduced plasma ALT levels and less severe histological injury compared with WT controls. Histological analysis further revealed fewer TUNEL-positive hepatocytes and reduced fibrotic deposition in NKO mice. Transcriptomic profiling indicated that NQO1 ablation suppressed the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and inflammation pathways, while enhancing bile acids and xenobiotic metabolism. Mechanistically, periostin was identified as a key upstream factor capable of inducing NQO1 expression and promoting intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation. Periostin-induced NQO1 protein expression was attenuated by both BAY 11-7082 and N-acetyl-L-cysteine. Notably, the disruption of NQO1 blunted periostin-driven oxidative stress. However, this protective effect is limited to the early phase of the disease and is not maintained during prolonged cholestatic injury.

NQO1 can function as a context-dependent regulator that exacerbates oxidative stress and fibrosis during the initial stages of PSC and suggests that targeting the periostin-NQO1 axis may offer therapeutic benefits.

Evaluate alignment between calculated Diagnosis-Intervention Packet (DIP) payment entitlements and observed inpatient costs and identify insurance, regional, and disease-group patterns in payment-cost misalignment.

Retrospective observational study of all inpatient final settlements (2023-2024).

We analyzed 23,597 discharge settlements using linear mixed-effects models with DIP group as a random intercept and heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors to compare signed payment-cost gaps. Robustness was assessed with model diagnostics and 1st-99th percentile trimming; separate gamma-log mixed models evaluated total cost and calculated DIP payment. Analyses compared insurance type (UEBMI vs. URRBMI) and insured region (local vs. non-local); high-volume DIP groups were defined as those with at least 150 admissions, and groups with cost-to-calculated-payment ratios of at least 3.0 were profiled as descriptive outliers.

Mean total cost per admission was CNY 14,604 and mean calculated DIP payment was CNY 4865, equivalent to 33.3% of observed inpatient cost. The mean signed payment-cost gap was -CNY 9739. Adjusted gaps were more negative for UEBMI than URRBMI (-CNY 12,971 vs. -CNY 8936) and for non-local than local admissions (-CNY 11,796 vs. -CNY 9279); trimmed analyses were consistent. Ten high-volume cerebrovascular, oncologic, renal, cardiac, infectious, and pancreatic clusters accounted for 18.8% of episodes and 35.6% of the cohort's net payment-cost shortfall.

At this county hospital, calculated DIP payments were equivalent to about one-third of observed inpatient spending, with concentrated shortfalls in complex neurovascular and oncologic groups and larger adjusted gaps among UEBMI and non-local admissions. These associations should not be interpreted causally because detailed clinical severity and comorbidity were unavailable. Although site-specific, the findings suggest that case-based payment systems may require severity adjustment, outlier protection, and periodic recalibration to reduce systematic payment-cost misalignment while safeguarding access to complex care.
