💡 Conclusion
These updated national guidelines were developed jointly by the Saudi Gastroenterology Association, Saudi Society of Colon and Rectal Surgery, Saudi Society of Gastrointestinal Oncology, and Saudi Society of Family and Community Medicine, and provide evidence-based recommendations for colorectal cancer (CRC) screening in Saudi Arabia, superseding the 2015 national guidelines. This update incorporates new epidemiological data from Saudi Arabia, including updated incidence estimates, polyp prevalence studies, colonoscopy quality audit data, and national screening uptake surveys, to provide a comprehensively evidence-anchored and locally contextualized framework. A national multidisciplinary group formulated 25 key Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome (PICO) questions across seven domains. Systematic literature searches were performed from inception to March 2026. Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence profiles were prepared for critical outcomes, and recommendations were developed through an iterative consensus process requiring ≥80% agreement for strong recommendations. The Task Force strongly recommends implementing an organized national CRC screening program for all asymptomatic average-risk adults aged 45 to 75 years. Biennial fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) is recommended as the primary population-based screening modality. Colonoscopy every 10 years is a recommended alternative. All positive FIT results must be followed by timely, high-quality colonoscopy. Specific recommendations are provided for high-risk groups. High-certainty evidence from multiple randomized trials and large-scale implementation studies demonstrates that organized CRC screening substantially reduces CRC-specific mortality and incidence. A phased, FIT-based national implementation strategy is recommended, tailored to Saudi epidemiology, population preferences, and healthcare infrastructure, with iterative quality improvement as new evidence emerges.

▶High-Frequency Chest Wall Oscillation in Critical Care: A Systematic Review.

💡 Conclusion
High-frequency chest wall oscillation (HFCWO) represents a type of respiratory physiotherapy that facilitates the noninvasive mobilization of airway secretions using a vest that inflates to deliver high-frequency oscillations to the chest wall. This technique promotes the mobilization of mucus secretions for easier clearance or suctioning. The majority of scientific evidence regarding the efficacy of HFCWO has been reported for patients with chronic respiratory diseases, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or cystic fibrosis, with most studies carried out in outpatient or home healthcare settings. However, there has been limited scientific investigation of HFCWO use in the intensive care unit (ICU) settings, with considerable variability in study design, patient populations, and outcomes. The purpose of this systematic review was to compile existing scientific evidence regarding HFCWO in critically ill patients as this represents a clinically relevant question due to the major contribution of mucus hypersecretion to the failure of noninvasive ventilation and the potential to increase the risk of orotracheal intubation and the need for mechanical ventilation in patients with acute respiratory failure due to any cause.
Airway secretion retention represents a major clinical challenge in critically ill patients, promoting gas exchange impairment and atelectasis, precipitating failure of noninvasive respiratory support, and prolonging invasive mechanical ventilation, with an associated increase in respiratory-related complications and mortality. High-frequency chest wall oscillation (HFCWO) is a noninvasive airway clearance technique widely used in chronic respiratory diseases, but its role in the intensive care unit (ICU) remains unclear. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the available evidence on the efficacy and safety of HFCWO in adult ICU patients.

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane Library from inception to December 2025 in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. Studies evaluating HFCWO in adult ICU patients, including those receiving invasive or noninvasive respiratory support, were eligible. Primary outcomes included duration of mechanical ventilation, ICU length of stay, and mortality. Secondary outcomes included impact on secretion airway clearance and its radiographic/functional surrogates as well as safety and treatment tolerance.

A limited number of studies met the inclusion criteria, mainly small randomized controlled trials and observational studies, characterized by substantial heterogeneity in patient populations, intervention protocols, and assessed outcomes. HFCWO effects on clinically meaningful primary endpoints, including duration of mechanical ventilation, length of ICU stay, and mortality, remain variable and inconclusive. Overall, HFCWO was well tolerated and appeared to enhance secretion clearance, with some studies reporting modest improvements in oxygenation and respiratory mechanics.

Current evidence suggests that HFCWO is a safe and feasible airway clearance strategy in selected ICU patients with secretion burden and without proved effects on the main clinical outcomes. Lack of physiopathologically driven criteria for selection of patients more likely to respond to HFCWO either alone or integrated with other cough-assisting techniques may partially explain these scanty findings from the overall published studies. This precludes its routine use in clinical practice. Well-designed, adequately powered randomized trials are needed to define patient selection criteria, optimal timing, and the true clinical impact of HFCWO in the ICU setting.

💡 Conclusion
Several clinical and social factors were associated with increased risk of mood and anxiety outcomes in adult cancer survivors. These findings may support risk-based psychiatric screening and early intervention in resource-limited oncology settings.
Mood and anxiety disorders are common in cancer survivors, and are associated with worse outcomes; however, systematic identification of high-risk patients remains limited, despite screening and intervention guidelines. A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted to identify risk factors for mood and anxiety disorders, including diagnoses and clinically significant symptoms, in adult cancer survivors.

PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, PsycINFO, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched for cohort and case-control studies published from January 2000 to November 2024 that evaluated cancer-related, sociodemographic, lifestyle, and psychosocial risk factors in adults with breast, lung, colorectal, prostate, stomach, liver, or pancreatic cancer. Random-effects models with robust variance estimation pooled relative risks (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for factors assessed in ≥5 studies.

Fifty of the 53 eligible studies were included in the meta-analysis. Consistent risk factors included high tumor stage (vs. low; n = 28; RR, 1.55; 95% CI, 1.21-1.97), more physical comorbidities (vs. fewer; n = 36; RR, 1.57; 95% CI, 1.34-1.84), prior psychiatric history (high vs. low burden; n = 13; RR, 4.85; 95% CI, 2.77-8.50), female sex (vs. male; n = 19; RR, 1.52; 95% CI, 1.32-1.75), underweight body mass index (vs. normal; n = 6; RR, 1.93; 95% CI, 1.55-2.44), unmarried status (vs. married; n = 25; RR, 1.35; 95% CI, 1.14-1.58), and current smoking (vs. never; n = 12; RR, 1.27; 95% CI, 1.12-1.46). Suggestive associations, although less consistent in sensitivity analyses, were observed for high tumor grade, more extensive surgery, low income, low education, and low social support.

Several clinical and social factors were associated with increased risk of mood and anxiety outcomes in adult cancer survivors. These findings may support risk-based psychiatric screening and early intervention in resource-limited oncology settings.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).
Complex perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease are often refractory to the recommended combination of surgery and biological drug therapy. We investigated the efficacy and safety of minimally manipulated, microfragmented, autologous adipose tissue ("MFat"), after surgical debridement of the fistula, in patients with Crohn's disease in whom standard treatments had failed.

This double-blind, randomized controlled trial enrolled 80 adults who all underwent cone-like partial fistulectomy of a complex fistula (one inner orifice and up to three external openings) followed by either MFat injection or suture of the inner orifice and saline injection. MFat was prepared from lipoaspirates using Lipogems medical devices immediately before fistulectomy and transplanted around the inner orifice and along the residual tracts immediately after fistulectomy. The primary efficacy outcome was combined (clinicoradiological) remission at 24 weeks in the per-protocol populations. Safety was assessed in the all-patients-treated populations.

After randomization, 37 patients in each group were treated and 33 patients each were assessed. Combined remission occurred in 19 patients (57.6%) of the intervention group and six control patients (18.2%) (risk ratio 3.17; 95% CI, 1.45-6.91; P = .004; number needed to treat, 3). Clinical remission was found in 26 (78.8%) and 18 (54.5%) patients, respectively (P = .044), while clinical indices improved in both groups. Seven adverse events were reported, without significant differences between groups in safety endpoints.

Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).

▶Co-design and feasibility of an mHealth survivorship program for colorectal cancer survivors: a mix-methods study.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
This co-designed mHealth survivorship program showed encouraging engagement among CRC survivors after primary treatment, with exploratory findings suggesting potential effects on selected survivorship outcomes. Trial registration NCT07158671 on ClinicalTrials.gov. Registered on September 8, 2025.
Colorectal cancer (CRC) survivors experience persistent challenges that negatively affect health-related quality of life (HRQoL). While mobile health (mHealth) may offer a useful approach to survivorship care, limited user involvement and scarcity of structured programs in developing countries remain barriers. This study aimed to co-design and evaluate the feasibility of an mHealth survivorship program for CRC survivors following primary treatment.

A mixed-methods study was conducted in two phases. Phase 1 utilized a user-centered design framework involving 12 co-designers across nine online sessions to develop and refine the FORYOU mHealth program. Phase 2 was a pilot randomized controlled trial (RCT) with 30 CRC survivors to assess feasibility and preliminary effectiveness of the 12-week program, measured by HRQoL outcomes.

In phase 1, the co-designed program indicated above-average usability (System Usability Scale ≥ 68) and adequate acceptability (E-Acceptability Scale ≥ 24). In phase 2, the adherence rate was 73.3%; participants in the intervention group completed an average of 5.4 out of 6 sessions and accessed the app 9.7 times. Although no significant between-group difference was observed in overall HRQoL, exploratory findings were mixed. The intervention favored the subscale functional well-being (P < 0.05; g = 1.34), whereas the control group favored the secondary outcome, fatigue. These preliminary findings require cautious interpretation.

The co-designed mHealth program was usable and acceptable, and the pilot RCT was feasible among CRC survivors after primary treatment. Exploratory findings suggested possible benefits in selected outcomes, but these preliminary findings require confirmation in a larger adequately powered trial.

This co-designed mHealth survivorship program showed encouraging engagement among CRC survivors after primary treatment, with exploratory findings suggesting potential effects on selected survivorship outcomes. Trial registration NCT07158671 on ClinicalTrials.gov. Registered on September 8, 2025.

▶Fecal Microbiota Transplantation in Patients With Cirrhosis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Efficacy and Safety.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 USG 6개월 간격
💡 Conclusion
Low-certainty evidence from 4 small trials suggests that FMT may reduce HE recurrence in cirrhosis, with no signal of excess treatment-related harm. The evidence base is still limited, and future adequately powered randomised trials are still required to compare routes of administration, or to support a change in practice.
Cirrhosis constitutes a major burden, owing to frequent decompensations, rehospitalizations, and significant clinical implications. Current standard, symptom-targeted therapies are limited in effectiveness. Targeting the gut-liver axis as the upstream driver of decompensation, fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) may be beneficial.

Following PRISMA, we searched Scopus, ScienceDirect, PubMed, Cochrane, Embase, and ClinicalTrials.gov for studies investigating FMT for cirrhosis. Quality of randomized trials was evaluated using the Cochrane Risk-of-Bias (RoB) 2.0, nonrandomized studies using Risk Of Bias In Nonrandomised Studies-of Interventions (ROBINS-I), and case series using the Joanna Briggs Institute tool. Inverse-variance random-effects models were fitted in R 4.5.2, with between-study variance estimated by restricted maximum likelihood. Pooled risk ratios (RR) were derived from participant-level event counts for binary outcomes and Hartung-Knapp adjusted standardised mean differences (SMD) for continuous outcomes. Certainty of evidence was rated using GRADE.

Nine studies with 245 participants were included; 4 contributed to the quantitative synthesis. FMT reduced hepatic encephalopathy (HE) recurrence (4 comparisons, 80 participants, 16 events; RR=0.22 [95% CI: 0.08-0.62]; P=0.0040; I²=0%) and liver-related hospitalisation [4 comparisons, 80 participants, 19 events; RR=0.29 (95% CI: 0.12-0.69); P=0.0050; I²=0%], although the hospitalisation effect was not retained when all-cause admissions were analysed [RR=0.69 (95% CI: 0.35-1.36); P=0.28]. Serious adverse events were borderline lower with FMT [5 comparisons, 100 participants, 42 events; RR=0.54 (95% CI: 0.29-0.99); P=0.0461; I²=13.2%], but this estimate was not robust to leave-one-out analysis. Only one serious event across all 9 studies was adjudicated as FMT-attributable. FMT did not significantly change MELD scores [SMD= -0.12 (95% CI: -0.48 to 0.24)] or serum ammonia [SMD= -0.27 (95% CI: -0.67 to 0.12)]. Certainty of evidence was low for HE recurrence and very low for all other outcomes.

Low-certainty evidence from 4 small trials suggests that FMT may reduce HE recurrence in cirrhosis, with no signal of excess treatment-related harm. The evidence base is still limited, and future adequately powered randomised trials are still required to compare routes of administration, or to support a change in practice.

▶Long-Term Prognosis and Transition Period until Progression in Fibrotic Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Nearly half of fHP patients progressed to PPF within a few years, with markedly reduced survival. Early recognition of high-risk patients may support timely antifibrotic treatment and improve outcomes.
Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (HP) is an interstitial lung disease that may present as fibrotic (fHP) or non-fibrotic (nfHP). Fibrosis is associated with progression and poor outcomes; however, the transition to progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) and its predictors remain unclear.

We retrospectively reviewed adult patients diagnosed with HP at a tertiary hospital (2010 and 2022). Patients were classified as having fHP or nfHP and were followed up for at least 1 year. Clinical, functional, radiological, and histological variables were analyzed. PPF was defined according to the 2022 clinical practice guideline of the American Thoracic Society (ATS), European Respiratory Society (ERS), Japanese Respiratory Society (JRS), and Asociación Latinoamericana de Tórax (ALAT).

Of 298 HP patients, 191 were included (153 fHP and 38 nfHP). Among patients with fHP, 76 (49.7%) developed PPF after a median of 3.2 years. Median survival was 4.8 years in fHP with PPF versus 6.3 years without, with 1.6 years from PPF onset to death or transplantation. The independent predictors of PPF and survival were lower forced vital capacity, elevated lactate dehydrogenase, fibroblastic foci, and microscopic honeycombing.

Nearly half of fHP patients progressed to PPF within a few years, with markedly reduced survival. Early recognition of high-risk patients may support timely antifibrotic treatment and improve outcomes.

Catheter ablation for persistent atrial fibrillation remains less effective than for paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, despite pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) forming the foundation of both procedures. Ablating beyond the pulmonary veins, including treatment of non-pulmonary vein triggers, posterior wall isolation, linear lesions, vein of Marshall ethanol infusion, isolation of the superior vena cava, coronary sinus or left atrial appendage, and modification of regions identified by fibrosis or low voltage has grown interest. Functional outcomes (International Knee Documentation Committee, Knee injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score, Lysholm, and Tegner) also did not differ meaningfully between sexes. Heterogeneity was moderate, and subgroup analyses by graft type, follow-up duration, and physical activity level did not alter findings.

Sex alone does not appear to independently determine graft failure, RTS success, or functional recovery after ACL reconstruction. Instead, postoperative outcomes are more likely driven by modifiable factors such as graft characteristics, rehabilitation quality, neuromuscular recovery, and sport exposure. These findings suggest that individualized functional assessment may be more informative for clinical decision-making than sex alone.

Low-GL diet reshapes the serum metabolome of T2DM patients and increases fucose abundance. Fucose enhances pancreatic β-cell function, partly through suppression of ubiquitination and Rnf183. These findings provide mechanistic insights into dietary interventions for diabetes management.
Our previous studies demonstrated that low-glycemic load (GL) diets reduced diabetes risk and promoted remission in type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear.

Serum samples from newly diagnosed T2DM patients with random 3-month low-GL dietary intervention or conventional energy-restricted diet were analyzed using LC-MS-based metabolomics. High glucose (HG)-treated MIN6 cells with or without fucose were used to assess β-cell function, apoptosis, and transcriptomic sequencing. Rnf183 was knocked down in MIN6 cells.

Metabolomic analysis revealed that metabolites identified by LC-MS were mostly classified as carboxylic acids and derivatives, fatty acyls, and glycerophospholipids. Fucose exhibited the highest VIP score among all differential metabolites and was significantly upregulated in T2DM patients with a low-GL diet compared to those without a low-GL diet. KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that fucose-associated fructose and mannose metabolism was affected by the low-GL diet. Fucose enhanced the expression of key β-cell functional genes (Pdx1, Nkx6.1, Mafa, and Insulin2) and attenuated HG-induced apoptosis in MIN6 cells. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that fucose regulated T2DM progression-related pathways (Notch signaling and insulin secretion) and protein ubiquitination in HG-induced MIN6 cells. Correspondingly, fucose reduced global ubiquitination of MIN6 cells and downregulated ubiquitination regulator Rnf183. Rnf183 knockdown increased expression levels of key β-cell functional genes in HG-treated MIN6 cells.

Low-GL diet reshapes the serum metabolome of T2DM patients and increases fucose abundance. Fucose enhances pancreatic β-cell function, partly through suppression of ubiquitination and Rnf183. These findings provide mechanistic insights into dietary interventions for diabetes management.
