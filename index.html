Persistent myocardial fibrosis and dysregulated energy metabolism following MI contribute to adverse cardiac remodeling and are associated with poor clinical prognosis. Currently, no guideline-recommended therapy provides effective long-term intervention simultaneously targeting both of these core pathological mechanisms. Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic autoimmune liver disease in which long-term clinical outcomes are difficult to assess within conventional trial durations. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and bilirubin are established biochemical endpoints in regulatory and clinical practice, and their relationship with long-term clinical outcomes has been demonstrated through a robust literature of analyses across different datasets. However, the magnitude and consistency of their association with long-term clinical outcomes have not been systematically quantified through meta-analysis.

This systematic literature review and meta-analysis aimed to identify, evaluate, and summarize evidence on the correlation between surrogate biochemical endpoints and long-term clinical outcomes in patients with PBC.

A comprehensive search of key biomedical databases and reporting was conducted, in accordance with Cochrane, NICE and PRISMA guidelines. Meta-analysis was performed using Stata 19.5 SE software, with the DerSimonian and Laird (DL) random-effects model employed in the base-case, inverse-variance fixed-effect and DL + Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman random effects model were used in sensitivity analyses. Other sensitivity analyses were performed based on study/follow-up duration, study quality, publication type, and removal of heterogeneous studies.

Overall, 129 studies were included in the systematic literature review, among which 89 studies met criteria for meta-analysis. Failure to achieve ALP normalization was associated with significantly increased risks of composite clinical outcomes (HR; 95% CI: 1.46; 1.26-1.69), liver-related events (1.82; 1.41-2.34), and overall death and/or liver transplantation (1.52; 1.30-1.79). Similar findings were reported for other ALP response definitions. Nonresponse to composite criteria was also associated with significantly worse long-term outcomes. Sensitivity analyses yielded directionally consistent results.

To our knowledge, this research represents the first comprehensive meta-analytic evaluation integrating multiple surrogate biochemical endpoints with a broad spectrum of long-term clinical outcomes in PBC across a global study population. Associations were directionally consistent across ALP-, bilirubin-, and composite-based definitions and across all analyses.

This meta-analysis provides robust evidence that ALP normalization, ALP-based response criteria, and composite biochemical response measures (comprising ALP and total bilirubin), were consistently associated with long-term clinical outcomes in PBC and may inform HTA, reimbursement, payer decision-making, and economic evaluation when mature long-term outcome data are unavailable.

Venlafaxine, a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, has been investigated in oncology for several supportive care indications and, more recently, for potential anticancer activity. However, the available evidence remains fragmented across clinical and preclinical studies.

This systematic review aimed to synthesize the available evidence regarding the role of venlafaxine in oncology, including established supportive care applications and emerging anticancer potential.

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science from database inception to 22 August 2026. Original clinical and preclinical studies evaluating venlafaxine in cancer populations or experimental cancer models were eligible. Clinical and preclinical evidence was synthesized separately using a narrative approach. Methodological quality was assessed using Joanna Briggs Institute tools for clinical studies and the SYRCLE Risk of Bias tool for animal studies.

25 studies were included, comprising 23 clinical studies and 2 preclinical studies. The strongest clinical evidence supported venlafaxine for treatment-related hot flashes, particularly among breast cancer survivors receiving endocrine therapy, with the strongest randomized, placebo-controlled trial evidence showing significantly greater reductions with venlafaxine than placebo (P < 0.0001 for higher doses); within-group percentage reductions are also reported but should not be interpreted as treatment effects. Evidence for chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy was inconsistent, with potential benefit for acute oxaliplatin-related neurotoxicity but insufficient support for prevention or routine treatment of chronic CIPN. Evidence for other supportive care outcomes, including oral mucositis-associated pain, fatigue, sleep quality, depressive symptoms, and quality of life, was limited. Venlafaxine was generally well tolerated, although adverse effects varied by dose and comparator. Preclinical studies investigated anticancer activity, but not equivalently: one in melanoma models showed direct antiproliferative/pro-apoptotic effects of venlafaxine via JNK1/2-Nur77 signaling, whereas a colorectal cancer study showed only context-dependent antagonism of norepinephrine-driven tumor progression, with no significant effect of venlafaxine alone. No clinical evidence currently supports venlafaxine as a direct anticancer treatment.

Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic autoimmune liver disease in which long-term clinical outcomes are difficult to assess within conventional trial durations. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and bilirubin are established biochemical endpoints in regulatory and clinical practice, and their relationship with long-term clinical outcomes has been demonstrated through a robust literature of analyses across different datasets. However, the magnitude and consistency of their association with long-term clinical outcomes have not been systematically quantified through meta-analysis.

This systematic literature review and meta-analysis aimed to identify, evaluate, and summarize evidence on the correlation between surrogate biochemical endpoints and long-term clinical outcomes in patients with PBC.

A comprehensive search of key biomedical databases and reporting was conducted, in accordance with Cochrane, NICE and PRISMA guidelines. Meta-analysis was performed using Stata 19.5 SE software, with the DerSimonian and Laird (DL) random-effects model employed in the base-case, inverse-variance fixed-effect and DL + Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman random effects model were used in sensitivity analyses. Other sensitivity analyses were performed based on study/follow-up duration, study quality, publication type, and removal of heterogeneous studies.

Overall, 129 studies were included in the systematic literature review, among which 89 studies met criteria for meta-analysis. Failure to achieve ALP normalization was associated with significantly increased risks of composite clinical outcomes (HR; 95% CI: 1.46; 1.26-1.69), liver-related events (1.82; 1.41-2.34), and overall death and/or liver transplantation (1.52; 1.30-1.79). Similar findings were reported for other ALP response definitions. Nonresponse to composite criteria was also associated with significantly worse long-term outcomes. Sensitivity analyses yielded directionally consistent results.

To our knowledge, this research represents the first comprehensive meta-analytic evaluation integrating multiple surrogate biochemical endpoints with a broad spectrum of long-term clinical outcomes in PBC across a global study population. Associations were directionally consistent across ALP-, bilirubin-, and composite-based definitions and across all analyses.

This meta-analysis provides robust evidence that ALP normalization, ALP-based response criteria, and composite biochemical response measures (comprising ALP and total bilirubin), were consistently associated with long-term clinical outcomes in PBC and may inform HTA, reimbursement, payer decision-making, and economic evaluation when mature long-term outcome data are unavailable.

💡 Conclusion
The twenty-fifth anniversary of the discovery of obeticholic acid (OCA) in 2027 offers an opportunity to reconsider its scientific contribution and clinical trajectory. First described in 2002 as 6α-ethyl-chenodeoxycholic acid, OCA helped establish the farnesoid X receptor (FXR) as a therapeutic target linking bile acid homeostasis to inflammation and fibrosis. This review follows the molecule from its discovery and early biological characterization through pharmaceutical development to the withdrawal of its primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) authorizations in the European Union and the United States. Clinical trials demonstrated biochemical efficacy in PBC and biopsy-defined fibrosis improvement in non-cirrhotic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis but did not establish corresponding long-term clinical benefit. Pruritus, adverse lipoprotein changes, and hepatic safety concerns complicated treatment, while the COBALT confirmatory trial failed to verify benefit in its randomized analysis despite favorable external-control comparisons. This history highlights the distinction between pharmacological activity, surrogate responses, and patient benefit. It also raises questions about whether earlier prioritization of lower exposure and complementary therapies could have improved tolerability, without assuming that these strategies would have prevented withdrawal. OCA's legacy extends to subsequent FXR ligands and approaches emphasizing molecular selectivity, tissue distribution, and temporal control of receptor activation. Its experience supports continued FXR investigation while underscoring that therapeutic value requires sustained clinical benefit and acceptable safety.

The aim of the present clinical trial was to evaluate the efficacy and toxicity of ramucirumab in combination with nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine as first-line treatment in locally advanced or metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

This was a prospective, single-arm, phase Ib-II trial. In phase Ib part of the study, the recommended doses were determined. In phase II part of the study, all patients were treated with the combination at the recommended doses to assess efficacy and toxicity. According to the study primary endpoint, at least 16 patients should respond to the treatment by RECIST criteria. The primary endpoint was overall response rate by RECIST 1.1, defined as best overall response without mandatory confirmation.

From 1/2019 to 10/2021, 53 patients were enrolled. Ten patients (19.2%) had locally advanced disease. The recommended doses determined during phase Ib were Ramucirumab 8mg/Kg Day 1 and 15, Nab-paclitaxel 100mg/m2 day 1,8,15 and gemcitabine 800mg/m2 day 1,8,15 in 28 days cycle. In total, 14 (26.4%) patients responded to treatment (all partial responses). Consequently, the primary endpoint was not met. The median duration of response was 9.9 months (95%CI: 5.4-15 months), median progression-free survival was 4 months (95%CI: 4-10 months) and median overall survival was 14 months (95%CI: 7.3-18 months). Twenty of 52 patients (38%) required a dose reduction and 11 (21%) permanently discontinued study treatment for toxicity.

The trial did not meet its primary endpoint nor did it generate robust evidence of efficacy to warrant further assessment in unselected pancreatic cancer patients CLINICAL TRIALS GOV: NCT03745430.

💡 Conclusion
Verapamil has attracted interest as a potential means of preserving pancreatic β-cell function through suppression of thioredoxin-interacting protein (TXNIP), raising the question of whether it differs from other calcium channel blockers in the risk of incident type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to quantify that association and assess the certainty of the evidence. PubMed, Embase, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP were searched from inception to 10 September 2026. Randomised trials and observational cohort studies comparing verapamil with another calcium channel blocker and reporting incident T2DM were eligible. Adjusted hazard ratios were pooled using a random-effects model with the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman method. Risk of bias was assessed using ROBINS-I and certainty of evidence using GRADE. Two population-based cohort studies were included, one from Taiwan and one from Australia. The pooled estimate favoured verapamil but was imprecise (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.48-1.26), and was compatible with a substantial reduction in risk, with no difference, and with a modest increase. Statistical significance depended on the method used to account for between-study uncertainty, being present under the DerSimonian-Laird estimator (0.78, 95% CI 0.68-0.88) but not under the primary analysis. Both studies were at serious risk of bias from residual confounding, and the certainty of evidence was very low. The available evidence is insufficient to inform the choice of calcium channel blocker, and a randomised comparison with prespecified glycaemic ascertainment is required.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), DNA-protein complexes released by neutrophils, promote metastasis and immunosuppression in various cancers, but the prognostic value of NETs-related genes in pancreatic cancer (PC) remains unclear. We integrated multiple PC transcriptomic datasets from GEO with a curated NETs gene set. Differential expression analysis and WGCNA identified candidates, and three feature-selection methods (LASSO, SVM-RFE, random forest) selected core genes. A four-gene risk score model was constructed and validated in an independent cohort, with additional immune infiltration and drug-gene network analyses. In vitro, elevated SLC2A1 in PANC-1 cells promoted proliferation, invasion, and NETs formation in neutrophil co-cultures. This multi-cohort analysis identifies a novel NETs-related four-gene signature and establishes a prognostic risk model for PC, highlighting the critical role of NETs biology in PC progression and provide a potential prognostic tool to facilitate patient stratification and guide precision oncology applications, with a foundation for future validation.
