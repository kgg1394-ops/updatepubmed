Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), has been inconsistently linked to incident atrial fibrillation (AF) across observational cohorts. We performed an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies, following the Meta-analysis Of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) guidelines, searching PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science through July 25, 2026, for studies comparing incident AF risk in adults with versus without NAFLD/MASLD. Adjusted effect estimates were pooled using a random-effects model, with heterogeneity assessed via the I² statistic and explored through subgroup and univariable meta-regression analyses. Eighteen cohort studies comprising over 15 million participants from Asia, Europe, and North America met inclusion criteria. Pooling all studies demonstrated a significantly increased risk of incident AF among individuals with NAFLD/MASLD compared with those without (pooled risk ratio (RR) 1.23, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.12-1.34, p < 0.001), with substantial statistical heterogeneity (I² = 99.4%). In an exploratory analysis of three studies, advanced disease with hepatic fibrosis was also associated with increased AF risk (RR 1.19, 95% CI 1.11-1.28). In the present meta-analysis, NAFLD/MASLD was associated with a modestly higher risk of incident AF after adjustment for conventional cardiometabolic risk factors, although heterogeneity was very high. These findings support attention to AF risk in this population and the use of standardized diagnostic approaches in future research.

Venlafaxine, a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, has been investigated in oncology for several supportive care indications and, more recently, for potential anticancer activity. However, the available evidence remains fragmented across clinical and preclinical studies.

This systematic review aimed to synthesize the available evidence regarding the role of venlafaxine in oncology, including established supportive care applications and emerging anticancer potential.

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science from database inception to 22 August 2026. Original clinical and preclinical studies evaluating venlafaxine in cancer populations or experimental cancer models were eligible. Clinical and preclinical evidence was synthesized separately using a narrative approach. Methodological quality was assessed using Joanna Briggs Institute tools for clinical studies and the SYRCLE Risk of Bias tool for animal studies.

25 studies were included, comprising 23 clinical studies and 2 preclinical studies. The strongest clinical evidence supported venlafaxine for treatment-related hot flashes, particularly among breast cancer survivors receiving endocrine therapy, with the strongest randomized, placebo-controlled trial evidence showing significantly greater reductions with venlafaxine than placebo (P < 0.0001 for higher doses); within-group percentage reductions are also reported but should not be interpreted as treatment effects. Evidence for chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy was inconsistent, with potential benefit for acute oxaliplatin-related neurotoxicity but insufficient support for prevention or routine treatment of chronic CIPN. Evidence for other supportive care outcomes, including oral mucositis-associated pain, fatigue, sleep quality, depressive symptoms, and quality of life, was limited. Venlafaxine was generally well tolerated, although adverse effects varied by dose and comparator. Preclinical studies investigated anticancer activity, but not equivalently: one in melanoma models showed direct antiproliferative/pro-apoptotic effects of venlafaxine via JNK1/2-Nur77 signaling, whereas a colorectal cancer study showed only context-dependent antagonism of norepinephrine-driven tumor progression, with no significant effect of venlafaxine alone. No clinical evidence currently supports venlafaxine as a direct anticancer treatment.

Venlafaxine has an established evidence-based role in supportive oncology for treatment-related hot flashes, whereas its role in CIPN and other supportive care indications remains uncertain. Emerging anticancer findings are limited to preclinical models and should be considered hypothesis-generating. Further randomized clinical trials are needed for supportive care indications, while additional mechanistic and preclinical validation is required before venlafaxine can be considered for clinical evaluation as an anticancer therapy.

Adjuvant chemotherapy (ACT) indication in stage II colorectal cancer (CRC) remains challenging, as clinical, pathological, and ctDNA inadequately predict benefit. Immune infiltration correlates with outcome, yet transcriptional signatures have not been systematically evaluated in prospective trial cohorts. We aimed to identify a prognostic transcriptional signature in high-risk stage II CRC patients treated with ACT within the TOSCA trial.

From the TOSCA trial, 124 high-risk stage II patients were selected using a matched case-control design, with matching based on center, pT stage, ACT regimen, and treatment duration. Whole-transcriptome sequencing was performed on FFPE samples. MSI status was inferred bioinformatically. Prognostic associations were assessed using log-rank tests and multivariable Cox models adjusting for MSI status and tumor sidedness.

Seventy-two samples met RNA quality criteria. Median follow-up was 70.9 months; 40% were right-sided, 60% received FOLFOX, 54% 3-month ACT, and 19% were MSI-high. Transcriptomic analyses highlighted immune-regulatory and TGF-β/stromal programs associated with outcome. A biologically defined Immune Exclusion (IE) phenotype, characterized by low ICR and high Hallmark TGF-β signaling, was observed in approximately 28% of patients and was associated with shorter DFS (HR=2.22, p = 0.036). A reduced seven-gene IE signature (rIE) was subsequently derived and was also associated with shorter DFS after multivariable adjustment (HR=2.83, p = 0.012).

The IE phenotype and its reduced rIE signature were associated with shorter DFS in high-risk stage II colon cancer and represent exploratory candidate prognostic biomarkers requiring validation in independent cohorts.

💡 Conclusion
Circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) detection after curative-intent treatment is one of the strongest prognostic biomarkers in colorectal cancer (CRC), but its clinical utility remains incompletely established. Randomised trials have shown that ctDNA-informed management can reduce adjuvant chemotherapy exposure in selected stage II colon cancer, yet non-inferiority was not established in stage III disease, and cytotoxic escalation for ctDNA-positive molecular residual disease (MRD) has not improved recurrence-free survival. These mixed interventional results do not diminish the prognostic validity of ctDNA, but suggest that its clinical utility depends on four interacting considerations: the biological state represented by ctDNA positivity, the timing of testing and intervention, assay and sampling heterogeneity, and trial design. Serial ctDNA dynamics may provide additional biological and prognostic information, as sustained clearance, transient clearance, persistence, and re-emergence are unlikely to represent equivalent states of MRD. Western Pacific studies have led the field, including GALAXY and ALTAIR in Japan and Taiwan, DYNAMIC in Australia, FIND and CINTS-R in China, and CLAUDIA in Korea. However, regional implementation is constrained by uneven laboratory infrastructure, cost, regulatory access, and limited evidence from Southeast Asia and Pacific lower-resource settings. Outside clinical trials, ctDNA should refine prognosis rather than dictate omission, intensification, or initiation of treatment. Future trials should align assay performance, biological timing, and intervention mechanism, and use patient-centred endpoints capable of demonstrating that acting on ctDNA improves outcomes.

Verapamil has attracted interest as a potential means of preserving pancreatic β-cell function through suppression of thioredoxin-interacting protein (TXNIP), raising the question of whether it differs from other calcium channel blockers in the risk of incident type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to quantify that association and assess the certainty of the evidence. PubMed, Embase, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP were searched from inception to 10 September 2026. Randomised trials and observational cohort studies comparing verapamil with another calcium channel blocker and reporting incident T2DM were eligible. Adjusted hazard ratios were pooled using a random-effects model with the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman method. Risk of bias was assessed using ROBINS-I and certainty of evidence using GRADE. Two population-based cohort studies were included, one from Taiwan and one from Australia. Both studies were at serious risk of bias from residual confounding, and the certainty of evidence was very low. Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), DNA-protein complexes released by neutrophils, promote metastasis and immunosuppression in various cancers, but the prognostic value of NETs-related genes in pancreatic cancer (PC) remains unclear. We integrated multiple PC transcriptomic datasets from GEO with a curated NETs gene set.
