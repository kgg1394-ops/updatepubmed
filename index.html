💡 Conclusion
Colonoscopy is central to colorectal cancer prevention because it enables early detection and removal of premalignant lesions. However, lesions can still be missed, and endoscopic differentiation of neoplastic from nonneoplastic polyps remains variable, particularly for diminutive lesions. The potential of artificial intelligence (AI) as a supportive technology for improving both polyp detection and characterization during colonoscopy has therefore been investigated. Since early studies of convolutional neural network-based computer-aided detection (CADe), the focus has shifted from retrospective image-based validation to real-time randomized trials, meta-analyses, and clinical guideline discussions. Accumulating evidence suggests that CADe improves surrogate detection outcomes, including the adenoma detection rate (ADR), polyp detection rate, adenomas per colonoscopy, and adenoma miss rate. Although a higher ADR is associated with a lower risk of postcolonoscopy colorectal cancer, it remains unknown whether the incremental improvement in the ADR achieved by CADe translates into lower postcolonoscopy colorectal cancer incidence or colorectal cancer-related mortality. Recent multicenter data further support the effectiveness of real-time CADe systems in improving detection-related outcomes, while also indicating that benefit varies with lesion characteristics and user experience. In parallel, computer-aided diagnosis (CADx) has been developed to assist the optical characterization of colorectal polyps, particularly for diminutive lesions and potential resect-and-discard or diagnose-and-leave strategies. CADx systems using white-light imaging and structured high-confidence diagnostic rules show promise, but the clinical implementation of CADx remains less mature than that of CADe. This review summarizes current evidence for AI-assisted polyp detection and characterization and discusses limitations and future directions for integrating CADe and CADx into human-centered colonoscopy practices.

Pumitamig, an investigational bispecific antibody targeting programmed death ligand-1 and vascular endothelial growth factor A, has shown preliminary efficacy across several tumour types. We aimed to evaluate the safety and activity of pumitamig plus nab-paclitaxel as first-line treatment in patients with advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer.

In this single-arm, phase 1b/2 trial conducted at ten sites (hospitals and cancer centres) in China, patients aged 18-75 years with histologically documented, unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 1 or less, and one or more measurable lesion (as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours, version 1.1) received first-line pumitamig (20 mg/kg intravenously once every 2 weeks) plus nab-paclitaxel (100 mg/m

Between June 27, 2022, and March 15, 2023, 42 patients were enrolled, received one or more doses of pumitamig plus nab-paclitaxel (median duration of exposure 9·0 months, 95% CI 6·9-11·7) and had one or more tumour assessments. All patients were Chinese and female; median age was 53 years (IQR 41-60). At a median follow-up of 35·2 months (95% CI 33·0-36·1), the confirmed objective response rate was 73·8% (95% CI 58·0-86·1; 31 of 42 patients), including one complete response and 30 partial responses. The most frequent treatment-related adverse events of grade 3 or worse were decreased neutrophil count (13 [31%] of 42 patients), decreased white blood cell count (11 [26%]), proteinuria (six [14%]), and febrile neutropenia (five [12%]). Nine (21%) patients reported 14 treatment-related serious adverse events (alanine aminotransferase increased n=2; febrile neutropenia n=2; pancreatitis n=2; and ascites, aspartate aminotransferase increased, abdominal distension, γ-glutamyltransferase increased, blood alkaline phosphatase increased, cataract, macular oedema, and retinal vein occlusion all n=1). Treatment-related adverse events led to discontinuation of both nab-paclitaxel and pumitamig in four (10%) patients, discontinuation of nab-paclitaxel only in three (7%) patients, and discontinuation of pumitamig only in two (5%) patients. There were no treatment-related deaths.

Treatment with first-line pumitamig plus nab-paclitaxel showed encouraging antitumour activity in patients with advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer and a safety profile consistent with the mode of actions of both agents. These findings warrant further clinical evaluation in global phase 2 and 3 trials.

Prior analyses of PALLAS (NCT02513394), a phase III trial that randomized patients with stage II-III hormone receptor-positive breast cancer to receive adjuvant palbociclib plus endocrine therapy (ET) or ET alone, did not show benefit of the combination. Separate data show extent of treatment-related neutropenia is associated with improved response to palbociclib and other cell cycle-specific therapies. We conducted an exploratory analysis to assess whether grade of treatment-related neutropenia could identify benefit from palbociclib in the adjuvant setting.

Patients receiving palbociclib were classified into those with treatment-emergent high-grade neutropenia (HGN) with maximum grade ≥3 (absolute neutrophil count <1000/mm3), or low-grade/no neutropenia (LGN) with maximum grade ≤2. These groups were compared to each other and to the ET-alone group for 5-year invasive disease-free survival (iDFS).

5736 patients were included: 2840 in the palbociclib+ET arm and 2896 in the ET-alone arm. Patients receiving palbociclib+ET consisted of 1006 (35.4%) LGN and 1834 (64.6%) HGN. Lower body mass index (BMI), Asian race, prior chemotherapy, and mastectomy were associated with HGN (all p < 0.05). Patients receiving ET alone had inferior 5-year iDFS compared with patients who developed HGN by cycle 6 on palbociclib+ET (hazard ratio [HR] 1.19, 95% confidence interval [CI]:1.01-1.40; p = 0.04), an association that persisted after adjustment for prior chemotherapy, race, and BMI (HR 1.20, 95% CI:1.02-1.42; p = 0.03).

In this exploratory analysis of the PALLAS trial, palbociclib+ET showed benefit over ET alone in patients who developed HGN in the first 6 treatment cycles. Neutropenia could be a useful biomarker for palbociclib efficacy.

💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer is becoming more common in adults under age 50, but little is known about the symptoms these patients experience. This study is a secondary analysis of baseline data examining fatigue, pain, and emotional distress in 165 adults aged 18-50 with colorectal cancer who were enrolled in a symptom management study. Participants completed questionnaires about their symptoms at the beginning of the study. Researchers used statistical methods to identify groups of patients with similar symptom patterns. Overall, participants reported high levels of fatigue, moderate distress, and lower levels of pain. Two distinct symptom groups emerged. About 60% of participants had lower pain with moderate fatigue and distress, while 40% had moderate pain along with high fatigue and high distress. Symptom patterns differed between men and women and by body mass index. These findings suggest that younger adults with colorectal cancer commonly experience multiple symptoms at the same time, particularly fatigue and emotional distress. Recognizing these symptom patterns may help healthcare providers identify patients who need additional support. The results also highlight the need for treatments that address multiple symptoms together rather than focusing on a single symptom. Because this study included only patients who were already experiencing symptoms and enrolled in a symptom management trial, future research should confirm these findings in larger and more diverse groups of younger adults with colorectal cancer.

Sex-based and BMI differences in class membership exist; findings warrant replication. Findings support developing multi-symptom interventions tailored to the needs of EO-CRC patients.

▶Context-dependent functions of insulin, IGF, and GLP signaling in inflammatory bowel disease: linking metabolism, immunity, and intestinal homeostasis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), encompassing Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, is a chronic relapsing inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract characterized by dysregulated immune responses, impaired epithelial barrier function, and alterations in the gut microbiota. Although considerable progress has been made in understanding the immunopathogenesis of IBD, increasing evidence indicates that metabolic signaling pathways are integral regulators of intestinal inflammation, tissue repair, and disease progression. Among these, insulin, insulin-like growth factors (IGFs), and glucagon-like peptides (GLPs) constitute an interconnected immunometabolic network that links nutrient sensing, energy metabolism, epithelial regeneration, and immune homeostasis. Insulin signaling plays context-dependent roles in IBD by regulating glucose metabolism, epithelial repair, inflammatory responses, and interactions with the gut microbiota and bile acid metabolism. While insulin promotes mucosal healing and barrier restoration in intestinal epithelial cells, excessive activation of insulin signaling in immune cells may exacerbate chronic inflammation through epigenetic and cytokine-mediated mechanisms. The IGF axis is also profoundly altered during active IBD, with systemic suppression of IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 accompanying disease activity, whereas local IGF signaling promotes epithelial regeneration, wound healing, and immune regulation. In addition, IGF-binding proteins (IGFBPs) function as active modulators of inflammation, fibrosis, and colitis-associated colorectal cancer in an isoform-specific manner. GLP signaling further integrates metabolism with intestinal immunity; GLP-1 receptor agonists exhibit anti-inflammatory, metabolic, and microbiota-modulating effects, whereas GLP-2 primarily enhances epithelial proliferation, barrier integrity, and mucosal repair. Emerging preclinical and observational clinical studies suggest that GLP-based therapies may improve disease outcomes, although prospective randomized trials are still required. This review summarizes current knowledge regarding the biological functions and molecular mechanisms of insulin, IGFs, IGFBPs, and GLPs in IBD, highlighting their coordinated roles in regulating metabolism, immunity, epithelial homeostasis, and tissue remodeling. A better understanding of these interconnected signaling pathways may facilitate the development of novel immunometabolic therapeutic strategies aimed at promoting mucosal healing, limiting chronic inflammation, and improving long-term outcomes in patients with IBD.

Gallbladder carcinoma (GBC) is the most common biliary tract malignancy and is usually diagnosed at an advanced stage. Artificial intelligence (AI), including radiomics, machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), is increasingly applied to its diagnosis, surgical management and prognostication, but how far the reported performance can be trusted is unclear.

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library and Google Scholar were searched to 5 May 2026 using database-specific search strategies. Each primary prediction-model study was characterized separately by design, number of centers and validation type (internal, temporal or external).

Of 31 primary AI studies in GBC, 29 were retrospective, 22 were single-center and 24 reported internal validation only. In independent test or external cohorts, DL and ML models discriminated benign from malignant gallbladder lesions with areas under the curve (AUCs) of approximately 0.80-0.89; a multicenter model distinguishing xanthogranulomatous cholecystitis from GBC achieved an external AUC of 0.88, and automated computed tomography (CT) segmentation an external Dice score of 0.76. The best recurrence model achieved an AUC of 0.76 on internal (random-split) validation; none of the reviewed prognostic models reported a quantified external-validation estimate. Intraoperative AI is technically feasible but has no outcome data, and federated learning has been tested only in simulation.

AI could plausibly assist several steps of GBC care, but the evidence is dominated by retrospective, single-center, internally validated studies from Asia, and metrics from different studies are not directly comparable. Prospective multicenter external validation, shared annotated datasets and standardized reporting are required before clinical adoption.

▶Continued burden of nontuberculous mycobacteria in the screened subpopulations of people with cystic fibrosis receiving CFTR modulator therapy.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Nontuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) are respiratory pathogens associated with poor clinical outcomes in people with cystic fibrosis (pwCF). CFTR modulator therapy (CFTRm), particularly elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ETI), has dramatically improved outcomes in pwCF and is associated with reductions in Pseudomonas aeruginosa and methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus. However, impact on NTM remains unclear. We conducted parallel retrospective analyses of NTM trends among pwCF from 2017-2024 using CF Foundation Patient Registry (CFFPR) Annual Reports and surveillance data from National Jewish Health (NJH) Adult CF Program. Annual surveillance rates and NTM culture positivity were evaluated. For NJH longitudinal data (2020-2025), a generalized linear mixed model was used to assess NTM positivity trends, including a random intercept for subjects to account for repeated measures and intermittent missing cultures. From 2017-2024, U.S. and NJH NTM epidemiology showed parallel trends: higher NTM positivity among pwCF with cultures before CFTRm expansion and a decline in years 2020-2021, with increased rates by 2024. NJH consistently reported higher NTM positivity among cultured pwCF, yet patient-level NTM positivity aligned closely with U.S. rates. During the study, respiratory cultures substantially decreased, from 50.3% to 30.2% nationally and from 83.5% to 46.4% at NJH. NTM positivity is stable to increasing. In adjusted models, odds of NTM positivity increased annually by 4.2% nationally and 11.4% at NJH from 2020 to 2024. These findings suggest that CFTRm use decreased NTM culture surveillance, but NTM positivity is increasing. The concordance between national and referral-center data highlights the importance of reprioritizing NTM surveillance among pwCF taking CFTRm.

💡 Conclusion
The TopoEfficientViT model had high predictive performance for staging liver fibrosis by integrating multi-scale persistent homology topology with convolutional networks.
Early-stage liver fibrosis is reversible; thus, accurate staging of liver fibrosis is of great importance.. Liver biopsy is the gold standard for the diagnosis of liver fibrosis; however, its invasive nature limits its clinical application. Accurate noninvasive staging of liver fibrosis remains challenging. Thus, there is an urgent need for a precise and reliable noninvasive diagnostic approach.

A topological deep learning model combining topological data analysis and EfficientViT framework (TopoEfficientViT) was developed to effectively capture global contextual relationships in hepatobiliary phase MR images for assessment of staging liver fibrosis.

Our retrospective study included patients with chronic liver diseases from June 2018 to July 2025, which was approved by our Institutional Review Board. Our data from a hospital site (Site A) in our institution were divided into a training set (n = 275) and a validation set (n = 75). The test set (n=65) was composed of data from a separate hospital site (Site B) in the same institution.

💡 Conclusion
In 1935, Allen Oldfather Whipple performed the first one-stage pancreatoduodenectomy, establishing the foundation for an operation that has since undergone nearly a century of technical refinement. Advances in surgical technique, perioperative care, training, and technology have progressively improved the safety and reproducibility of the pancreatoduodenectomy. More recently, the introduction of minimally invasive approaches-and particularly robotic surgery-has prompted another potential paradigm shift in the operative management of pancreatic disease. As robotic pancreatoduodenectomy (RPD) has expanded over the past decade, an important question has emerged: has the robotic pancreatoduodenectomy matured sufficiently for widespread adoption or will it remain an operation confined to selected surgeons and specialized centers? A growing body of evidence has addressed the safety, feasibility, and potential benefits of RPD. Although early experience was derived from retrospective and observational studies, more recent randomized controlled trials have provided higher-level evidence regarding its clinical performance. Outcomes remain dependent on surgeon experience, institutional volume, and completion of a substantial learning curve. Accordingly, the transition of RPD into routine clinical practice should be accompanied by structured training, mentorship, outcomes reporting, and appropriate institutional and procedural volume standards. These safeguards will be essential to ensure that expansion of RPD preserves the outcomes achieved in experienced, high-volume centers.
