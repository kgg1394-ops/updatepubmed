💡 Conclusion
These updated national guidelines were developed jointly by the Saudi Gastroenterology Association, Saudi Society of Colon and Rectal Surgery, Saudi Society of Gastrointestinal Oncology, and Saudi Society of Family and Community Medicine, and provide evidence-based recommendations for colorectal cancer (CRC) screening in Saudi Arabia, superseding the 2015 national guidelines. This update incorporates new epidemiological data from Saudi Arabia, including updated incidence estimates, polyp prevalence studies, colonoscopy quality audit data, and national screening uptake surveys, to provide a comprehensively evidence-anchored and locally contextualized framework. A national multidisciplinary group formulated 25 key Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome (PICO) questions across seven domains. Systematic literature searches were performed from inception to March 2026. Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) evidence profiles were prepared for critical outcomes, and recommendations were developed through an iterative consensus process requiring ≥80% agreement for strong recommendations. The Task Force strongly recommends implementing an organized national CRC screening program for all asymptomatic average-risk adults aged 45 to 75 years. Biennial fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) is recommended as the primary population-based screening modality. Colonoscopy every 10 years is a recommended alternative. All positive FIT results must be followed by timely, high-quality colonoscopy. Specific recommendations are provided for high-risk groups. High-certainty evidence from multiple randomized trials and large-scale implementation studies demonstrates that organized CRC screening substantially reduces CRC-specific mortality and incidence. A phased, FIT-based national implementation strategy is recommended, tailored to Saudi epidemiology, population preferences, and healthcare infrastructure, with iterative quality improvement as new evidence emerges.

💡 Conclusion
Several clinical and social factors were associated with increased risk of mood and anxiety outcomes in adult cancer survivors. These findings may support risk-based psychiatric screening and early intervention in resource-limited oncology settings.
Mood and anxiety disorders are common in cancer survivors, and are associated with worse outcomes; however, systematic identification of high-risk patients remains limited, despite screening and intervention guidelines. A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted to identify risk factors for mood and anxiety disorders, including diagnoses and clinically significant symptoms, in adult cancer survivors.

PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, PsycINFO, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched for cohort and case-control studies published from January 2000 to November 2024 that evaluated cancer-related, sociodemographic, lifestyle, and psychosocial risk factors in adults with breast, lung, colorectal, prostate, stomach, liver, or pancreatic cancer. Random-effects models with robust variance estimation pooled relative risks (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for factors assessed in ≥5 studies.

Fifty of the 53 eligible studies were included in the meta-analysis. Consistent risk factors included high tumor stage (vs. low; n = 28; RR, 1.55; 95% CI, 1.21-1.97), more physical comorbidities (vs. fewer; n = 36; RR, 1.57; 95% CI, 1.34-1.84), prior psychiatric history (high vs. low burden; n = 13; RR, 4.85; 95% CI, 2.77-8.50), female sex (vs. male; n = 19; RR, 1.52; 95% CI, 1.32-1.75), underweight body mass index (vs. normal; n = 6; RR, 1.93; 95% CI, 1.55-2.44), unmarried status (vs. married; n = 25; RR, 1.35; 95% CI, 1.14-1.58), and current smoking (vs. never; n = 12; RR, 1.27; 95% CI, 1.12-1.46). Suggestive associations, although less consistent in sensitivity analyses, were observed for high tumor grade, more extensive surgery, low income, low education, and low social support.

Several clinical and social factors were associated with increased risk of mood and anxiety outcomes in adult cancer survivors. These findings may support risk-based psychiatric screening and early intervention in resource-limited oncology settings.

💡 Conclusion
Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).
Complex perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease are often refractory to the recommended combination of surgery and biological drug therapy. We investigated the efficacy and safety of minimally manipulated, microfragmented, autologous adipose tissue ("MFat"), after surgical debridement of the fistula, in patients with Crohn's disease in whom standard treatments had failed.

This double-blind, randomized controlled trial enrolled 80 adults who all underwent cone-like partial fistulectomy of a complex fistula (one inner orifice and up to three external openings) followed by either MFat injection or suture of the inner orifice and saline injection. MFat was prepared from lipoaspirates using Lipogems medical devices immediately before fistulectomy and transplanted around the inner orifice and along the residual tracts immediately after fistulectomy. The primary efficacy outcome was combined (clinicoradiological) remission at 24 weeks in the per-protocol populations. Safety was assessed in the all-patients-treated populations.

After randomization, 37 patients in each group were treated and 33 patients each were assessed. Combined remission occurred in 19 patients (57.6%) of the intervention group and six control patients (18.2%) (risk ratio 3.17; 95% CI, 1.45-6.91; P = .004; number needed to treat, 3). Clinical remission was found in 26 (78.8%) and 18 (54.5%) patients, respectively (P = .044), while clinical indices improved in both groups. Seven adverse events were reported, without significant differences between groups in safety endpoints.

Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).

💡 Conclusion

Cirrhosis constitutes a major burden, owing to frequent decompensations, rehospitalizations, and significant clinical implications. Current standard, symptom-targeted therapies are limited in effectiveness. Targeting the gut-liver axis as the upstream driver of decompensation, fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) may be beneficial.

Following PRISMA, we searched Scopus, ScienceDirect, PubMed, Cochrane, Embase, and ClinicalTrials.gov for studies investigating FMT for cirrhosis. Quality of randomized trials was evaluated using the Cochrane Risk-of-Bias (RoB) 2.0, nonrandomized studies using Risk Of Bias In Nonrandomised Studies-of Interventions (ROBINS-I), and case series using the Joanna Briggs Institute tool. Inverse-variance random-effects models were fitted in R 4.5.2, with between-study variance estimated by restricted maximum likelihood. Pooled risk ratios (RR) were derived from participant-level event counts for binary outcomes and Hartung-Knapp adjusted standardised mean differences (SMD) for continuous outcomes. Certainty of evidence was rated using GRADE.

Nine studies with 245 participants were included; 4 contributed to the quantitative synthesis. FMT reduced hepatic encephalopathy (HE) recurrence (4 comparisons, 80 participants, 16 events; RR=0.22 [95% CI: 0.08-0.62]; P=0.0040; I²=0%) and liver-related hospitalisation [4 comparisons, 80 participants, 19 events; RR=0.29 (95% CI: 0.12-0.69); P=0.0050; I²=0%], although the hospitalisation effect was not retained when all-cause admissions were analysed [RR=0.69 (95% CI: 0.35-1.36); P=0.28]. Serious adverse events were borderline lower with FMT [5 comparisons, 100 participants, 42 events; RR=0.54 (95% CI: 0.29-0.99); P=0.0461; I²=13.2%], but this estimate was not robust to leave-one-out analysis. Only one serious event across all 9 studies was adjudicated as FMT-attributable. FMT did not significantly change MELD scores [SMD= -0.12 (95% CI: -0.48 to 0.24)] or serum ammonia [SMD= -0.27 (95% CI: -0.67 to 0.12)]. Certainty of evidence was low for HE recurrence and very low for all other outcomes.

Low-certainty evidence from 4 small trials suggests that FMT may reduce HE recurrence in cirrhosis, with no signal of excess treatment-related harm. The evidence base is still limited, and future adequately powered randomised trials are still required to compare routes of administration, or to support a change in practice.

▶Injectable Hyaluronic Acid for Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause.

💡 Conclusion
Injectable hyaluronic acid-based therapies are emerging non-hormonal interventions for vulvovaginal symptoms associated with GSM. Current evidence does not yet establish their role as routine or guideline-recommended treatments. This evidentiary position should be considered separately from the intended purpose and regulatory status of individual devices. Larger independent trials, longer-term safety data, and health-economic evaluations are needed to define their comparative clinical role.
Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) is a chronic condition resulting from oestrogen deficiency and characterised by persistent genital, sexual, and urinary symptoms that can substantially impair quality of life. While local oestrogen therapy is highly effective, a significant proportion of women are unable or unwilling to use hormonal treatments, highlighting the need for effective non-hormonal options.

This narrative review examines local, non-hormonal approaches to GSM, with a particular emphasis on injectable hyaluronic acid-based therapies.

Hyaluronic acid is integral to tissue hydration, viscoelasticity, and the maintenance of extracellular matrix integrity, processes that are compromised in GSM. Topical formulations are commonly used to provide surface hydration and symptom relief, while injectable approaches deliver hyaluronic acid to deeper tissue layers; the potential implications for the magnitude and duration of clinical benefit require further evaluation. Among injectable formulations, hybrid cooperative complex hyaluronic acid (HCC-HA) has been evaluated in small prospective studies and one randomised trial in women with sexual dysfunction rather than strictly defined GSM. Available studies report potential improvements in some symptoms, including vaginal dryness and dyspareunia. However, improvements were not demonstrated across all assessed outcomes, including certain sexual-function and quality-of-life measures. No serious adverse events were reported in the available studies, although larger studies with longer follow-up are needed to characterise the safety profile more fully.

Injectable hyaluronic acid-based therapies are emerging non-hormonal interventions for vulvovaginal symptoms associated with GSM. Current evidence does not yet establish their role as routine or guideline-recommended treatments. This evidentiary position should be considered separately from the intended purpose and regulatory status of individual devices. Larger independent trials, longer-term safety data, and health-economic evaluations are needed to define their comparative clinical role.

👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: ✅ 우선 적용 고려
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Catheter ablation for persistent atrial fibrillation remains less effective than for paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, despite pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) forming the foundation of both procedures. Ablating beyond the pulmonary veins, including treatment of non-pulmonary vein triggers, posterior wall isolation, linear lesions, vein of Marshall ethanol infusion, isolation of the superior vena cava, coronary sinus or left atrial appendage, and modification of regions identified by fibrosis or low voltage has grown interest. Although observational and mechanistic studies have often been encouraging, randomized trials of empirical adjunctive ablation have produced inconsistent or neutral results. Pulsed field ablation has renewed the debate by facilitating rapid ablation of extra-pulmonary vein tissue. The PIFPAF-PFA trial did not demonstrate a significant reduction in its primary endpoint of atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence lasting at least 30 seconds with adjunctive posterior wall isolation. However, separation of the recurrence curves and exploratory signals for reduced arrhythmia burden and fewer prolonged episodes raise questions about lesion strategy and the clinical relevance of conventional recurrence definitions. These findings do not establish routine posterior wall isolation, but they illustrate why a neutral binary endpoint may not fully characterize treatment effects on recurrent AF. The controversy now extends beyond whether additional ablation is beneficial to which mechanisms should be targeted, which patients should receive adjunctive lesions, how energy source affects lesion durability and safety, and whether success should be defined by any brief recurrence or by clinically meaningful arrhythmia burden. This article examines the evidence supporting and opposing ablation beyond PVI in persistent AF.

💡 Conclusion
Bristol Myers Squibb, Cancer Research UK, Associazione Italiana per la Ricerca sul Cancro, and Foundation for Liver Research.
Nivolumab plus ipilimumab improves survival in advanced hepatocellular carcinoma. We evaluated this combination as neoadjuvant therapy and investigated its effect on peripheral immunity and the tumour microenvironment.

PRIME-HCC is a single-arm, phase 1b trial in adults (aged ≥18 years) with early-stage resectable hepatocellular carcinoma, Child-Pugh A liver function, and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0-1. Patients received nivolumab (3 mg/kg intravenously on days 1 and 22 of the first and second cycle) and ipilimumab (1 mg/kg intravenously on day 1 of the first cycle only) before liver resection. The primary endpoint was safety, assessed by treatment-related delays to surgery and adverse events in the intention-to-treat population. Translational analyses included imaging mass cytometry and spatial transcriptomics on paired tumour samples, peripheral immunophenotyping, T-cell receptor (TCR) β sequencing, and longitudinal tumour-agnostic circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) profiling. This trial was registered with EudraCT (2018-000987-27) and ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03682276); follow-up is currently ongoing.

Between Sept 18, 2019, and Sept 28, 2023, of 43 patients assessed for eligibility, 33 patients were enrolled (25 [76%] male and 8 [24%] female; median age 68 years [range 38-80; IQR 63-73]); median follow-up was 25·7 months (95% CI 21·2-30·1). Any-grade adverse events occurred in 31 (94%) of patients, with treatment-related adverse events (trAEs) in 25 (76%) patients. Grade 3 trAEs occurred in three (9%) patients (transaminase elevation, anaemia, and dyspnoea). Serious adverse events were recorded in three (9%) patients, and no grade 4-5 events or treatment-related deaths occurred. 30 (91%) patients underwent surgery, including two (6%) who had immune checkpoint inhibitor-related surgical delays.
