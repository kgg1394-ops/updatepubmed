Infliximab is a treatment commonly used for people with inflammatory bowel disease, including Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis. It is usually given through a vein in hospital, but a newer version can be injected under the skin at home. This study compared patients who continued infliximab through a vein with patients who changed to the under-the-skin injection. We studied 251 adults with inflammatory bowel disease who were already well controlled and not using steroids. Of these, 134 continued intravenous infliximab and 117 switched to subcutaneous infliximab. Comparing intravenous (IV) infliximab (IFX) with subcutaneous (SC) CT-P13 in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) may inform clinical practice, particularly in patients in stable remission receiving maintenance therapy.

To compare IV IFX and SC CT-P13 as maintenance therapy in patients with IBD.

Retrospective, single-centre, observational cohort study using propensity score weighting.

Adult patients with Crohn's disease (CD) or ulcerative colitis (UC) in stable steroid-free remission while receiving maintenance IV IFX, 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks, were included. Patients switched to SC CT-P13 were compared with patients who continued IV IFX. A propensity score-weighted analysis using inverse probability of treatment weighting was applied. The primary endpoint was time to treatment failure, defined as a composite endpoint including re-switch from SC to IV IFX, IV IFX dose intensification, steroid-requiring flare, switch to another advanced therapy, or IBD-related surgery.

Among 251 patients, 135 had CD (53.8%) and 116 had UC (46.2%); 134 remained on IV IFX and 117 switched to SC CT-P13. Mean follow-up was 24.7 ± 29.8 months in the IV group and 20.9 ± 14.9 months in the SC group. Time to treatment failure was not significantly different between groups (log-rank p = 0.10; HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.45-1.12). A significant time-dependent effect was observed (p = 0.005): discontinuation risk was initially higher after switching, but progressively decreased over time, becoming lower after approximately 8 months.

In patients with IBD in stable steroid-free remission, switching to SC CT-P13 may provide treatment persistence, safety, and disease control comparable to continued IV IFX.

Radical resection combined with intestinal autotransplantation (RRCIA) has emerged as a potential curative option for highly selected patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC) and extensive mesenteric vascular involvement. This project aimed to develop expert consensus recommendations on indications, surgical strategies, perioperative management, and long-term care of RRCIA in patients with LAPC.

An international multidisciplinary panel of experts in pancreatic surgery, transplant surgery, medical oncology, radiation oncology, radiology, critical care, and perioperative care was convened. A modified Delphi process was used to develop consensus recommendations. The survey comprised six domains with a total of 20 questions covering patient selection, neoadjuvant therapy, surgical strategies, perioperative management, adjuvant therapy, and follow-up. Consensus was predefined as ≥80% agreement.

Twenty consensus statements were developed. The quality of evidence was low for 17 statements and moderate for 3. Six recommendations were strong and 14 conditional. RRCIA was recommended only for highly selected patients at experienced high-volume centres following adequate neoadjuvant therapy and multidisciplinary evaluation. Surgical strategies should be individualized according to tumour location and vascular involvement. No definitive minimum residual bowel length was established, and preservation of well-perfused bowel and the ileocaecal valve was emphasized. Postoperative management should be tailored, including anticoagulation, nutritional support, selective implementation of enhanced recovery principles, adjuvant therapy, and structured long-term follow-up.

This consensus provides the first international framework to guide the application of RRCIA for LAPC, emphasizing its use in carefully selected patients treated at experienced centres with multidisciplinary expertise.

Use of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) for diabetes and obesity is increasing, but effects on uncommon or long-latency cancers are uncertain. We synthesised meta-analytic evidence for overall and site-specific cancer risk and assessed methodological quality, overlap, outcome-level certainty, and credibility.

We searched PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from inception to 30 June 2026 for meta-analyses of GLP-1RAs or the dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide and incident cancer. Randomised and observational evidence was considered separately; review-level estimates were not recombined. Older gastric cancer survivors after radical gastrectomy are vulnerable to frailty progression. Family function may influence frailty by enhancing individual psychological resilience. However, few studies have examined these longitudinal associations. We aimed to evaluate the longitudinal associations among family function, psychological resilience, and frailty.

A total of 412 older gastric cancer survivors who underwent radical gastrectomy were enrolled and assessed at hospital discharge, and again at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively.
