The clinical efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy (± targeted agents) in patients with proficient mismatch repair (pMMR) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) remains unclear. This study systematically evaluated the efficacy of this regimen.

We searched PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov for relevant randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published up to May 2026. The primary outcomes were progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS); effect sizes were estimated as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (95%CIs).

These subgroup findings are exploratory, and interaction tests were not performed.

PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy (with or without targeted agents) may improve the prognosis of patients with pMMR mCRC and represent a potential first-line treatment option.

To synthesize the best available evidence on early mobilization following radical surgery for colorectal cancer, both domestically and internationally, with the aim of providing evidence-based guidance for clinical healthcare providers.

We conducted a literature search using the "6S" evidence model to identify studies on early mobilization following colorectal cancer surgery. Given the limited number of studies exclusively focusing on laparoscopic approaches, we included evidence from broader elective colorectal surgery populations to provide comprehensive guidance. We searched relevant domestic and international websites, evidence-based databases, association websites, and primary literature databases via a computerized system. The search period spanned from the inception of each database to December 19, 2025. Two researchers independently assessed the quality of the literature using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) quality assessment tool, and conducted evidence extraction and synthesis for the included studies.

A total of 19 studies were ultimately included, comprising 4 guidelines, 6 expert consensus documents, 3 systematic reviews, 1 evidence summary, and 5 randomized controlled trials. A total of 23 pieces of evidence were identified across five areas: core principles, preoperative preparation, key elements of activity management, postoperative activity protocols, and activity support.

This study synthesizes the best available evidence regarding early mobilization following laparoscopic radical resection for colorectal cancer. It recommends that nursing staff tailor their approach to individual patients based on clinical circumstances and patient preferences to promote postoperative recovery and enhance the quality of clinical care.

Regorafenib is an approved later-line therapy for metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). Combining regorafenib with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade may enhance antitumor immunity by modifying the tumor microenvironment. This meta-analysis compared the combination with regorafenib monotherapy and explored preliminary treatment signals in microsatellite-stable/proficient mismatch repair (MSS/pMMR) mCRC.

A comprehensive search was performed in PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science, and Chinese databases (Wanfang, VIP, CNKI, CBM) up to February 20, 2026. RCTs and RCSs comparing combination therapy with monotherapy were included. Outcomes were ORR, DCR, PFS, and OS, etc. Study quality was assessed using the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for observational studies. Analyses were performed using RevMan 5.4, with RR/HR and 95% CIs. Heterogeneity was assessed using I², publication bias via funnel plots, and Egger's/Begg's tests when applicable. Evidence certainty was graded using GRADE.

Eight studies (two RCTs and six RCSs; 992 patients) were included. Compared with regorafenib monotherapy, combination therapy improved ORR (RR = 1.81, 95% CI: 1.15-2.85), DCR (RR = 1.34, 95% CI: 1.10-1.62), and PR (RR = 1.66, 95% CI: 1.07-2.59), and reduced the proportion of patients with PD at tumor assessment (RR = 0.65, 95% CI: 0.50-0.86). Combination therapy was also associated with longer PFS (HR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.37-0.87), whereas the OS estimate was borderline and did not establish a robust survival benefit (HR = 0.73, 95% CI: 0.54-1.00; P = 0.05). Post hoc exploratory analyses based on extractable MSS/pMMR data suggested possible improvements in disease control. Combination therapy was associated with increased odds of hypertension, hypothyroidism, hand-foot skin reaction, liver function abnormalities, and nausea; however, comparative evidence for grade ≥ 3 toxicity was insufficient because reporting was inconsistent.

Regorafenib plus PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors may improve tumor response and PFS compared with regorafenib monotherapy in refractory mCRC, but a definitive OS advantage has not been established. The MSS/pMMR findings remain exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Adequately powered, biomarker-stratified randomized trials with standardized MSI/MMR assessment, consistent safety reporting, and longer follow-up are required before this combination can be considered a standard later-line treatment.

Prior analyses of PALLAS (NCT02513394), a phase III trial that randomized patients with stage II-III hormone receptor-positive breast cancer to receive adjuvant palbociclib plus endocrine therapy (ET) or ET alone, did not show benefit of the combination. Separate data show extent of treatment-related neutropenia is associated with improved response to palbociclib and other cell cycle-specific therapies. We conducted an exploratory analysis to assess whether grade of treatment-related neutropenia could identify benefit from palbociclib in the adjuvant setting.

Patients receiving palbociclib were classified into those with treatment-emergent high-grade neutropenia (HGN) with maximum grade ≥3 (absolute neutrophil count <1000/mm3), or low-grade/no neutropenia (LGN) with maximum grade ≤2. 5736 patients were included: 2840 in the palbociclib+ET arm and 2896 in the ET-alone arm. Patients receiving palbociclib+ET consisted of 1006 (35.4%) LGN and 1834 (64.6%) HGN. Lower body mass index (BMI), Asian race, prior chemotherapy, and mastectomy were associated with HGN (all p < 0.05). The management of locally advanced rectal cancer is increasingly moving beyond a uniform cytotoxic framework toward biomarker-informed multimodal treatment. Short-course radiotherapy (SCRT) is an established and convenient radiotherapy option and may also provide an immunomodulatory context for immune checkpoint blockade. Preclinical evidence suggests that hypofractionated radiotherapy can promote immunogenic cell death and modulate the tumor immune microenvironment; however, whether the fractionation schedule of SCRT provides a clinically meaningful immunological advantage over long-course chemoradiotherapy remains uncertain. ▶Context-dependent functions of insulin, IGF, and GLP signaling in inflammatory bowel disease: linking metabolism, immunity, and intestinal homeostasis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 Dose escalation 고려
💡 Conclusion
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), encompassing Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, is a chronic relapsing inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract characterized by dysregulated immune responses, impaired epithelial barrier function, and alterations in the gut microbiota. Although considerable progress has been made in understanding the immunopathogenesis of IBD, increasing evidence indicates that metabolic signaling pathways are integral regulators of intestinal inflammation, tissue repair, and disease progression. Among these, insulin, insulin-like growth factors (IGFs), and glucagon-like peptides (GLPs) constitute an interconnected immunometabolic network that links nutrient sensing, energy metabolism, epithelial regeneration, and immune homeostasis. Insulin signaling plays context-dependent roles in IBD by regulating glucose metabolism, epithelial repair, inflammatory responses, and interactions with the gut microbiota and bile acid metabolism. While insulin promotes mucosal healing and barrier restoration in intestinal epithelial cells, excessive activation of insulin signaling in immune cells may exacerbate chronic inflammation through epigenetic and cytokine-mediated mechanisms. The IGF axis is also profoundly altered during active IBD, with systemic suppression of IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 accompanying disease activity, whereas local IGF signaling promotes epithelial regeneration, wound healing, and immune regulation. In addition, IGF-binding proteins (IGFBPs) function as active modulators of inflammation, fibrosis, and colitis-associated colorectal cancer in an isoform-specific manner. GLP signaling further integrates metabolism with intestinal immunity; GLP-1 receptor agonists exhibit anti-inflammatory, metabolic, and microbiota-modulating effects, whereas GLP-2 primarily enhances epithelial proliferation, barrier integrity, and mucosal repair. Emerging preclinical and observational clinical studies suggest that GLP-based therapies may improve disease outcomes, although prospective randomized trials are still required. This review summarizes current knowledge regarding the biological functions and molecular mechanisms of insulin, IGFs, IGFBPs, and GLPs in IBD, highlighting their coordinated roles in regulating metabolism, immunity, epithelial homeostasis, and tissue remodeling. A better understanding of these interconnected signaling pathways may facilitate the development of novel immunometabolic therapeutic strategies aimed at promoting mucosal healing, limiting chronic inflammation, and improving long-term outcomes in patients with IBD.

Early-onset colorectal cancer (EO-CRC) is a growing global health challenge with poorly understood etiology. While gut microbiota and metabolites are implicated in colorectal carcinogenesis, EO-CRC-associated signatures remain poorly characterized. This study presents the first large-scale multi-omics analysis in Japan cohort to characterize gut microbial and metabolic features of EO-CRC, with cross-cohort validation in an independent China cohort.

We enrolled 510 participants (EO-CRC n = 120, late-onset CRC [LO-CRC] n = 282, young healthy controls [yHC] n = 59, old healthy controls [oHC] n = 49); after age- and sex-matching, 324 samples were analyzed using metagenomics, metabolomics, and quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR). Fourteen species formed a shared CRC-associated microbiome core enriched in both EO-CRC and LO-CRC relative to age-matched controls, of which seven were reproducible across both Japan and China cohorts. Beyond this core, 34 and 21 species were enriched exclusively in EO-CRC and LO-CRC, respectively. These reductions corresponded to a median difference in the plozasiran groups as compared with the placebo groups (placebo-adjusted change) of -45 percentage points (95% confidence interval [CI], -51 to -38) and -47 percentage points (95% CI, -54 to -39). A triglyceride level of less than 500 mg per deciliter at 12 months was reported in 91% and 93% of the patients in the plozasiran groups and in 51% and 50% of those in the placebo groups (P<0.001). The rates of acute pancreatitis were 1 per 100 patient-years with plozasiran and 5 per 100 patient-years with placebo (rate ratio, 0.22; P = 0.008). Adverse events during treatment occurred in 73% of the patients in both the pooled plozasiran and placebo groups.

Among patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia, the use of plozasiran resulted in lower triglyceride levels and a lower risk of acute pancreatitis than placebo at 12 months. (Funded by Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals; SHASTA-3 ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT06347003; SHASTA-4, NCT06347016.).
