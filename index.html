Antibiotics are frequently overused in acute pancreatitis despite guideline recommendations against routine prophylactic therapy. Procalcitonin (PCT)-guided antibiotic stewardship may reduce unnecessary antibiotic exposure without compromising clinical outcomes. This study evaluated the efficacy of a PCT-guided algorithm in patients with acute pancreatitis.

This prospective, single-centre, patient-blinded randomized controlled trial enrolled adults with acute pancreatitis. Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive either PCT-guided antibiotic therapy (PCT arm) or standard guideline-based management (non-PCT arm). Antibiotics in the intervention group were initiated and discontinued according to serial serum PCT measurements using a threshold of 1 ng/mL. The primary outcome was antibiotic usage measured as defined daily dose (DDD).

Of 152 randomized patients, 144 completed follow-up (73 non-PCT and 71 PCT). Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. Median antibiotic usage was similar between the non-PCT and PCT groups (DDD: 7 [5-10] vs. 7 [4-10]; p = 0.872). Fewer patients received antibiotics in the PCT arm (36.6% vs. 45.2%; p = 0.295). Median antibiotic duration was 7 versus 5 days (p = 0.178), while median hospital stay was 6 days in both groups (p = 0.178). Mortality (5.6% vs. 8.2%), infected necrotic collections (7.0% vs. 6.8%), and multidrug-resistant bacteremia (1.4% vs. 1.3%) were comparable. Among patients with mild pancreatitis, antibiotic use was lower with PCT guidance (7.9% vs. 18.4%; p = 0.098).

In this single-centre randomized trial, PCT-guided antibiotic therapy did not result in a statistically significant reduction in antibiotic consumption compared with standard care. Larger, appropriately powered multicentre trials, particularly among patients at risk of infected pancreatic necrosis, are required to determine whether PCT-guided therapy has a clinically meaningful role in antibiotic stewardship.

Sepsis is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with gastrointestinal (GI) malignancies, with in-hospital mortality rates of 19.8% to 28%, significantly higher than in non-cancer patients. While sepsis outcomes have been extensively studied in the general population, large-scale population-level analyses specific to GI cancers remain limited. This study aimed to identify the outcomes of sepsis in gastric, colon, hepatic, and pancreatic cancers in the United States.

We conducted a retrospective analysis of the National Inpatient Sample (2017-2021) using ICD-10 codes, including adults ≥18 years with gastric (n=205,599), colon (n=851,120), hepatocellular (n=433,145), or pancreatic (n=557,204) cancer. Propensity matching and multivariate regression were used to adjust for confounders. A p-value of ≤0.05 was considered significant.

Sepsis prevalence was 13.79% in gastric, 12.6% in colon, 18.76% in hepatocellular, and 19.22% in pancreatic cancer hospitalizations. In-hospital mortality was significantly higher with sepsis across all four malignancies (gastric 23.57% vs. 5.09%; colon 17.72% vs. 2.74%; hepatocellular 22.34% vs. 6.22%; pancreatic 17.26% vs. 5.11%). After propensity matching and adjustment, sepsis was independently associated with increased mortality in gastric (aOR 1.87), colon (aOR 2.39), hepatocellular (aOR 1.60), and pancreatic cancer (aOR 1.43) (all p<0.001). Mortality among patients requiring ICU-level care was 70.28%, 70.88%, 72.97%, and 67%, respectively. Acute kidney injury, acute respiratory failure, invasive ventilation, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and shock were consistent mortality predictors across all malignancies (p<0.05). Sepsis was also associated with significantly higher hospitalization charges and longer length of stay (p<0.001).

Our study revealed that patients with gastrointestinal malignancies experience high mortality rates when affected by sepsis, with acute organ dysfunction and ICU-level care as key predictors of poor outcomes. These findings highlight GI cancer as a high-risk population for adverse sepsis outcomes and support the need for prospective studies evaluating early recognition and guideline-directed therapies.

Postoperative aspirin has produced inconsistent results in molecularly unselected colorectal cancer, whereas ALASCCA supports a biomarker-selected strategy. We synthesize randomized, molecular, and mechanistic evidence and propose a cautious framework for clinical consideration.

This clinically oriented narrative review searched PubMed/MEDLINE, ClinicalTrials.gov, jRCT, PROSPERO, ASCO and ESMO materials, and reference lists, with a final update on September 27, 2026. Randomized trials and prospective biomarker-selected evidence were prioritized.

ASCOLT and EPISODE-III/JCOG1503C did not meet their primary disease-free survival endpoints in unselected populations. SAKK 41/13 was underpowered but directionally supportive. In ALASCCA, aspirin 160 mg daily for 3 years reduced 3-year colorectal cancer recurrence in group A PIK3CA exon 9/20 hotspot tumors (7.7% vs. 14.1%; HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.24-0.98). Low-dose platelet COX-1/thromboxane inhibition is pharmacologically established; direct tumor-cell COX-2/PI3K effects remain less certain. Mature overall-survival data are lacking.

Adjuvant aspirin should not be used universally after colorectal cancer resection. The strongest evidence applies to ALASCCA group A, while group B requires variant-level caution. Any use should preserve standard adjuvant therapy and incorporate bleeding risk, local guidance, and shared decision-making.

💡 Conclusion
Pudexacianinium is an effective visualization agent without invasive instrumentation. NIR-F imaging with pudexacianinium enhances ureter identification compared with WL-only, which may reduce risk of intraoperative ureter injury.
This phase III, multicenter, randomized, open-label study (NCT05754333) evaluated pudexacianinium chloride (ASP5354), a novel near-infrared fluorescence (NIR-F) imaging agent, for intraoperative ureter identification in minimally invasive and open abdominopelvic surgeries.

Patients with normal renal function, mild renal impairment, or patients with moderately impaired renal function received one intravenous dose of pudexacianinium 3 mg intraoperatively. Investigators scored ureter conspicuity under white light (WL) or (WL+)NIR-F using a validated 5-point Likert scale. Primary and key secondary endpoints included intra-participant differences in conspicuity scores for WL versus (WL+)NIR-F, supported by blinded central image review and quantitative contrast enhancement factor (CEF) analysis.

Adults with normal renal function or mild renal impairment were randomly assigned in a 5:1 ratio to WL+NIR-F arm (intent-to-treat population, n = 72 [median age 55.0 years; 68.1% female]) or to WL-only arm (n = 13 [median age 64.0 years; 76.9% female]). Eight adults with moderate renal impairment (median age 70.0 years; 75.0% female) and 14 adolescents with normal renal function or mild renal impairment (median age 14.5 years; 57.1% female) were imaged under WL+NIR-F (no randomization). No drug-related serious adverse events occurred, and most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild- or moderate-grade. Adults with moderate renal impairment had higher mean plasma pudexacianinium concentrations and slower urinary excretion than adults with normal renal function or mild renal impairment. Adolescents had numerically lower mean urine production rates and higher mean urine pudexacianinium concentrations than adults.

Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a common complication following laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG), yet the role of omentopexy in mitigating postoperative reflux remains unclear. We conducted a double-blind, randomized controlled trial to compare the incidence of de novo GERD after LSG with and without omentopexy.

Between 2023 and 2024, 290 adult patients (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² with comorbidities or ≥ 40 kg/m²) without preexisting GERD or significant hiatal hernia were randomized (1:1) to LSG with omentopexy (Group A, n = 145) or LSG alone (Group B, n = 145). No significant differences emerged by age, sex, or endoscopic findings.

Omentopexy performed during LSG did not significantly reduce overall de novo GERD incidence at one year, although a trend toward benefit and a significant effect in patients with substantial weight loss were observed. Further large-scale trials incorporating objective reflux measurements are warranted to clarify the potential protective role of omental fixation in bariatric practice.

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major public health concern worldwide, and participation in screening programs remains inadequate in many rural and underserved populations. This study assessed the effectiveness of a culturally tailored educational intervention based on the PEN-3 model in improving uptake of the fecal immunochemical test (FIT) among adults in rural Iran.

A community-based quasi-experimental pre-post study was conducted in Daran, Isfahan Province, Iran, in 2022. A total of 388 adults aged 50-70 years were recruited using stratified random sampling from two comprehensive health centers. The intervention was developed according to the PEN-3 model and addressed individual perceptions, family support, and community-level enabling factors. Data were collected before and three months after the intervention using a validated 51-item questionnaire assessing knowledge, perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, perceived barriers, response efficacy, and self-efficacy. Paired t-tests and chi-square tests were used for statistical analysis.

Among the 365 participants who completed the study, 134 individuals (36.7%) underwent FIT screening within three months after the intervention. Significant improvements were observed in knowledge, perceived susceptibility, and self-efficacy (all p < 0.001). Women demonstrated higher screening uptake than men (46.1% vs. 27.8%, p < 0.05), and FIT participation was inversely associated with educational level.

The findings suggest that FIT uptake and several PEN-3-related psychosocial constructs improved following implementation of the educational intervention. However, the absence of a control group limits causal inference. Further controlled studies are needed to confirm the effectiveness and long-term impact of culturally tailored educational interventions for colorectal cancer screening.

💡 Conclusion
Interactive foundation models such as SAM struggle to generalize across clinical deployment sites. Among existing adaptation strategies, test-time adaptation (TTA) is the most promising, yet current TTA methods trade one problem for another: removing the physician eliminates clinical grounding, while retaining the physician reintroduces a costly annotation burden. In this work, we propose Clinical-SAM, a two-phase agentic physician-in-the-loop TTA framework that teaches a frozen SAM to approximate what a physician would accept - directly and continuously, without requiring spatial annotations. In Phase I (pre-deployment), a self-prompting agent trained via reinforcement learning generates prompts autonomously, eliminating per-case expert spatial input at inference. In Phase II (deployment), the agent policy is refined online by the one signal physicians already produce: a binary accept/reject feedback on each proposed mask. As the binary feedback accumulates across deployed cases, the agent converges toward a policy that produces masks meeting a reference acceptance standard designed to approximate physician judgment, with progressively fewer interactions. We evaluate Clinical-SAM in a pre-clinical setting on six oncological benchmarks spanning CT (colon, pancreas, liver, kidney) and PET (head & neck, lung) tumor segmentation, using agreement with reference-standard segmentations as a proxy for physician acceptance. Clinical-SAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on all six datasets, outperforming fine-tuning, interactive, TTA, and online-learning baselines, while requiring only 3.3 binary judgments per case on average across the six datasets. Both known and novel T2D loci contribute to ADD in Chinese people and may be used to detect high risk individuals for early intervention to prevent or delay onset of YOD.
We investigated genetic variations driving young age-at-diabetes-diagnosis (ADD) and developed a genetic risk score (GRS) to identify Chinese individuals at risk of young-onset diabetes (YOD with ADD<40).

We performed genome-wide association studies (GWAS) for ADD with internal and external replication in 22,611 individuals with type 2 diabetes (T2D, 20% with YOD) and identified Chinese-prioritized loci by conditioning on the polygenic risk score (PRS) of ADD constructed from publicly available datasets. We constructed ADD-GRS using genome-wide independent variants (p<5E-08) and examined its associations with glycemic traits in diabetes and non-diabetes cohorts.

We identified three independent signals at genome-wide significance (P < 5×10-8) mapping to CDKAL1 (rs2206734, β=-0.75 (SE:0.13) per copy of T allele, effect allele frequency (EAF)=0.38, p=2.12E-09), PAX4 (rs61160304, β=-1.01 (SE:0.17) per copy of T allele, EAF=0.19, p=2.51E-09), and KCNQ1 (rs2299620, β=-0.77 (SE:0.14) per copy of G allele, EAF=0.70, p=2.80E-08). The variants at PAX4 and KCNQ1 were independent of the genetic liability estimated by ADD-PRS from Europeans and South Indians. In the diabetes cohort, the ADD-GRS was associated with younger ADD, lower C-peptide, higher HbA1c and pathway-specific T2D-PRS for beta-cell+proinsulin and residual glycemia. Using YOD versus later-onset diabetes as a secondary outcome, LINC02783/PADI1 (rs72895704, OR:1.52 per copy of A allele, EAF=0.02, p=1.93E-06) and TARS2P1 (rs145218437, OR: 1.73 per copy of C allele, EAF=0.02, p=7.57E-08) showed suggestive significance.

Both known and novel T2D loci contribute to ADD in Chinese people and may be used to detect high risk individuals for early intervention to prevent or delay onset of YOD.
