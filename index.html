Managing patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and concomitant lung cancer is a major clinical challenge due to increased incidence and negative impact on quality of life and mortality. Evidence-based recommendations are lacking. This European Respiratory Society (ERS) clinical practice guideline provides recommendations to balance oncological benefits against treatment-associated toxicity.

An ERS multidisciplinary Task Force of experts and patient representatives produced 11 conditional recommendations across seven PICO (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) questions and two narrative questions; for one PICO question no recommendation was issued. Systematic reviews were performed for each question. Evidence certainty was assessed using the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation) approach (for PICOs) and recommendations were formulated using the Evidence-to-Decision framework.

For early-stage lung cancer, the Task Force suggests surgical resection in medically fit patients. Postoperative risk necessitates systematic preoperative evaluation, perioperative antifibrotic therapy discussion and protective perioperative strategies. For stereotactic radiotherapy, a conditional recommendation for either the intervention or the comparison was issued, balancing potential oncological benefit against radiation pneumonitis. In locally advanced lung cancer, the Task Force suggests not administering concurrent radio-chemotherapy, due to very high acute exacerbation rates. For advanced lung cancer, chemotherapy is suggested in medically fit patients, where survival benefits outweigh increased toxicity risks. For stage IV lung cancer, evidence for immunotherapy was limited, thus a conditional recommendation for either the intervention or the comparison was issued. Evidence for targeted therapy for lung cancer in patients with IPF was insufficient to support a recommendation.

Given the poor prognosis of both conditions and treatment-associated risks, management requires multidisciplinary assessment of competing risks and patient preferences in experienced interstitial lung disease and oncology centres.

Radical resection combined with intestinal autotransplantation (RRCIA) has emerged as a potential curative option for highly selected patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC) and extensive mesenteric vascular involvement. This project aimed to develop expert consensus recommendations on indications, surgical strategies, perioperative management, and long-term care of RRCIA in patients with LAPC.

An international multidisciplinary panel of experts in pancreatic surgery, transplant surgery, medical oncology, radiation oncology, radiology, critical care, and perioperative care was convened. A modified Delphi process was used to develop consensus recommendations. The survey comprised six domains with a total of 20 questions covering patient selection, neoadjuvant therapy, surgical strategies, perioperative management, adjuvant therapy, and follow-up. Consensus was predefined as ≥80% agreement.

Twenty consensus statements were developed. The quality of evidence was low for 17 statements and moderate for 3. Six recommendations were strong and 14 conditional. RRCIA was recommended only for highly selected patients at experienced high-volume centres following adequate neoadjuvant therapy and multidisciplinary evaluation. Surgical strategies should be individualized according to tumour location and vascular involvement. No definitive minimum residual bowel length was established, and preservation of well-perfused bowel and the ileocaecal valve was emphasized. Postoperative management should be tailored, including anticoagulation, nutritional support, selective implementation of enhanced recovery principles, adjuvant therapy, and structured long-term follow-up.

This consensus provides the first international framework to guide the application of RRCIA for LAPC, emphasizing its use in carefully selected patients treated at experienced centres with multidisciplinary expertise.

Pancreatic cancer is often difficult to remove by surgery when it grows around major blood vessels that supply the intestine. In a small number of carefully selected patients, a highly complex operation can be performed in which the affected pancreas and blood vessels are removed, part of the intestine is temporarily removed and preserved, and the intestine is then reimplanted to the body after the blood vessels have been reconstructed. Guidance on when and how to perform this operation remains limited.

The authors aimed to develop international expert recommendations for the safe and appropriate use of this operation in patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer.

A group of 33 experts in pancreatic cancer surgery, transplantation, imaging, cancer treatment, and postoperative care reviewed the available evidence. They then used two rounds of voting to reach agreement on 20 clinical questions.

The experts agreed that this operation should be considered only for carefully selected patients who have responded well to systemic treatment before surgery. It should be performed in experienced centres by multidisciplinary teams. The recommendations also cover the type of operation, reconstruction of blood vessels and the intestine, nutrition, prevention of blood clots, treatment after surgery, and long-term follow-up. This guideline provides a framework to improve consistency and safety in performing this type of operation.

Persistent reflux-like symptoms during proton pump inhibitor (PPI) therapy may reflect ongoing reflux, reflux hypersensitivity, functional heartburn, or overlapping disorders of gut-brain interaction. We assessed adjunctive flupentixol-melitracen in Chinese patients labelled as having refractory gastroesophageal reflux disease (rGERD).

We reviewed controlled trials explicitly described as randomized. Quasi-randomized and non-randomized allocation were ineligible. Eight databases were searched from inception to 28 July 2026. Post-treatment arm-level data were used consistently for continuous outcomes. Random-effects models, 95% prediction intervals (PIs), exploratory subgroup and sensitivity analyses, small-study-effect tests, and GRADE assessments were performed.

Twenty-nine trials (3242 participants), all conducted in China, were retained after source re-audit. Adjunctive therapy was associated with higher total clinical effectiveness (26 trials; RR 1.24, 95% CI 1.20 to 1.29; I²=0%), but this was a non-standard composite assessed largely without blinding. Anxiety (23 trials; SMD - 2.38, 95% CI - 2.93 to - 1.84; I²=97.0%; 95% PI - 5.01 to 0.24), depression (24 trials; SMD - 2.36, 95% CI - 2.86 to - 1.85; I²=96.5%; 95% PI - 4.82 to 0.11), and RDQ scores (8 trials; MD - 4.01, 95% CI - 5.27 to - 2.74; I²=98.6%; 95% PI - 7.67 to - 0.35) favored adjunctive therapy on average, but effect magnitude was highly uncertain. Eight trials sparsely reported adverse events (RR 0.55, 95% CI 0.37 to 0.84); this cannot establish a protective effect or comparative safety. Small-study effects were detected for total effectiveness and the principal psychological outcomes. Certainty was very low.

In Chinese trials of patients labelled as having rGERD, adjunctive flupentixol-melitracen was associated with short-term improvements in composite response and symptom scores. ▶A propensity score weighted comparison of intravenous and subcutaneous infliximab as maintenance treatment in patients with inflammatory bowel disease: The CISI study.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 Anti-TNF 유지/TDM 고려
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Infliximab is a treatment commonly used for people with inflammatory bowel disease, including Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis. It is usually given through a vein in hospital, but a newer version can be injected under the skin at home. This study compared patients who continued infliximab through a vein with patients who changed to the under-the-skin injection. We studied 251 adults with inflammatory bowel disease who were already well controlled and not using steroids. Of these, 134 continued intravenous infliximab and 117 switched to subcutaneous infliximab. We looked at how long patients could continue their assigned treatment without needing an important change, such as switching back to intravenous treatment, increasing the intravenous dose, needing steroids for a flare, changing to another advanced treatment, or having surgery related to inflammatory bowel disease. Overall, the time to treatment failure was not significantly different between the two groups. Patients who switched to subcutaneous infliximab had a slightly higher risk of stopping or changing treatment in the first months after the switch, but this risk decreased over time. After about 8 months, treatment persistence appeared more favourable in the subcutaneous group. Side effects and measures of disease activity were similar between groups during follow-up. These findings suggest that, in selected patients whose disease is stable, switching from intravenous to subcutaneous infliximab may be a reasonable option. However, because this was a retrospective real-world study and not a randomized trial, the results should be interpreted with caution.
Comparing intravenous (IV) infliximab (IFX) with subcutaneous (SC) CT-P13 in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) may inform clinical practice, particularly in patients in stable remission receiving maintenance therapy.

To compare IV IFX and SC CT-P13 as maintenance therapy in patients with IBD.

Retrospective, single-centre, observational cohort study using propensity score weighting.

Adult patients with Crohn's disease (CD) or ulcerative colitis (UC) in stable steroid-free remission while receiving maintenance IV IFX, 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks, were included. Patients switched to SC CT-P13 were compared with patients who continued IV IFX. A propensity score-weighted analysis using inverse probability of treatment weighting was applied. The primary endpoint was time to treatment failure, defined as a composite endpoint including re-switch from SC to IV IFX, IV IFX dose intensification, steroid-requiring flare, switch to another advanced therapy, or IBD-related surgery.

Among 251 patients, 135 had CD (53.8%) and 116 had UC (46.2%); 134 remained on IV IFX and 117 switched to SC CT-P13. Mean follow-up was 24.7 ± 29.8 months in the IV group and 20.9 ± 14.9 months in the SC group. Time to treatment failure was not significantly different between groups (log-rank p = 0.10; HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.45-1.12). A significant time-dependent effect was observed (p = 0.005): discontinuation risk was initially higher after switching, but progressively decreased over time, becoming lower after approximately 8 months.

In patients with IBD in stable steroid-free remission, switching to SC CT-P13 may provide treatment persistence, safety, and disease control comparable to continued IV IFX.

Older gastric cancer survivors after radical gastrectomy are vulnerable to frailty progression. Family function may influence frailty by enhancing individual psychological resilience. However, few studies have examined these longitudinal associations. We aimed to evaluate the longitudinal associations among family function, psychological resilience, and frailty.

A total of 412 older gastric cancer survivors who underwent radical gastrectomy were enrolled and assessed at hospital discharge, and again at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively. Family function was assessed using the family adaptability and cohesion evaluation scale II, psychological resilience with the Connor-Davidson resilience scale, and frailty using the Tilburg frailty indicator. The random intercept cross-lagged panel model was used to assess within- and between-person longitudinal associations. Mediation effect analysis was conducted at the within-person level.

Family cohesion was indirectly associated with lower subsequent frailty through higher psychological resilience. The estimated indirect effects from T1 to T3 and from T2 to T4 were -.025 (p = .012) and -.032 (p = .004), respectively. While family adaptability was associated with psychological resilience at the between-person level (B = .707, p = .006), and psychological resilience was negatively associated with frailty (B = -2.458, p = .003), the direct association between family adaptability and frailty was not significant (B = -.097, p = .069).

Greater family cohesion was associated with lower subsequent frailty, with psychological resilience representing a potential pathway. These findings highlight the potential of resilience-focused approaches to enhance the benefits of family support in addressing frailty.

This study aims to investigate the occurrence of chemotherapy-induced diarrhea (CID) in patients with colorectal cancer and analyze its associated influencing factors. Based on this, a random forest model was introduced to screen and model potential influencing factors. Combined with feature importance ranking and interpretability analysis, the contribution of each factor to the occurrence of CID was further explored, thereby improving the accuracy of high-risk population identification, supporting future individualized risk assessment and nursing management.

This was a retrospective study using convenience sampling. A total of 508 patients with colorectal cancer who received chemotherapy at a tertiary first-class hospital in Gansu Province from February 2025 to September 2025 were selected as the study subjects. Data on general information, disease-related information, and laboratory indicators were collected. The occurrence of chemotherapy-induced diarrhea was used as the outcome variable. Univariate analysis was used to screen relevant factors. Based on this, the random forest recursive feature elimination (RF-RFE) method was applied to select the optimal feature subset, and a random forest-based risk identification model was developed. Five-fold cross-validation was used to evaluate model performance. The SHAP method was used for interpretability analysis of the model.

Among the 508 patients, some developed CID during chemotherapy. Univariate analysis showed that BMI, ECOG score, hemoglobin, albumin, total bilirubin, serum creatinine, diabetes, permanent stoma, and irinotecan use were associated with the occurrence of CID (P < 0.05). The random forest model selected seven optimal features (total bilirubin, irinotecan use, albumin, ECOG score, BMI, serum creatinine, and diabetes). The model achieved an AUC of 0.88, accuracy of 0.85, sensitivity of 0.65, and specificity of 0.93 in the test set. SHAP analysis indicated that total bilirubin and irinotecan use contributed the most to the occurrence of CID.

The incidence of CID is relatively high in patients with colorectal cancer during chemotherapy. Its occurrence is closely related to factors such as liver function status, chemotherapy regimen, nutritional status, and underlying diseases. The random forest-based prediction model demonstrated good predictive performance and may provide a potential tool for early identification of patients at higher risk of CID and support future individualized nursing management.

Managing patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and concomitant lung cancer is a major clinical challenge due to increased incidence and negative impact on quality of life and mortality. Evidence-based recommendations are lacking. This European Respiratory Society (ERS) clinical practice guideline provides recommendations to balance oncological benefits against treatment-associated toxicity.

Among 3,800 individuals with prediabetes, 24.3% exhibited isolated HbA1c elevation (HbA1c+/OGTT-). Within this discordant subgroup, 51.8% had elevated 1-h PG (≥8.6 mmol/L). The 1-h PG+ subgroup demonstrated lower β-cell function (DI = 40.47), delayed glucose peaks (60 min), impaired early-phase insulin secretion, and greater overall glycaemic exposure, whereas the 1-h PG- subgroup exhibited preserved or enhanced β-cell function (DI = 85.19 vs. 75.56 in NGT, P < 0.001). Multivariable linear regression analysis demonstrated that 1-h PG remained significantly associated with β-cell function after adjustment for age, sex, and HbA1c.

Among individuals with HbA1c-defined prediabetes and normal Fasting and 2-Hour Glucose, 1-hour plasma glucose identifies subgroups with differing metabolic profiles and β-cell function. These findings highlight substantial metabolic heterogeneity within this population. However, given the cross-sectional design of the study, longitudinal investigations are required to determine whether these metabolic differences translate into differences in future diabetes risk and clinical outcomes.
