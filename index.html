Sepsis is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with gastrointestinal (GI) malignancies, with in-hospital mortality rates of 19.8% to 28%, significantly higher than in non-cancer patients. While sepsis outcomes have been extensively studied in the general population, large-scale population-level analyses specific to GI cancers remain limited. This study aimed to identify the outcomes of sepsis in gastric, colon, hepatic, and pancreatic cancers in the United States.

We conducted a retrospective analysis of the National Inpatient Sample (2017-2021) using ICD-10 codes, including adults ≥18 years with gastric (n=205,599), colon (n=851,120), hepatocellular (n=433,145), or pancreatic (n=557,204) cancer. Propensity matching and multivariate regression were used to adjust for confounders. A p-value of ≤0.05 was considered significant.

Sepsis prevalence was 13.79% in gastric, 12.6% in colon, 18.76% in hepatocellular, and 19.22% in pancreatic cancer hospitalizations. In-hospital mortality was significantly higher with sepsis across all four malignancies (gastric 23.57% vs. 5.09%; colon 17.72% vs. 2.74%; hepatocellular 22.34% vs. 6.22%; pancreatic 17.26% vs. 5.11%). After propensity matching and adjustment, sepsis was independently associated with increased mortality in gastric (aOR 1.87), colon (aOR 2.39), hepatocellular (aOR 1.60), and pancreatic cancer (aOR 1.43) (all p<0.001). Mortality among patients requiring ICU-level care was 70.28%, 70.88%, 72.97%, and 67%, respectively. Acute kidney injury, acute respiratory failure, invasive ventilation, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and shock were consistent mortality predictors across all malignancies (p<0.05). Sepsis was also associated with significantly higher hospitalization charges and longer length of stay (p<0.001).

Our study revealed that patients with gastrointestinal malignancies experience high mortality rates when affected by sepsis, with acute organ dysfunction and ICU-level care as key predictors of poor outcomes. These findings highlight GI cancer as a high-risk population for adverse sepsis outcomes and support the need for prospective studies evaluating early recognition and guideline-directed therapies.

Postoperative aspirin has produced inconsistent results in molecularly unselected colorectal cancer, whereas ALASCCA supports a biomarker-selected strategy. We synthesize randomized, molecular, and mechanistic evidence and propose a cautious framework for clinical consideration.

This clinically oriented narrative review searched PubMed/MEDLINE, ClinicalTrials.gov, jRCT, PROSPERO, ASCO and ESMO materials, and reference lists, with a final update on September 27, 2026. Randomized trials and prospective biomarker-selected evidence were prioritized.

ASCOLT and EPISODE-III/JCOG1503C did not meet their primary disease-free survival endpoints in unselected populations. SAKK 41/13 was underpowered but directionally supportive. Low-dose platelet COX-1/thromboxane inhibition is pharmacologically established; direct tumor-cell COX-2/PI3K effects remain less certain. Mature overall-survival data are lacking.

Adjuvant aspirin should not be used universally after colorectal cancer resection. The strongest evidence applies to ALASCCA group A, while group B requires variant-level caution. Any use should preserve standard adjuvant therapy and incorporate bleeding risk, local guidance, and shared decision-making.

💡 Conclusion
Pudexacianinium is an effective visualization agent without invasive instrumentation. NIR-F imaging with pudexacianinium enhances ureter identification compared with WL-only, which may reduce risk of intraoperative ureter injury.
This phase III, multicenter, randomized, open-label study (NCT05754333) evaluated pudexacianinium chloride (ASP5354), a novel near-infrared fluorescence (NIR-F) imaging agent, for intraoperative ureter identification in minimally invasive and open abdominopelvic surgeries.

Patients with normal renal function, mild renal impairment, or patients with moderately impaired renal function received one intravenous dose of pudexacianinium 3 mg intraoperatively. Investigators scored ureter conspicuity under white light (WL) or (WL+)NIR-F using a validated 5-point Likert scale. Primary and key secondary endpoints included intra-participant differences in conspicuity scores for WL versus (WL+)NIR-F, supported by blinded central image review and quantitative contrast enhancement factor (CEF) analysis.

Adults with normal renal function or mild renal impairment were randomly assigned in a 5:1 ratio to WL+NIR-F arm (intent-to-treat population, n = 72 [median age 55.0 years; 68.1% female]) or to WL-only arm (n = 13 [median age 64.0 years; 76.9% female]). Eight adults with moderate renal impairment (median age 70.0 years; 75.0% female) and 14 adolescents with normal renal function or mild renal impairment (median age 14.5 years; 57.1% female) were imaged under WL+NIR-F (no randomization). In the same participants of intent-to-treat population, (WL+)NIR-F yielded significantly higher conspicuity scores than WL, both 30 min (± 15) postdose and at end of surgery, confirmed by central review and CEF analysis. Benefits persisted to 210 min post-injection (timepoints with ≥ 5 evaluable patients). No drug-related serious adverse events occurred, and most treatment-emergent adverse events were mild- or moderate-grade. Adults with moderate renal impairment had higher mean plasma pudexacianinium concentrations and slower urinary excretion than adults with normal renal function or mild renal impairment. Adolescents had numerically lower mean urine production rates and higher mean urine pudexacianinium concentrations than adults.

Pudexacianinium is an effective visualization agent without invasive instrumentation. NIR-F imaging with pudexacianinium enhances ureter identification compared with WL-only, which may reduce risk of intraoperative ureter injury.

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major public health concern worldwide, and participation in screening programs remains inadequate in many rural and underserved populations. This study assessed the effectiveness of a culturally tailored educational intervention based on the PEN-3 model in improving uptake of the fecal immunochemical test (FIT) among adults in rural Iran.

A community-based quasi-experimental pre-post study was conducted in Daran, Isfahan Province, Iran, in 2022. A total of 388 adults aged 50-70 years were recruited using stratified random sampling from two comprehensive health centers. The intervention was developed according to the PEN-3 model and addressed individual perceptions, family support, and community-level enabling factors. Data were collected before and three months after the intervention using a validated 51-item questionnaire assessing knowledge, perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, perceived barriers, response efficacy, and self-efficacy. Paired t-tests and chi-square tests were used for statistical analysis.

Among the 365 participants who completed the study, 134 individuals (36.7%) underwent FIT screening within three months after the intervention. Significant improvements were observed in knowledge, perceived susceptibility, and self-efficacy (all p < 0.001). Women demonstrated higher screening uptake than men (46.1% vs. 27.8%, p < 0.05), and FIT participation was inversely associated with educational level.

The findings suggest that FIT uptake and several PEN-3-related psychosocial constructs improved following implementation of the educational intervention. However, the absence of a control group limits causal inference. Further controlled studies are needed to confirm the effectiveness and long-term impact of culturally tailored educational interventions for colorectal cancer screening.

Although the carbohydrate biomarker CA19-9 is widely used for cancer screening, its diagnostic specificity is severely compromised by false-positive elevations in benign inflammatory conditions. Characterizing specific CA19-9 protein carriers provides a promising alternative, yet high-throughput, micro-volume profiling directly from serum remains a challenge. Furthermore, comparative sub-analysis resolved a critical clinical dilemma by identifying 59 upregulated protein carriers in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma relative to benign gallstone-induced cholangitis. The rising incidence and poor prognosis of early-onset colorectal cancer (EOCRC) highlight an urgent need for effective diagnostic biomarkers. This study integrates RNA sequencing data with machine learning algorithms to identify EOCRC-specific genes and construct a diagnostic model to improve molecular diagnosis and explore the functional role of key genes.

We analyzed RNA sequencing data from EOCRC patients (age ≤ 50) obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between EOCRC and normal tissues were identified and subjected to construct a diagnostic model through random forest analysis and artificial neural network modeling. The diagnostic performance of the model was evaluated in a training set and validated in an independent set. CIBERSORT was utilized to investigate the immune cell infiltration patterns. The expression levels of key DEGs were further verified in 15 paired clinical tissues. Moreover, RT-qPCR and western blotting were applied to detect the expression of solute carrier member 26 family 2 (SLC26A2), which is one of the top-ranked genes, in CRC cell lines. The functional role of SLC26A2 was investigated in EOCRC cell lines (SW480 and HT29) through lentivector-mediated ectopic overexpression experiments assessing proliferation, migration and invasion. The association between SLC26A2 and the cytotoxicity of vanadium-containing compounds (BMOV, VO(acac)₂) was explored, focusing on redox modulation (ROS/GSH levels) using inhibitors (BSO) and antioxidants (NAC).

We identified 22 DEGs between EOCRC tumor and normal tissues. A machine learning-based diagnostic model based on seven DEGs (SLC26A2, FCGBP, SLC7A5, CA2, STC2, SLC4A4 and THBS2) for EOCRC was constructed. The model achieved an AUC of 0.987 (training) and 0.874 (validation). Immune profiling revealed altered infiltration of monocytes, M0 macrophages, and γδ T cells in EOCRC. Experimental validation confirmed the dysregulation of the seven key genes. SLC26A2 was downregulated in EOCRC tissues and its overexpression significantly inhibited cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro. Transcriptomic analysis linked SLC26A2 overexpression to glutathione (GSH) metabolism. EOCRC cells exhibited sensitivity to vanadium compounds, which was enhanced by SLC26A2 overexpression. SLC26A2 expression correlated with compound-induced cytotoxicity alongside modulation of ROS/GSH levels, as evidenced by the synergistic effect of BSO (GSH inhibitor) and the attenuating effect of NAC (antioxidant).

We developed and validated a robust machine learning-based diagnostic model for EOCRC using a 7-gene signature. The downregulated gene SLC26A2 acts as a tumor suppressor in EOCRC and is associated with cellular sensitivity to vanadium-containing compounds, potentially through a redox-dependent mechanism linked to GSH metabolism. These findings provide novel insights into the identification of diagnostic biomarkers and highlight the therapeutic potential for EOCRC.

Both known and novel T2D loci contribute to ADD in Chinese people and may be used to detect high risk individuals for early intervention to prevent or delay onset of YOD.

This study aimed to investigate the potential association between gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and periodontitis (PD) and to explore possible biological mechanisms underlying this link.

In the first part of this study, a two-sample Mendelian randomisation (MR) analysis was conducted to evaluate the potential effect of PD on the risk of GDM, and vice versa. In the second part, transcriptomic data and network-based bioinformatics analyses were integrated, including differential expression analysis and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction, to screen for common differentially expressed genes and significantly enriched biological pathways. In the third part, a simplified in vitro β-cell challenge system was established using the human pancreatic β-cell line EndoC-βH1. Cells were exposed to high-glucose and progesterone conditions with or without Porphyromonas gingivalis to examine inflammatory and oxidative stress responses. Differences in the expression of MR-genes, inflammatory cytokines, and reactive oxidative species were evaluated. All analyses were performed using R version 4.2.2 (R CoreTeam, Vienna, Austria).

The forward MR analysis showed evidence consistent with a modest positive association of genetically predicted PD with GDM risk (OR = 1.14, P = 0.006), whereas the reverse analysis did not provide evidence for an association of genetically predicted GDM with PD risk (OR = 1.08, P = 0.222). Sensitivity analyses supported the robustness of the MR results. Transcriptomic bioinformatic analyses identified multiple key genes that were dysregulated in both PD and GDM, including inflammatory mediators (IL1RN and TGFB1) and metabolic regulators (AKT1, NOS3, and TCF7L2). Functional enrichment revealed that the overlapping genes were mainly involved in critical biological processes like apoptotic signalling, cell adhesion, and inflammatory responses. The PPI network suggested a synergistic role of PD and GDM in inflammation and metabolic regulation. Compared with the GDM model, β-cells in the GDM+PD model exhibited significantly enhanced inflammatory responses and oxidative stress. Additionally, the MR-genes showed significant expression changes in the GDM+PD model: HSPBAP1, APBA1, and SHC4 were upregulated, whereas CDH1, NDUFAF6, and DGKB were downregulated.

These findings provide evidence consistent with a potential biological link between PD and GDM, in which periodontal infection-related inflammatory responses and oxidative stress may aggravate β-cell stress under GDM-like conditions.

💡 Conclusion
Somatostatin analogues (SSAs) are the cornerstone of treatment for patients with neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), with established anti-secretory activity in functioning NETs and anti-proliferative activity in grade 1 and 2 (Ki-67 levels, up to 10%) gastro-entero-pancreatic NETs. However, several clinically relevant scenarios regarding their role still remain insufficiently addressed by available guidance, including the following: patient selection for dose-escalation or interval-shortening after disease progression, their anti-proliferative effect in tumours with a > 10% Ki-67 index, their role in pulmonary carcinoids, Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN1)-related pancreatic NETs and uncommon neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs), their adjuvant use after surgery, and their continuation beyond disease progression, as well as the presence of predictive biomarkers, quality-of-life endpoints, and emerging somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-targeted strategies. This manuscript addresses 10 practical questions regarding SSA use beyond established indications. The available evidence supports dose-intensification mainly for improved hormonal symptom control and, in selected patients, temporary disease stabilisation, while objective tumour responses remain uncommon. Anti-proliferative effects may also be observed in some grade 2 NETs with a Ki-67 index higher than 10%, albeit with diminishing activity in the presence of increasing proliferative activity, whereas it is limited in grade 3 tumours. Evidence in pulmonary carcinoids, MEN1-related pancreatic NETs, and other uncommon somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-positive NETs remains heterogeneous, and mostly based on small or non-randomised studies. In contrast, routine adjuvant treatment after complete tumour resection is not currently supported, although retrospective studies suggest a potential benefit in selected patients with resected pancreatic NETs at high risk of recurrence. Quantitative SSTR imaging, tissue SSTR2 expression and circulating molecular markers are promising tools for patient selection, but none is currently validated for routine predictive use. New formulations and SSTR-directed agents with receptor-binding properties distinct from those of conventional SSAs are expected to broaden future treatment options, although their clinical value will require prospective confirmation.
