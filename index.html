The incidence of pediatric choledocholithiasis is rising, yet the optimal initial treatment strategy remains debated. Clinicians must choose between an endoscopy-first approach (preoperative ERCP followed by laparoscopic cholecystectomy) and a surgery-first strategy (laparoscopic cholecystectomy with intraoperative cholangiography and bile duct exploration). Direct pediatric data comparing these two strategies remain scarce, and clinical decision-making therefore continues to rely heavily on evidence extrapolated from adult populations.

We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies comparing surgery-first versus endoscopy-first management of pediatric choledocholithiasis, following PRISMA 2020 guidelines. MEDLINE, Embase, and Cochrane databases were searched from inception to December 2025. Pooled estimates were calculated utilizing risk ratios (RR) and mean differences (MD) via random-effects models with the REML estimator. Risk of bias was assessed using the ROBINS-I tool.

Surgery-first management reduced hospital length of stay (MD -1.61 days; 95% CI -2.50 to -0.71; p < 0.001) but correspondingly increased operative time (MD 31.45 min; 95% CI 15.60 to 47.29; p < 0.001). We found no significant difference regarding overall complications (RR 0.46; 95% CI 0.09 to 2.33; p = 0.22) or bleeding (RR 0.28; 95% CI 0.06 to 1.32; p = 0.11). Surgery-first patients demonstrated a lower risk of postoperative pancreatitis (RR 0.21; 95% CI 0.06 to 0.73; p = 0.01) and 30-day readmission (RR 0.42; 95% CI 0.18 to 0.95; p = 0.04).

Surgery-first was consistently associated with lower rates of postoperative pancreatitis, shorter hospital length of stay, and fewer 30-day readmissions, at the cost of longer operative time. Because the evidence remains observational, with low-to-very-low certainty by GRADE, these associations should be interpreted as hypothesis-strengthening rather than definitive. Surgery-first may be a reasonable first-line option for appropriately selected children at centers with laparoscopic biliary expertise, pending higher-quality prospective data.

The inferior mesenteric artery (IMA) is a crucial artery that supplies the colon and rectum. As colorectal cancer is most commonly localized in the descending and sigmoid colon, as well as in the rectum, detailed knowledge of the anatomy of the IMA is of utmost importance for oncological surgeons performing hemicolectomies.

An extensive search of major databases was performed up to April 2026 for studies detailing the morphometry, anatomy, and variations of the IMA. The study strictly adhered to the PRISMA guidelines. Out of 143,884 initial records, 66 original studies met the inclusion criteria. A random effects model was applied in the statistical analyses, and heterogeneity was evaluated using Chi-squared and I-squared statistics.

The IMA most often originated at the L3 vertebral level (60.35%). The number of sigmoid arteries varied across the study groups, with 2 sigmoid arteries variant being the most common variant at 38.42%, followed by 3 sigmoid arteries variant at 31.30%. Across IMA branching pattern classification systems, the most prevalent anatomical variations were Type 1 in the modified Yada scale (47.39%), Type 1 in Latarjet scale (47.39%), Type 1B in McSweeney scale (45.51%), and Type B in modified Zebrowski scale (20.35%).

To standardize complex branching patterns, we propose supplementing the modified Yada scale with the exact number of sigmoid arteries for clinical practice, while recommending the McSweeney scale for prospective research. Ultimately, accurate preoperative visualization and standardized IMA categorization are crucial for optimal surgical outcomes.

Immunotherapy is a type of treatment that helps the body’s immune system find and attack cancer cells. In colorectal (bowel) cancer that has spread to other parts of the body, it works well for only a small group of patients. Their tumors have a fault in the way they repair their DNA. Most bowel cancers do not have this fault, and immunotherapy alone does little for them. Stereotactic ablative radiotherapy is a very precise type of radiation treatment. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are effective in the approximately 5% of metastatic colorectal cancers (mCRC) that are microsatellite instability-high or mismatch repair-deficient (MSI-H/dMMR), whereas microsatellite-stable or mismatch repair-proficient (MSS/pMMR) disease rarely responds to ICI monotherapy. Stereotactic ablative radiotherapy (SABR) has been proposed to prime antitumor immunity in MSS disease. Preclinical studies show that high-dose-per-fraction radiation can induce immunogenic cell death, release tumor antigens and activate the cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes (cGAS-STING) pathway, which may increase T-cell infiltration and, in some models, cause regression of non-irradiated lesions (the abscopal effect). Clinical data come mainly from small, single-arm, early-phase studies that report acceptable safety but modest response rates, and no colorectal-specific randomized trial has shown a survival benefit from adding SABR to immunotherapy. This review summarizes the biological rationale, critically appraises the clinical evidence, including chemo-immuno-radiotherapy in rectal cancer, and discusses toxicity, patient selection, dose-fractionation and sequencing. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched from January 2000 to February 2026. SABR-induced immune priming in MSS mCRC is biologically plausible but should be tested in biomarker-stratified randomized trials.

This study aimed to investigate the potential association between gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and periodontitis (PD) and to explore possible biological mechanisms underlying this link.

In the first part of this study, a two-sample Mendelian randomisation (MR) analysis was conducted to evaluate the potential effect of PD on the risk of GDM, and vice versa. In the second part, transcriptomic data and network-based bioinformatics analyses were integrated, including differential expression analysis and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction, to screen for common differentially expressed genes and significantly enriched biological pathways. In the third part, a simplified in vitro β-cell challenge system was established using the human pancreatic β-cell line EndoC-βH1. Cells were exposed to high-glucose and progesterone conditions with or without Porphyromonas gingivalis to examine inflammatory and oxidative stress responses. Differences in the expression of MR-genes, inflammatory cytokines, and reactive oxidative species were evaluated. All analyses were performed using R version 4.2.2 (R CoreTeam, Vienna, Austria).

The forward MR analysis showed evidence consistent with a modest positive association of genetically predicted PD with GDM risk (OR = 1.14, P = 0.006), whereas the reverse analysis did not provide evidence for an association of genetically predicted GDM with PD risk (OR = 1.08, P = 0.222). Sensitivity analyses supported the robustness of the MR results. Transcriptomic bioinformatic analyses identified multiple key genes that were dysregulated in both PD and GDM, including inflammatory mediators (IL1RN and TGFB1) and metabolic regulators (AKT1, NOS3, and TCF7L2). Functional enrichment revealed that the overlapping genes were mainly involved in critical biological processes like apoptotic signalling, cell adhesion, and inflammatory responses. The PPI network suggested a synergistic role of PD and GDM in inflammation and metabolic regulation. Compared with the GDM model, β-cells in the GDM+PD model exhibited significantly enhanced inflammatory responses and oxidative stress. Additionally, the MR-genes showed significant expression changes in the GDM+PD model: HSPBAP1, APBA1, and SHC4 were upregulated, whereas CDH1, NDUFAF6, and DGKB were downregulated.

These findings provide evidence consistent with a potential biological link between PD and GDM, in which periodontal infection-related inflammatory responses and oxidative stress may aggravate β-cell stress under GDM-like conditions.

Somatostatin analogues (SSAs) are the cornerstone of treatment for patients with neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), with established anti-secretory activity in functioning NETs and anti-proliferative activity in grade 1 and 2 (Ki-67 levels, up to 10%) gastro-entero-pancreatic NETs. However, several clinically relevant scenarios regarding their role still remain insufficiently addressed by available guidance, including the following: patient selection for dose-escalation or interval-shortening after disease progression, their anti-proliferative effect in tumours with a > 10% Ki-67 index, their role in pulmonary carcinoids, Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN1)-related pancreatic NETs and uncommon neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs), their adjuvant use after surgery, and their continuation beyond disease progression, as well as the presence of predictive biomarkers, quality-of-life endpoints, and emerging somatostatin receptor (SSTR)-targeted strategies. Quantitative SSTR imaging, tissue SSTR2 expression and circulating molecular markers are promising tools for patient selection, but none is currently validated for routine predictive use. New formulations and SSTR-directed agents with receptor-binding properties distinct from those of conventional SSAs are expected to broaden future treatment options, although their clinical value will require prospective confirmation.

💡 Conclusion
Although the carbohydrate biomarker CA19-9 is widely used for cancer screening, its diagnostic specificity is severely compromised by false-positive elevations in benign inflammatory conditions. Characterizing specific CA19-9 protein carriers provides a promising alternative, yet high-throughput, micro-volume profiling directly from serum remains a challenge. Here, we present a 96-well chemical proteomic platform for the high-throughput profiling of CA19-9-modified proteins. Utilizing a tailored trifunctional chemical probe, photo-activated covalent crosslinking between anti-CA19-9 antibodies and target carriers enables high-stringency enrichment within streptavidin-coated microplates. The workflow identified 509 carriers in cancer cell lines and achieved highly sensitive quantification of established targets, including CD44, LGALS3BP, and mucin family, using as little as 10 μL serum. Notably, this represents the first mass spectrometry-based direct identification of mucin family in peripheral serum. In a clinical cohort of 91 hyper-CA19-9 individuals, CA19-9-modified CD44 was enriched in healthy controls, whereas CA19-9-modified MUC5B was significantly elevated in benign lesions over colorectal cancer (CRC), validated by ELISA across two independent cohorts with AUCs 0.715 and 0.756. Integrating the protein carrier dataset into a random forest machine learning classifier effectively discriminated CRC from benign lesions with an AUC of 0.864. Furthermore, comparative sub-analysis resolved a critical clinical dilemma by identifying 59 upregulated protein carriers in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma relative to benign gallstone-induced cholangitis. Overall, this 96-well chemical proteomic strategy enables parallelized isolation of CA19-9 modified proteins from low-volume samples, establishing a reproducible and scalable tool for the differential profiling of clinically relevant carbohydrate antigen carriers.
