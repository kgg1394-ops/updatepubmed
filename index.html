Patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) have poor health-related quality of life.

To explore the feasibility and potential impact of a modified yoga program on health-related quality of life in patients with IPF compared with usual care.

We conducted a randomized, controlled, nonblinded, 12-week pilot clinical trial with two arms involving 63 adults with IPF diagnosed according to the 2018 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT clinical practice guideline. The yoga arm attended one-hour virtual group sessions twice weekly for 12 weeks, consisting of seated postures, breathing exercises, and meditation designed for patients with IPF, while the control arm continued usual care.

ments: Health-related quality of life was assessed using seven patient-reported outcome measures and their associated domains.

To explore the relationship between recurrent otitis media (ROM), chronic media otitis with effusion (COM), and laryngopharyngeal reflux disease (LPRD) in pediatric populations.

PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane databases.

Two investigators independently screened papers with PRISMA guidelines considering the following primary outcomes: LPRD prevalence in pediatric ROM and COM, diagnostic methods used, and detection rates of gastroduodenal markers in middle ear effusions. Two random-effects meta-analyses were performed to estimate the pooled prevalence of pepsin/pepsinogen detection in middle ear effusions and to compare pepsin concentrations between COM/ROM patients and controls using standardized mean differences (Hedges' g).

Twenty-eight studies were included (2070 pediatric patients). Alkaline-activated gastroduodenal mediators were assessed in 3 studies, with detection rates ranging from 39.6% to 100% for trypsin and from 32% to 42% for bile salts. Substantial heterogeneity was found for COM/ROM and reflux disease diagnostic criteria, gastroduodenal biomarker measurements, and outcomes.

The integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) into neoadjuvant strategies is increasingly being studied in locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC), to improve complete response (CR), organ preservation, and potentially overall survival (OS). However, the clinical impact of conventional PD-1 blockade still remains limited in proficient mismatch repair/microsatellite-stable (pMMR/MSS) disease, suggesting that its simple addition to standard neoadjuvant therapy may be insufficient. Moving from empiric ICI addition toward biologically informed and rationally designed immune-based strategies may represent the next step to maximize complete response and organ preservation in rectal cancer.

💡 Conclusion
Two-year prospective data support the clinical applicability of 3D-printed complete dentures. All fractures occurred in maxillary dentures with a thin palatal thickness setting, suggesting that attention to the denture design may help prevent fracture.
To evaluate 2-year cumulative success and survival rates, changes in oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL), and patient satisfaction among edentulous patients who continued to wear complete dentures fabricated by three-dimensional (3D) printing (CAD-3DPs) after a crossover randomized controlled trial (RCT).

Eighteen edentulous patients (36 dentures) wearing maxillary and mandibular CAD-3DPs after the RCT were prospectively followed. Events were denture fracture, repair, relining, or occlusal reconstruction (success rate) and discontinuation of use (survival rate). OHRQoL (total and seven subdomain scores) and patient satisfaction (visual analogue scale) were assessed at RCT conclusion (T0) and every 6 months up to 24 months (T4), and compared with T0.

Twenty-four dentures (12 patients) completed 2-year observation. In the success analysis, the only events were fractures of four maxillary dentures, all occurring within the first year; in the survival analysis, four dentures (two patients) were discontinued after maxillary fracture. Two-year cumulative success and survival rates were 88% and 87%, respectively. Palatal thickness of fractured dentures had been set at 2.0-2.1 mm, at the lower end of the design range (2.0-2.5 mm). No significant changes from T0 were detected in the OHRQoL total score, any of its seven subdomains, or patient satisfaction during follow-up.

CAD-3DPs showed high 2-year success and survival rates, with maintained OHRQoL and patient satisfaction, and can be an effective prosthetic treatment option for edentulous patients.

Two-year prospective data support the clinical applicability of 3D-printed complete dentures. All fractures occurred in maxillary dentures with a thin palatal thickness setting, suggesting that attention to the denture design may help prevent fracture.

The prevalence of colorectal cancer in young adults is increasing worldwide but there is limited data from Latin American countries. In Mexico, the burden and clinical profile of colorectal adenocarcinoma in young patients are not well characterized. Our study aimed to estimate the prevalence of colorectal adenocarcinoma and describe its clinical characteristics, in young adult patients in Mexico.

The PubMed and Web of Science databases were systematically searched from database inception to September 2024. The prevalence of colorectal adenocarcinoma in young adults in Mexico was evaluated, comparing the clinical characteristics of early-onset and late-onset cases. A pooled meta-analysis was carried out, using random effects models. Heterogeneity was assessed through the I² statistic, meta-regression, and subgroup analyses. The risk of bias was evaluated using the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklist for Studies Reporting Prevalence Data.

The pooled prevalence of colorectal adenocarcinoma in young adults was 10% (95% CI 6-15 %) in patients ≤40 years of age and 19% (95% CI 18-21 %) in those under 50 years of age. Meta-regression indicated a growing trend in the under 40 age group over time and the predominating clinical factors were male sex (52%), rectal involvement (44%), stage IV disease at diagnosis (32%), and a greater number of poorly differentiated tumors.

💡 Conclusion
The clinical efficacy of currently available third- and later-line treatment regimens for patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) remains suboptimal. This study was designed to investigate fruquintinib plus TAS-102 regimen in this patient population. This study was a prospective, multi-center, single-arm phase II trial. A total of 50 patients with mCRC who had experienced treatment failure to at least two standard therapies were enrolled. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS) and secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and safety. Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. With a median follow-up duration of 25.1 months, the median PFS was 6.07 months (95% CI: 4.07-7.87) and the median OS was 14.10 months (95% CI: 9.84-23.28). The ORR was 10.0% (95% CI: 3.3-21.8), with a DCR of 66.0% (95% CI: 51.2-78.8). All the patients experienced treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), with 68.0% reporting grade ≥ 3 TRAEs. No serious adverse events (AEs), deaths, or new safety concerns were reported. In conclusion, fruquintinib combined with TAS-102 showed preliminary anti-tumor activity with a manageable safety profile in patients with refractory mCRC, which highlights its potential as a viable later-line therapeutic option.

Biobanks play an important role in biomedical research because of the availability of vast quantities of high-quality biological specimens and related data. However, biobank-specific standards such as ISO 20387:2018 have been infrequently implemented. This study aimed to develop a practical implementation roadmap to assist ISO 9001:2015-certified biobanks in adopting ISO 20387:2018.

The implementation roadmap was developed through a scientific literature review and a comparative, clause-level analysis (crosswalk) of ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 20387:2018. The analysis identified areas of overlap between the standards and requirements specific to ISO 20387:2018.

The crosswalk demonstrated substantial overlap between the two standards while identifying additional ISO 20387:2018 requirements related to financial viability assessment, plans for future expansion, additional specimen-collection standards, authentication protocols, and reporting requirements. Based on these findings, we developed a practical, time-bound implementation framework that leverages existing ISO 9001:2015 systems to help structure the transition to ISO 20387:2018. The proposed roadmap can be completed within an estimated 17-20 weeks.

▶Investigating the role of artificial intelligence in predicting risk factors after liver transplantation: A systematic review.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Liver transplantation can save the lives of people with severe liver disease. However, many patients face serious complications after surgery, such as kidney injury, cancer recurrence, infections, or heart problems. These issues can reduce quality of life or even lead to death if not detected early. In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI), especially machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), has shown promise in health care. These tools can analyze large amounts of patient data and help doctors predict complications before they happen. However, it is unclear how well these models really work for liver transplant patients. In this systematic review, we examined 14 studies that used AI models to predict different outcomes after liver transplantation. We found that some models—especially those using advanced methods like DL—performed well in predicting serious complications, like acute kidney injury, cancer recurrence, and sepsis. In some cases, they were more accurate than traditional methods. Despite the promise, most models were tested only in limited settings. Many lacked external validation, and very few were tested in real-time hospital environments. Also, many AI tools are still “black boxes,” meaning doctors can't easily understand how they work or why they make certain predictions. This research highlights the exciting potential of AI in improving transplant care—but it also shows that we're not there yet. Future work needs to focus on testing these tools across different hospitals, making them more transparent, and ensuring they fit into real clinical workflows. If done right, AI could help doctors catch problems early, personalize care, and improve survival for liver transplant patients.
Liver transplantation (LT) is the treatment of choice for patients with end-stage liver disease, yet its success is often compromised by serious complications such as acute kidney injury (AKI), graft failure, recurrence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and infections. Recent advances in AI-especially machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL)-offer promising tools for predicting these outcomes and supporting early clinical interventions. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the performance and limitations of AI-based models in predicting key post-LT complications.

PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar were comprehensively searched from January 2000 to December 2025. Studies applying AI algorithms to predict post-LT outcomes in humans were included. Non-English-language articles, non-original studies, and articles without predictive modelling were excluded. Two reviewers independently screened and extracted data, including study design, AI type, input data, validation method, and performance metrics. Due to high heterogeneity, no meta-analysis was performed.

Of 6,744 articles identified, 14 met the inclusion criteria. ML models (eg, random forest, XGBoost, support vector machines, and neural networks) were used in most studies; DL architectures (eg, CNNs, DeepSurv, and CapsNets) were used in a few, and reported AUROC ranges were 0.61-0.96. Only four studies used external validation. Commonly predicted outcomes included AKI, HCC recurrence, sepsis, graft-versus-host disease, and malignancy.

AI algorithms show strong potential for predicting post-LT complications, particularly when ensemble or imaging-based DL models are used. However, limited validation and interpretability remain challenges. Future research should focus on multicentre validation, transparent reporting, and clinical integration.

Current guidelines for the management of patients with HCC are predominantly focused on well-resourced healthcare systems. In 2024, the IHPBA Legacy committee initiated a process to develop global resource-sensitive guidelines for the management of HCC.

An IHPBA global guideline group produced and circulated statements on the management of HCC from diagnosis to treatment, discussed these in online webinars and generated guidelines. This document focuses on the diagnosis and management of patients with HCC in resource-constrained healthcare settings.

Initial diagnosis by ultrasound is supported by cross-sectional imaging. Alfa-feto protein and assessment of liver synthetic function are recommended at diagnosis. Lesion biopsy is not mandated. Advice for the care of the patient with advanced or terminal disease is provided.
