B vitamins are crucial cofactors in metabolic regulation; however, their role in the pathogenesis of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) remains unclear. Evidence for individual B vitamins is often contradictory. This systematic review evaluated the findings of observational studies examining the association between B vitamin intake and MASLD risk, severity, and outcomes.

A systematic search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar for studies published up to January 2026, adhering to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. All included studies were observational in design, encompassing cross-sectional, case-control, cohort, and Mendelian randomization (MR) studies that assessed dietary, supplemental, or biological B-vitamin status in relation to MASLD diagnoses. Study quality was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa scale (NOS). No quantitative meta-analysis was performed and the evidence was narratively synthesized.

Fifty-nine studies were included (38 cross-sectional, 13 case-control, 6 cohort, and 2 MR studies). Folate has been extensively studied, with most evidence suggesting a protective role for serum folate and 5-mTHF against MASLD and fibrosis. However, this was contradicted by positive associations for RBC folate and unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA). Cohort studies have linked higher folate status to reduced all-cause mortality. Vitamin B12 findings were highly controversial, with studies reporting protective, detrimental, and null associations; MR studies suggested a positive causal link with MASLD risk. Choline intake was inversely associated with steatosis but positively correlated with fibrosis in specific subgroups, including men and postmenopausal women. Evidence for niacin and pyridoxine was inconsistent, though often suggesting an inverse relationship with MASLD.

Observational evidence suggests significant, albeit contradictory, associations between specific B-vitamins (particularly folate and B12), choline, and MASLD progression. However, given the massive methodological heterogeneity and heavy reliance on cross-sectional data, the certainty of these findings is very low. The inherent uncertainty of these associations rather than concluding a probable clinical benefit should also be emphasized. Rigorous prospective cohorts and randomized controlled trials are required to establish causality and clarify the true clinical utility of B-vitamin status in MASLD.

Sleep disorders are increasingly recognized as contributors to metabolic dysfunction and chronic liver disease, but their associations with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and cirrhosis remain unclear. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the relationship between sleep disorders and liver-related outcomes.

Following PRISMA guidelines (PROSPERO: CRD420261342781), we searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library through February 2026 for cohort studies assessing sleep disorders and liver outcomes. Adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) or odds ratios (ORs) were pooled using random-effects models. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses were performed, and risk of bias was assessed using ROBINS-I.

Findings were robust in sensitivity analyses, with no significant publication bias.

Sleep disorders, particularly insomnia, are associated with increased risks of HCC and liver-related outcomes. These findings highlight sleep disturbances as potentially modifiable risk factors in liver disease progression.

💡 Conclusion
This trial is registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07381764).
In patients with RAS/BRAF wild-type (WT) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC), anti-epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) monoclonal antibodies combined with chemotherapy represent an established first-line option and may provide clinically meaningful tumor shrinkage. However, acquired resistance may develop through the emergence of mechanism/s of acquired resistance. After progression to first- and second-line regimens, the prognosis of patients with mCRC remains poor. Available later-line options exhibit a cytostatic activity and are associated with limited objective response rate (ORR). Of note, the dynamic decay of resistant subclones after withdrawal of EGFR blockade supports the rationale to explore anti-EGFR rechallenge therapy.

ROMANCE trial is a multicenter, randomized phase II study. Overall, 150 patients with mCRC with circulating tumor RAS/BRAF/EGFR/PIK3CA exon 20/MAP2K1/MET WT and HER2 nonamplified (negative hyperselected tumors) mCRC, at baseline liquid biopsy, will be randomized 1:1 to irinotecan plus cetuximab rechallenge compared with trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab as third-line therapy. The primary endpoint is ORR assessed by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors 1.1 by blinded independent central review; secondary endpoints include progression-free survival, overall survival, safety, and quality of life.

Given the limited ORRs achieved with currently available options after second-line treatment for refractory mCRC, strategies capable of inducing clinically meaningful tumor shrinkage are urgently required. ROMANCE will evaluate whether cetuximab plus irinotecan retreatment can improve ORR compared with standard third-line trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab in patients with circulating tumor DNA-negative hyperselected mCRC and will clarify the role of anti-EGFR rechallenge in the continuum of care of refractory mCRC.

This trial is registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07381764).

💡 Conclusion
In 2024, the International Anal Neoplasia Society (IANS) published the first consensus guidelines for anal cancer screening, outlining recommendations for patients who are most impacted by anal cancer. This study assesses provider knowledge and practice of these guidelines. A 37-item cross-sectional survey was distributed electronically to physicians, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants across ten specialties within a single health system. The survey assessed familiarity with anal cytology, guideline knowledge, screening practices, and barriers to screening. Descriptive statistics and bivariate analyses compared primary care providers (PCPs) to subspecialists. 87 providers completed the survey (62% physicians, 21% nurse practitioners, 17% physician assistants); 60% were Primary Care Physicians (PCPs) and 40% subspecialists. 55% had never heard of the IANS guidelines, and 76% had never performed an anal Pap test. Among the 70 providers completing the knowledge assessment, the median score was 10/20 (Inter Quartile Range (IQR) 8-12). Providers performed worst when identifying screening recommendations for solid organ transplant recipients (7% correct) and cisgender women with HIV (13% correct). No significant differences were found between PCPs and subspecialists across any measure. Qualitative responses identified scope of practice uncertainty (78%), lack of training (20%), and limited access to high-resolution anoscopy (9%) as primary barriers. Provider knowledge and utilization of anal cancer screening guidelines remain critically low across specialties. These findings highlight the urgent need for institution-wide education, standardized ordering pathways, and clear referral processes to improve screening rates.

💡 Conclusion
This study's findings suggest that centralized mailed FIT outreach could increase CRC screening in FQHCs serving populations with persistent disparities, although maintaining intervention fidelity and providing ongoing implementation support over time are needed for consistent impact.
Colorectal cancer (CRC) screening is suboptimal among people in traditionally underserved populations, many of whom receive primary care at federally qualified health centers (FQHCs). Mailed fecal immunochemical test (FIT) outreach improves screening, especially in underserved populations, but questions remain about impact over time.

To evaluate the effectiveness of centralized annual mailed FIT outreach delivered over 3 years to increase CRC screening in FQHCs.

This cluster randomized clinical trial was conducted between June 1, 2021, and May 31, 2024. Sixteen clinics from 2 FQHCs in Southern California were randomly assigned to mailed FIT outreach or usual care for patients aged 50 to 75 years and not up to date with screening.

Intervention components followed best practices for mailed FIT outreach and included educational primers, FIT kits, and telephone reminders.

The primary outcome was the proportion of individuals up to date with any CRC screening after 3 years of intervention (point prevalence); screening at 2 years was a secondary outcome. Due to a resource constraint-related protocol modification, mailed FIT outreach began earlier in year 3, such that some individuals who completed screening in year 2 were not yet due for repeat FIT.

Among 16 clinics serving 20 250 patients, 6 clinics serving 8334 individuals at baseline (4435 aged 55-64 years [53.2%]; 4896 female [58.7%]; 341 Asian [4.1%], 238 Black [2.9%], 2390 Hispanic [28.7%], and 4371 non-Hispanic White [52.4%]) were randomized to intervention and 10 clinics serving 11 916 individuals at baseline (6443 aged 55-64 years [54.1%]; 7250 female [60.8%]; 699 Asian [5.9%], 355 Black [3.0%], 4635 Hispanic [38.9%], and 4436 non-Hispanic White [37.2%]) were randomized to usual care. At year 3, screening was 39.4% (95% CI, 36.3% to 42.6%) in intervention clinics vs 36.5% (95% CI, 31.2% to 42.2%) in usual care clinics, which was not a statistically significant difference (2.9 percentage points; 95% CI, -3.2 to 9.0 percentage points). At year 2, screening was 37.8% (95% CI, 35.2% to 40.4%) in intervention clinics vs 27.0% (95% CI, 21.8% to 32.9%) in usual care clinics (difference, 10.8 percentage points; 95% CI, 4.9 to 16.7 percentage points).

This study's findings suggest that centralized mailed FIT outreach could increase CRC screening in FQHCs serving populations with persistent disparities, although maintaining intervention fidelity and providing ongoing implementation support over time are needed for consistent impact.

The clinical course of primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)-associated inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) after liver transplantation (LTx) varies significantly across studies. This meta-analysis aims to provide a comprehensive estimation of incidence rates (IR) for IBD-related outcomes and PSC recurrence.

Online databases (January 2000-June 2025) were searched for studies on PSC patients with or without IBD at LTx. Studies reporting posttransplant outcomes of interest were included. The pooled IR were calculated with the "meta" package (R Studio [V.4.3.2]) using a random effects model.

A total of 34 studies (3828 patients; 67% with IBD at transplantation) were included. The IR of preexiting IBD exacerbation was 36.1 per 1000 person-years (95% confidence intervals [CI], 23.0-56.5). Medically refractory IBD requiring colectomy occurred at an IR of 12.4 per 1000 person-years (95% CI, 8.9-17.4). The IRs of colorectal neoplasia and advanced neoplasia were 10.3 (95% CI, 6.4-16.7) and 7.7 (95% CI, 5.6-10.7) per 1000 person-years, respectively. Among posttransplant PSC-only patients, the IR of newly diagnosed IBD was 20.0 (95% CI, 12.0-33.4). PSC recurrence occurred more frequently in PSC-IBD patients (31.9; 95% CI, 25.0-40.5) than in PSC-only patients (21.4; 95% CI, 11.3-40.6), corresponding to an incidence rate ratio of 2.16 (95% CI, 1.72-2.72; P < 0.001).

After LTx for PSC, patients remain at substantial risk of colorectal neoplasia and de novo IBD. PSC-IBD is associated with a higher risk of disease recurrence. These findings support continued endoscopic surveillance and close monitoring of IBD activity.

Phase 3 evidence for antifibrotic-directed therapies in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) has expanded, but direct head-to-head trials are lacking. Forced vital capacity (FVC) decline remains the principal trial outcome, although it may not fully capture clinically relevant differences among therapies.

We systematically reviewed phase 3 randomized placebo-controlled trials of antifibrotic-directed therapies in IPF. FVC effects versus placebo were descriptively summarized using standardized 48-52-week estimates. All-cause treatment discontinuation and all-cause mortality were analyzed using placebo-controlled odds ratios. Sensitivity analyses assessed follow-up duration, background antifibrotic therapy, and trial-level consistency.

Ten trials including 5,386 randomized participants were included. Pirfenidone and nintedanib showed consistent reductions in FVC decline versus placebo. Inhaled treprostinil showed a favorable FVC signal in the combined TETON-1/2 evidence, largely as add-on therapy with background antifibrotic treatment. Without background antifibrotic therapy, several therapies showed directionally favorable but imprecise FVC effects, without evidence of equivalence. Nintedanib and inhaled treprostinil were associated with higher odds of all-cause treatment discontinuation versus placebo: OR 1.39 (95% CI, 1.02-1.88) and OR 1.39 (95% CI, 1.08-1.78), respectively. Pirfenidone was the only therapy associated with lower odds of all-cause mortality (OR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.31-0.83), based on published pooled ASCEND/CAPACITY mortality data and the Japanese phase 3 trial.

FVC decline remains essential but may not fully distinguish antifibrotic-directed therapies in IPF. Integrating FVC effects, background therapy, treatment continuation, and mortality provides a cautious, context-dependent framework for interpreting expanding phase 3 evidence.
