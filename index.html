▶Evolving indications for metabolic and bariatric surgery.

💡 Conclusion
This chapter addresses patient selection for metabolic and bariatric surgery (MBS) in the modern obesity care landscape, where anti-obesity medications (AOMs) such as semaglutide and tirzepatide have changed the clinical decision-making framework. We organize the evidence around four questions a clinician actually asks: who qualifies under current guidelines, who benefits most and from which procedure, who is poorly served by surgery, and when pharmacotherapy is the better answer. We review the evolution of indications from the 1991 NIH Consensus Conference through the 2022 ASMBS/IFSO joint guidelines, with attention to the randomized evidence supporting expanded indications at BMI 30-34.9 kg/m² with type 2 diabetes. Finally, we provide a comparative analysis of MBS and modern AOMs across cardiometabolic outcomes, with attention to the methodological limitations of current cohort comparisons and the role of combination and sequential strategies. The chapter concludes that the two modalities are converging on a complementary framework in which patient phenotype, not BMI alone, determines the right intervention.

💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major global health burden, and increasing evidence indicates that disruption of trace element homeostasis may contribute to colorectal carcinogenesis and disease progression. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to critically discuss and evaluate evidence on trace element alterations in CRC from studies employing inductively coupled plasma (ICP)-based analytical techniques. Following PRISMA guidelines and a PECO framework, PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus were systematically searched up to September 17, 2026, for original human studies using inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) or optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) to determine trace elements in CRC-related biological matrices compared to healthy controls. Methodological quality was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), and random-effects meta-analyses were conducted to calculate pooled standardized mean differences (SMD) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Nineteen eligible studies, published between 1994 and 2026, were included. The quantitative synthesis demonstrated a pronounced systemic depletion of circulating selenium (SMD = -0.74, alongside a significant circulatory accumulation of copper (SMD = 0.61), lead (SMD = 0.50), and arsenic (SMD = 0.46) in patients with CRC compared with controls. In tumor tissues, the microenvironment was characterized by a localized hyper-accumulation of manganese (SMD = 0.22). These changes suggest that trace element dysregulation in CRC is highly matrix-dependent. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed across studies, likely due to differences in patient characteristics, pre-analytical procedures, and biological matrices. The present findings therefore support the biological relevance of trace element dysregulation in CRC, highlighting the potential of multi-element profiles (e.g., Cu/Zn ratios) while emphasizing the importance of standardized analytical and pre-analytical procedures. Rather than establishing ICP-based techniques as novel analytical tools, this review provides substantial discussion related to the CRC-associated elemental alterations and identifies tissue-dependent profiles; however, observational methodological limitations should be considered for further biomarker and clinical translation studies. This study was supported by Brazilian funding agencies (CNPq, INCTBio-LK, FAPESP, FAPERGS) and the review protocol was registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF; osf.io/zjb3y).

Compensated MASH cirrhosis is an advanced disease with no current approved pharmacologic therapy. FGF analogues have shown biological activity, but individual trials have not established histologic efficacy. We aim to evaluate their effects across histologic and non-invasive outcomes in this population.

Colonoscopy surveillance is recommended following colorectal cancer (CRC) surgery, yet the evidence supporting the recommendations has major quality issues. This study assessed the independent impact of colonoscopy surveillance within 5 years after CRC surgery.

A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted. Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched from inception until 18 June 2026 for randomized clinical trials comparing follow-up protocols including colonoscopy surveillance (intervention) to those without colonoscopy surveillance (control) within 5 years after CRC surgery. Risk of bias was assessed using the RoB 2 tool. Random-effects meta-analyses were conducted to estimate the strength of associations with recurrence detection, reoperation rate, and survival. The number needed to scope (NNS) to detect one recurrence and health economic considerations were estimated. The study protocol has been registered in PROSPERO.

Of 4381 records, five trials with 2337 patients were included. Four trials enrolled patients between 1983 and 1994, predating subsequent advances in CRC treatment. All trials used additional surveillance modalities, leading to a high risk of bias. Follow-up protocols with colonoscopy were not associated with recurrence profile, with risk ratios (RRs) of 0.74 (95% confidence interval (c.i.) 0.32 to 1.69), 1.23 (95% c.i. 0.93 to 1.63), and 0.93 (95% c.i. 0.76 to 1.14) for intraluminal, locoregional, and distant recurrence, respectively. Colonoscopy during follow-up was associated with a higher rate of asymptomatic recurrence (RR 2.38; 95% c.i. 1.53 to 3.69). More curative-intent reoperations were performed in the intervention than control group (RR 1.66; 95% c.i. 1.16 to 2.37), but this was not translated into improved survival (hazard ratio 0.92; 95% c.i. 0.77 to 1.11). The NNS was 159 (95% c.i. 101 to 267), resulting in estimated cost per colonoscopy-detected recurrence of €104 100 (95% c.i. €66 200 to 174 900).

Current evidence from randomized trials does not clearly demonstrate that postoperative colonoscopy surveillance improves survival, although interpretation is limited by the age of the trials and confounding from concurrent surveillance modalities. Contemporary randomized trials are required to better define the independent contribution of colonoscopy within modern multimodal surveillance pathways.

Adjuvant chemotherapy is a standard component of care for patients with resected stage II-III colorectal cancer (CRC), improving survival outcomes but often at the expense of quality of life (QoL) and functional status. Exercise and physical activity (PA) have emerged as a potential strategy to mitigate treatment-related toxicities and improve oncologic outcomes. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of PA and exercise and its interventions during adjuvant chemotherapy.

A systematic review was performed using the MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, SCOPUS, CINAHL, and PsycINFO databases up to June 2025. Studies including adult CRC patients undergoing or recently completed adjuvant chemotherapy and participating in structured exercise program or PA assessment were eligible.

Nine studies encompassing 4070 patients were included, of whom 631 participated in structured exercise interventions. The interventions varied in modality, incorporating aerobic, resistance, and combined programs delivered in both supervised and home-based settings. Engagement in PA was associated with improvements in overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in selected studies, although measures and outcomes were highly heterogeneous. QoL findings were variable but generally favored exercise, with reported benefits in physical functioning, fatigue, cardiorespiratory fitness, and psychological well-being. In addition, exercise was linked in some studies to improved chemotherapy tolerance, including higher treatment completion rates and reduced treatment-related toxicities such as fatigue and neurotoxicity.

Exercise during adjuvant chemotherapy in CRC is feasible and appears to improve functional and patient-reported outcomes, with a consistent signal toward improved survival. Supervised interventions may offer additional benefits through enhanced adherence and optimization of exercise delivery. However, heterogeneity in study design and outcome reporting limits definitive conclusions. Further high-quality randomized trials are needed to establish standardized exercise protocols and clarify their impact on long-term oncologic outcomes.

Colorectal liver metastasis (CRLM) is a leading cause of mortality in colorectal cancer (CRC), yet current surveillance often misses occult micrometastases, underscoring an urgent need for early non-invasive biomarkers and mechanistic insights.

This study aimed to investigate gut microbiota dysbiosis and functional alterations associated with CRLM and explore potential non-invasive biomarkers.

Fecal metagenomic sequencing was performed in 60 participants, comprising healthy controls, patients with non-metastatic CRC, and patients with synchronous CRLM (n = 20 per group). Microbial composition, functional pathways, co-occurrence networks, and clinical associations were systematically analyzed, and a random forest model was constructed to identify potential diagnostic biomarkers. However, prospective validation in larger multicenter cohorts is required before clinical translational utility.

Collectively, our findings suggest that gut microbiota dysbiosis and functional reprogramming are strongly associated with CRLM progression, potentially through remodeling the hepatic pre-metastatic niche via the gut-liver axis.
