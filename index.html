Guidelines recommend preprocedural interruption of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs), but it requires strict adherence and may lead to unnecessary anticoagulant interruption in patients without polypectomy. This study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficiency of "on-demand" DOAC cessation after polypectomy compared with "scheduled" cessation before colonoscopy for all patients.

This multicenter, randomized, open-label, noninferiority trial conducted at 33 hospitals in Japan compared a "scheduled" preprocedural DOAC cessation strategy for all patients with an "on-demand" strategy of continuing DOACs until the day of colonoscopy, followed by DOAC cessation after polypectomy if performed. The primary endpoint was polypectomy-related clinically significant bleeding, with a noninferiority margin of -5%.

Among 314 patients receiving DOACs who underwent colonoscopic polypectomy ("Scheduled" group, n=159, "On-demand" group, n=155), polypectomy-related clinically significant bleeding occurred in 7 patients (4.4%) in the "Scheduled" group and 5 patients (3.2%) in the "On-demand" group (risk difference, 1.2%; 90% CI, -2.4% to 4.7%), showing noninferiority. Intraprocedural controllable bleeding was more frequent in the "On-demand" group (Scheduled 3.4% vs. On-demand 7.0%; P = 0.0037). Periprocedural DOAC exposure was higher in the "On-demand" group than in the "Scheduled" group among patients without polypectomy. No thromboembolic events were observed.

In this open-label, multicenter, randomized trial, "on-demand" DOAC cessation after polypectomy was noninferior to "scheduled" preprocedural cessation regarding postpolypectomy bleeding, with fewer missed DOAC doses in patients without polypectomy but modestly more controllable intraprocedural bleeding, and may offer a practical alternative. To evaluate the efficacy and safety of immune checkpoint inhibitors in the first-line treatment of microsatellite instability or mismatch repair-deficient metastatic colorectal cancer through a systematic review and adjusted indirect comparison, determining whether they are equivalent therapeutic alternatives.

A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed and Embase following PRISMA 2020 recommendations. Phase III randomized controlled trials evaluating immune checkpoint inhibitors in microsatellite instability or mismatch repair-deficient metastatic colorectal cancer were eligible. Efficacy was assessed using Bucher's adjusted indirect comparison method, with progression-free survival as the primary endpoint. Therapeutic equivalence was evaluated according to the Equivalent Therapeutic Alternatives framework, applying a non-inferiority margin (Δ) of 0.65. Safety outcomes included overall and treatment-related adverse events, immune-related adverse events, discontinuations due to adverse events and grade 5 events.

NI demonstrated a higher probability of superior efficacy over pembrolizumab, with an adjusted Hazard Ratio of 0.53 (95% CI: 0.34 to 0.84) for progression-free survival, exceeding the equivalence margin. Thus, pembrolizumab and nivolumab+ipilimumab cannot be considered Equivalent Therapeutic Alternatives regarding efficacy. In terms of safety, nivolumab+ipilimumab was associated with fewer treatment-related grade ≥ 3 adverse events but higher rates of treatment discontinuation and immune-related adverse events compared to pembrolizumab.

Nivolumab+ipilimumab appears to offer greater efficacy than pembrolizumab in first-line treatment of microsatellite instability or mismatch repair-deficient metastatic colorectal cancer, but with a distinct safety profile. Based on current interim data, these agents should not be regarded as equivalent therapeutic alternatives. Further data, including final efficacy analyses and quality-of-life assessments, are warranted to guide optimal treatment selection.

💡 Conclusion
Drug repurposing builds on pharmacological and safety information already available for an approved drug, but this advantage requires additional studies to establish the biological effect and related mechanisms of action. We conducted a clinically focused review of non-oncology drugs tested as active cancer treatments in interventional studies from 1 January 2021 to 31 July 2026. Randomized trials, phase II-III studies, biomarker-selected cohorts, and studies reporting pharmacokinetics or human target engagement were prioritized. Despite the large number of registered studies, the landscape is still dominated by early-phase, academically sponsored trials. Several widely studied candidates, including metformin, nelfinavir, disulfiram-copper, losartan, and propofol, were negative in randomized evaluations. Conversely, aspirin reduced recurrence in localized colorectal cancer with PI3K-pathway alterations, while preoperative peritumoral lidocaine administration was associated with improved disease-free survival and overall survival in a randomized breast cancer trial. Smaller randomized trials investigating pharmacological ascorbate in pancreatic cancer and simvastatin in recurrent small-cell lung cancer provided preliminary efficacy signals that require confirmation in larger studies. Across drug classes, unsuccessful programs were often limited by inadequate exposure, uncertain tumor penetration, absent pharmacodynamic evidence, heterogeneous patient populations, or weak continuity between exploratory and confirmatory development. Progress will depend on more selective clinical hypotheses, early demonstration of human pharmacology, prospective biomarkers, and clear regulatory ownership.

Artificial intelligence (AI)-based computer-aided detection (CADe) systems are increasingly used in colonoscopy but are trained mainly in non-inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) populations. The performance of AI-based CADe for detecting colitis-associated dysplasia compared with established imaging techniques remains unclear.

This study compared colorectal dysplasia miss rates (DMR) between a non-IBD-trained CADe system (ENDO-AID CADe, Olympus) and narrow-band imaging (NBI) in patients with IBD undergoing surveillance colonoscopy.

We conducted a unicentric, open-label, quasi-randomized crossover trial including IBD patients undergoing colorectal cancer surveillance. Each patient underwent colonoscopy with both CADe and NBI in alternating order. The primary outcome was the per-lesion DMR; a per-patient analysis was also performed. Sample size was pragmatically set at 60 patients based on previously published crossover studies comparing chromoendoscopy and NBI in this population.

Sixty patients were included. In the per-lesion analysis, DMR was 42.9% (6/14) for NBI and 23.8% (5/21) for CADe (risk difference 19.0%; 95% CI, -11.2 to 46.9; P = .28). In the per-patient analysis, DMR was 16.7% (1/6) for NBI and 22.2% (2/9) for CADe (risk difference -5.6%; 95% CI, -40.8 to 37.2; P = 1.00). CADe detected more lesions overall than NBI (125 vs 75; P = .006), but a higher proportion were inflammatory (53.6% vs 13.3%; P < .001). NBI detected more hyperplastic lesions (66.7% vs 28%; P < .001).

Dysplasia miss rates did not differ significantly between CADe and NBI, although wide CIs limit definitive conclusions and findings should be considered hypothesis generating. These results support further evaluation of AI as an adjunct to established endoscopic imaging, while highlighting the need for AI models specifically trained in IBD populations.

Messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines offer a flexible approach to colorectal cancer (CRC) immunotherapy, with applications ranging from postoperative residual disease to metastatic cancer. This narrative review examines antigen selection, RNA engineering, nanodelivery, and clinical development, drawing on literature available through 21 October 2026. Tumor-associated antigens, shared driver mutations, and personalized neoantigens offer different advantages in population coverage, specificity, and manufacturing. Their activity also depends on RNA chemistry and carrier design, including lipid composition, endosomal release, APC targeting, tissue distribution, stability, and repeated dosing. Early clinical studies have demonstrated antigen-specific T-cell responses in selected patients, while randomized melanoma data provide a benchmark for combining vaccination with checkpoint blockade. The termination of the CRC adjuvant autogene cevumeran monotherapy trial highlights the difficulty of translating immune priming into recurrence prevention. Antigen heterogeneity, impaired HLA presentation, stromal exclusion, and liver-associated immune suppression remain important barriers in CRC. Advances in neoantigen prediction, self-amplifying RNA, and mechanism-based combinations may improve vaccine design, but their clinical value will depend on disease-specific trials that account for manufacturing feasibility and established oncological endpoints. The available evidence remains heterogeneous, with small CRC cohorts and limited randomized outcomes; the non-systematic search also introduces potential selection bias.

Patients with advanced solid tumors who progress after standard therapies have limited treatment options. SDH-Combi is an autologous cellular immunotherapy integrating natural killer cells and tumor-primed T lymphocytes, designed to address tumor heterogeneity and immune escape through complementary immune mechanisms.

This single-arm, phase 1 study evaluated SDH-Combi in patients with recurrent or progressive solid tumors. SDH-Combi was administered every 2 weeks for up to eight infusions. The primary endpoint was safety and tolerability in all treated patients (N = 9): Safety was assessed as treatment-emergent adverse events graded by CTCAE v5.0 with investigator-assigned causality, and tolerability as the proportion of patients completing all eight planned infusions, the relative dose intensity, and discontinuation due to toxicity. Secondary endpoints were objective response rate, disease control rate, progression-free survival, and overall survival, with tumor response evaluated according to RECIST v1.1. Quality of life (QoL), assessed with the EORTC QLQ-C30, was an exploratory endpoint.

Nine patients received at least one infusion. For the primary endpoint, no grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse event occurred (0 of 9; 95% CI, 0.0-33.6) and no patient discontinued because of toxicity; six of nine patients (66.7%) completed all eight planned infusions and 64 of 72 planned infusions (88.9%) were administered. No cytokine release syndrome, anaphylaxis, or treatment-related serious adverse events occurred. One patient (Patient 8) experienced a grade 4 anemia event (hemoglobin nadir 4.5 g/dL) judged unlikely related to study therapy; all other events were grades 1-2. In the full analysis set (N = 9), the disease control rate was 66.7%, and the objective response rate was 11.1%. Mean changes in five of the six functional domains (global health status/QoL, physical, role, emotional, and social functioning) and in all nine symptom domains met the EORTC minimal clinically important difference threshold of ≥ 10 points. The change in constipation (- 11.1 points) was marginal, and cognitive functioning did not reach the threshold. While median overall survival had not been reached at the safety assessment time point and five of nine patients had died by the 14 January 2026 follow-up, formal statistical inference remains limited by the small, single-arm sample.

SDH-Combi was feasible and well tolerated, with preliminary signals of disease stabilization and QoL improvement in previously treated patients (1-3 prior systemic lines). These findings support further clinical evaluation of this biologically rational combination cellular immunotherapy.

Chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection remains a major cause of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Bovine lactoferrin (bLF), a glycoprotein with antiviral and immunomodulatory properties, has been investigated as a potential therapeutic agent. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy of bLF on virologic and biochemical outcomes in patients with chronic HCV infection. PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched from inception through November 5, 2024 (CRD42024621752). Eligible studies were randomized controlled trials comparing bLF with placebo or no treatment. Pooled analyses used a random-effects inverse-variance model. Heterogeneity was assessed using I², with exploratory subgroup analyses and meta-regression. Five randomized controlled trials were included, four contributing to quantitative analyses. No statistically significant differences were observed for virologic response (RR = 1.00, 95% CI: 0.74-1.36; I² = 31%), biochemical response (RR = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.71-1.53; I² = 0%), or sustained virologic response (RR = 1.06, 95% CI: 0.63-1.76; I² = 0%). Subgroup analyses and meta-regression identified no significant sources of heterogeneity. The available randomized evidence does not demonstrate a statistically significant benefit of bLF on virologic or biochemical responses. Further studies using contemporary treatment strategies and mechanistic endpoints are warranted.

Combined Bio-Bentall and LVAD implantation may achieve bridge to recovery in carefully selected young patients with end-stage valvular disease, but patient selection remains critical and outcomes are not guaranteed.
End-stage valvular heart disease with severe myocardial fibrosis is traditionally managed with heart transplantation.

A 31-year-old man with bicuspid aortic valve, severe regurgitation, ejection fraction of 16%, and extensive fibrosis (extracellular volume, 39.1%) underwent a Bio-Bentall procedure with left ventricular assist device (LVAD) implantation. After 27 months of support with optimized guideline-directed medical therapy, ejection fraction recovered to 66%, permitting minimally invasive device explantation. Paired histopathology demonstrated collagen volume fraction regression from 40.26% to 0.65%.

This case challenges the dogma that severe fibrosis precludes myocardial recovery. The outcome likely reflects synergistic effects of volume overload correction, sustained mechanical unloading, and aggressive neurohormonal blockade. However, bridge to recovery remains uncommon, requires long-term commitment, and is not reproducible in all comparable patients.

Combined Bio-Bentall and LVAD implantation may achieve bridge to recovery in carefully selected young patients with end-stage valvular disease, but patient selection remains critical and outcomes are not guaranteed.

💡 Conclusion
Myocarditis is an inflammatory disease that may be associated with severe acute and chronic complications, including progression to inflammatory cardiomyopathy, characterized by persistent inflammation, cardiac dysfunction, and adverse ventricular remodeling. Arrhythmias are major determinants of morbidity and mortality across this disease spectrum and have traditionally been attributed to structural substrates such as myocardial edema, necrosis, and fibrosis. However, as the role of the immune system in myocarditis is increasingly recognized, growing attention has been focused on immune-mediated mechanisms capable of directly perturbing cardiac excitability. Circulating cardiac autoantibodies are frequently detected in patients with myocarditis. Beyond their potential diagnostic value, specific autoantibody subsets have been hypothesized to exert pathogenic effects by targeting receptors, ion channels, contractile proteins, and intercalated disk components involved in impulse generation, conduction, repolarization, calcium handling, and myocardial structural integrity. This narrative review evaluates the mechanistic basis of autoimmune arrhythmias, the available preclinical and clinical evidence linking autoantibodies to atrial and ventricular arrhythmias, and the potential role of autoantibody profiling in refining arrhythmic risk stratification. Particular attention is given to distinguishing direct evidence obtained in patients with myocarditis and inflammatory cardiomyopathy from findings derived from other cardiac or systemic diseases and experimental models, whose applicability to myocardial inflammatory disorders remains uncertain. Finally, we critically evaluate whether autoantibody testing may help identify patients more likely to benefit from immunomodulatory or immunosuppressive strategies, while emphasizing the current limitations related to small observational cohorts and the lack of randomized trials evaluating antibody-guided treatment strategies with dedicated arrhythmic endpoints.

💡 Conclusion
Drug repurposing builds on pharmacological and safety information already available for an approved drug, but this advantage requires additional studies to establish the biological effect and related mechanisms of action. We conducted a clinically focused review of non-oncology drugs tested as active cancer treatments in interventional studies from 1 January 2021 to 31 July 2026. Randomized trials, phase II-III studies, biomarker-selected cohorts, and studies reporting pharmacokinetics or human target engagement were prioritized. Despite the large number of registered studies, the landscape is still dominated by early-phase, academically sponsored trials. Several widely studied candidates, including metformin, nelfinavir, disulfiram-copper, losartan, and propofol, were negative in randomized evaluations. Conversely, aspirin reduced recurrence in localized colorectal cancer with PI3K-pathway alterations, while preoperative peritumoral lidocaine administration was associated with improved disease-free survival and overall survival in a randomized breast cancer trial. Smaller randomized trials investigating pharmacological ascorbate in pancreatic cancer and simvastatin in recurrent small-cell lung cancer provided preliminary efficacy signals that require confirmation in larger studies. Across drug classes, unsuccessful programs were often limited by inadequate exposure, uncertain tumor penetration, absent pharmacodynamic evidence, heterogeneous patient populations, or weak continuity between exploratory and confirmatory development. Progress will depend on more selective clinical hypotheses, early demonstration of human pharmacology, prospective biomarkers, and clear regulatory ownership.

💡 Conclusion
These data support urinary metabolomic profiling as a promising noninvasive tool for pancreatic disease detection and characterization, providing justification for more comprehensive investigation.
Chronic pancreatitis (CP) is an inflammatory disease lacking diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers. Metabolomics has been previously reported by our research group to detect differences in urinary metabolites within a small group of subjects with pancreatitis. We screened urine samples collected from subjects with CP for metabolites that may serve as biomarkers. Here we perform a discovery, pilot study using an advanced platform to investigate urinary metabolite signatures in CP.

Urine samples from control (n = 40) and definitive CP (n = 40) PROCEED subjects were analyzed by ALEX-CIS GC-TOF Mass Spectrometry. Each urine sample was analyzed across 328 metabolites:139 named and identified; 189 un-identified. Metabolite levels were normalized to sample median metabolite abundances for further analyses.

Direct comparison of urinary metabolite abundances identified 21 metabolites that were differentially expressed in CP versus control groups with 6 metabolites reduced and 15 enriched in urine samples. All 21 showed good discriminatory performance (AUC range; 0.77 - 0.83) with top classifiers (AUC > 0.8) including phenoxyacetic acid, mannose, glycerol-3-galactoside (G3G), xylulose, and ribonic acid (RA). Correlation and hierarchical clustering analysis identified two distinct metabolite clusters. Univariate and subgroup analyses further show several metabolites are also associated with CP-related clinical factors, such as diabetes and exocrine pancreatic dysfunction (EPD), supporting a urinary metabolic signature reflective of both disease status and relevant metabolic comorbid conditions.

These data support urinary metabolomic profiling as a promising noninvasive tool for pancreatic disease detection and characterization, providing justification for more comprehensive investigation.
