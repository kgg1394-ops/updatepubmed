Sepsis causes deaths worldwide each day, with hemodynamic resuscitation being central to management. Guidelines recommend a 65-mm Hg mean arterial pressure (MAP) target, yet the evidence for elevated targets remains fragmented across mortality, renal, and splanchnic outcomes.

This review aims to comprehensively appraise the impact of elevated MAP targets on splanchnic ischemia, mortality, and organ-specific outcomes in septic resuscitation.

Following the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) 2020 guidelines, we systematically searched PubMed, the Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar for studies comparing MAP targets in septic shock. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2; randomized controlled trials [RCTs]) and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (nonrandomized studies). The findings were synthesized narratively according to the Synthesis Without Meta-Analysis guidelines, with random-effects meta-analysis for mortality outcomes. Evidence certainty was graded using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) framework.

From 3254 records, 11 studies (2895 patients; 4 RCTs, 7 nonrandomized) met the inclusion criteria. Pooled 90-day mortality across 3 trials showed a nonsignificant trend toward higher mortality with elevated MAP targets (320/720, 44.4% vs 280/722, 38.8%; OR 1.31, 95% CI 0.97-1.77). Pooled 28- or 30-day mortality showed a similar nonsignificant trend (OR 1.25, 95% CI 0.98-1.61), which was robust to sensitivity analysis. Narratively, mortality findings across nonpooled observational studies were inconsistent; some linked low MAP to higher death risk, and others found no significant association. Renal outcomes were inconsistent, with some studies showing reduced renal replacement therapy or improved acute kidney injury recovery at higher targets, particularly in hypertensive or cirrhotic subgroups. Elevated MAP targets consistently increased vasopressor exposure and were associated with more arrhythmias and fewer organ support-free days. No study reported splanchnic ischemia as a discrete outcome; mesenteric ischemia rates were low and similar between arms. Overall certainty of evidence was very low.

Higher MAP targets show no clear mortality or splanchnic perfusion benefit and may increase overall harm, though select subgroups, chronic hypertension, and cirrhosis, may benefit, supporting individualized, risk-stratified MAP targeting over uniformly elevated-target strategies.

▶Procalcitonin in Acute Pancreatitis: Diagnostic Utility for Differentiating Infected from Sterile Pancreatic Necrosis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Acute pancreatitis (AP) is an acute inflammatory disorder of the pancreas that disrupts its normal function and often requires hospitalization because of severe abdominal pain. While most patients recover without major complications, some develop pancreatic necrosis, infected pancreatic necrosis (IPN), persistent organ failure, and other severe complications associated with increased morbidity and mortality. A key clinical challenge is distinguishing infected pancreatic necrosis from sterile pancreatic inflammation, as delayed or inaccurate diagnosis may result in inappropriate antibiotic use and poorer clinical outcomes. This review aimed to evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic utility of procalcitonin (PCT) in differentiating infected pancreatic necrosis from sterile inflammation and to assess its role in supporting antimicrobial decision-making in patients with AP. A structured narrative literature review was conducted using PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science to identify English-language human studies, meta-analyses, randomized controlled trials, and international guidelines published between January 2000 and December 2025. A total of 60 studies were included in the narrative synthesis. The reviewed evidence indicates that PCT may provide greater diagnostic specificity for bacterial infection than conventional inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and leukocyte count in selected clinical settings. Elevated or persistently increasing PCT concentrations have been associated with IPN, sepsis, organ failure, and increased mortality. Furthermore, PCT-guided antibiotic stewardship strategies may reduce unnecessary antibiotic exposure without adversely affecting clinical outcomes in selected settings. Overall, PCT represents a potentially valuable adjunctive biomarker for differentiating IPN from sterile inflammation in patients with AP. When interpreted alongside clinical assessment and imaging findings, PCT may facilitate the assessment of infection risk, prognostic stratification, and antimicrobial stewardship. However, further large-scale prospective multicenter studies are warranted to establish standardized diagnostic thresholds and optimize its integration into routine clinical practice.

▶Radioembolization for Colorectal Liver Metastases: Indications and Outcomes.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with the liver representing the predominant site of metastatic spread. For patients with colorectal liver metastases (CRLM) who are not candidates for surgical resection, systemic chemotherapy provides disease control but rarely achieves durable hepatic responses. Radioembolization with yttrium-90 (Y-90) microspheres, delivered via the hepatic artery to exploit the preferential arterial supply of liver tumors, has emerged as an important locoregional option in this setting. This review examines the current evidence base for radioembolization in CRLM, covering patient selection, indications across lines of therapy, dosimetric advances, and clinical outcomes. Appropriate candidacy requires careful assessment of performance status, hepatic tumor burden, tumor biology, lung shunt fraction, and extrahepatic disease extent, with the strongest outcomes observed in patients selected earlier in their disease course. Randomized controlled trials support second-line and third-line use, while unselected first-line use has not demonstrated a benefit. Dosimetric advances, including multicompartment personalized dosing, radiation segmentectomy, and radiation lobectomy, are enabling ablative and bridge-to-resection strategies in appropriately selected patients. Future investigation should prioritize biomarker-driven patient selection, personalized dosimetry, and combination strategies with systemic therapy to determine whether durable hepatic disease control can translate into a survival benefit.

▶Lactose, Lactose Maldigestion and Lactose Intolerance: Definitions, Misunderstandings, Mechanisms, and Public Health Implications.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Lactose is the principal carbohydrate in milk, serving a critical role in infant energy metabolism as well as contributing to neurodevelopment, and the establishment of a healthy gut microbiota. Later in life, lactose digestion capacity varies widely across human populations owing to the genetically determined decline of intestinal lactase expression after weaning-a condition termed 'lactase non-persistence'. Approximately two-thirds of the global adult population exhibits reduced lactase activity, yet the clinical consequences of this enzymatic reduction are far less uniform than commonly assumed. This review provides a comprehensive examination of lactose functionality, the physiology of lactose digestion, and the consequences of lactase persistence and non-persistence. We present formal definitions for the spectrum of terms used in lactose research-including lactase persistence, lactase non-persistence, lactose maldigestion, and lactose intolerance-and argue that the critical distinction between lactose maldigestion (a physiological state) and lactose intolerance (a clinical syndrome) is frequently overlooked in both research and public health. Drawing on evidence from double-blind randomised controlled trials , microbiome intervention studies, and global epidemiological data, the evidence indicates that the majority of individuals with lactose maldigestion do not develop clinically meaningful symptoms at moderate intake levels of most dairy products. We further discuss the mechanism of colonic adaptation, whereby gradual lactose exposure selectively enriches Bifidobacterium populations capable of fermenting lactose without excessive gas production resulting in greater lactose tolerance. Finally, we examine the public health implications of these findings, noting that food-based dietary guidelines worldwide-including those in populations with high prevalence of lactase non-persistence-consistently recommend dairy consumption owing to its established associations with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes, colorectal cancer, and improved bone health.

▶GLP-1 RA Prescribing in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes and Cancer.

💡 Conclusion
In this retrospective study of Medicare beneficiaries with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1 RA use increased substantially and primarily was associated with metabolic risk profile, with limited associations with cancer status. Variation across cancer types and stages suggests oncologic context may influence prescribing.
Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are increasingly used to treat type 2 diabetes, yet little is known about its utilization patterns in patients with cancer history, given limited oncology-specific guidance.

To evaluate trends and factors associated with GLP-1 RA use among Medicare beneficiaries with type 2 diabetes, comparing those with and without cancer history.

This retrospective cohort study used Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results-Medicare linked data to identify patients diagnosed with 7 common cancers in 2010 to 2021 and preexisting type 2 diabetes before cancer diagnosis. A noncancer cohort with type 2 diabetes was identified from a 5% random sample of Medicare beneficiaries and matched 1:1 to patients with cancer based on sociodemographic characteristics; controls were assigned a pseudo-index date corresponding to their matched patient's cancer diagnosis date. Beneficiaries were followed up annually until death or December 31, 2022, for TM or December 31, 2021, for MA. Data were analyzed from December 2025 to July 2026.

Diagnosis of 1 of 7 common cancers.

Any GLP-1 RA use. Proportions of beneficiaries with any GLP-1 RA use annually were stratified by traditional Medicare (TM) and Medicare Advantage (MA). Multivariable logistic regression examined (1) differences in GLP-1RA use between cancer and noncancer cohorts with and without adjusting for sociodemographic characteristics and comorbidities and diabetes-related complications; (2) cancer-specific factors associated with GLP-1RA use among the cancer cohort after 2018.

A total of 373 278 Medicare beneficiaries (mean [SD] age, 76.1 [6.6] years; 188 746 [50.6%] female) with cancer and diabetes and 373 278 noncancer controls (153 358 unique beneficiaries) were included. GLP-1 RA use increased from 85 beneficiaries (0.5%) to 9083 beneficiaries (8.2%) and from 138 beneficiaries (0.9%) to 9300 beneficiaries (6.2%) in the cancer and noncancer cohorts, respectively, from 2010 to 2022 under TM; a similar increase was observed under MA. Despite higher unadjusted GLP-1 RA use among the cancer cohort than the noncancer cohort (probability difference, TM: 1.19 [95% CI, 0.94 to 1.44] percentage points [pp]; P < .001; MA: 0.45 [95% CI, 0.21 to 0.68] pp, P < .001), adjusted analysis showed no significant differences in GLP-1 RA use under TM (adjusted probability difference, 0.16 [95% CI, -0.10 to 0.41,] pp; P = .22) and lower use among cancer cohort under MA (-0.52 [95% CI, -0.76 to -0.28] pp; P < .001). Complications or comorbidities, including obesity (adjusted probability difference, TM: 3.35 [95% CI, 3.10 to 3.61] pp; P < .001; MA: 2.42 [95% CI, 2.19 to 2.64] pp; P < .001), dyslipidemia (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.26 [95% CI, 2.03 to 2.50] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.80 [95% CI, 1.57 to 2.03] pp; P < .001), neuropathy (adjusted probability difference: TM: 3.91 [95% CI, 3.64 to 4.19] pp; P < .001; MA: 3.17 [95% CI, 2.94 to 3.41] pp; P < .001), retinopathy (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.02 [95% CI, 1.78 to 2.27] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.84 [95% CI, 1.63 to 2.06] pp; P < .001), and chronic kidney disease (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.01 [95% CI, 1.76 to 2.26] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.63 [95% CI, 1.40 to 1.85] pp; P < .001) were associated with greater likelihood of GLP-1RA use. Within the cancer cohort, breast (adjusted probability difference, TM: 1.05 [95% CI, 0.43 to 1.66] pp; P = .001; MA: 0.89 [95% CI, 0.37 to 1.41] pp; P = .001), gynecologic (adjusted probability difference, MA: 1.13 [95% CI, 0.45 to 1.81] pp; P = .001), and prostate (adjusted probability difference, TM: 1.30 [95% CI, 0.72 to 1.88] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.07 [95% CI, 0.56 to 1.58] pp; P < .001) cancers were associated with higher GLP-1 RA use than colorectal cancer, while pancreatic cancer was associated with lower GLP-1 RA use (adjusted probability difference, TM: -2.55 [95% CI, -3.17 to -1.93] pp; P < .001; MA: -1.64 [95% CI, -2.23 to -1.05] pp; P < .001).

In this retrospective study of Medicare beneficiaries with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1 RA use increased substantially and primarily was associated with metabolic risk profile, with limited associations with cancer status. Variation across cancer types and stages suggests oncologic context may influence prescribing.

This narrative review critically appraises the epidemiological, experimental, and interventional evidence proposed to link chronic intermittent hypoxia (CIH) and sleep fragmentation to hepatic steatosis, steatohepatitis, and fibrosis. We summarize the candidate mechanisms, including mitochondrial oxidative stress, hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha signaling, sympathetically driven lipolysis, and Kupffer and stellate cell activation and then examine the limits of the evidence supporting them: hypoxia-induced hypophagia and the scarcity of pair-fed controls in rodent models, species differences in the physiological response to hypoxia, publication bias, common-cause confounding by age and systemic inflammation, and the rarity of progressive liver disease in lean patients with anatomically driven OSA. Thyroid eye disease (TED), the principal extrathyroidal manifestation of Graves' disease, is an autoimmune orbitopathy. The pathophysiology involves aberrant thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor (TSHR) and insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R) signaling on orbital fibroblasts, leading to glycosaminoglycan accumulation, adipogenesis, inflammatory edema, and extraocular-muscle enlargement. The active phase is typically self-limited yet medically modifiable, characterized primarily by inflammation, edema, and tissue expansion, whereas chronic inactive disease is dominated by fibrosis and fixed structural change. This US-oriented review aims to provide updates in the field through 2026: (1) summarizing the molecular pathophysiology linking thyroid autoimmunity to orbital remodeling and current drug targets; (2) discussing activity-versus-severity assessment, with appraisal of the Clinical Activity Score (CAS); and (3) explaining the complementary roles of different structural and functional imaging modalities in the evaluation and management of TED. We then follow the therapeutic ladder, from conservative measures through the employment of glucocorticoids and steroid-sparing immunosuppression to the use of targeted biologics. We give particular attention to IGF-1R inhibition, including the use of teprotumumab and veligrotug, and to post-approval results, including otologic and metabolic adverse-event burden, recurrence, treatment sequencing, and the emerging efficacy of the treatment of chronic and low-activity disease. The CAS's limited inter-observer reliability identifies additional reproducible activity measures as an open need.

Acute pancreatitis (AP) is an acute inflammatory disorder of the pancreas that disrupts its normal function and often requires hospitalization because of severe abdominal pain. While most patients recover without major complications, some develop pancreatic necrosis, infected pancreatic necrosis (IPN), persistent organ failure, and other severe complications associated with increased morbidity and mortality. A key clinical challenge is distinguishing infected pancreatic necrosis from sterile pancreatic inflammation, as delayed or inaccurate diagnosis may result in inappropriate antibiotic use and poorer clinical outcomes. This review aimed to evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic utility of procalcitonin (PCT) in differentiating infected pancreatic necrosis from sterile inflammation and to assess its role in supporting antimicrobial decision-making in patients with AP. A structured narrative literature review was conducted using PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science to identify English-language human studies, meta-analyses, randomized controlled trials, and international guidelines published between January 2000 and December 2025. A total of 60 studies were included in the narrative synthesis. The reviewed evidence indicates that PCT may provide greater diagnostic specificity for bacterial infection than conventional inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and leukocyte count in selected clinical settings. Elevated or persistently increasing PCT concentrations have been associated with IPN, sepsis, organ failure, and increased mortality. Furthermore, PCT-guided antibiotic stewardship strategies may reduce unnecessary antibiotic exposure without adversely affecting clinical outcomes in selected settings. Overall, PCT represents a potentially valuable adjunctive biomarker for differentiating IPN from sterile inflammation in patients with AP. When interpreted alongside clinical assessment and imaging findings, PCT may facilitate the assessment of infection risk, prognostic stratification, and antimicrobial stewardship. However, further large-scale prospective multicenter studies are warranted to establish standardized diagnostic thresholds and optimize its integration into routine clinical practice.

Metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) has historically been managed with systemic therapy, and local therapies have, in general, been avoided in the absence of symptoms directly related to a specific metastasis. Even if a specific site of disease, such as a local recurrence, had the potential to cause symptoms in the future, there was a bias against prophylactic palliation. It was well-established that most patients die of systemic progression. This approach is now under pressure from four directions. Retrospective case series have reported survival durations longer than anticipated in highly selected patients with low-volume, single-organ disease. A randomized phase II trial reported that metastasis-directed therapy prolonged progression-free survival. Biologic data indicate that the site of metastatic relapse is encoded in gene expression profiles already present in the primary tumor. Second-line systemic therapy has become substantially more effective, which lengthens the interval over which control of an untreated metastasis may become clinically important. We trace the oligometastatic concept from the 1995 Hellman and Weichselbaum hypothesis to the 2026 OligoPanc consensus definition of up to three lesions confined to a single organ, examine the criteria proposed for patient selection, summarize the surgical and ablative radiotherapy literature, and consider the trials that will determine whether this is a treatable disease state or a favorable prognostic phenotype. We conclude that induction systemic therapy, followed by response assessment that weighs the absence of disease progression, Carbohydrate Antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) kinetics, and performance status, is a defensible route to local therapy directed at the metastases and the primary tumor, and warrants prospective study.

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are increasingly used to treat type 2 diabetes, yet little is known about its utilization patterns in patients with cancer history, given limited oncology-specific guidance.

To evaluate trends and factors associated with GLP-1 RA use among Medicare beneficiaries with type 2 diabetes, comparing those with and without cancer history.

This retrospective cohort study used Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results-Medicare linked data to identify patients diagnosed with 7 common cancers in 2010 to 2021 and preexisting type 2 diabetes before cancer diagnosis. A noncancer cohort with type 2 diabetes was identified from a 5% random sample of Medicare beneficiaries and matched 1:1 to patients with cancer based on sociodemographic characteristics; controls were assigned a pseudo-index date corresponding to their matched patient's cancer diagnosis date. Beneficiaries were followed up annually until death or December 31, 2022, for TM or December 31, 2021, for MA. Data were analyzed from December 2025 to July 2026.

Diagnosis of 1 of 7 common cancers.

Any GLP-1 RA use. Proportions of beneficiaries with any GLP-1 RA use annually were stratified by traditional Medicare (TM) and Medicare Advantage (MA). Multivariable logistic regression examined (1) differences in GLP-1RA use between cancer and noncancer cohorts with and without adjusting for sociodemographic characteristics and comorbidities and diabetes-related complications; (2) cancer-specific factors associated with GLP-1RA use among the cancer cohort after 2018.

A total of 373 278 Medicare beneficiaries (mean [SD] age, 76.1 [6.6] years; 188 746 [50.6%] female) with cancer and diabetes and 373 278 noncancer controls (153 358 unique beneficiaries) were included. GLP-1 RA use increased from 85 beneficiaries (0.5%) to 9083 beneficiaries (8.2%) and from 138 beneficiaries (0.9%) to 9300 beneficiaries (6.2%) in the cancer and noncancer cohorts, respectively, from 2010 to 2022 under TM; a similar increase was observed under MA. Despite higher unadjusted GLP-1 RA use among the cancer cohort than the noncancer cohort (probability difference, TM: 1.19 [95% CI, 0.94 to 1.44] percentage points [pp]; P < .001; MA: 0.45 [95% CI, 0.21 to 0.68] pp, P < .001), adjusted analysis showed no significant differences in GLP-1 RA use under TM (adjusted probability difference, 0.16 [95% CI, -0.10 to 0.41,] pp; P = .22) and lower use among cancer cohort under MA (-0.52 [95% CI, -0.76 to -0.28] pp; P < .001). Complications or comorbidities, including obesity (adjusted probability difference, TM: 3.35 [95% CI, 3.10 to 3.61] pp; P < .001; MA: 2.42 [95% CI, 2.19 to 2.64] pp; P < .001), dyslipidemia (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.26 [95% CI, 2.03 to 2.50] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.80 [95% CI, 1.57 to 2.03] pp; P < .001), neuropathy (adjusted probability difference: TM: 3.91 [95% CI, 3.64 to 4.19] pp; P < .001; MA: 3.17 [95% CI, 2.94 to 3.41] pp; P < .001), retinopathy (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.02 [95% CI, 1.78 to 2.27] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.84 [95% CI, 1.63 to 2.06] pp; P < .001), and chronic kidney disease (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.01 [95% CI, 1.76 to 2.26] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.63 [95% CI, 1.40 to 1.85] pp; P < .001) were associated with greater likelihood of GLP-1RA use.
