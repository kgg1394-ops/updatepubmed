▶A randomized controlled double-blinded trial to assess the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus in non-anemic pregnant women who receive oral iron supplementation.

📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
CTRI/2024/04/065301 [registered on 05/04/2024]-trial registered prospectively.
Pregnant women in India are routinely prescribed oral iron supplements as part of the national Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB) program for anemia control. In women with iron-deficiency anemia, the benefits of iron supplementation during pregnancy, both for the mother and child, are very clear. In non-anemic pregnant women, however, the benefits of iron supplementation vis-à-vis associated risks that have been reported have not been studied adequately. Studies have suggested that iron may impair insulin secretion, thus increasing the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM).

To determine the incidence of GDM among non-anemic pregnant women receiving 60 mg/day of elemental iron as oral supplementation, compared with those receiving 30 mg/day.

To assess beta-cell functional capacity and quantify unmethylated insulin promoter DNA (a sensitive and specific marker of beta-cell damage) in blood samples from the two study groups.

This study is a randomized, parallel-group, double-blinded clinical trial, with 800 non-anemic primigravidae in each arm. One arm will receive oral supplementation at a dose of 60 mg per day (as per current national guidelines), while the other arm will receive 30 mg per day, for a duration of 12 weeks in the second trimester. The incidence of GDM in the two arms will be compared. Insulin secretion kinetics will be assessed using an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) done at the end of the intervention. Additionally, the unmethylated insulin promoter DNA in circulating free DNA (a marker of beta-cell damage) will be quantified in both arms.

The study's findings will provide important evidence relevant to the current national guidelines in India on universal oral iron supplementation during pregnancy. The results are expected to inform future policy decisions on whether universal supplementation for all pregnant women is appropriate or whether it should be targeted to those with low iron status.

Simple pre-elastography tools may help prioritize adults for liver stiffness assessment. We evaluated the Simple Clinical Objective Predictor for fibrosis Evaluation (SCOPE) in two National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) cycles.

We conducted a retrospective secondary analysis of NHANES 2017 to March 2020 as the development cohort and NHANES 2021-2023 as the temporal validation cohort. SCOPE combines age, diabetes status, gamma-glutamyl transferase, and weight-to-height ratio. Adults aged ≥20 yr with valid liver stiffness measurements and complete predictor data were included after excluding major competing chronic liver disease etiologies. Elevated liver stiffness was defined as liver stiffness measurement (LSM) ≥9.7 kPa. Discrimination was assessed using survey-weighted area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. Two evaluation-cohort cutpoints approximating 90% sensitivity and 90% specificity were applied unchanged in temporal validation. Decision utility was assessed using survey-weighted decision-curve analysis, with secondary probability calibration.

The analytic sample included 5488 adults in development and 3994 in temporal validation; weighted prevalence was 5.31% and 6.11%, respectively. SCOPE showed higher discrimination than Fibrosis-4, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease Fibrosis Score, and steatosis-associated fibrosis estimator in both cohorts. SCOPE area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was 0.866 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.837-0.899) in development and 0.823 (95% CI, 0.790-0.859) in temporal validation. Sensitivity at the lower boundary decreased from 89.8% to 80.6%, whereas upper-boundary specificity remained near 90%; temporal validation positive predictive value was 27.5%. Fixed SCOPE referral strategies provided greater net benefit than comparator strategies across prespecified threshold probability ranges. The temporal validation Brier score was 0.0492, with calibration intercept 0.242 and slope 0.893.

SCOPE showed higher discrimination than comparator scores for identifying adults with LSM ≥9.7 kPa who may warrant confirmatory assessment. Its modest positive predictive value precludes diagnostic use. Neither threshold is diagnostic, and the lower boundary should not be used to withhold guideline-indicated testing without external validation.

The phase III CheckMate 8HW trial met its dual primary endpoints of superior progression-free survival (PFS) with nivolumab plus ipilimumab (NIVO+IPI) versus chemotherapy (first-line [1L]) and versus NIVO (all lines) in patients with centrally confirmed microsatellite instability-high/mismatch repair deficient (MSI-H/dMMR) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). We report prespecified final overall survival (OS) analyses and updated efficacy outcomes.

Immunotherapy-naïve adults with mCRC across different lines and MSI-H/dMMR by local testing were randomized 2:2:1 to NIVO (240 mg) plus IPI (1 mg/kg) Q3W for four doses, then NIVO 480 mg Q4W; NIVO (240 mg) Q2W (six doses), then 480 mg Q4W; or chemotherapy ± targeted therapies. Secondary endpoints include PFS in NIVO+IPI versus NIVO (1L), and OS and objective response rate (ORR) in the centrally confirmed MSI-H/dMMR population.

839 patients across all lines were randomized; efficacy analyses were conducted in those with centrally confirmed MSI-H/dMMR. Median follow-up across all lines and treatment groups was 68.0 months (range 37.6-81.4). NIVO+IPI demonstrated significant OS benefit versus NIVO across all lines (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.46-0.82; P=0.0009) and versus chemotherapy in 1L (HR 0.37; 95% CI 0.24-0.57; P<0.0001]). Clinically meaningful OS benefit was noted with 1L NIVO+IPI versus NIVO (HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.44-0.99; P=0.0432), not meeting the prespecified threshold for significance (P<0.006). PFS continued to favor NIVO+IPI across all three comparisons: NIVO (all lines; HR 0.60), chemotherapy (1L; HR 0.21), and NIVO (1L: HR: 0.68). Grade 3/4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 78 (22%) versus 50 (14%) patients treated with NIVO+IPI versus NIVO across all lines, with no new safety concerns or additional treatment-related deaths from the previous follow-up.

Background Flexible laryngoscopy is routinely performed by otolaryngologists for unexplained chronic cough. The objectives were twofold: first, to ascertain institutional outcomes and, second, to understand community provider practice patterns to further inform its role in evaluation. Methodology This retrospective case series and cross-sectional survey was conducted at a single tertiary-care academic institution (2018-2024) and a regional otolaryngology continuing medical education conference (February 2025). For the retrospective case series, the study included adult patients seen at the University of Arizona's tertiary laryngology center for unexplained chronic cough from 2018 to 2024. Patients met the inclusion criteria if cough persisted for >8 weeks and they either had not responded to prior proton pump inhibitor (PPI) and asthma therapy or had gastroesophageal reflux disease and asthma ruled out by specialists. The senior author routinely performed flexible laryngoscopy for all patients presenting with unexplained chronic cough. Normal and abnormal laryngeal findings in this retrospective study simply referred to the presence or absence of a mass, ulceration, or paralysis. For the cross-sectional survey, optional anonymous paper surveys were distributed to otolaryngology providers attending a regional otolaryngology continuing medical education conference in Tucson, Arizona, in February 2025. Results In our retrospective case series, 53 of 56 (94.6%) patients had normal laryngoscopic exams in their initial evaluation for unexplained chronic cough. Three (5.4%) patients had abnormalities on laryngoscopy, which included paradoxical vocal fold movement (3.6%, N = 2) and known vocal fold paralysis (1.8%, N = 1). Within our cross-sectional survey of 55 providers, 43 (78.2%) reported frequently performing laryngoscopy. Half of providers (49.1%, N = 27) indicated that findings rarely changed treatment. Common laryngoscopy findings included arytenoid erythema/edema (54.5%, N = 30) or normal exams (41.8%, N = 23). PPIs were the most frequent first-line therapy initiated (69.1%, N = 38). Conclusions In this single-institution small case series, laryngoscopy findings in unexplained chronic cough were predominantly normal or benign, with no malignancies identified. These findings suggest that patients with unexplained chronic cough that significant laryngeal pathology may be uncommon, while still supporting the importance of direct visualization before diagnosing unexplained cough. In this limited cross-sectional survey, flexible laryngoscopy was not universally adopted among community providers, highlighting variability in the evaluation for chronic cough. Empiric PPI therapy remains frequently prescribed despite guideline recommendations favoring neuromodulators and cough suppression therapy.

Telisotuzumab adizutecan (ABBV-400, Temab-A) is an antibody-drug conjugate targeting c-Met (MET) protein with adizutecan, a novel topoisomerase 1 inhibitor payload. In monotherapy dose escalation, Temab-A demonstrated a manageable safety profile and promising antitumor activity in advanced colorectal cancer (CRC; NCT05029882). ▶Robot-assisted radical surgery for colorectal cancer: technical advances, lymphadenectomy, natural orifice specimen extraction, and ongoing controversies.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: ✅ 우선 적용 고려
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Robot-assisted surgery expands the technical options for colorectal cancer resection, but its clinical value depends on the operative setting and the outcome assessed. This narrative review examines lymphadenectomy and natural orifice specimen extraction surgery (NOSES), distinguishing colon from rectal cancer and technical feasibility from demonstrated clinical benefit. Literature published through 1 July 2026 was considered. Randomized evidence in rectal cancer is heterogeneous: ROLARR and COLRAR did not demonstrate superiority for their respective primary outcomes, whereas REAL reported better selected perioperative outcomes and lower 3-year locoregional recurrence after robotic surgery. These findings do not establish an overall survival advantage or a benefit mediated specifically by more extensive lymphadenectomy. Evidence for robotic colectomy remains less definitive. Lymph node yield, dissection extent, vascular ligation and specimen quality should be evaluated separately, with attention to geographical differences in practice. Robotic NOSES may reduce pain and wound morbidity in selected patients; a recent randomized trial supports short-term benefits, while long-term oncological equivalence remains unproven. Selection bias, limited external validity, cost and variable surgeon experience constrain broader conclusions. Adoption should therefore be guided by procedure-specific evidence, appropriate oncological resection and patient priorities rather than technical capability alone.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Multidisciplinary prehabilitation did not reduce postoperative complications after CRC surgery. However, exploratory analyses suggest that patients with greater preoperative disability derive greater benefit, supporting targeted prehabilitation strategies in higher-risk populations. Registration number: NCT04909567 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/).
Colorectal cancer (CRC) surgery remains associated with substantial postoperative morbidity despite implementation of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS). Prehabilitation improves surgical outcomes, but evidence remains inconsistent. This study evaluated whether multidisciplinary prehabilitation reduces postoperative complications and whether treatment effects vary according to baseline physical function.

This single-centre randomized clinical trial was conducted at a tertiary referral hospital in Stockholm, Sweden (28 October 2021-23 April 2025). Adults with histologically confirmed CRC scheduled for elective resection were randomized 1 : 1 to multidisciplinary prehabilitation (medication review, nutritional counselling, supervised exercise, and targeted risk optimization) or standard care (ERAS). Analyses followed a modified intention-to-treat (mITT) principle. Prehabilitation was initiated within 3 days of enrolment. The primary outcome of interest was postoperative complications (Clavien-Dindo grade ≥ I). Secondary outcomes included severe complications (Comprehensive Complication Index), reoperations, length of hospital stay (LOS), postoperative weight loss, physical performance and function (6-minute walk test, Timed Up and Go test, and 30-second chair stand test), health-related quality of life (EORTC QLQ-C30), mental health (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale), and correlation with the Disability Rating Index (DRI) score.

Among 328 randomized patients, 242 underwent CRC surgery and were included in the mITT analysis (120 control, 122 intervention). Postoperative complications occurred in 46.2% of patients, with no significant difference between the intervention and control groups (47.5% versus 45.0%, respectively). Severe complications occurred in 19.0% of patients and were numerically less frequent in the intervention group, but the difference was not statistically significant. LOS and reoperation rates were similar. The intervention group had lower postoperative weight loss and modest improvements in physical performance and patient-reported physical function at 30 days. In exploratory analyses, a significant interaction was observed between treatment allocation and baseline DRI score, indicating greater reduction in severe complications among patients with higher levels of preoperative disability (P = 0.011).

Multidisciplinary prehabilitation did not reduce postoperative complications after CRC surgery. However, exploratory analyses suggest that patients with greater preoperative disability derive greater benefit, supporting targeted prehabilitation strategies in higher-risk populations. Registration number: NCT04909567 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/).

▶14-day versus 28-day trifluridine-tipiracil plus bevacizumab for refractory metastatic colorectal cancer (PRABITAS): a pragmatic, multicentre, open-label, randomised, phase 3, non-inferiority trial in Japan.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Japan Society for the Promotion of Science KAKENHI Grant-in-Aid for Challenging Research (Pioneering).
Trifluridine-tipiracil (FTD-TPI) plus bevacizumab is the standard treatment for refractory metastatic colorectal cancer. However, the conventional schedule is frequently associated with haematological and gastrointestinal toxicity, which is clinically significant and relevant to patients. A simplified 14-day schedule has been proposed to reduce toxicity, but whether this treatment preserves survival has not been established in a randomised trial. We therefore aimed to assess whether the 14-day schedule is non-inferior to the conventional schedule for overall survival.

PRABITAS was a pragmatic, multicentre, open-label, randomised, phase 3, non-inferiority trial conducted in 178 hospitals in Japan. Patients with refractory metastatic colorectal cancer were randomly assigned (1:1) using a minimisation algorithm, stratified by Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, RAS mutational status, and institution, to FTD-TPI (35 mg/m

Between Dec 26, 2023, and May 20, 2025, we randomly assigned 972 patients, of whom 970 (99%) constituted the intention-to-treat population after two were excluded because they were randomly assigned before site approval (486 [50%] to the 28-day treatment group, 292 [60%] men and 194 [40%] women]; and 484 [50%] to the 14-day treatment group, 264 [55%] men and 220 [45%] women). Median follow-up was 17·9 months (IQR 14·3-22·6). Median overall survival was 10·3 months (95% CI 9·5-11·6) in the 28-day group and 11·3 months (10·2-12·5) in the 14-day group (stratified hazard ratio 0·93 [95% CI 0·80-1·08]). The 12-month overall survival rates were 44·0% (95% CI 39·5-48·5) in the 28-day group and 47·5% (42·9-52·0) in the 14-day group. Neutropenia grade 3 or worse was reduced in the 14-day group (13% vs 39%; absolute difference 25 percentage points [95% CI 20-31]), as was anorexia grade 2 or worse (15% vs 26%; absolute difference 11 percentage points [6-16]). Two treatment-related deaths occurred in the 14-day group and none in the 28-day group.

A 14-day schedule of FTD-TPI plus bevacizumab was non-inferior to the conventional 28-day schedule for overall survival, while reducing haematological and gastrointestinal toxicity. These findings support this simplified schedule as the new standard for FTD-TPI plus bevacizumab in refractory metastatic colorectal cancer.
