Chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection is a leading cause of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma, affecting an estimated 50-57 million people worldwide. Although direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) have revolutionized HCV therapy, achieving >90-95% cure rates, hepatitis B virus (HBV)/HCV coinfection is still present in 1-15% of patients, raising HCC risk. This meta-analysis aims to estimate the pooled incidence of HBV reactivation in adults with chronic HCV infection treated with DAAs and to identify risk factors associated with reactivation. A comprehensive and systematic literature search was conducted across major databases including PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science, according to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. Eight randomized controlled trials and 19 observational cohort studies were included, which evaluated adult patients with chronic hepatitis C infection who were seropositive for hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and received DAA therapy. The analysis of 27 studies encompassing 1707 HBsAg-positive patients revealed a substantial risk of virological HBV reactivation, with a pooled prevalence of 0.29 [95% confidence interval (CI) = 0.15-0.46; I2 = 97%]. The pooled prevalence of clinical reactivation was 0.04 (95% CI = 0.02-0.07; I2 = 67%), which was also lower in the prophylaxis subgroup (0.03 vs. 0.07). The prevalence of alanine aminotransferase flare and HBV-associated hepatitis was 0.04 (95% CI = 0.02-0.06; I2 = 67%) for both, while the pooled prevalence of HBV DNA reappearance was 0.33 (95% CI = 0.18-0.50; I2 = 94%). HBV-related death was negligible (0.00, 95% CI = 0.00-0.01; I2 = 0%). Significant heterogeneity and publication bias were observed across most outcomes. HBV reactivation remains a clinically relevant problem in chronically HCV-infected patients treated with DAAs, particularly in HBsAg-positive patients or those not receiving HBV prophylaxis. Validated prognostic tools for people living with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) are needed. We evaluated the role of LSM in predicting disease-relevant outcomes in this large, retrospective, multi-centre study.

The association between LSM and the composite outcome (decompensation, transplant, death) was assessed using baseline and time-dependent Cox proportional hazards (PH) models. Time-dependent models included log-transformed LSM (logLSM) or categorical LSM as per Baveno VIII, adjusted for age, sex, and biochemistry. Dynamic LSM measures (slope, % change, average annual % change [AAPC]) were assessed. Final models derived from the Toronto cohort were externally validated in 3 cohorts: the Consortium for Autoimmune Liver Disease (CALiD; U.S.), Edmonton (Canada), and Hamburg (Germany). Each site also independently derived models using identical variables, with pooled adjusted HR estimated with random-effects meta-analysis.

A total of 4,549 LSMs from 1,330 patients with 289 events were included (6165.7 person-years from index LSM to event/censoring). In all models, LSM was predictive of the outcome: logLSM was independently associated with the outcome in both derivation and pooled analyses (pooled aHR 3.64, 95%CI 3.05-4.35). An LSM of 10-15 kPa was variably associated with the outcome (pooled aHR 2.42, 95%CI 1.53-3.82); LSM >15 kPa was consistently prognostic (pooled aHR 8.72, 95%CI 5.05-15.08). External validation of the Toronto models demonstrated excellent discrimination and good to excellent calibration. Dynamic LSM measures (slope, % change, or AAPC) were associated with the outcome when considered alone but did not remain predictive when combined with most recent LSM.

We robustly validate liver stiffness as predictive of clinically significant outcomes in PSC, supporting its application in routine care and as part of clinical trial design.

💡 Conclusion
Diet is a key therapeutic target in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet responses vary widely between patients. We distinguish conventional dietary therapy, applied by clinical indication; stratified nutrition, selected on predefined clinical or biological features such as phenotype or disease location; and precision nutrition, which uses individual-level clinical, biological, microbial, metabolic and digital data to predict response and adapt dietary interventions. PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase and Scopus were searched from 2010 to June 2026, combining IBD terms with dietary, microbiome, multi-omics, artificial intelligence (AI) and digital health terms; only English-language articles were included. Priority was given to randomized trials, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, guidelines, and landmark studies, defined as those first establishing a therapeutic principle or biological association subsequently confirmed, appraised for design, sample size, risk of bias and consistency with the literature. Exclusive enteral nutrition and the Crohn's disease exclusion diet have the strongest randomized support for inducing remission in Crohn's disease; the Mediterranean diet is best supported for maintenance, and the low-FODMAP diet improves persistent gut symptoms without changing inflammatory markers. Preliminary, uncontrolled studies suggest that combining diet with advanced therapies may improve selected outcomes, but synergy has not been demonstrated. Precision nutrition is a promising paradigm for personalized IBD management, but IBD-specific prospective trials, validated multimodal biomarkers and multidisciplinary implementation are required before routine use.

Colonoscopy is widely performed for colorectal cancer screening, yet evidence guiding the choice between pediatric and adult colonoscopes in adult patients remains inconsistent. Pediatric colonoscopes may offer advantages in maneuverability and comfort, but their comparative effectiveness has not been systematically established.

We searched Cochrane, PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science through 20 November 2025. We included randomized controlled trials and comparative observational studies comparing pediatric and adult colonoscopes in adult patients undergoing colonoscopy. Risk ratios (RR) or mean differences with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Pooled analyses were conducted using random-effects models. The certainty of evidence was evaluated.

Twelve studies comprising 9385 patients were included. No significant differences were observed between pediatric and adult colonoscopes for cecal intubation rate (RR = 0.99, 95% CI: 0.98-1.00; P = 0.065), terminal ileum intubation (RR = 0.97, 95% CI: 0.88-1.08), cecal intubation duration (mean difference = -0.12 min, 95% CI: -0.84 to 0.60), or adverse events (RR = 1.6, 95% CI: 0.37-7.55). Adult colonoscopes were associated with higher pain scores (mean difference = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.04-1.10; P = 0.037). Most outcomes demonstrated low or very low certainty evidence, primarily because of risk of bias and imprecision.

Pediatric and adult colonoscopes demonstrate comparable procedural success, efficiency, and safety in routine adult colonoscopy. The lower pain scores associated with pediatric colonoscopes may offer a patient-centered advantage, though the overall certainty of evidence is limited.

💡 Conclusion
Gastrointestinal immune-related adverse events (GI-irAEs) are among the most consequential toxicities of immune checkpoint inhibitors. Their spectrum extends from histological inflammation beneath normal-appearing mucosa to fulminant enterocolitis. Management is difficult because stool-frequency grade incompletely represents mucosal injury, several infectious and non-immune disorders mimic immune-mediated disease, and treatment sequencing rests largely on guidelines and observational evidence. This Review integrates current guidance, including the transition to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 6.0, with endoscopic, histological, microbiome, single-cell and spatial evidence. We frame GI-irAEs as multidimensional mucosal syndromes defined by anatomical site, inflammatory phenotype, physiological or structural risk, and treatment trajectory. The framework informs care but is neither a validated score nor a guideline. Budesonide may be considered in selected stable patients with biopsy-defined microscopic-colitis-like disease, based on small, non-randomised evidence. By contrast, macroscopic ulceration or physiological compromise warrants early systemic treatment and planning for selective immunosuppression. Upper-GI, small-bowel and chronic active/IBD-like phenotypes require site-specific investigation and follow-up. Infliximab and vedolizumab remain the best-supported selective immunosuppressive options, although non-randomised comparisons establish no universal preference. Concurrent selective immunosuppression during rechallenge after biologic-requiring enterocolitis is an emerging retrospective strategy; faecal microbiota transplantation, ustekinumab and Janus-kinase inhibitors remain experimental rescue approaches. The clinical priority is to recognise high-risk mucosal disease early, limit ineffective corticosteroid exposure, document recovery and treat rechallenge as a renewed multidisciplinary oncological benefit-risk decision.

Immunocytokines are antibody–cytokine fusion proteins designed to trigger immune responses at tumor sites. SAR445710 (KD033) is a novel fusion molecule engineered for intravenous administration; it is a combination of a fully human, high-affinity antihuman programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) immunoglobulin G1 antibody and the sushi domain of the human interleukin (IL)-15/IL-15 receptor alpha complex.
