Operating in acute surgical settings is significantly different from elective surgery. One aspect is the complexity of acutely ill or injured patients with abnormal physiology, unoptimized comorbidities, and unplanned interventions. Traditional visual and tactile assessments of the surgical field anatomy and tissue viability have documented limitations. The need for integrated technology to augment such assessments is described. Indocyanine green fluorescence-guided surgery (ICG-FGS) is a promising tool, given its history, safety, low cost, and accumulated experience in elective settings for many surgeries, to help transform precision surgery into acute settings (trauma and emergency surgery). An updated review is needed based on new evidence outlining the current landscape of ICG in acute settings.

We performed a comprehensive review of literature update in the last two decades through June 2026, covering PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, and published societal guidelines, including, but not limited to, the World Society of Emergency Surgery (WSES) consensus position paper and the Society of American Gastrointestinal and Endoscopic Surgeons (SAGES) review.

ICG is applicable across the full spectrum of acute surgical care, classified under two principal functions: anatomical mapping (biliary, ureteric, thoracic duct, and vascular structures) and perfusion assessment (bowel and mesenteric ischemia, anastomotic perfusion, pancreatic necrosis, burns, soft tissue, extremity trauma, and organ injury). Emerging and less commonly reported applications include necrotizing fasciitis debridement guidance, traumatic brain injury monitoring, penetrating abdominal trauma, gonadal torsion, fistula detection, damage control surgery, and AI-augmented quantitative fluorescence. The WSES consensus and SAGES meta-analysis provided strong prospective validation to date regarding anastomotic leakage reduction. Current gaps include the absence of standardized dosing protocols in acute settings, limited randomized data, and the subjectivity inherent to qualitative fluorescence interpretation.

ICG fluorescence-guided surgery is a safe, low-cost, and rapidly deployable adjunct for emergency surgery. Its breadth of applicability warrants wider consideration and integration into acute surgical workflows to inform surgical decision making based specific considerations including specific indications, evidence support, equipment availability, local expertise, workflow readiness and clinical judgement. ▶Difficult biliary cannulation during ERCP: techniques and an evidence-informed practical algorithm.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Difficult biliary cannulation remains the pivotal technical barrier in therapeutic endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and is a major modifiable driver of post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP). Contemporary practice has moved from prolonged standard attempts toward earlier recognition of difficult access, guidewire-based primary cannulation, targeted rescue techniques, and routine pancreatitis prophylaxis. This review summarizes current evidence and proposes a practical, evidence-informed algorithm for native papilla ERCP. Difficult cannulation is best recognized using objective procedural thresholds, including more than five papillary contacts, more than 5 min after papillary visualization, or more than one unintended pancreatic duct (PD) cannulation or opacification. Primary cannulation should use a wire-guided approach with careful control of papillary trauma and contrast injection. Once difficulty is declared, rescue technique selection should follow procedural anatomy and clinical context rather than a rigid sequence: repeated PD access favors double-guidewire (DGW), transpancreatic biliary sphincterotomy (TPBS), or PD stent-assisted approaches; a bulging or favorably oriented papilla favors early needle-knife fistulotomy (NKF); and failed access despite endoscopist expertise should prompt escalation rather than continued trauma. Rectal nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs should be administered before or during ERCP in patients without contraindications; prophylactic PD stenting should be used after repeated or deep PD access and according to the rescue technique and overall PEP risk, with appropriate hydration in suitable patients. Local postprocedural cryoprevention with papillary ice-water irrigation is an emerging adjunct supported by one multicenter randomized trial, although its incremental benefit alongside standard prophylaxis remains uncertain. When ERCP fails, repeat expert ERCP, endoscopic ultrasound-guided rendezvous (EUS-RV) or biliary drainage (EUS-BD), and percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) should be selected according to indication, urgency, anatomy, patient stability, and local expertise. The algorithm distinguishes guideline-supported decision nodes from areas in which comparative evidence is limited and sequencing represents a practical expert synthesis.

Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic, often progressive, type 2 inflammatory disease characterized by eosinophilic infiltration, and esophageal narrowing and dysfunction. This analysis presents 24-week outcomes from the phase 4 REMODEL trial, evaluating long-term esophageal distensibility in adults with EoE.

Adult EoE patients were randomized 2:1 to dupilumab 300 mg once weekly or placebo. Patients were assessed overall and by low (<17 mm) and normal (≥17 mm) baseline distensibility plateau (DP) subgroups. Endpoints at week (W) 24 included: absolute change from baseline in esophageal DP diameter on endoluminal functional lumen imaging probe (primary), and changes from baseline in Endoscopic Reference Scores (EREFS) and EoE histology scoring system (EoEHSS) grade/stage scores. Safety was assessed.

Sixty-nine patients (46 dupilumab, 23 placebo) were randomized. Evaluable patients in the DP subgroups were: 30 low (19 dupilumab, 11 placebo) and 33 normal (22 dupilumab, 11 placebo) baseline DP. At W24 overall, dupilumab improved esophageal DP diameter vs placebo; least squares mean (LSM) (±SE) absolute change from baseline: +1.28 mm (0.29) and -0.01 mm (0.42); LSM difference: +1.30 mm (95% CI: 0.31, 2.29; P < 0.05). LSM difference vs placebo in the low DP subgroup was +2.21 mm (95% CI: 0.78, 3.63; nominal P = 0.0024). Overall LSM difference for dupilumab vs placebo was -4.96 (-6.06, -3.85) in EREFS, and -0.71 (-0.86, -0.57) and -0.65 (-0.80, -0.51) in EoEHSS grade and stage scores, respectively (all P < 0.0001). No new safety signals were identified.

Dupilumab significantly improved W24 esophageal distensibility outcomes, with greater improvements in the low baseline DP subgroup compared to the overall population. (ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT06101095).

PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane databases.

Two-year prospective data support the clinical applicability of 3D-printed complete dentures. All fractures occurred in maxillary dentures with a thin palatal thickness setting, suggesting that attention to the denture design may help prevent fracture.
To evaluate 2-year cumulative success and survival rates, changes in oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL), and patient satisfaction among edentulous patients who continued to wear complete dentures fabricated by three-dimensional (3D) printing (CAD-3DPs) after a crossover randomized controlled trial (RCT).

Eighteen edentulous patients (36 dentures) wearing maxillary and mandibular CAD-3DPs after the RCT were prospectively followed. Events were denture fracture, repair, relining, or occlusal reconstruction (success rate) and discontinuation of use (survival rate). OHRQoL (total and seven subdomain scores) and patient satisfaction (visual analogue scale) were assessed at RCT conclusion (T0) and every 6 months up to 24 months (T4), and compared with T0.

Twenty-four dentures (12 patients) completed 2-year observation. In the success analysis, the only events were fractures of four maxillary dentures, all occurring within the first year; in the survival analysis, four dentures (two patients) were discontinued after maxillary fracture. Two-year cumulative success and survival rates were 88% and 87%, respectively. Palatal thickness of fractured dentures had been set at 2.0-2.1 mm, at the lower end of the design range (2.0-2.5 mm). No significant changes from T0 were detected in the OHRQoL total score, any of its seven subdomains, or patient satisfaction during follow-up.

CAD-3DPs showed high 2-year success and survival rates, with maintained OHRQoL and patient satisfaction, and can be an effective prosthetic treatment option for edentulous patients.

Two-year prospective data support the clinical applicability of 3D-printed complete dentures. All fractures occurred in maxillary dentures with a thin palatal thickness setting, suggesting that attention to the denture design may help prevent fracture.

The incidence of early-onset colorectal cancer continues to increase among adults younger than 50 years, yet delays in diagnosis remain common.

As an initial step of the Early-Onset Colorectal Cancer Awareness campaign, we evaluated U.S. healthcare providers' knowledge, attitudes, diagnostic practices, and perceived barriers related to the timely recognition and evaluation of early-onset colorectal cancer.

This survey established baseline measures for a national early-onset colorectal cancer awareness campaign.

We conducted a national cross-sectional survey of physicians, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants practicing family medicine, internal medicine, emergency medicine/urgent care, and obstetrics and gynecology.

The survey assessed early-onset colorectal cancer awareness, screening recommendations, evaluation of symptomatic younger adults, perceived barriers to diagnosis, and educational needs. Liver cirrhosis leads to portal hypertension and related complications, including ascites, esophageal varices, and hepatic encephalopathy (HE). Non-selective beta blockers (NSBBs) decrease portal pressure, but it is not yet clear whether they contribute to HE development. Therefore, we examined the association between NSBB therapy and HE in randomized controlled trials (RCTs) involving patients with cirrhosis.

We systematically searched PubMed, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, and the Cochrane Library (from inception to December 31, 2024) for RCTs comparing NSBBs with a placebo, endoscopic band ligation, or injection sclerotherapy for variceal bleeding prevention in relation to HE. The risk ratios (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were pooled using a random-effects model. Subgroups were defined according to the comparator, NSBB type, and hepatic function status (weighted mean Child-Pugh score ≤6 vs. >6). The risk of bias was assessed using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2).

There were 12 relevant studies, which included 775 NSBB-treated and 771 comparator patients (54 and 57 HE events). NSBB therapy was not associated with a statistically significant change in HE risk (RR 1.04, 95% CI 0.73-1.47; p = 0.84; I² = 0%). Estimates were consistent across all subgroups of hepatic function statuses (compensation, RR 1.18, 0.44-3.14; decompensation, RR 1.02, 0.70-1.48; p = 0.79), comparators (placebo, RR 1.19, 0.79-1.80; endoscopic therapy, RR 0.72, 0.38-1.39; p = 0.20), and NSBB types (propranolol/timolol, RR 1.06, 0.72-1.55; carvedilol, RR 0.95, 0.42-2.15; p = 0.82).

Current evidence does not indicate a statistically significant association between NSBB therapy and HE development in patients with cirrhosis, regardless of disease severity.

💡 Conclusion
This document provides resource-sensitive HCC guidelines that prioritise feasibility, maximise benefit, and reduce default reliance on best supportive care alone and are produced for the IHPBA by discussion with the global community of IHPBA.
Current guidelines for the management of patients with HCC are predominantly focused on well-resourced healthcare systems. In 2024, the IHPBA Legacy committee initiated a process to develop global resource-sensitive guidelines for the management of HCC.

An IHPBA global guideline group produced and circulated statements on the management of HCC from diagnosis to treatment, discussed these in online webinars and generated guidelines. This document focuses on the diagnosis and management of patients with HCC in resource-constrained healthcare settings.

Initial diagnosis by ultrasound is supported by cross-sectional imaging. Alfa-feto protein and assessment of liver synthetic function are recommended at diagnosis. Lesion biopsy is not mandated. Liver resection is the recommended treatment of choice for single HCC in patients with preserved liver function and up to 3 lesions ≤3 cm. Radiofrequency and microwave are regarded as equivalent and can be used if resection is not available. Ethanol injection can be used if thermal ablation is not available but should be restricted to small lesions. TACE and TAE can be used for intermediate stage disease. Atezolizumab/bevacizumab combination is recommended as first line systemic therapy with sorafenib and Lenvatinib being recommended if first line is not an option. Advice for the care of the patient with advanced or terminal disease is provided.

This document provides resource-sensitive HCC guidelines that prioritise feasibility, maximise benefit, and reduce default reliance on best supportive care alone and are produced for the IHPBA by discussion with the global community of IHPBA.

💡 Conclusion
Difficult biliary cannulation remains the pivotal technical barrier in therapeutic endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and is a major modifiable driver of post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP). Contemporary practice has moved from prolonged standard attempts toward earlier recognition of difficult access, guidewire-based primary cannulation, targeted rescue techniques, and routine pancreatitis prophylaxis. This review summarizes current evidence and proposes a practical, evidence-informed algorithm for native papilla ERCP. Difficult cannulation is best recognized using objective procedural thresholds, including more than five papillary contacts, more than 5 min after papillary visualization, or more than one unintended pancreatic duct (PD) cannulation or opacification. Primary cannulation should use a wire-guided approach with careful control of papillary trauma and contrast injection. Once difficulty is declared, rescue technique selection should follow procedural anatomy and clinical context rather than a rigid sequence: repeated PD access favors double-guidewire (DGW), transpancreatic biliary sphincterotomy (TPBS), or PD stent-assisted approaches; a bulging or favorably oriented papilla favors early needle-knife fistulotomy (NKF); and failed access despite endoscopist expertise should prompt escalation rather than continued trauma. Rectal nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs should be administered before or during ERCP in patients without contraindications; prophylactic PD stenting should be used after repeated or deep PD access and according to the rescue technique and overall PEP risk, with appropriate hydration in suitable patients. Local postprocedural cryoprevention with papillary ice-water irrigation is an emerging adjunct supported by one multicenter randomized trial, although its incremental benefit alongside standard prophylaxis remains uncertain. When ERCP fails, repeat expert ERCP, endoscopic ultrasound-guided rendezvous (EUS-RV) or biliary drainage (EUS-BD), and percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage (PTBD) should be selected according to indication, urgency, anatomy, patient stability, and local expertise. The algorithm distinguishes guideline-supported decision nodes from areas in which comparative evidence is limited and sequencing represents a practical expert synthesis.

Current guidelines for the management of patients with HCC are predominantly focused on well-resourced healthcare systems. In 2024, the IHPBA Legacy committee initiated a process to develop global resource-sensitive guidelines for the management of HCC.

An IHPBA global guideline group produced and circulated statements on the management of HCC from diagnosis to treatment, discussed these in online webinars and generated guidelines. This document focuses on the diagnosis and management of patients with HCC in resource-constrained healthcare settings.

Initial diagnosis by ultrasound is supported by cross-sectional imaging. Alfa-feto protein and assessment of liver synthetic function are recommended at diagnosis. Lesion biopsy is not mandated. Liver resection is the recommended treatment of choice for single HCC in patients with preserved liver function and up to 3 lesions ≤3 cm. Radiofrequency and microwave are regarded as equivalent and can be used if resection is not available. Ethanol injection can be used if thermal ablation is not available but should be restricted to small lesions. TACE and TAE can be used for intermediate stage disease. Atezolizumab/bevacizumab combination is recommended as first line systemic therapy with sorafenib and Lenvatinib being recommended if first line is not an option. Advice for the care of the patient with advanced or terminal disease is provided.

This document provides resource-sensitive HCC guidelines that prioritise feasibility, maximise benefit, and reduce default reliance on best supportive care alone and are produced for the IHPBA by discussion with the global community of IHPBA.

Claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2), a tight junction protein largely confined to normal gastric mucosa, is overexpressed and mislocalized on the surface of gastric, gastroesophageal, pancreatic, and other gastrointestinal cancers. This pattern has made CLDN18.2 a leading target in solid tumor immunotherapy. It is unclear whether recurrence-focused treatment (RFT) improves survival in all patients with recurrent pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). This study aimed (1) to evaluate post-recurrence survival (PRS) in patients treated with RFT versus best supportive care (BSC)-only, and (2) to assess potential effect modification by prognosis at recurrence.

Patients with unknown RFT or survival status were excluded. Prognosis at recurrence was defined using a previously established PRS risk score, stratifying patients into worse (≥174.5) and better (<174.5) prognostic subgroups. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed in the overall cohort, and within subgroups. Median PRS in RFT versus BSC-only patients was estimated and compared using Kaplan-Meier curve analysis and the log-rank test. Multivariable Cox regression with robust standard errors clustered by matched pair was used to adjust for residual imbalance after PSM, with results presented as hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (95%CI).

Interleukins 27 (IL-27), 33 (IL-33), 35 (IL-35) and 37 (IL-37) occupy potentially important but biologically non-equivalent positions at the interface between inflammatory amplification, tissue protection and immune restraint in acute pancreatitis (AP). This narrative review critically synthesizes AP-specific evidence, distinguishing mechanistic participation from biomarker association and defining translational priorities.

PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus and Google Scholar were searched through July 2026. The review was structured with reference to the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (SANRA). Of 215 records identified, 43 underwent full-text assessment and 35 were included in the narrative synthesis (32 original studies and 3 reviews). Primary AP studies were prioritized, with landmark mechanistic studies used to interpret cytokine biology.

IL-33/ST2 was the most extensively studied axis and showed phase-, compartment- and molecular-form-dependent effects. Soluble ST2 correlated with severity but has not been validated for incremental prediction.
