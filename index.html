▶Pragmatic application of ultrasound in the follow-up of intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm. Should we overcome MRI-only surveillance?

📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
US-based surveillance was associated with improved compliance, guideline adherence and cost-effectiveness, without detectable safety signals. An MRI-limited approach in well-visualized BD-IPMN <20 mm appears promising for long term surveillance.
Guidelines recommend MRI-based surveillance for BD-IPMNs, imposing a burden on healthcare systems. Abdominal ultrasound (US) reliably visualizes IPMNs and some worrisome features (WF). We aimed to evaluate the safety and potential benefits of integrating US into surveillance protocols.

Retrospective study of 644 patients with BD-IPMN, stratified by surveillance strategy: US-assisted (≥2 US during follow-up) versus standard surveillance, and MRI-limited (MRI only for significant changes on US) versus MRI-prevalent surveillance.

Both US-assisted and MRI-limited strategies showed no significant differences in clinical outcomes with reference groups (mortality, unresectable cancer, appropriate surgery; all p>0.09), lower loss-to-follow-up (12.0% vs 32.9%, p<0.01; 13.2% vs 28.4%, p<0.01), higher guideline adherence (80.3% vs 68.1%, p<0.01; 79.5% vs 70.8%, p=0.03), and lower costs (€478.3 vs €780.9; €272.7 vs €857.4, p<0.01). Multivariable logistic regression confirmed lower loss-to-follow-up after adjustment for confounders (adjusted OR 0.28 and 0.34, both p<0.01), while adjusted adherence remained significantly higher for US-assisted surveillance (OR 0.42, p<0.01). In a generalized linear model including IPMN <20 mm, MRI-limited surveillance independently reduced costs by 67% (adjusted cost ratio 0.33, 95% CI 0.26-0.42, p<0.01) regardless of baseline WF and cyst modification. In an exploratory analysis, even for cysts <20 mm with no baseline WF, risk of developing a new WF persisted beyond the 5-year threshold (conditional probability of 40% between years 5 and 10).

US-based surveillance was associated with improved compliance, guideline adherence and cost-effectiveness, without detectable safety signals. An MRI-limited approach in well-visualized BD-IPMN <20 mm appears promising for long term surveillance.

💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a recognized complication of longstanding colonic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), driven largely by chronic inflammation and cumulative inflammatory burden. Although historical CRC risks in ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s colitis were substantial, more recent data show a marked decline, likely due to improved medical therapies, early diagnosis, and advances in endoscopic surveillance and resection. Modern IBD surveillance has shifted from random biopsies to high-definition, targeted detection using chromoendoscopy and advanced imaging, with increasing emphasis on endoscopic resection of visible dysplasia and risk-stratified follow-up. Although earlier studies reported substantial CRC risks, more recent population-based data suggest these risks have declined, likely owing to improved inflammatory control, earlier diagnosis, risk-stratified surveillance, and advances in endoscopic detection and resection.

This review summarizes current understanding of dysplasia surveillance in colonic IBD, including epidemiology, pathogenesis, and risk factors for colorectal neoplasia. It outlines guideline-based recommendations for screening initiation and surveillance intervals and reviews advances in endoscopic detection, imaging, biopsy strategies, and resection techniques. It discusses dysplasia classification and management, implementation challenges, practice variability, and emerging approaches including individualized risk prediction and artificial intelligence. A PubMed search identified studies (2021-2026) relevant to this review.

Over the past two decades, dysplasia surveillance has shifted from random biopsies toward high-definition targeted detection and resection, allowing many lesions to be managed endoscopically rather than surgically. Despite these advances, implementation remains limited by procedural complexity, inadequate reimbursement, and unequal access to expertise. Future progress will depend on improved risk stratification, longitudinal data, AI integration, and equitable access to high-quality surveillance.

▶Beyond oncologic cure: optimizing surgical outcomes in older adults with early colorectal cancer.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Early colorectal cancer in older adults requires a balance between oncologic benefit and the risks of postoperative functional decline, loss of independence, and competing mortality. Chronological age alone is insufficient to guide this balance because physiological reserve, functional status, comorbidity, cognition, nutritional status, and life expectancy vary substantially among individuals of the same age. Frailty and sarcopenia provide complementary information regarding vulnerability to surgical stress, but frailty should not be used as an automatic indication for treatment de-escalation. Rather, frailty screening should identify patients who require multidimensional geriatric assessment to characterize clinically relevant vulnerabilities and guide individualized preoperative management. This review proposes an optimization-based framework for individualized surgical decision-making in older adults with early colorectal cancer. Patients with evidence of geriatric vulnerability should undergo comprehensive geriatric assessment, including evaluation of functional independence, physical performance, muscle strength, nutritional status, comorbidity, medication burden, cognition, and social support. When oncologically safe, a time-limited period of individualized optimization, including exercise, nutritional intervention, medical optimization, medication review, and psychosocial support, may be considered before definitive surgical planning. Reassessment should emphasize the direction of change in functional reserve and recovery potential rather than categorical reversal of frailty. Patients with a favorable trajectory after optimization may remain candidates for guideline-concordant oncologic surgery. In contrast, persistently limited reserve should prompt multidisciplinary and shared decision-making that considers tumor-specific oncologic benefit, functional consequences, competing mortality, and patient priorities. This approach reframes frailty assessment from static risk prediction toward an individualized, optimization-based strategy while preserving oncologic principles.

▶Neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemoradiotherapy versus chemoradiotherapy alone for locally advanced rectal cancer: meta-analysis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
The addition of immunotherapy to neoadjuvant therapy for LARC markedly improves pathological response but increases high-grade toxicity and surgical morbidity. Although inCRT is a potent intensification strategy, stringent patient selection and regimen optimization are required before widespread adoption.
Standard neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) for locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) achieves variable pathological responses. Incorporating immune checkpoint inhibitors into nCRT (inCRT) is a promising intensification strategy, but its efficacy and impact on surgical safety remain poorly defined. This meta-analysis evaluated the oncological benefits and surgical risks of inCRT versus nCRT in both proficient and deficient mismatch repair LARC.

A systematic search of the PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library databases was conducted for randomized clinical trials (RCTs) published from inception up to February 2026. The primary outcomes were pathological complete response (pCR) and grade ≥ III severe adverse events (SAEs) according to the Clavien-Dindo complication grading system. Secondary outcomes included major pathological response (MPR) and postoperative complications. Pooled risk ratios (RRs) were calculated using random-effects or fixed-effects models.

Seven RCTs including 1117 patients were identified. Five trials did not restrict enrolment based on mismatch repair or microsatellite instability status, whereas two trials exclusively enrolled patients with mismatch repair-proficient/microsatellite-stable tumours. Although inCRT significantly increased the rate of pCR (31.9% versus 19.3%; RR 1.70; 95% confidence interval (c.i.) 1.37 to 2.10; P < 0.001) and MPR (55.1% versus 40.3%; RR 1.38; 95% c.i. 1.20 to 1.59; P < 0.001) versus nCRT, it was associated with a significantly higher risk of SAEs (30.1% versus 26.8%; RR 1.24; 95% c.i. 1.03 to 1.49; P = 0.02) and overall postoperative complications (32.8% versus 25.6%; RR 1.27; 95% c.i. 1.02 to 1.58; P = 0.03). Subgroup analysis indicated that the pCR benefit was most pronounced in cohorts receiving short-course radiotherapy and consolidation immunotherapy.

The addition of immunotherapy to neoadjuvant therapy for LARC markedly improves pathological response but increases high-grade toxicity and surgical morbidity. Although inCRT is a potent intensification strategy, stringent patient selection and regimen optimization are required before widespread adoption.

Survival outcomes in colorectal cancer (CRC) vary within TNM stages, motivating investigation of complementary prognostic markers. This study aimed to prioritize lipid metabolism-related candidates and examine the prognostic association and model-comparison performance of FABP4 beyond TNM staging.

Lipid metabolism-related candidates were explored using TCGA-based differential-expression analysis, Cox survival screening, and random survival forest prioritization. Fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4) expression was subsequently evaluated by immunohistochemistry in a retrospective cohort of 268 patients with CRC. Overall survival (OS) was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression methods. A prognostic nomogram was developed and evaluated using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration plots, and decision curve analysis (DCA).

FABP4 was translationally prioritized from the exploratory bioinformatic candidate set and subsequently evaluated in the independent pathological cohort. Clinically, FABP4 positivity was associated with adverse clinicopathological features and poorer OS. The full model showed an optimism-corrected C-index of 0.765 and corrected 3- and 5-year AUCs of 0.793 and 0.846. Adding FABP4 to TNM staging improved Cox model fit (LR χ² = 5.05, df = 1, P = 0.025). Corrected discrimination estimates were numerically higher (C-index, 0.728 to 0.754; 3-year AUC, 0.745 to 0.773; 5-year AUC, 0.803 to 0.835), but all corresponding paired-bootstrap intervals for the differences included zero.

💡 Conclusion
Greater efficacy was observed among etrasimod induction responders for all assessed endpoints at week 52 when compared with the total ELEVATE UC 52 population, contextualizing the efficacy of etrasimod given the treat-through design of the study.
Most trials studying advanced therapies for ulcerative colitis (UC) adopted a responder re-randomization design. By contrast, a treat-through design was utilized in the ELEVATE UC 52 trial to assess etrasimod, an oral, once-daily, selective sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor modulator. It is therefore important to contextualize the efficacy of etrasimod given this different study design. We evaluated the efficacy of etrasimod versus placebo at week 52 among induction clinical responders.

In this post hoc analysis of ELEVATE UC 52, selected efficacy endpoints were evaluated at week 52 among all week 12 clinical responders (induction responders), defined as a ≥2-point and ≥30% decrease from baseline in modified Mayo score, with a ≥1-point decrease from baseline in rectal bleeding subscore (or an absolute value ≤1).

In total, 182 patients receiving etrasimod and 52 patients receiving placebo were induction responders. Of these patients, a greater proportion who received etrasimod versus placebo met the criteria for clinical remission (48.9% vs. 19.2%; p < 0.001) and all other endpoints assessed at week 52 (p < 0.05). Greater proportions of induction responders compared with the total trial population treated with etrasimod met the criteria for clinical remission (48.9% vs. 32.5%), endoscopic improvement (56.6% vs. 39.1%), and all other endpoints.

Greater efficacy was observed among etrasimod induction responders for all assessed endpoints at week 52 when compared with the total ELEVATE UC 52 population, contextualizing the efficacy of etrasimod given the treat-through design of the study.

▶A randomized controlled trial of rotational thromboelastometry-guided blood component transfusion for difficult-to-treat variceal bleeding in ACLF.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
ROTEM guidance reduced coagulation-directed blood component administration and TAEs compared with SCTs without compromising rebleeding or survival, supporting a restrictive approach in ACLF with difficult-to-treat AVB.
Difficult-to-treat acute variceal bleeding (AVB) in acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) may require substantial red blood cell transfusions and coagulation-directed blood components (CDBCs), including fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate, and platelets, thereby predisposing to transfusion-related adverse events (TAEs). We assessed rebleeding, mortality, and CDBC requirements with rotational thromboelastometry (ROTEM)-guided versus standard coagulation test (SCT)-guided transfusion strategies.

In this single-centre, randomised controlled trial, patients with ACLF and difficult-to-treat AVB were assigned (1:1) to ROTEM-guided or SCT-guided CDBC transfusion. The primary endpoint was first rebleeding by Day 42. Other endpoints included CDBC requirements, TAEs, and survival.

Of 1,341 patients screened between November 2021 and January 2026, 766 had well-controlled bleeding and 313 were ineligible; 262 were randomised (ROTEM, n=132; SCT, n=130). Mean age was 45.1±11.6 years, MELD-Na 24.2±9.7, 64.5% had alcohol-associated ACLF, and 73% presented with shock. Bleeding sources were esophageal varices (85.9%) and gastric varices (14.1%); 113 patients had multiple bleeding sites, 112 had active spurting, and 37 had failure of endoscopic bleeding control, with 17 TIPS and 20 Danis-Ella stents insertions. First rebleeding by Day-42 occurred in 12.9% with ROTEM versus 13.9% with SCT (absolute risk difference [ARD], -1.0 percentage points [pp]; 95% CI: -9.2 to 7.3). All-cause mortality through the end of study was 31.1% versus 38.5%, respectively (ARD, -7.4 pp; 95% CI: -18.6 to 4.1). CDBCs were not required in 75.8% versus 52.3% (ARD, +23.5 pp; 95% CI:11.9 to 34.2). TAEs occurred in 6.8% versus 14.6% (ARD, -7.8 pp; 95% CI:-15.6 to -0.2).

ROTEM guidance reduced coagulation-directed blood component administration and TAEs compared with SCTs without compromising rebleeding or survival, supporting a restrictive approach in ACLF with difficult-to-treat AVB.

▶Advancing the field of TAVR: expanding indications and emerging technologies.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) was first developed for patients with severe aortic stenosis (AS) at prohibitive risk for surgery. Over the past two decades, the continuous evolution in device technologies, procedural techniques and operator experience, has dramatically improved the safety and efficacy of TAVR, fuelling its expansion to newer patient cohorts. Current guideline recommendations now advocate for TAVR in younger, low surgical-risk patients. Accumulating evidence highlighting the impact of adverse cardiac remodelling and cardiac fibrosis in patients with severe AS, has prompted earlier treatment in patients with asymptomatic severe or moderate aortic stenosis. As more patients are treated earlier and at a younger age, the goals of TAVR now shift beyond the acute procedural result, towards ensuring the implanted prosthesis is optimized for longer-term valve performance and clinical outcomes. Procedural techniques to optimize stent frame expansion together with novel stent frame and leaflet design technologies are being developed to optimize long-term valve durability. Despite this, the need for re-interventions or redo-TAVR procedures is expected to increase. The challenges faced in planning and executing these complex procedures is being met with developments in pre-procedural imaging and simulation technologies, together with the development of leaflet modification techniques, designed to overcome coronary challenges. Further development of dedicated devices for cerebral embolic protection, calcium modification for transfemoral access and treatment of severe pure native aortic regurgitation, also continue to expand the applicability for TAVR. In this review, we summarise the evidence in these contemporary patient populations, as well as the procedural techniques and technologies that continue to advance the frontiers of TAVR.

▶Rebooting immunity: how stem cell therapy is rewriting the future of autoimmune thyroid disease.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Autoimmune thyroid diseases (AITDs), including Hashimoto's thyroiditis and Graves' disease, are the most prevalent organ-specific autoimmune disorders worldwide. Current management remains symptomatic rather than curative, often necessitating lifelong therapy.
