Colonoscopy is central to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) management, but repeated procedures may be limited by anxiety, discomfort and recovery burden. Remimazolam may provide faster recovery than conventional benzodiazepines while preserving the practical advantages of moderate sedation. We compared patient-reported satisfaction, recovery, safety and endoscopic performance with remimazolam versus midazolam in patients with IBD undergoing ileocolonoscopy.

In this single-centre, prospective, patient-blinded, non-inferiority study using randomized weekly time-block allocation, 200 adults with IBD undergoing elective ileocolonoscopy received remimazolam plus fentanyl or midazolam plus fentanyl. Patients were unaware of the sedative administered. Satisfaction was assessed using a self-administered four-point Likert scale immediately after the procedure and at 24 hours. The primary outcome was the proportion of patients reporting at least "satisfied" immediately after the procedure. Secondary outcomes included "very satisfied" responses, PADSS-defined discharge readiness at 10 and 20 min, adverse events and endoscopic performance. Bayesian analyses used a prespecified non-inferiority margin of -10 percentage points.

Overall satisfaction was high in both groups, and remimazolam was non-inferior to midazolam: estimated rates were 96.4% versus 97.3%; risk difference -0.9% (95% credible interval [CrI] -5.8% to 3.7%); posterior probability of non-inferiority 99.94%. "Very satisfied" responses were more frequent with remimazolam: 62.2% versus 48.3%; posterior mean difference 15.8% (95% CrI 3.3% to 28.1%); posterior probability of superiority 99.3%. At 24 hours, satisfaction was 95.0% (remimazolam) and 94.0% (midazolam). PADSS-defined discharge readiness favoured remimazolam at 10 min (65.0% vs 27.5%) and 20 min (88.3% vs 64.2%). Hypotension (3.0% vs 2.0%), hypoxaemia (3.0% vs 4.0%) and flumazenil use (3.0% vs 2.0%) occurred (remimazolam vs midazolam); no severe adverse events occurred. Endoscopic performance was comparable.

A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library identified English-language studies on statin use and CRC mortality published through March 2025. Adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) were pooled to calculate summary HRs. Heterogeneity was assessed using the Q-test and

Twenty studies involving 207, 041 patients with CRC were included. Ten studies (131, 599 patients) evaluated prediagnostic statin use, and 13 studies (75, 442 patients) evaluated postdiagnostic statin use. For prediagnostic use, fixed-effects models yielded pooled HRs of 0.87 (95% CI, 0.81-0.92) for all-cause mortality (ACM) and 0.82 (95% CI, 0.78-0.86) for cancer-specific mortality (CSM). 💡 Conclusion
European colorectal cancer (CCR) screening programmes reduce mortality, yet participation in France remains low, particularly among individuals with limited health literacy (HL) and lower socioeconomic position. The DECODE trial assessed whether a combined HL intervention for General Practitioners (GPs) and eligible patients could improve CRC screening uptake after 1 year in French underserved areas. A two-arm cluster-randomised controlled trial in GP practices in underserved areas across four French regions was conducted. Practices were block-randomised (1:1) to intervention or usual care. The intervention combined HL-focussed interactive GP training with a patient-oriented pictorial brochure and video explaining the 'at-home' faecal immunochemical test (FIT). Patients aged 50-74 years, eligible for screening were recruited during routine consultations. Intervention GPs used their HL training to explain the FIT, and provided it alongside the patient-oriented materials; control arm GPs provided the FIT in their usual way. The primary outcome was FIT uptake at 1 year. Secondary outcomes, together with sociodemographic data, precarity, and HL levels, were examined. Mixed-effects logistic regression was performed accounting for clustering and baseline differences. Fifty-seven GPs (27 practices) were randomised and 1065 patients recruited (494 intervention, 571 control); 1022 were analysed. CRC screening uptake in the intervention arm (70.8%, 335/473) was increased compared to the control (64.5%, 354/549) (OR: 2.28, CI: 1.01-5.53, P = .046). No differences were observed for secondary outcomes. A multicomponent HL intervention targeting GP-patient interactions in underserved populations can improve CRC screening uptake within 1 year and may reduce related inequities, with future policy implications. 👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
This study provides strong evidence for the causal relationship between MetS and CRC by defining MetS as a single genetic entity. Clinically, these findings highlight the importance of early detection and management of MetS to potentially reduce CRC burden.
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a leading cause of cancer death worldwide. Metabolic syndrome (MetS), a prevalent and modifiable condition, is a suggested risk factor for CRC, though observational evidence remains inconsistent. This study investigated whether MetS, as an integrated clinical and genetic construct, exerts a causal effect on CRC.

Genetic association data for this study were primarily sourced from Taiwan Biobank, BioBank Japan Project, UK Biobank and NCBI to ensure robust cross-ancestry representation. Genomic structural equation modeling was applied to derive genetic instruments reflecting the shared genetic basis of MetS. A two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) design was then employed to assess the causal effect of MetS on CRC. The primary analysis was conducted using the inverse-variance weighted method, supplemented by MR-Egger, weighted median, LASSO, and MR-PRESSO to evaluate consistency and account for potential pleiotropy and heterogeneity.

A significant causal relationship is observed between genetically predicted MetS and CRC. Among the methods reaching statistical significance (IVW, LASSO, MR-PRESSO), the estimated odds ratios ranged from 1.45 to 1.55 in East Asians and 1.22 to 1.25 in Europeans; weighted median and MR-Egger estimates were directionally consistent but did not reach significance in either population. Cochran's Q test indicates heterogeneity; however, the MR-Egger intercept suggests no directional pleiotropy. IVW, LASSO, and MR-PRESSO consistently supported a causal effect of MetS on CRC, while weighted median and MR-Egger estimates were directionally consistent but did not reach statistical significance, and the leave-one-out analysis confirmed the robustness of the causal estimates.

This study provides strong evidence for the causal relationship between MetS and CRC by defining MetS as a single genetic entity. Clinically, these findings highlight the importance of early detection and management of MetS to potentially reduce CRC burden.

Intravenous albumin is widely used in decompensated cirrhosis, but its efficacy across clinical indications remains inconsistent. Therefore, we evaluated the effect of albumin on mortality, major complications, and safety separately for each indication.

We searched PubMed (MEDLINE), Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, and Web of Science from inception to December 2025. Primary analyses included only randomized trials in which albumin was the randomized contrast; within each indication, comparisons with standard care or placebo were analyzed separately from comparisons with an alternative plasma expander. Non-randomized studies were analyzed separately. Random-effects risk ratios (RRs) were calculated.

Thirty-two studies were included (27 randomized); 22 randomized trials with 3,582 participants reported mortality. Albumin reduced mortality in spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP; 2 trials; RR, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.26-0.77) and hepatic encephalopathy (3 trials; 0.54; 0.35-0.83), but not in large-volume paracentesis (8 trials; 0.86; 0.65-1.13), long-term administration for ascites (3 trials; 0.93; 0.72-1.19), or infection other than SBP (5 trials; 0.97; 0.83-1.12); the test for differences between indications was significant (P=0.003). Findings were similar in a single trial of hospitalized patients. After paracentesis, albumin reduced paracentesis-induced circulatory dysfunction (0.46; 0.35-0.61), hyponatremia (0.47; 0.31-0.72), and acute kidney injury (0.63; 0.43-0.93); in SBP it reduced renal impairment (0.28; 0.15-0.51). Pulmonary complications were more frequent with albumin (5 trials; 2.66; 1.62-4.39). The secondary estimate combining indications was 0.89 (0.78-1.01; 95% prediction interval, 0.64-1.22).

The effect of albumin in decompensated cirrhosis is indication-specific, with a survival benefit demonstrated only in SBP and hepatic encephalopathy and more frequent pulmonary complications across indications, supporting indication-based use with monitoring for fluid overload.

▶Epidemiology and clinical characteristics of progressive pulmonary fibrosis in Asia.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) represents a progressive phenotype across various fibrosing interstitial lung diseases (ILDs). The epidemiology of PPF is confounded by differences in the defining criteria for PPF, data capture sources, as well as the heterogeneity of ILD subtype diagnoses. In Asian PPF cohorts, although some heterogeneity exists across countries, connective tissue disease (CTD)-associated ILD is the most frequently identified underlying cause of PPF. Within CTD-ILD, rheumatoid arthritis-associated ILD and myositis-related ILD account for a particularly large proportion of patients who develop PPF. From a prognostic standpoint, PPF is consistently associated with high mortality, similar to that of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Although randomized trials have demonstrated the efficacy of antifibrotic therapy in PPF, real-world use remains inconsistent because of differences in regulatory approval, reimbursement, and clinical practice. These observations underscore critical gaps in data comparability and treatment access. This narrative review synthesizes contemporary data from Asian PPF cohorts to delineate the epidemiology, clinical features, regional heterogeneity, treatment patterns, and prognosis of PPF.

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains a highly lethal malignancy, with activating KRAS mutations present in approximately 90% of tumors. Nearly a quarter-century separated the discovery of Harvey murine sarcoma virus in 1964 from recognition of frequent KRAS mutations in pancreatic cancer in 1988. Decades of unsuccessful therapeutic development followed, reflecting the biochemical challenges of direct inhibition and the limitations of indirect pathway blockade. Structural and mechanistic advances subsequently enabled inhibitors that trap KRAS G12C in its inactive state, establishing clinical proof of druggability but benefiting only a small subset of PDAC. However, resistance to RAS(ON) inhibitors remains a challenge. This review traces the historical and molecular foundations of KRAS targeting, examines unsuccessful approaches, and evaluates the transition to clinically effective RAS inhibition. It also discusses resistance through KRAS amplification, pathway reactivation, and alternative survival programs, and considers how these mechanisms inform future treatment. Emerging strategies include earlier intervention, complementary RAS inhibitors, targeted KRAS degradation, combinations exploiting methylthioadenosine phosphorylase loss through protein arginine methyltransferase 5 inhibition, and KRAS-directed immunotherapies. Although many approaches remain investigational, the accelerating pace of translation provides opportunities to improve disease control. Biomarker-guided treatment and randomized evaluation will be essential to convert these advances into durable survival while preserving quality of life.

We screened urine samples collected from subjects with CP for metabolites that may serve as biomarkers. 💡 Conclusion
The goals of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) management are gradually expanding from glycemic control alone to weight management, cardiorenal protection, and disease remission. Growing evidence suggests that, in some patients with a relatively short disease duration and preserved β-cell function, substantial and sustained weight loss and metabolic interventions can reduce ectopic fat in the liver and pancreas, improve insulin resistance, and promote the recovery of β-cell function, thereby achieving glycemic remission after discontinuation of glucose-lowering medications and indicating that T2DM may be reversible to some extent at specific stages. This narrative review summarizes the evolution of the concept of T2DM remission, its biological basis, the major approaches to achieving remission, and the key factors influencing remission, and on this basis re-examines the potential role of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM). Existing TCM research has primarily focused on improvements in metabolic indicators such as HbA1c, blood glucose, insulin resistance, and body weight, resulting in a clear "evidence-endpoint mismatch" with the evidentiary standards for remission, which center on sustained normoglycemia after medication withdrawal, duration of remission, and risk of relapse. However, direct evidence that TCM itself induces diabetes remission or modifies the established biological mechanisms of remission remains limited. Future studies should incorporate enrichment of populations with greater reversibility, standardized background treatment and medication reduction/discontinuation procedures, internationally harmonized remission endpoints, long-term follow-up, and high-quality randomized controlled trials. Imaging, multi-omics, mechanistic mediation analyses, and individualized treatment-effect prediction may further support the development of a modern remission-oriented evidence framework and help clarify the potential clinical value of TCM in T2DM remission and long-term disease management.

Endoscopic ultrasound-guided radiofrequency ablation (EUS-RFA) is an emerging minimally invasive treatment for pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, particularly insulinomas. Traditionally, insulinomas are managed surgically, often requiring major pancreatic resections associated with significant morbidity, prolonged hospitalization, and the risk of long-term complications including pancreatic insufficiency and diabetes. We report the initial clinical experience with EUS-RFA at a tertiary center in Sweden, including three patients with symptomatic insulinomas. Although further prospective and randomized studies are needed to establish long-term outcomes and optimal patient selection, EUS-RFA has the potential to represent a paradigm shift in the management of insulinomas and other selected pancreatic lesions.
