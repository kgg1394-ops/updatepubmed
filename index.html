Bone and joint diseases, including osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, and genetic skeletal disorders, are increasingly studied in the context of aging and metabolic dysfunction. To investigate these underlying pathways, preclinical research requires precise tools to functionally interrogate specific genes within defined skeletal cell populations. Accurately delivering gain- or loss-of-function agents to bone or cartilage compartments is essential for elucidating molecular mechanisms and validating therapeutic targets. Therefore, achieving spatial precision in skeletal tissue delivery is a critical methodological requirement. Targeted delivery systems serve as specific pharmacological tools that localize bioactive molecules to bone or cartilage to study the function and pathogenic mechanisms of specific molecules. Safety, efficacy, and cost-effectiveness are paramount for clinical translation of the targeted delivery systems discussed herein. However, their effectiveness heavily relies on the choice of targeting moiety. Different tissues, cell types, and experimental objectives require distinct ligands, and inappropriate selection may lead to inefficient targeting or misleading conclusions. Despite their importance, clear guidance for selecting skeletal targeting moieties in preclinical studies remains limited. To address this need, the Translational European-Asian Network (TEA-NET) of Academia Europaea Translational Medicine Working Group initiated and coordinated this guideline. Through a structured literature review and a predefined evidence-based evaluation framework, this practical guide assesses available skeletal-targeted delivery strategies, emphasizing the selection and functional integration of targeting moieties. The resulting recommendations provide practical guidance for choosing targeting ligands in preclinical studies and for designing delivery systems with potential for future clinical translation.

Colonoscopy is central to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) management, but repeated procedures may be limited by anxiety, discomfort and recovery burden. Remimazolam may provide faster recovery than conventional benzodiazepines while preserving the practical advantages of moderate sedation. We compared patient-reported satisfaction, recovery, safety and endoscopic performance with remimazolam versus midazolam in patients with IBD undergoing ileocolonoscopy.

The erector spinae plane (ESP) block has been shown to improve recovery in abdominal procedures, but its efficacy in the IBD population remains unknown. We investigated whether bilateral ESP blocks improve postoperative quality of recovery in this population.

In this prospective, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial, adults with IBD scheduled for elective laparoscopic-assisted abdominal surgery were randomized to receive bilateral ESP blocks with either 0.5% ropivacaine or normal saline before induction of anesthesia. 💡 Conclusion

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a leading cause of cancer death worldwide. Metabolic syndrome (MetS), a prevalent and modifiable condition, is a suggested risk factor for CRC, though observational evidence remains inconsistent. This study investigated whether MetS, as an integrated clinical and genetic construct, exerts a causal effect on CRC.

Genetic association data for this study were primarily sourced from Taiwan Biobank, BioBank Japan Project, UK Biobank and NCBI to ensure robust cross-ancestry representation. Genomic structural equation modeling was applied to derive genetic instruments reflecting the shared genetic basis of MetS. A two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) design was then employed to assess the causal effect of MetS on CRC. The primary analysis was conducted using the inverse-variance weighted method, supplemented by MR-Egger, weighted median, LASSO, and MR-PRESSO to evaluate consistency and account for potential pleiotropy and heterogeneity.

A significant causal relationship is observed between genetically predicted MetS and CRC. Among the methods reaching statistical significance (IVW, LASSO, MR-PRESSO), the estimated odds ratios ranged from 1.45 to 1.55 in East Asians and 1.22 to 1.25 in Europeans; weighted median and MR-Egger estimates were directionally consistent but did not reach significance in either population. Cochran's Q test indicates heterogeneity; however, the MR-Egger intercept suggests no directional pleiotropy. IVW, LASSO, and MR-PRESSO consistently supported a causal effect of MetS on CRC, while weighted median and MR-Egger estimates were directionally consistent but did not reach statistical significance, and the leave-one-out analysis confirmed the robustness of the causal estimates.

This study provides strong evidence for the causal relationship between MetS and CRC by defining MetS as a single genetic entity. Clinically, these findings highlight the importance of early detection and management of MetS to potentially reduce CRC burden.

Intravenous albumin is widely used in decompensated cirrhosis, but its efficacy across clinical indications remains inconsistent. Therefore, we evaluated the effect of albumin on mortality, major complications, and safety separately for each indication.

We searched PubMed (MEDLINE), Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, and Web of Science from inception to December 2025. Primary analyses included only randomized trials in which albumin was the randomized contrast; within each indication, comparisons with standard care or placebo were analyzed separately from comparisons with an alternative plasma expander. Non-randomized studies were analyzed separately. Random-effects risk ratios (RRs) were calculated.

Thirty-two studies were included (27 randomized); 22 randomized trials with 3,582 participants reported mortality. Albumin reduced mortality in spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP; 2 trials; RR, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.26-0.77) and hepatic encephalopathy (3 trials; 0.54; 0.35-0.83), but not in large-volume paracentesis (8 trials; 0.86; 0.65-1.13), long-term administration for ascites (3 trials; 0.93; 0.72-1.19), or infection other than SBP (5 trials; 0.97; 0.83-1.12); the test for differences between indications was significant (P=0.003). The effect of albumin in decompensated cirrhosis is indication-specific, with a survival benefit demonstrated only in SBP and hepatic encephalopathy and more frequent pulmonary complications across indications, supporting indication-based use with monitoring for fluid overload.

Progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) represents a progressive phenotype across various fibrosing interstitial lung diseases (ILDs). The epidemiology of PPF is confounded by differences in the defining criteria for PPF, data capture sources, as well as the heterogeneity of ILD subtype diagnoses. In Asian PPF cohorts, although some heterogeneity exists across countries, connective tissue disease (CTD)-associated ILD is the most frequently identified underlying cause of PPF. Participants with MASLD were randomly assigned to receive either aspirin or a placebo. The goals of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) management are gradually expanding from glycemic control alone to weight management, cardiorenal protection, and disease remission. Growing evidence suggests that, in some patients with a relatively short disease duration and preserved β-cell function, substantial and sustained weight loss and metabolic interventions can reduce ectopic fat in the liver and pancreas, improve insulin resistance, and promote the recovery of β-cell function, thereby achieving glycemic remission after discontinuation of glucose-lowering medications and indicating that T2DM may be reversible to some extent at specific stages. Imaging, multi-omics, mechanistic mediation analyses, and individualized treatment-effect prediction may further support the development of a modern remission-oriented evidence framework and help clarify the potential clinical value of TCM in T2DM remission and long-term disease management.
