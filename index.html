Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided drainage is the standard of care for walled-off necrosis (WON); however, the optimal timing of subsequent endoscopic necrosectomy remains uncertain. We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing immediate versus on-demand necrosectomy following EUS-guided drainage.

We searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library through March 2026 for RCTs comparing immediate (≤ 72 h) versus on-demand necrosectomy in adults with necrotizing pancreatitis. Primary outcomes were technical success, clinical success, and the proportion of patients undergoing necrosectomy. Secondary outcomes included length of hospital stay, costs, need for percutaneous or multigate drainage, and adverse events. Pooled risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using random-effects models.

Four RCTs comprising 215 patients, of whom 192 (89.3%) had WON, were included. Immediate and on-demand approaches demonstrated similar technical success (RR 1.01, 95%CI 0.98-1.05) and clinical success (RR 1.17, 95%CI 0.98-1.40). Immediate necrosectomy was associated with a higher proportion of patients undergoing necrosectomy (RR 1.65, 95%CI 1.13-2.41). No significant differences were observed in length of hospital stay, costs, need for additional drainage, procedure- or disease-related adverse events, mortality, bleeding, stent migration, or pneumoperitoneum.

Immediate endoscopic necrosectomy did not demonstrate a consistent clinical advantage over an on-demand approach. Low- to very-low-certainty evidence suggests little or no difference in clinical efficacy or safety outcomes, while very-low-certainty evidence suggests that immediate necrosectomy may increase the proportion of patients undergoing necrosectomy. However, the magnitude and clinical significance of this effect remain uncertain. These findings should be interpreted cautiously given the limited number of randomized trials, modest sample sizes, and low or very low certainty of evidence for several outcomes.

This study was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261366884).

💡 Conclusion
Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), including anti-CTLA-4, anti-PD-1, and anti-PD-L1 agents, have revolutionized cancer therapy by enhancing T-cell-mediated antitumor responses and significantly improving survival across multiple malignancies. Despite these advances, ICIs are associated with immune-related adverse events (irAEs), with ICI-induced colitis representing one of the most common and severe gastrointestinal toxicities. Clinically and histopathologically, this condition often resembles inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and can substantially impact treatment continuity and patient quality of life. Corticosteroids remain the standard first-line therapy; however, up to one-third of patients exhibit steroid-refractory disease or develop significant steroid-related toxicity, highlighting the need for alternative therapeutic strategies. Biologic agents, particularly infliximab and vedolizumab, have demonstrated high efficacy in this setting, with infliximab associated with more rapid symptom control and vedolizumab offering a favorable steroid-sparing profile. In patients with multi-refractory disease, additional agents such as ustekinumab, tofacitinib, and tocilizumab have shown promise, although current evidence is limited and primarily derived from small studies and case series. This review critically examines current treatment paradigms for ICI-induced colitis, focusing on efficacy, safety, and potential implications for oncologic outcomes. Despite increasing use of advanced therapies, clinical decision-making remains largely guided by retrospective data, and key uncertainties persist regarding optimal treatment sequencing, patient selection, and long-term safety. Future research should prioritize prospective randomized trials incorporating both oncologic and gastrointestinal endpoints, as well as mechanistic studies aimed at identifying predictive biomarkers and therapeutic targets that preserve anti-tumor immunity.

💡 Conclusion
The evaluation of peripheral pulmonary lesions (PPLs) is a persistent clinical challenge, made more pressing by the expansion of low-dose CT lung cancer screening. Conventional and earlier navigational bronchoscopy, including electromagnetic navigation, historically achieved diagnostic yields of approximately 60-70%, with CT-to-body divergence contributing to nondiagnostic sampling. This narrative review, prepared using the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (SANRA), summarizes robotic-assisted bronchoscopy (RAB) platform technology, procedural optimization, diagnostic performance, safety, molecular tissue adequacy, and emerging applications. A structured search was updated through September 2026; this is not a systematic review. Using the strict definition recommended by the American Thoracic Society/American College of Chest Physicians (ATS/ACCP), the most comprehensive meta-analysis reported a pooled RAB diagnostic yield of 69.6%, while the prospective multicenter TARGET study reported an adjudicated strict yield of 61.6%. Higher pooled estimates of approximately 84-87% generally use intermediate definitions that the ATS/ACCP statement recommends against as primary diagnostic-yield measures. RAB pneumothorax rates are approximately 2% in pooled analyses, compared with approximately 20-25% for CT-guided transthoracic needle biopsy. In the randomized RELIANT trial, RAB was non-inferior, but not superior, to electromagnetic navigation bronchoscopy; in VERITAS, navigational bronchoscopy as a category was non-inferior to transthoracic needle biopsy for 12-month diagnostic accuracy, with fewer pneumothoraces. RAB is a useful option for selected PPLs, but strict-definition performance is more modest than frequently quoted intermediate yields. Outcomes depend on lesion characteristics, sampling strategy, operator experience, center volume, and imaging infrastructure.

Transient elastography may overestimate fibrosis in autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) because hepatic inflammation independently increases liver stiffness measurement (LSM). We aimed to determine which inflammation markers confound LSM in AIH, whether algebraic correction improves accuracy, and when LSM becomes reliable for fibrosis staging.

Single-center retrospective cohort of adults with AIH, paired diagnostic Metavir biopsy, and at least one FibroScan visit with same-visit alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and immunoglobulin G (IgG). Mixed-effects linear regression modeled LSM on biopsy stage, ALT, IgG, and time since diagnosis with a patient-level random intercept. An inflammation-adjusted LSM was computed by subtracting estimated ALT and IgG contributions. Spearman correlation and DeLong-tested AUROC for advanced fibrosis (F3-F4) compared measured and adjusted LSM, stratified by inflammation state and treatment timing.

We analyzed 208 FibroScan observations from 119 AIH patients (49 with advanced fibrosis). IgG was independently associated with LSM (β = +0.003 kPa per mg/dL, p < 0.001) while ALT was not (β ≈ 0, p = 0.68). The IgG-LSM relationship varied by treatment timing (IgG × 0-6 months interaction p = 0.001; IgG × 12-24 months p = 0.004). Algebraic correction did not improve discrimination (full-cohort AUROC measured 0.76 vs adjusted 0.72, DeLong p = 0.18). LSM accuracy for advanced fibrosis varied by timing: AUROC 0.71 pretreatment (n = 35), 0.64 at 12-24 months (n = 22), and 0.83 beyond 24 months (n = 72).

In AIH, IgG-not ALT-independently inflates LSM, with a time-dependent effect strongest early after diagnosis. Algebraic correction did not improve fibrosis discrimination, but LSM accuracy improved substantially beyond 24 months after AIH diagnosis. Timing rather than algebraic correction is the more actionable clinical strategy for FibroScan interpretation in AIH.

▶Beyond Weight Loss: Rethinking Dietary and Lifestyle Interventions for Liver Disease Modification in MASLD.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is commonly managed with dietary and lifestyle intervention, and weight loss is often used as a key marker of treatment success. However, changes in body weight alone do not fully describe the effects of lifestyle intervention on liver disease. This narrative review asks when lifestyle-induced changes can support a claim of liver disease modification. Current guidance generally associates sustained weight loss of at least 5% with reduced liver fat, 7-10% with reduced liver inflammation, and at least 10% with improvement in fibrosis. The review distinguishes weight loss from biological response, pathological improvement, and long-term clinical benefit. It considers how intervention exposure, biological response, outcome type, and duration of follow-up should be interpreted together. It also addresses differences in patient response, long-term adherence, and the limits of different study designs. Randomized trials, long-term observational studies, and target-trial emulation are considered in relation to what they can establish about causality and long-term benefit. We propose a five-question framework for evaluating lifestyle-induced liver disease modification: Who? What? Through which pathway? What outcome? For how long and with what evidence? Weight loss remains an important treatment target, but it should not be considered synonymous with liver disease modification. Claims of disease modification should reflect the level of evidence available. This framework provides a clearer way to distinguish short-term improvement from meaningful and durable modification of MASLD.

Procalcitonin (PCT), a serum biomarker that responds preferentially to bacterial infection, has theoretical promise for differentiating infected from sterile necrosis. Earlier systematic reviews, though, are limited by small sample sizes, shallow subgroup analysis, and a lack of formal evidence grading. An updated synthesis that focuses specifically on IPN as the clinical endpoint is missing.
