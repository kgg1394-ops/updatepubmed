💡 Conclusion
The guideline emphasizes early diagnosis, optimization of first-line therapy, risk-based treatment escalation, symptom management, and prevention of post-transplantation disease recurrence to improve patient outcomes and quality of life.
Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic cholestatic liver disease associated with progressive fibrosis, cirrhosis, liver transplantation, and premature mortality. Despite effective therapies, important challenges remain in the diagnosis and management of PBC, including identification of antimitochondrial antibody-negative disease, assessment of response to ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA), risk stratification, treatment of patients with inadequate response, management of cholestatic pruritus, and prevention of recurrent disease after liver transplantation. The aim of this guideline was to develop evidence-based recommendations addressing these key areas of clinical care.

The American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) and the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) convened a multidisciplinary panel to develop clinical practice recommendations for adults with PBC. Systematic reviews were performed for 6 prioritized clinical questions. The Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework was used to prioritize clinical outcomes and assess evidence, and its Evidence to Decision Framework was used to develop recommendations.

The guideline panel agreed on 6 recommendations. These recommendations address the use of PBC-specific antinuclear antibodies in antimitochondrial antibody-negative suspected PBC, UDCA as first-line therapy, liver stiffness measurement for prognostication, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor agonists for patients with inadequate response or intolerance to UDCA, linerixibat for moderate to severe pruritus, and UDCA prophylaxis post-transplantation to reduce disease recurrence. The certainty of evidence ranged from moderate to very low.

The guideline emphasizes early diagnosis, optimization of first-line therapy, risk-based treatment escalation, symptom management, and prevention of post-transplantation disease recurrence to improve patient outcomes and quality of life.

CTRI/2024/04/065301 [registered on 05/04/2024]-trial registered prospectively.
Pregnant women in India are routinely prescribed oral iron supplements as part of the national Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB) program for anemia control. In women with iron-deficiency anemia, the benefits of iron supplementation during pregnancy, both for the mother and child, are very clear. In non-anemic pregnant women, however, the benefits of iron supplementation vis-à-vis associated risks that have been reported have not been studied adequately. Studies have suggested that iron may impair insulin secretion, thus increasing the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM).

To determine the incidence of GDM among non-anemic pregnant women receiving 60 mg/day of elemental iron as oral supplementation, compared with those receiving 30 mg/day.

To assess beta-cell functional capacity and quantify unmethylated insulin promoter DNA (a sensitive and specific marker of beta-cell damage) in blood samples from the two study groups.

This study is a randomized, parallel-group, double-blinded clinical trial, with 800 non-anemic primigravidae in each arm. One arm will receive oral supplementation at a dose of 60 mg per day (as per current national guidelines), while the other arm will receive 30 mg per day, for a duration of 12 weeks in the second trimester. The incidence of GDM in the two arms will be compared. Insulin secretion kinetics will be assessed using an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) done at the end of the intervention. Additionally, the unmethylated insulin promoter DNA in circulating free DNA (a marker of beta-cell damage) will be quantified in both arms.

The study's findings will provide important evidence relevant to the current national guidelines in India on universal oral iron supplementation during pregnancy. The results are expected to inform future policy decisions on whether universal supplementation for all pregnant women is appropriate or whether it should be targeted to those with low iron status.

💡 Conclusion
This expert statement proposes that the next phase of hepatobiliary oncology requires harmonized, biology-driven definitions of resectability, transplantability, and futility. Comparative evaluation of regional strategies is essential to clarify how treatment sequencing affects recurrence and survival, an effort that is time-sensitive as advances in systemic therapy may soon obscure strategy-specific effects. Hepatobiliary oncology is at an inflection point. Parallel advances in surgical technique, locoregional therapy, systemic treatment, and understanding of tumour biology have expanded the boundaries of curative-intent care yet have also exposed a critical reality that technical resectability is not synonymous with oncologic resectability. Technically successful resection or liver transplantation may be futile when tumour biology is aggressive, leading to early recurrence and limited survival benefit. Modern hepatobiliary surgical and transplant oncology must therefore move beyond anatomy-driven decision-making towards biologically informed strategies that incorporate tumour behaviour, treatment response, and temporal disease stability.

The Asia-Pacific Transplant Oncology Symposium (APTOS) 2024 examined how Eastern and Western paradigms intersect across hepatocellular carcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, and colorectal liver metastases, identifying pathways towards integration. Eastern and Western programmes have historically approached hepatobiliary oncology through distinct and longstanding philosophies. Eastern centres, frequently operating within living donor liver transplantation-dominant systems characterized by organ scarcity and the ethical principle of double equipoise, have traditionally leveraged high operative volume and technical expertise in complex resections. These environments have favoured surgery-first approaches when resection appears technically feasible, alongside response-driven conversion strategies aimed at expanding operability. Western centres, shaped by deceased-donor allocation frameworks and trial-oriented oncology models, have more consistently prioritized biological selection and longitudinal validation of disease control before proceeding to curative-intent intervention, particularly in borderline-resectable disease. These differences are driven by infrastructural and ethical factors, including donor availability, transplant systems, and biomarker integration, rather than surgical capability. This article is an expert statement reflecting the opinion of an international multidisciplinary faculty of hepatobiliary surgeons, transplant surgeons, hepatologists, and medical and clinical oncologists from Eastern and Western centres. It is a narrative expert perspective and not a formal consensus or guideline.

This expert statement proposes that the next phase of hepatobiliary oncology requires harmonized, biology-driven definitions of resectability, transplantability, and futility. Comparative evaluation of regional strategies is essential to clarify how treatment sequencing affects recurrence and survival, an effort that is time-sensitive as advances in systemic therapy may soon obscure strategy-specific effects. Rather than competing models, Eastern procedural expertise and Western biology-centred frameworks should be viewed as complementary, supporting context-dependent or sequential approaches that integrate surgery with biology-guided systemic or locoregional therapy. Aligning these paradigms within ethical stewardship of donor organs can establish globally coherent, biology-driven standards that maximize durable survival while minimizing futile surgery and transplantation.

Colonoscopy is the gold standard for colorectal cancer detection and prevention, but its performance depends on operator skill and lesion features. Artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted colonoscopy has been introduced to improve adenoma detection.

A comprehensive search was conducted in PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, and major Chinese databases to identify randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing AI-assisted and conventional colonoscopy. Meta-analyses were performed using Review Manager 5.4 and Stata 14.0. The quality of evidence was graded using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) system, while trial sequential analysis (TSA) was applied to assess the robustness and information sufficiency of the results.

A total of 46 RCTs encompassing 37,012 participants were included. Compared with conventional colonoscopy, AI-assisted colonoscopy was associated with a higher ADR (RR = 1.22, 95% CI = 1.17-1.27, P < 0.00001), polyp detection rate (PDR: RR = 1.22, 95% CI = 1.16-1.29, P < 0.00001), polyp detection per colonoscopy (PPC: MD = 0.28, 95% CI = 0.21-0.36), and adenoma detection per colonoscopy (APC: MD = 0.22, 95% CI = 0.15-0.29). The detection rate of sessile serrated lesions (SSL) was also higher in the AI-assisted group (RR = 1.20, 95% CI: 1.05-1.36, P = 0.007), whereas no significant difference was observed in advanced adenoma detection rate (AADR).

AI-assisted colonoscopy was associated with improvements in several detection-related quality indicators, including ADR, PDR, PPC, APC, and SSL detection rate. However, given the very low GRADE certainty for ADR and PDR and the lack of long-term outcome data, these findings should be interpreted cautiously.

Oxaliplatin-induced peripheral neuropathy (OIPN) is a dose-limiting adverse effect of FOLFOX chemotherapy in colorectal cancer (CRC) patients. We aimed to evaluate the protective effect of N-acetylcysteine (NAC) against OIPN. Additionally, we investigated its mechanistic effects on serum biomarkers including total antioxidant capacity (TAC), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and neurotensin.

Forty-six patients with stage III or high-risk stage II CRC receiving adjuvant modified FOLFOX-6 chemotherapy were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either oral NAC 1200 mg (effervescent tablets or granules) or placebo one hour before each oxaliplatin infusion for four cycles. Serum levels of TAC, IL-6, and neurotensin were measured before and after treatment. Neuropathy was assessed using the FACT/GOG-NTX-12 and NCI-CTCAE scales, and pain was estimated by using the Brief Pain Inventory-Short Form.

Neurotensin was significantly decreased in the NAC group compared to controls after treatment (P < 0.001), and was significantly lower post-treatment versus pre-treatment in the NAC group (P = 0.001). IL-6 was significantly reduced in the NAC group versus controls after treatment (P < 0.001), and significantly decreased post-treatment in both groups (P < 0.05). FACT/GOG-NTX-12 and NCI-CTCAE scores were significantly lower in the NAC group at the end of the first two cycles and at the end of treatment (P < 0.05). Pain interference, but not pain severity, was significantly reduced in the NAC group at both time points (P < 0.001).

NAC administration during FOLFOX chemotherapy significantly reduced OIPN severity, pain interference, and serum levels of IL-6 and neurotensin, suggesting both clinical and mechanistic neuroprotective effects.

While sodium glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have been evaluated in MASLD through several meta-analyses, their specific role in biopsy-proven MASH remains understudied. SGLT2i significantly improved hepatic, metabolic, cardiometabolic risk, and most histological parameters; compared to baseline. The poor control of blood glucose levels that lies at the heart of this disease leads to debilitating complications such as renal failure, vision loss, and amputations and has associations with heart disease and a host of other conditions. Finally, we used mRNA display methods to screen large cyclic peptide libraries using the RaPID (Random nonstandard Peptide Integrated Discovery) system in collaboration with the Suga lab. Screening this cyclic peptide library, comprising ∼1012 peptides, yielded a 9-amino acid lariat peptide that is a potent (Ki = 7 nM) and selective inhibitor of HPA. Structural studies of the complex revealed once again a motif in which two tyrosines, separated by a proline, engage the active site residues. The structure of its complex with the highly similar porcine pancreatic α-amylase (PPA) shows it blocking the active site, using two tyrosines and a histidine to engage the catalytic residues, reminiscent of the "phenol sandwich" found with MbA while deriving considerable additional affinity from the burial of surface area. Finally, we used mRNA display methods to screen large cyclic peptide libraries using the RaPID (Random nonstandard Peptide Integrated Discovery) system in collaboration with the Suga lab. Screening this cyclic peptide library, comprising ∼1012 peptides, yielded a 9-amino acid lariat peptide that is a potent (Ki = 7 nM) and selective inhibitor of HPA. Structural studies of the complex revealed once again a motif in which two tyrosines, separated by a proline, engage the active site residues. Thus, through screening of three completely distinct libraries, natural products from terrestrial and marine sources, as well as a large cyclic peptide library, a very similar inhibitory motif has been recovered in each case but from different classes of molecules-essentially a case of convergent inhibitor evolution.
