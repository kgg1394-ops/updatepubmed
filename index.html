This phase III, randomized, double-blind study (daNIS-2) evaluated the efficacy and safety of NIS793, an anti-transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) monoclonal antibody, in combination with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel [standard of care (SOC)] versus placebo and SOC as first-line treatment in patients with metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (mPDAC).

The study included a safety run-in (n = 21 patients enrolled) followed by a randomized part (n = 490 patients randomly assigned). Patients were assigned 1 : 1 to receive NIS793 or placebo with SOC. The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall response rate (ORR), safety, pharmacokinetics, and exploratory biomarker analyses.

The study was terminated early following an interim analysis due to an unfavorable benefit-risk profile. Median OS was 9.2 months in the NIS793 arm versus 11.2 months in the placebo arm (hazard ratio 1.26, 95% confidence interval 1.01-1.59). The estimates of ORR and median PFS indicate a less favorable treatment effect with NIS793. Safety profiles were comparable between arms, although higher rates of anemia, epistaxis, and rash were observed with NIS793; serious adverse events were more frequent in the NIS793 arm, with most being grade ≥3. Pharmacodynamic markers such as Pro-C3 supported target engagement. However, such target engagement did not translate into clinical benefit and early circulating tumor DNA dynamics were associated with less favorable outcomes. Biomarker analyses suggested that unfavorable benefit-risk profile of NIS793 was most pronounced in patients with a low epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition signature and a classical molecular subtype.

NIS793 in combination with SOC did not improve survival outcomes in first-line mPDAC and was associated with an unfavorable benefit-risk profile. Exploratory biomarker findings highlight the complexity of TGF-β signaling and underscore the need for refined patient selection strategies in future trials.

Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors (JAKis) are increasingly used to treat inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), but most pivotal data come from randomized controlled trials. Real-world evidence is needed to clarify their effectiveness and safety in routine clinical practice.

We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of real-world studies reporting outcomes in IBD patients treated with JAKis (tofacitinib, upadacitinib, or filgotinib). PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched from inception to August 11, 2025. Primary outcomes were clinical response and clinical remission; secondary outcomes included steroid-free clinical remission, endoscopic and biochemical responses, and safety outcomes. 63 studies involving 8130 IBD patients were included. In ulcerative colitis (UC), pooled clinical response and remission rates were 55% and 43% for tofacitinib, 77% and 65% for upadacitinib, and 69% and 55% for filgotinib; steroid-free remission with tofacitinib approached 50% at 1 year. In Crohn's disease (CD), upadacitinib achieved clinical response and remission in around two thirds of patients. Across agents, safety profiles were acceptable: mortality was 0%; hospitalization and treatment discontinuation rates were 8% and 28% for tofacitinib, 5% and 12% for upadacitinib, and 4% and 15% for filgotinib; serious adverse events occurred in ≤8% of patients.

Descriptive real-world data show that tofacitinib, upadacitinib, and filgotinib all provide clinically meaningful effectiveness in UC and CD, complementing evidence from randomized trials. Across real-world cohorts, JAKis have generally acceptable safety profiles, with low rates of serious adverse events and no observed drug-related mortality.

IgG4-related disease is a chronic, immune-mediated, systemic condition that presents as tumour-like masses. Since publication of the last international consensus statement on the treatment of IgG4-related disease in 2015, the related literature has expanded considerably, with two phase 3, randomised, placebo-controlled trials published within the past 2 years. This foundation of evidence for treatment decisions justified new, dedicated treatment guidelines. 62 investigators-comprising 52 physicians, one medical reference librarian, eight patients, and one caregiver from 15 countries across six continents-were assembled to form a treatment guidelines group. This effort provides cohesive, consensus-based guidelines from a geographically and professionally diverse group of experts in IgG4-related disease, reflecting the evolution in the field since 2015. Direct comparative evidence on the hinotori and da Vinci robotic systems in colorectal cancer surgery remains limited. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to compare their short-term perioperative outcomes. PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched for studies directly comparing hinotori with da Vinci robotic surgery in patients with colorectal cancer. Study-level data were pooled using random-effects models, with mean differences (MDs) for continuous outcomes and odds ratios (ORs) for dichotomous outcomes, each with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Five retrospective propensity-score-matched studies involving 462 patients (hinotori, n = 204; da Vinci, n = 258) were included. Operative time (MD 30.57 min, 95% CI 17.51-43.64; I² = 0%) and console time (MD 30.72 min, 95% CI 17.29-44.16; I² = 42%) were significantly longer with hinotori, whereas length of hospital stay was significantly shorter (MD - 1.58 days, 95% CI - 2.92 to - 0.24; I² = 63%). No statistically significant differences were detected for estimated blood loss (MD 3.42 mL, 95% CI - 6.98 to 13.82; I² = 66%) or for postoperative complications (grade I-II: OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.36-1.69; grade III-IV: OR 0.54, 95% CI 0.16-1.76; overall: OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.37-1.46). In this meta-analysis of propensity-score-matched studies, hinotori was associated with longer operative and console times and a shorter hospital stay than da Vinci, whereas no statistically significant difference was detected in blood loss or postoperative complications. Because clinically important differences in safety could not be excluded and residual confounding could not be ruled out, these findings should be regarded as preliminary; larger prospective comparative studies are warranted.

Drug-induced enterocolitis (DIC) consists of a heterogeneous group of gastrointestinal inflammatory disorders with clinical, endoscopic, and histological features that often overlap with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Given the increasing recognition of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced enterocolitis and the lack of standardized management algorithms, the European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) convened a multidisciplinary panel to review DIC, with a particular focus on ICI-induced disease.

Gastroenterologists, histopathologists, and oncologists systematically reviewed the literature using Medline, PubMed, EMBASE, and Scopus. Evidence was synthesized into ECCO Current Practice Positions addressing diagnostic principles, histopathological patterns, therapeutic strategies, and ICI-specific considerations. Statements were refined and approved through structured consensus.

Twenty-three practice positions were agreed upon. DIC may present with diverse histological patterns, including IBD-like/chronic enterocolitis, acute enterocolitis, microscopic colitis, ischemic-type injury, and mixed features, none of which are pathognomonic. Close clinicopathological collaboration is therefore essential. In suspected ICI-induced enterocolitis, assessment should include Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events-based grading, stool testing for pathogens, fecal calprotectin, endoscopy with biopsies, and imaging when indicated. Corticosteroids remain first-line therapy, while mild or microscopic disease may respond to budesonide or beclomethasone. Corticosteroid-refractory or corticosteroid-dependent disease should prompt early escalation to infliximab or vedolizumab. Surgery is reserved for refractory or complicated cases. Rechallenge with ICIs may be considered following multidisciplinary evaluation.

DIC represents a diagnostically complex spectrum of disorders that require integrated multidisciplinary evaluation. These ECCO Current Practice Positions provide a practical framework for diagnostic work-up and treatment, particularly for ICI-induced enterocolitis.

💡 Conclusion
AI-enabled digital medicine platforms in Europe are developed within overlapping rules for personal data, medical devices, AI, clinical research, health data access, and health technology assessment. This viewpoint represents the authors' experience and interpretation from the preimplementation phase of INTERCEPT, an Innovative Health Initiative consortium that plans to validate biomarkers and develop a blood-based risk score for early Crohn disease interception. It does not report original empirical research, a systematic regulatory review, a deployed platform, or a validated AI model. Our aim is to explain how compliance-by-design can be translated into an auditable governance architecture and to identify what still requires scientific evaluation. We advance 3 messages. First, governance must begin with intended purpose and boundary mapping: a digital platform may contain research, operational, and medical device software functions, and conformity of one module cannot be inferred for the platform as a whole. Second, overlapping obligations can be operationalized through 5 linked controls: modular responsibility and change control, federated data governance, traceable AI life cycle management, consent and participant rights orchestration, and a common cross-border governance core with local adaptations. Third, compliance is not a surrogate for model validity, clinical benefit, trust, scalability, or market access. Before clinical deployment, planned AI components require prespecified labels and cohorts, leakage prevention, missing data and imbalance strategies, calibration, subgroup assessment, external validation, human factors evaluation, and postdeployment algorithmovigilance. A hypothetical biomarker risk score update illustrates how the 5 controls interact. INTERCEPT is informative because it combines cross-border biomarker research, planned predictive analytics, and heterogeneous institutional settings while key design decisions remain modifiable. Its contribution is nevertheless conceptual: the proposed framework is a testable governance hypothesis, not evidence of implementation success. Prospective studies should compare it with alternative governance models and assess safety, workload, participant experience, evidence generation, and regulatory performance.

A questionnaire-based study using two clinical vignettes was conducted by the ENETS MEN1 Task Force across all 68 ENETS CoEs, with a predefined participation target of 70%. Additional non-ENETS expert centers were also invited. Dedicated multidisciplinary teams provided institutional consensus responses. Scenarios included non-metastatic disease, locally metastatic disease, and distant metastatic disease. Centers were stratified by volume using a cutoff of 30 MEN1 patients in active care. Data were analyzed descriptively, comparing management strategies between low- and high-volume centers. Fifty-seven centers participated (52 of 68 ENETS CoEs [76.5%] and 5 additional non-ENETS expert centers). All 57 centers completed the questionnaire in full. All cases received symptomatic therapy with proton pump inhibitors. In non-metastatic disease, management was heterogeneous, with 56.1% recommending surgery. Centre volume strongly influenced decisions (73.1% vs. 41.9% surgery in low- vs. high-volume centers, p = .018). Preferred surgical approaches included duodenotomy with regional lymphadenectomy (43.9%) and the Thompson procedure (left pancreatectomy, duodenal exploration with excision of tumors and node dissection, enucleation of pancreatic head lesions; 26.3%). In locally metastatic disease, surgical management increased to 78.9%, with a shift toward more extensive procedures including pancreaticoduodenectomy (35.1%). In distant metastatic disease, somatostatin analogues were the systemic therapy of choice (50.9%), followed by curative-intent surgery with pancreaticoduodenectomy or duodenotomy with regional lymphadenectomy combined with liver resection or thermal ablation of liver lesions (35.1%), while no other systemic treatments were selected by any center. A liver biopsy was recommended by 43.9% of centers. Management of MEN1-related ZES demonstrates stage-dependent convergence. In early disease, decision-making-particularly regarding surgical versus medical management-is highly variable and influenced by center experience, whereas advanced disease shows more consistent, guideline-aligned treatment patterns. The high participation rate across ENETS CoEs suggests that these findings are likely to be relevant to expert neuroendocrine tumor practice.

Over 17% of gastrointestinal (GI) malignancies involve deficient DNA damage repair (DDR) mechanisms that have poor outcomes on standard chemotherapy but are sensitive to targeted therapies like immune checkpoint inhibitors and poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors. Lurbinectedin is an alkylating agent that demonstrated a 35.2% overall response rate (ORR) as second-line therapy in small-cell lung cancer patients who progressed on platinum-based chemotherapies. The drug inhibits DDR, promoting apoptosis in tumor cells through double-stranded DNA breaks (DSBs). Since repair-deficient tumors cannot efficiently and adequately fix these DSBs, DNA damage accumulates and results in enhanced tumor killing. Therefore, lurbinectedin's mechanism makes it suitable for use in cancers with DDR mutations. Our study evaluated the efficacy of lurbinectedin monotherapy in patients with advanced GI malignancies who harbored DDR mutations.

This was a prospective open-label, single-arm phase II study at a single institution. Participants were age ≥18 years, had Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score ≤1, and locally advanced unresectable or metastatic GI cancer with ≥1 deleterious DDR mutation. Intravenous lurbinectedin was administered at 3.2 mg/m

Eight participants (7 pancreatic cancer, 1 rectal cancer) with at least two prior therapy lines were enrolled and had DDR mutations in ATM (25.0%), ARID1A (62.5%), BRCA1 (25.0%), CHEK2 (12.5%), and FANC (12.5%).
