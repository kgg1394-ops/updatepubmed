Bacterial infections are a major cause of mortality in cirrhosis, with antibiotic prophylaxis and empirical treatment embedded in international guidelines. However, the prevalence of multidrug-resistant organisms and the epidemiology of infections have shifted considerably in recent decades. This review critically appraises the evidence underlying guideline recommendations on antibiotic prophylaxis for spontaneous bacterial peritonitis and acute variceal bleeding in cirrhosis, and antimicrobial management in acute liver failure. Colorectal cancer (CRC), is a highly prevalent malignant tumor worldwide, and early screening is key to improving its prognosis. Currently, CRC screening research is growing exponentially, but its core research hotspots, evolutionary trends, and interdisciplinary features are not clear. In this study, we used bibliometric analysis to analyze the distribution of literature, research hotspots, and cutting-edge developments in the field of CRC screening worldwide, and to provide a scientific basis for optimizing screening strategies.

Publications on CRC screening from 2015 to 2024 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. VOSviewer (v1.6.20), RStudio (v4.4.1), and CiteSpace (v6.4.2) were used for bibliometric and visual analyses. The main analyses included publication trends, countries, authors, journals, references, and keywords.

A total of 3245 publications were included in the analysis. Global publications on CRC screening showed a steady upward trend over the past decade, with an annual growth rate of 2.17%. The United States contributed the highest number of publications, and the University of North Carolina ranked as the most productive institution. GASTROENTEROLOGY and GUT were the most frequently cited journals in this field. Lansdorp‑Vogelaar I was among the most prolific authors. The most cited article was "CRC screening for average-risk adults: 2018 guideline update from the American Cancer Society." Keyword analysis showed that the research hotspots in this field included colonoscopy, biomarker, adenoma detection rate, artificial intelligence, etc.

Current research in CRC screening focuses on traditional screening methods, population-related factors, and emerging technologies, particularly artificial intelligence. Stronger international collaboration and interdisciplinary studies are needed to advance the field.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
German Clinical Trials Register, DRKS00036327 (retrospectively registered, 22.04.2025).
Early mobilization is a key component of enhanced recovery after elective colorectal surgery. This pilot trial evaluated the safety and feasibility of an early supervised postoperative exercise intervention in addition to standard care.

In this single-center randomized controlled pilot trial, 31 patients undergoing elective colorectal surgery were randomized to an intervention group (n = 16) or control group (n = 15). One patient withdrew after randomization, leaving 30 patients for modified intention-to-treat analysis. The intervention consisted of individualized, physiotherapist-supervised exercise sessions on postoperative days 1, 3, and 5. The control group received standard postoperative care according to the institutional ERAS protocol. The originally registered primary endpoint was time to first bowel movement; prior to final analysis, safety and feasibility were designated as the primary outcomes in accordance with the pilot nature of the study. The change was documented transparently. Secondary outcomes included physical performance (Short Physical Performance Battery [SPPB], handgrip strength), time to first bowel movement, patient-reported outcomes, and postoperative complications.

Thirty patients completed the study (retention 96.8%). Adherence to the intervention was 100%, and no intervention-related adverse events occurred. The intervention group showed significantly better preservation of physical performance, reflected by a more favorable change in SPPB score from baseline to postoperative assessment. No significant between-group differences were observed for time to first bowel movement, handgrip strength, postoperative complications, or other secondary outcomes.

Early supervised postoperative exercise after elective colorectal surgery was safe and feasible. Although no differences were found for most secondary outcomes, the intervention improved postoperative physical performance. These exploratory findings warrant confirmation in adequately powered randomized trials.

▶Exercise as anticancer medicine? Evidence, mechanisms and implementation in oncology.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Research over the past two decades has repositioned exercise from a general lifestyle recommendation to a potential anticancer intervention. Pooled analyses of more than 1.4 million participants link regular physical activity with lower incidence of at least 13 tumour types, and prospective cohort data report cancer-specific mortality 24-31% lower among the most active patients with breast, colorectal, prostate and lung cancer, although these estimates derive from observational designs using self-reported activity. In 2025, a phase III trial in stage III colon cancer provided the first randomised evidence that a structured exercise and behaviour-change programme improves disease-free and overall survival after adjuvant chemotherapy; a randomised trial in ovarian cancer subsequently did not show a progression-free survival benefit. Candidate mechanisms include insulin and growth-factor signalling, myokine secretion, immune mobilisation, reduced chronic inflammation and gut microbiome remodelling, although these rest on evidence ranging from cell culture to human trials and are rarely distinguished by level. Despite this evidence, structural, educational and economic barriers prevent integration of exercise into routine oncology care. This Review synthesises the available evidence and identifies the steps required for clinical adoption.

⚡ Action: ⚠️ 기존 유지 권장

💡 Conclusion
The epidemiology of nutritional disorders in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) remains poorly characterized. Existing evidence is fragmented across specific conditions or confined to single regions, and no study has simultaneously assessed the principal nutritional disorders-malnutrition, overweight or obesity, and sarcopenia-in a unified framework. To address this gap, we systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and China Biology Medicine from inception to July 31, 2024 and updated on July 30, 2026. Observational studies reporting prevalence based on standard diagnostic criteria were included. Pooled estimates with 95% confidence interval (CI) and prediction interval (PI) were derived using a random-effects model. Pre-specified subgroup analyses were conducted by sex, age, disease subtype and activity, diagnostic criteria, study setting and gepgraphic variation. Study quality and publication bias were also assessed. After literature screening, 201 studies across 32 countries were included. Underweight affected 11.5% (95% CI: 9.0-14.6%, 95% PI : 1.0-61.7), whereas consensus-based malnutrition [GLIM (Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition), ESPEN (European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism), MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment), SGA/PG-SGA (Subjective Global Assessment or its variant Patient-Generated SGA) affected 27.6% (95% CI: 20.5-36.1%, 95% PI : 3.6-79.7). Notably, this malnutrition estimate was derived almost exclusively from hospital-based settings (over 80%) and predominantly from a single region. Overweight and obesity affected 23.1% (95% CI : 20.6-25.9, 95%PI : 8.1-50.8) and 13.5% (95%CI : 11.7-15.6, 95%PI : 2.9-44.6), respectively. For muscle depletion, low muscle mass defined by skeletal muscle index was identified in 48.3% (95% CI: 41.8-55.0%, 95% PI : 19.7-78.1), whereas consensus-based sarcopenia [EWGSOP (European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People) or AWGS (Asian Working Group on Sarcopenia)] affected 12.6% (95% CI: 6.1-24.2%, 95% PI : 0.9-70.6). Of note, the majority of included studies for malnutrition and sarcopenia were hospital-based. Active disease was associated with significantly higher prevalence of malnutrition. No significant differences were observed between disease subtypes. In conclusion, our findings provide a broad overview of the prevalence of major nutritional disorders in patients with IBD, highlighting that nutritional care in IBD should extend beyond simple weight monitoring. However, given the wide prediction intervals, these pooled estimates should be viewed as averages across highly heterogeneous settings rather than a precise global figure. Future research, particularly population-based studies with standardized diagnostic criteria across diverse regions, is needed to validate our observation.

▶Joint Model for Assessing the Effect of Frailty and Longitudinal Measures in Time-To-Event Outcome.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Joint modeling provides a robust statistical framework for simultaneously modeling longitudinal covariate and time-to-event outcome. Apart from the interdependence between longitudinal and survival outcomes there may exist some unobserved random heterogeneity which can be modeled using a joint frailty model (JFM). Unlike existing JFMs, where two survival events are modeled using a frailty, our framework incorporates frailty into the survival sub-model to accommodate an additional source of variation at the survival endpoint. So here we developed a novel JFM and estimated the parameters using maximum-likelihood estimation method. The analyses were performed using R Software and illustrated using colorectal cancer data. The frailty component was introduced in colorectal cancer data to account for the unobserved variability in survival risk beyond the observed clinical characteristics. The highly significant frailty variance confirmed the presence of unobserved heterogeneity in survival outcomes. When compared with the standard Joint model (JM), the JFM demonstrated improved model fit and captured additional unexplained heterogeneity among individuals, leading to more precise hazard estimates. Further we conducted Monte Carlo simulations to compare the performance of the proposed model under various simulation scenarios (censoring [20% and 50%], frailty variance (≈0, 0.5, 1), and sample sizes [n = 100, 250, 500]). Performance was assessed using bias, root mean squared error and model fit indices. The findings highlight the importance of incorporating the frailty term in joint modeling, particularly in cancer prognostic studies where individual heterogeneity plays a crucial role.

▶Deploying machine learning and metagenomics for predicting gut microbial drug biotransformation in colorectal cancer.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the leading causes of death globally. Chemotherapy is considered as one of the standard treatment option for CRC. Although effective in many cases, drugs used in chemotherapy are often associated with adverse toxicity. Inter-individual variations in drug response are often attributed to pharmacogenomic associations, and current evidence suggests involvement of gut microbes as a contributor to drug response variations. While several studies have implemented machine learning to predict drug responses mediated by gut microbial enzymes, these predictions mainly rely on substrate information and bacterial genome data. In this study, we have developed CRCGMMeta Predictor, a tool that predicts gut microbial enzymes and species capable of biotransforming drugs. Unlike existing tools, CRCGMMeta Predictor integrates enzyme prediction with species-level abundance in CRC metagenomes using eight geographically diverse metagenomic studies. The tool was trained on 437,079 species-specific enzyme data, incorporating 3457 substrate and protein-encoding features. Multiple machine learning models were evaluated, with the random forest model achieving 99% accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score and ROC-AUC metrics. Validation of the generated model with a blind set achieved 97% accuracy, while 10-fold cross-validation achieved an accuracy of 0.831 ± 0.068. Based on the above results, the random forest model was selected to build the CRCGMMeta Predictor tool. This was further assessed against fluoropyrimidine drugs and its ability to predict enzymes supported by literature evidence and those predicted by existing computational tools. These findings highlight the potential of CRCGMMeta Predictor in identifying microbiome-mediated drug responses.

Complement activation is a key driver of disease progression in IgA nephropathy (IgAN). While previous syntheses were limited to phase II evidence, pivotal phase III data-including the 24-month results of the APPLAUSE-IgAN trial-have recently emerged, necessitating an updated appraisal of complement-targeted therapies.

We conducted a systematic literature search in PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane up to April 24, 2026, for randomized controlled trials of complement inhibitors in adults with biopsy-proven IgA nephropathy. Due to marked mechanistic heterogeneity across agents targeting distinct complement nodes, a quantitative meta-analysis was not performed. Instead, we performed a structured descriptive synthesis, a predefined, domain-based comparative method, focusing on efficacy signals, safety profiles, and methodological disparities across trials.

Four RCTs comprising 584 patients were included, representing factor B and C5 inhibition. The phase III iptacopan trial demonstrated robust proteinuria reduction, eGFR slope attenuation, and a decreased risk of kidney failure, but a higher serious-infection rate and confirmed pneumococcal infections highlighted that alternative-pathway inhibition attenuates rather than abolishes encapsulated-bacteria risk. Phase II trials of ravulizumab, iptacopan, and cemdisiran provided early efficacy signals with no encapsulated-bacterial events reported, though small size and short follow-up limit safety inference. All trials required prophylactic vaccination, and infection-risk obligations differed by target-terminal-pathway inhibitors carrying the strictest REMS requirements. Methodological heterogeneity-particularly regarding biopsy timing and fibrosis exclusion-emerged as a major barrier to cross-trial comparisons. Beyond these RCTs, a dynamic pipeline of ASO, siRNA, and small-molecule inhibitors continues to evolve.

Complement inhibitors show promising but heterogeneous signals across individual trials; however, marked differences in molecular targets, trial design, and eligibility preclude any inference of a uniform class effect. Infection risk mitigation differs by complement target-terminal-pathway inhibitors carry the highest encapsulated-bacterial burden and strictest regulatory constraints, whereas alternative-pathway inhibitors preserve partial vaccine-mediated defence but still require comprehensive vaccination and surveillance. These observations are hypothesis-generating and underscore the need for mechanism-specific phase III programmes with hard renal endpoints and individual patient-level meta-analyses to advance precision complement therapeutics in IgAN.

This study aimed to compare the performance of nomograms and deep learning (DL) models in predicting the pre-operative pathological grade of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) using clinical data and ultrasound images, with an emphasis on model robustness and generalizability across institutions.

We retrospectively analyzed 2404 pre-operative ultrasound images from 601 patients with pathologically confirmed HCC across two medical centers. Two distinct modeling strategies were employed: two nomograms integrating ultrasound imaging features (B-mode and contrast-enhanced ultrasound [CEUS]) with clinical variables, and a series of DL models based on B-mode, CEUS at arterial phases, portal phases and delayed phases, and a combined B + CEUS modality. The nomograms demonstrated strong and consistent performance in predicting HCC pathological grade, with AUCs of 0.771 (internal validation) and 0.762 (external validation) when trained on Center 1 data, and AUCs of 0.814 (internal) and 0.818 (external) when trained on Center 2 data. The nomogram exhibited superior robustness and generalizability compared to DL models in heterogeneous clinical populations. These findings highlight the practical advantage of interpretable statistical models in settings with variable data distributions and reinforce the value of feature-based approaches when data diversity is limited.

This two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) study investigated the potential causal link between genetically determined insomnia and various cancers, including brain, colorectal, esophageal, gastric, hepatic bile duct, liver, lung, malignant lymphoma, pancreatic, skin, and thyroid cancers. Summary statistics from genome-wide association studies for insomnia and eleven cancer types were examined. instrumental variables (IVs) were selected from single nucleotide polymorphisms that passed strict quality control for minimal linkage disequilibrium and genome-wide significance. All IVs demonstrated strong predictive power for insomnia, with F-statistics exceeding the conventional threshold of 10. The primary analysis was conducted using the inverse variance weighted method. Sensitivity analyses were performed using MR-Egger regression, weighted median, and weighted mode methods to test robustness, pleiotropy, and heterogeneity. MR analysis suggested potential causal associations between insomnia and increased risk of hepatic bile duct cancer (odds ratio [OR] = 4.458, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.074-18.493, P = .040), reduced risk of hepatic cancer (OR = 0.312, 95% CI: 0.100-0.973, P = .045), and reduced risk of skin cancers (OR = 0.599, 95% CI: 0.439-0.818, P = .001). Sensitivity analyses showed no evidence of reverse causality or significant bias. These findings, though not retaining statistical significance after stringent multiple testing correction, provide suggestive evidence that genetically predicted insomnia may influence the risk of certain cancers, potentially through mechanisms involving genetic predisposition and biological pathways that modulate inflammation, immune function, and circadian rhythms. The study highlights the translational value of understanding insomnia's role in cancer risk, suggesting that clinicians should consider this association when managing patients with insomnia. Further research with larger sample sizes is warranted to validate these findings.
