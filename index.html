▶Optimizing first-line NALIRIFOX and exploring the role of the microbiota in second-line pancreatic cancer treatment: the PRODIGE 105-GERCOR 116 PANORAMIX trial.

⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
This two-step trial will address key therapeutic questions in mPDAC and support translational research.
Treatment of metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (mPDAC) relies on chemotherapy. Although NALIRIFOX (Nal-IRI, oxaliplatin, 5-fluorouracil [5-FU], leucovorin) improves survival, cumulative toxicity may limit duration, and maintenance strategies remain unexplored. Intratumoral microbiota, particularly Gammaproteobacteria expressing cytidine deaminase, may inactivate gemcitabine and contribute to resistance, a process potentially reversible with antibiotics. We aim to assess maintenance 5-FU after NALIRIFOX and microbiota modulation with fluoroquinolones to enhance gemcitabine sensitivity.

PANORAMIX is a multicenter, proof-of-concept, non-comparative phase II trial conducted across 18 French centers with two sequential randomizations. After 8 cycles of NALIRIFOX, patients with mPDAC are randomized (2:1) to either maintenance 5-FU or continuation of NALIRIFOX (Step 1). In Step 2, patients are randomized (1:1) to ciprofloxacin or placebo combined with gemcitabine-based chemotherapy (± paclitaxel). A total of 108 and 92 patients will be enrolled in Steps 1 and 2, respectively. Main inclusion criteria include age >18 years, confirmed mPDAC, ECOG performance status 0-1, and adequate organ function. Primary endpoints are 6-month progression-free survival (Step 1) and 6-month overall survival (Step 2). Secondary endpoints include toxicity and quality of life.

This two-step trial will address key therapeutic questions in mPDAC and support translational research.

▶Radioembolization for Colorectal Liver Metastases: Indications and Outcomes.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Colorectal cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with the liver representing the predominant site of metastatic spread. For patients with colorectal liver metastases (CRLM) who are not candidates for surgical resection, systemic chemotherapy provides disease control but rarely achieves durable hepatic responses. Radioembolization with yttrium-90 (Y-90) microspheres, delivered via the hepatic artery to exploit the preferential arterial supply of liver tumors, has emerged as an important locoregional option in this setting. This review examines the current evidence base for radioembolization in CRLM, covering patient selection, indications across lines of therapy, dosimetric advances, and clinical outcomes. Appropriate candidacy requires careful assessment of performance status, hepatic tumor burden, tumor biology, lung shunt fraction, and extrahepatic disease extent, with the strongest outcomes observed in patients selected earlier in their disease course. Randomized controlled trials support second-line and third-line use, while unselected first-line use has not demonstrated a benefit. Dosimetric advances, including multicompartment personalized dosing, radiation segmentectomy, and radiation lobectomy, are enabling ablative and bridge-to-resection strategies in appropriately selected patients. Future investigation should prioritize biomarker-driven patient selection, personalized dosimetry, and combination strategies with systemic therapy to determine whether durable hepatic disease control can translate into a survival benefit.

▶Lactose, Lactose Maldigestion and Lactose Intolerance: Definitions, Misunderstandings, Mechanisms, and Public Health Implications.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Lactose is the principal carbohydrate in milk, serving a critical role in infant energy metabolism as well as contributing to neurodevelopment, and the establishment of a healthy gut microbiota. Later in life, lactose digestion capacity varies widely across human populations owing to the genetically determined decline of intestinal lactase expression after weaning-a condition termed 'lactase non-persistence'. Approximately two-thirds of the global adult population exhibits reduced lactase activity, yet the clinical consequences of this enzymatic reduction are far less uniform than commonly assumed. This review provides a comprehensive examination of lactose functionality, the physiology of lactose digestion, and the consequences of lactase persistence and non-persistence. We present formal definitions for the spectrum of terms used in lactose research-including lactase persistence, lactase non-persistence, lactose maldigestion, and lactose intolerance-and argue that the critical distinction between lactose maldigestion (a physiological state) and lactose intolerance (a clinical syndrome) is frequently overlooked in both research and public health. Drawing on evidence from double-blind randomised controlled trials , microbiome intervention studies, and global epidemiological data, the evidence indicates that the majority of individuals with lactose maldigestion do not develop clinically meaningful symptoms at moderate intake levels of most dairy products. We further discuss the mechanism of colonic adaptation, whereby gradual lactose exposure selectively enriches Bifidobacterium populations capable of fermenting lactose without excessive gas production resulting in greater lactose tolerance. Finally, we examine the public health implications of these findings, noting that food-based dietary guidelines worldwide-including those in populations with high prevalence of lactase non-persistence-consistently recommend dairy consumption owing to its established associations with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes, colorectal cancer, and improved bone health.

💡 Conclusion
A simple endoscopic classification based on valve location, disruption, and hiatal opening reliably identifies fundoplication failure and correlates with symptoms, manometric features, and reflux burden.
We aimed to validate individual endoscopic parameters against clinical and functional testing and to build a simple classification of fundoplication integrity.

Endoscopy is the main tool to assess the gastroesophageal valve after antireflux surgery (ARS), but existing classifications are complex and have never been validated against objective pathophysiologic data.

This was a single-center, two-phase cohort study of adults undergoing endoscopy, high-resolution manometry (HRM), and reflux monitoring after ARS. Retroflexed videoclips were reviewed blinded to functional results. In the development cohort (2018-2024), endoscopic parameters were tested against pathologic GERD (Lyon 2.0 criteria); those correlating with reflux were included in the classification and the population was stratified according to the number of impaired mechanisms (0-3). In the second phase, the classification was validated prospectively (2024-2026).

Of 122 patients, 73 formed the development and 49 the validation cohort. Three parameters were associated with pathologic GERD: intrathoracic valve, disrupted valve, and visible hiatus. Clinical, manometric, and reflux metrics worsened stepwise with the number of impaired mechanisms, and pathologic GERD rose from 11.8% to 37.5% to 85.7%. The new classification discriminated pathologic GERD with an area under the curve of 0.860 in the development and 0.870 in the validation cohort (0.869 overall).

A simple endoscopic classification based on valve location, disruption, and hiatal opening reliably identifies fundoplication failure and correlates with symptoms, manometric features, and reflux burden.

▶Development and Validation of an UPLC-QqQ MS/MS Method for the Determination of Daraxonrasib, a New pan-KRAS Inhibitor, in Dog Plasma and Whole Blood and Its Application to Pharmacokinetic Studies.

💡 Conclusion
Daraxonrasib, an orally administered pan-RAS inhibitor, is undergoing clinical evaluation for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, colorectal cancer, and non-small cell lung cancer. Defining its pharmacokinetic profile is therefore critical for ongoing progress. Accordingly, a simple and sensitive UPLC-QqQ-MS/MS assay was developed and validated to quantify daraxonrasib in canine plasma and whole blood. Proteins were precipitated with a 20:80 (v/v) acetonitrile-methanol solution containing 0.1% formic acid. Chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY BEH C18 column (50 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm). The mobile phase consisted of 2 mM ammonium acetate and acetonitrile, each containing 0.1% formic acid, delivered at 0.4 mL/min. Multiple reaction monitoring transitions for daraxonrasib and the internal standard were m/z 811.5 → 680.3 and m/z 724.4 → 396.2, respectively. Calibration standards showed linear response from 1.0 to 1000 ng/mL (r > 0.998). The assay met validation criteria for specificity, recovery, limit of quantification, accuracy, and precision. Finally, this validated method was applied to a dog pharmacokinetic study of daraxonrasib. This report outlines the drug's pharmacokinetic characteristics and supports its continued development.

Medicines that reduce stomach acid are widely used to treat conditions such as heartburn, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and stomach infection caused by Helicobacter pylori. For many years, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) have been the standard treatment, but they can take time to work, need to be taken before meals, and may not fully control acidity during the night. A newer class of drugs, called potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs), has been developed to address some of these limitations. This review explains how one of these newer medicines, fexuprazan, works and summarizes current research on its effectiveness and safety. Studies show that fexuprazan can reduce stomach acid quickly and consistently, without being affected by food intake. In clinical trials, it healed esophageal damage from acid reflux as effectively as established treatments, with a trend for faster symptom relief and better night-time control. It may also help symptoms beyond typical heartburn, such as chronic cough or hoarseness. Early research suggests that fexuprazan could protect the lining of the digestive tract and improve treatment success for H. pylori infection. Overall, this medication may represent a flexible new option for managing acid-related diseases.

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are increasingly used to treat type 2 diabetes, yet little is known about its utilization patterns in patients with cancer history, given limited oncology-specific guidance.

To evaluate trends and factors associated with GLP-1 RA use among Medicare beneficiaries with type 2 diabetes, comparing those with and without cancer history.

This retrospective cohort study used Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results-Medicare linked data to identify patients diagnosed with 7 common cancers in 2010 to 2021 and preexisting type 2 diabetes before cancer diagnosis. A noncancer cohort with type 2 diabetes was identified from a 5% random sample of Medicare beneficiaries and matched 1:1 to patients with cancer based on sociodemographic characteristics; controls were assigned a pseudo-index date corresponding to their matched patient's cancer diagnosis date. Beneficiaries were followed up annually until death or December 31, 2022, for TM or December 31, 2021, for MA. Data were analyzed from December 2025 to July 2026.

Diagnosis of 1 of 7 common cancers.

Any GLP-1 RA use. Proportions of beneficiaries with any GLP-1 RA use annually were stratified by traditional Medicare (TM) and Medicare Advantage (MA). Multivariable logistic regression examined (1) differences in GLP-1RA use between cancer and noncancer cohorts with and without adjusting for sociodemographic characteristics and comorbidities and diabetes-related complications; (2) cancer-specific factors associated with GLP-1RA use among the cancer cohort after 2018.

A total of 373 278 Medicare beneficiaries (mean [SD] age, 76.1 [6.6] years; 188 746 [50.6%] female) with cancer and diabetes and 373 278 noncancer controls (153 358 unique beneficiaries) were included. GLP-1 RA use increased from 85 beneficiaries (0.5%) to 9083 beneficiaries (8.2%) and from 138 beneficiaries (0.9%) to 9300 beneficiaries (6.2%) in the cancer and noncancer cohorts, respectively, from 2010 to 2022 under TM; a similar increase was observed under MA. Despite higher unadjusted GLP-1 RA use among the cancer cohort than the noncancer cohort (probability difference, TM: 1.19 [95% CI, 0.94 to 1.44] percentage points [pp]; P < .001; MA: 0.45 [95% CI, 0.21 to 0.68] pp, P < .001), adjusted analysis showed no significant differences in GLP-1 RA use under TM (adjusted probability difference, 0.16 [95% CI, -0.10 to 0.41,] pp; P = .22) and lower use among cancer cohort under MA (-0.52 [95% CI, -0.76 to -0.28] pp; P < .001). Complications or comorbidities, including obesity (adjusted probability difference, TM: 3.35 [95% CI, 3.10 to 3.61] pp; P < .001; MA: 2.42 [95% CI, 2.19 to 2.64] pp; P < .001), dyslipidemia (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.26 [95% CI, 2.03 to 2.50] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.80 [95% CI, 1.57 to 2.03] pp; P < .001), neuropathy (adjusted probability difference: TM: 3.91 [95% CI, 3.64 to 4.19] pp; P < .001; MA: 3.17 [95% CI, 2.94 to 3.41] pp; P < .001), retinopathy (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.02 [95% CI, 1.78 to 2.27] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.84 [95% CI, 1.63 to 2.06] pp; P < .001), and chronic kidney disease (adjusted probability difference, TM: 2.01 [95% CI, 1.76 to 2.26] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.63 [95% CI, 1.40 to 1.85] pp; P < .001) were associated with greater likelihood of GLP-1RA use. Within the cancer cohort, breast (adjusted probability difference, TM: 1.05 [95% CI, 0.43 to 1.66] pp; P = .001; MA: 0.89 [95% CI, 0.37 to 1.41] pp; P = .001), gynecologic (adjusted probability difference, MA: 1.13 [95% CI, 0.45 to 1.81] pp; P = .001), and prostate (adjusted probability difference, TM: 1.30 [95% CI, 0.72 to 1.88] pp; P < .001; MA: 1.07 [95% CI, 0.56 to 1.58] pp; P < .001) cancers were associated with higher GLP-1 RA use than colorectal cancer, while pancreatic cancer was associated with lower GLP-1 RA use (adjusted probability difference, TM: -2.55 [95% CI, -3.17 to -1.93] pp; P < .001; MA: -1.64 [95% CI, -2.23 to -1.05] pp; P < .001).

Evaluate alignment between calculated Diagnosis-Intervention Packet (DIP) payment entitlements and observed inpatient costs and identify insurance, regional, and disease-group patterns in payment-cost misalignment.

Retrospective observational study of all inpatient final settlements (2023-2024).

We analyzed 23,597 discharge settlements using linear mixed-effects models with DIP group as a random intercept and heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors to compare signed payment-cost gaps. Robustness was assessed with model diagnostics and 1st-99th percentile trimming; separate gamma-log mixed models evaluated total cost and calculated DIP payment. Analyses compared insurance type (UEBMI vs. URRBMI) and insured region (local vs. non-local); high-volume DIP groups were defined as those with at least 150 admissions, and groups with cost-to-calculated-payment ratios of at least 3.0 were profiled as descriptive outliers.

Mean total cost per admission was CNY 14,604 and mean calculated DIP payment was CNY 4865, equivalent to 33.3% of observed inpatient cost. The mean signed payment-cost gap was -CNY 9739. Adjusted gaps were more negative for UEBMI than URRBMI (-CNY 12,971 vs. -CNY 8936) and for non-local than local admissions (-CNY 11,796 vs. -CNY 9279); trimmed analyses were consistent. Ten high-volume cerebrovascular, oncologic, renal, cardiac, infectious, and pancreatic clusters accounted for 18.8% of episodes and 35.6% of the cohort's net payment-cost shortfall.

At this county hospital, calculated DIP payments were equivalent to about one-third of observed inpatient spending, with concentrated shortfalls in complex neurovascular and oncologic groups and larger adjusted gaps among UEBMI and non-local admissions.
