These updated national guidelines were developed jointly by the Saudi Gastroenterology Association, Saudi Society of Colon and Rectal Surgery, Saudi Society of Gastrointestinal Oncology, and Saudi Society of Family and Community Medicine, and provide evidence-based recommendations for colorectal cancer (CRC) screening in Saudi Arabia, superseding the 2015 national guidelines. The study strictly followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines (Supplementary Material) and was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration ID: CRD42024557923).
The aim of this study is to quantitatively describe the characteristics of disease progression and its influencing factors in the placebo group of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis clinical trials, and to provide evidence for the optimal design of clinical trials.

PubMed, Embase and Cochrane Library were searched for placebo-randomized controlled trials on IPF up to November 12, 2024. We conducted a time-course modeling analysis of the placebo group based on commonly reported evaluation indicators in clinical trials. Using the established model, we simulated the distribution of the placebo effect for each index under various conditions and applied this to provide a quantitative external control.

A total of 32 studies were included. Common evaluation measures in clinical trials include all-cause mortality, forced vital capacity (FVC), 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) and Diffusing Capacity of the Lung for Carbon Monoxide (DLCO). Based on the final model, this study simulated typical effect sizes after different times of placebo intervention. Subgroup analysis showed that background treatment and gender also showed consistent trends in the effects on the above efficacy indicators. The potential utility of this model in drug effectiveness evaluation is demonstrated through a preliminary case study.

This study established a disease progression model for the placebo group based on common evaluation indicators in IPF clinical trials. This model helps conserve valuable clinical resources for rare diseases and promotes the development of new drugs.

The study strictly followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines (Supplementary Material) and was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration ID: CRD42024557923).

Mood and anxiety disorders are common in cancer survivors, and are associated with worse outcomes; however, systematic identification of high-risk patients remains limited, despite screening and intervention guidelines. A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted to identify risk factors for mood and anxiety disorders, including diagnoses and clinically significant symptoms, in adult cancer survivors.

PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, PsycINFO, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials were searched for cohort and case-control studies published from January 2000 to November 2024 that evaluated cancer-related, sociodemographic, lifestyle, and psychosocial risk factors in adults with breast, lung, colorectal, prostate, stomach, liver, or pancreatic cancer. Random-effects models with robust variance estimation pooled relative risks (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for factors assessed in ≥5 studies.

Fifty of the 53 eligible studies were included in the meta-analysis. Consistent risk factors included high tumor stage (vs. low; n = 28; RR, 1.55; 95% CI, 1.21-1.97), more physical comorbidities (vs. fewer; n = 36; RR, 1.57; 95% CI, 1.34-1.84), prior psychiatric history (high vs. low burden; n = 13; RR, 4.85; 95% CI, 2.77-8.50), female sex (vs. male; n = 19; RR, 1.52; 95% CI, 1.32-1.75), underweight body mass index (vs. normal; n = 6; RR, 1.93; 95% CI, 1.55-2.44), unmarried status (vs. married; n = 25; RR, 1.35; 95% CI, 1.14-1.58), and current smoking (vs. never; n = 12; RR, 1.27; 95% CI, 1.12-1.46). Suggestive associations, although less consistent in sensitivity analyses, were observed for high tumor grade, more extensive surgery, low income, low education, and low social support.

Several clinical and social factors were associated with increased risk of mood and anxiety outcomes in adult cancer survivors. These findings may support risk-based psychiatric screening and early intervention in resource-limited oncology settings.

▶Cancer Risk in Premature Ovarian Insufficiency: Current Evidence and Clinical Implications.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI), defined as loss of ovarian function before 40 years with elevated gonadotropins and oligo/amenorrhea, is increasingly recognized as a systemic condition with long-term health consequences. While cardiovascular, skeletal, and neurocognitive risks are well established, the relationship between POI and cancer risk remains less clearly understood. This mini-review summarizes current evidence on site-specific malignancy risks in women with POI and evaluates the influence of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) on cancer outcomes. Available epidemiological studies, cohort analyses, and guideline recommendations addressing cancer incidence in POI were reviewed, with emphasis on breast, ovarian, endometrial, colorectal, and autoimmune-associated malignancies. Women with spontaneous POI generally demonstrate a reduced risk of breast, ovarian, and endometrial cancers, largely attributed to lower lifetime estrogen exposure and fewer ovulatory cycles. However, cancer risk may vary by etiology: autoimmune POI is associated with increased thyroid cancer incidence, while genetic subgroups such as fragile X mental retardation 1 premutation carriers may exhibit altered ovarian cancer susceptibility. Importantly, physiological HRT in POI differs fundamentally from postmenopausal hormone therapy and does not appear to increase overall cancer risk when continued until the average age of natural menopause. POI is typically associated with lower risks of hormone-dependent malignancies, though subgroup-specific variations exist. Individualized counseling, appropriate progestin opposition, and further POI-specific cohort studies are essential to refine cancer surveillance strategies.

💡 Conclusion
INCB001158 was generally well tolerated and associated with increases in arginine levels and responses in the BTC cohort, suggesting that combination regimens with chemotherapy may provide clinical benefit for some patients.
Arginase is overexpressed in many advanced cancers, which can deplete arginine in tumors to promote immunosuppression. Arginase inhibition may enhance the efficacy of standard chemotherapy regimens by increasing arginine levels and modulating arginine-mediated immune function. INCB001158 is a potent and selective arginase inhibitor that was assessed in combination with chemotherapy.

This was an open-label, nonrandomized, phase 1/2 dose-escalation/expansion study in adults with advanced or metastatic solid tumors. INCB001158 was administered orally twice daily (bid) in combination with chemotherapy (mFOLFOX6, gemcitabine/cisplatin, or paclitaxel). Phase 2 tumor-type cohorts were colorectal cancer, biliary tract cancer (BTC), ovarian cancer (OC), gastroesophageal cancer, and endometrial cancer. Primary end points included safety, tolerability, and overall response rate (ORR).

Fifty-four patients were enrolled in phase 1 and 95 in phase 2. No dose-dependent adverse event trends were observed. Recommended phase 2 dose of INCB001158 was 100 mg bid. Two phase 2 expansion cohorts met criteria for Simon 2-stage expansion: patients with BTC treated with INCB001158 100 mg bid plus gemcitabine/cisplatin, and patients with platinum-resistant OC treated with INCB001158 100 mg bid plus paclitaxel. The overall response rate was 24.2% (8/33 patients, all partial responses) in the BTC cohort and 16.7% (1/24 complete response and 3/24 partial responses) in the OC cohort. Pharmacodynamic increase in arginine levels was observed systemically in blood plasma and locally detected by mass-spectrometry images of tumor tissue.

INCB001158 was generally well tolerated and associated with increases in arginine levels and responses in the BTC cohort, suggesting that combination regimens with chemotherapy may provide clinical benefit for some patients.

📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).
Complex perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease are often refractory to the recommended combination of surgery and biological drug therapy. We investigated the efficacy and safety of minimally manipulated, microfragmented, autologous adipose tissue ("MFat"), after surgical debridement of the fistula, in patients with Crohn's disease in whom standard treatments had failed.

This double-blind, randomized controlled trial enrolled 80 adults who all underwent cone-like partial fistulectomy of a complex fistula (one inner orifice and up to three external openings) followed by either MFat injection or suture of the inner orifice and saline injection. MFat was prepared from lipoaspirates using Lipogems medical devices immediately before fistulectomy and transplanted around the inner orifice and along the residual tracts immediately after fistulectomy. The primary efficacy outcome was combined (clinicoradiological) remission at 24 weeks in the per-protocol populations. Safety was assessed in the all-patients-treated populations.

After randomization, 37 patients in each group were treated and 33 patients each were assessed. Combined remission occurred in 19 patients (57.6%) of the intervention group and six control patients (18.2%) (risk ratio 3.17; 95% CI, 1.45-6.91; P = .004; number needed to treat, 3). Clinical remission was found in 26 (78.8%) and 18 (54.5%) patients, respectively (P = .044), while clinical indices improved in both groups. Seven adverse events were reported, without significant differences between groups in safety endpoints.

Partial fistulectomy alone led to substantial clinical improvement, but the addition of MFat significantly increased the likelihood of achieving MRI-confirmed healing. Registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05594862).

▶Time-restricted eating is associated with sustained improvement in steatosis and fibrosis in MASLD and MASH: a long-term real-world cohort study.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
ISRCTN74413912 (registration date: January 26, 2026).
Time-restricted eating (TRE), a form of intermittent fasting, has demonstrated favorable short-term metabolic effects in patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). However, evidence regarding its long-term effects on hepatic steatosis, fibrosis, and metabolic outcomes remains limited. We evaluated the long-term hepatic and metabolic outcomes associated with TRE in adults with MASLD and clinically suspected metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH).

In this prospective real-world observational cohort study, adults with MASLD or clinically suspected MASH selected one of five routine management strategies through shared clinical decision-making, including TRE, Orlistat-supported lifestyle modification, a 1500-kcal balanced diet, or exercise-focused lifestyle counselling. Participants were followed for 28-48 months. Primary outcomes were longitudinal changes in hepatic steatosis, fibrosis-related measures, and metabolic parameters. Hepatic steatosis and fibrosis were assessed using controlled attenuation parameter (CAP), liver stiffness measurement (LSM), and validated non-invasive fibrosis indices. Longitudinal analyses used multivariable linear mixed-effects models adjusted for predefined covariates, with inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) performed as a sensitivity analysis.

A total of 197 participants completed the follow-up. TRE was associated with the greatest and most sustained improvements in hepatic steatosis, fibrosis-related measures, and metabolic outcomes. Both TRE groups demonstrated substantial improvements in CAP and LSM, accompanied by steatosis regression in 75.5% of participants with MASLD and 62.5% with clinically suspected MASH, while fibrosis regression occurred in 35% of participants in both groups. Concordant improvement was observed across multiple independent imaging- and serum-based fibrosis assessments. Orlistat-supported lifestyle modification and a 1500-kcal balanced diet were associated with modest improvements, whereas exercise-focused lifestyle counseling produced minimal hepatic benefit. Findings remained consistent after multivariable adjustment and IPTW sensitivity analyses despite clinically relevant baseline differences between the management groups.

In this long-term real-world observational cohort, TRE was associated with durable improvement in hepatic steatosis, fibrosis-related measures, and metabolic dysfunction in adults with MASLD and clinically suspected MASH. Concordant improvement across multiple independent non-invasive fibrosis assessments supports the potential role of TRE as a sustainable lifestyle strategy for metabolic liver disease. Randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm causality.

💡 Conclusion
Injectable hyaluronic acid-based therapies are emerging non-hormonal interventions for vulvovaginal symptoms associated with GSM. Current evidence does not yet establish their role as routine or guideline-recommended treatments. This evidentiary position should be considered separately from the intended purpose and regulatory status of individual devices. Larger independent trials, longer-term safety data, and health-economic evaluations are needed to define their comparative clinical role.
Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) is a chronic condition resulting from oestrogen deficiency and characterised by persistent genital, sexual, and urinary symptoms that can substantially impair quality of life. While local oestrogen therapy is highly effective, a significant proportion of women are unable or unwilling to use hormonal treatments, highlighting the need for effective non-hormonal options.

This narrative review examines local, non-hormonal approaches to GSM, with a particular emphasis on injectable hyaluronic acid-based therapies.

Hyaluronic acid is integral to tissue hydration, viscoelasticity, and the maintenance of extracellular matrix integrity, processes that are compromised in GSM. Topical formulations are commonly used to provide surface hydration and symptom relief, while injectable approaches deliver hyaluronic acid to deeper tissue layers; the potential implications for the magnitude and duration of clinical benefit require further evaluation. Among injectable formulations, hybrid cooperative complex hyaluronic acid (HCC-HA) has been evaluated in small prospective studies and one randomised trial in women with sexual dysfunction rather than strictly defined GSM. Available studies report potential improvements in some symptoms, including vaginal dryness and dyspareunia. However, improvements were not demonstrated across all assessed outcomes, including certain sexual-function and quality-of-life measures. No serious adverse events were reported in the available studies, although larger studies with longer follow-up are needed to characterise the safety profile more fully.

Injectable hyaluronic acid-based therapies are emerging non-hormonal interventions for vulvovaginal symptoms associated with GSM. Current evidence does not yet establish their role as routine or guideline-recommended treatments. This evidentiary position should be considered separately from the intended purpose and regulatory status of individual devices. Larger independent trials, longer-term safety data, and health-economic evaluations are needed to define their comparative clinical role.

▶MASLD vs. MetALD: mechanistic insights, diagnostic advances, and therapeutic horizons.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 HCC surveillance 재설정
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 USG 6개월 간격
💡 Conclusion
Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and its inflammatory subtype, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), have become the most prevalent forms of chronic liver disease worldwide, representing leading causes of liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). In clinical practice, a substantial proportion of patients with metabolic disorders also present with clinically significant alcohol consumption. Metabolic risk factors and alcohol exposure act synergistically to exacerbate hepatocellular injury, inflammation, and fibrosis, giving rise to the overlapping clinical phenotype termed metabolic dysfunction and alcohol-related liver disease (MetALD). The introduction of the MetALD concept addresses key limitations of traditional exclusion-based diagnostic frameworks; however, operational alcohol-consumption ranges for classification remain consensus-based rather than evidence-derived, and fundamental challenges persist regarding synergistic pathogenic mechanisms, diagnostic accuracy of standard tools, and optimal personalized treatment strategies. This review provides a critical head-to-head comparison of MASLD and MetALD across mechanistic, diagnostic, and therapeutic domains. We quantitatively synthesize evidence on synergistic injury pathways, explicitly distinguishing preclinical findings from human clinical data; evaluate the performance of non-invasive diagnostic modalities in MetALD relative to MASLD, with standardized evidence grading; and critically appraise the evidence base for current and emerging therapies, delineating consensus-based recommendations from randomized trial evidence. We further identify key unresolved challenges and propose a prioritized research roadmap to advance precision diagnosis and management of steatotic liver disease across the metabolic-alcohol spectrum.
