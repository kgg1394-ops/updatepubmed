⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요

💡 Conclusion
Pirfenidone and pentoxifylline attenuated selected biochemical and histopathological components of acute cavernosal ischaemia-reperfusion injury in this experimental model. These findings are hypothesis-generating; additional studies incorporating long-term erectile function assessment, fibrotic remodelling markers, and pharmacokinetic evaluation are warranted to clarify the translational relevance.
Ischaemic priapism is a urological emergency in which sustained cavernosal veno-occlusion produces progressive hypoxia, acidosis, smooth muscle compromise, and ultimately corporal fibrosis. Cavernosal injury is not confined to the ischaemic phase, as reperfusion following detumescence may further amplify tissue damage through oxidative stress, inflammatory activation, and endothelial dysfunction; adjunctive pharmacological strategies targeting this early reperfusion phase remain insufficiently characterised. This study was designed to evaluate and compare the acute effects of pirfenidone, pentoxifylline, dexpanthenol, and selected combination regimens on biochemical and histopathological markers of cavernosal injury in a rat model of ischaemic priapism-related ischaemia-reperfusion injury.

Sixty-eight adult male Wistar albino rats were randomly allocated into seven groups: Sham, Control/IRI, pirfenidone, pentoxifylline, dexpanthenol, pirfenidone+dexpanthenol, and pentoxifylline+dexpanthenol. Ischaemic priapism was induced using a vacuum-assisted constriction-band model followed by a one-hour reperfusion period. Serum IL-1β, IL-10, malondialdehyde, and endothelial nitric oxide synthase levels were measured, and cavernosal tissues were evaluated by semi-quantitative histopathological scoring. Group comparisons used parametric or non-parametric tests with multiplicity adjustment. The study protocol was approved by the Animal Experiments Local Ethics Committee of Necmettin Erbakan University KONÜDAM Experimental Medicine Application and Research Center (22 December 2023; No. 2023-060). All procedures complied with institutional animal-care guidelines, and the study is reported in accordance with ARRIVE 2.0.

Sixty-seven animals were included in the final analysis. Relative to the Sham group, the Control/IRI group exhibited significantly elevated IL-1β, IL-10, malondialdehyde, and endothelial nitric oxide synthase levels alongside marked histopathological injury, confirming successful model induction. Pirfenidone significantly attenuated IL-1β and malondialdehyde levels and improved the composite pathology score. Pentoxifylline significantly reduced IL-1β, IL-10, malondialdehyde, and endothelial nitric oxide synthase levels and similarly improved the composite pathology score. Dexpanthenol attenuated malondialdehyde levels but did not consistently improve endothelial nitric oxide synthase or overall histopathological injury. Combination regimens produced endpoint-specific effects without consistently surpassing the corresponding monotherapies.

Pirfenidone and pentoxifylline attenuated selected biochemical and histopathological components of acute cavernosal ischaemia-reperfusion injury in this experimental model. These findings are hypothesis-generating; additional studies incorporating long-term erectile function assessment, fibrotic remodelling markers, and pharmacokinetic evaluation are warranted to clarify the translational relevance.

Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors (JAKis) are increasingly used to treat inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), but most pivotal data come from randomized controlled trials. Real-world evidence is needed to clarify their effectiveness and safety in routine clinical practice.

We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of real-world studies reporting outcomes in IBD patients treated with JAKis (tofacitinib, upadacitinib, or filgotinib). PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched from inception to August 11, 2025. Primary outcomes were clinical response and clinical remission; secondary outcomes included steroid-free clinical remission, endoscopic and biochemical responses, and safety outcomes. 63 studies involving 8130 IBD patients were included. In ulcerative colitis (UC), pooled clinical response and remission rates were 55% and 43% for tofacitinib, 77% and 65% for upadacitinib, and 69% and 55% for filgotinib; steroid-free remission with tofacitinib approached 50% at 1 year. In Crohn's disease (CD), upadacitinib achieved clinical response and remission in around two thirds of patients. Across agents, safety profiles were acceptable: mortality was 0%; hospitalization and treatment discontinuation rates were 8% and 28% for tofacitinib, 5% and 12% for upadacitinib, and 4% and 15% for filgotinib; serious adverse events occurred in ≤8% of patients.

Descriptive real-world data show that tofacitinib, upadacitinib, and filgotinib all provide clinically meaningful effectiveness in UC and CD, complementing evidence from randomized trials. Across real-world cohorts, JAKis have generally acceptable safety profiles, with low rates of serious adverse events and no observed drug-related mortality.

▶Short-term outcomes of the hinotori versus da Vinci robotic surgical systems in colorectal cancer surgery: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
👨‍⚕️ Clinical: 👉 임상 판단 필요
⚡ Action: 🧐 추가 판단 필요
📌 Order: 📌 환자 맞춤 판단
💡 Conclusion
Direct comparative evidence on the hinotori and da Vinci robotic systems in colorectal cancer surgery remains limited. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to compare their short-term perioperative outcomes. PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched for studies directly comparing hinotori with da Vinci robotic surgery in patients with colorectal cancer. Study-level data were pooled using random-effects models, with mean differences (MDs) for continuous outcomes and odds ratios (ORs) for dichotomous outcomes, each with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Five retrospective propensity-score-matched studies involving 462 patients (hinotori, n = 204; da Vinci, n = 258) were included. Operative time (MD 30.57 min, 95% CI 17.51-43.64; I² = 0%) and console time (MD 30.72 min, 95% CI 17.29-44.16; I² = 42%) were significantly longer with hinotori, whereas length of hospital stay was significantly shorter (MD - 1.58 days, 95% CI - 2.92 to - 0.24; I² = 63%). No statistically significant differences were detected for estimated blood loss (MD 3.42 mL, 95% CI - 6.98 to 13.82; I² = 66%) or for postoperative complications (grade I-II: OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.36-1.69; grade III-IV: OR 0.54, 95% CI 0.16-1.76; overall: OR 0.74, 95% CI 0.37-1.46). In this meta-analysis of propensity-score-matched studies, hinotori was associated with longer operative and console times and a shorter hospital stay than da Vinci, whereas no statistically significant difference was detected in blood loss or postoperative complications. Because clinically important differences in safety could not be excluded and residual confounding could not be ruled out, these findings should be regarded as preliminary; larger prospective comparative studies are warranted.

💡 Conclusion
DIC represents a diagnostically complex spectrum of disorders that require integrated multidisciplinary evaluation. These ECCO Current Practice Positions provide a practical framework for diagnostic work-up and treatment, particularly for ICI-induced enterocolitis.
Drug-induced enterocolitis (DIC) consists of a heterogeneous group of gastrointestinal inflammatory disorders with clinical, endoscopic, and histological features that often overlap with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Given the increasing recognition of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced enterocolitis and the lack of standardized management algorithms, the European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation (ECCO) convened a multidisciplinary panel to review DIC, with a particular focus on ICI-induced disease.

Gastroenterologists, histopathologists, and oncologists systematically reviewed the literature using Medline, PubMed, EMBASE, and Scopus. Evidence was synthesized into ECCO Current Practice Positions addressing diagnostic principles, histopathological patterns, therapeutic strategies, and ICI-specific considerations. Statements were refined and approved through structured consensus.

Twenty-three practice positions were agreed upon. DIC may present with diverse histological patterns, including IBD-like/chronic enterocolitis, acute enterocolitis, microscopic colitis, ischemic-type injury, and mixed features, none of which are pathognomonic. Close clinicopathological collaboration is therefore essential. In suspected ICI-induced enterocolitis, assessment should include Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events-based grading, stool testing for pathogens, fecal calprotectin, endoscopy with biopsies, and imaging when indicated. Corticosteroids remain first-line therapy, while mild or microscopic disease may respond to budesonide or beclomethasone. Corticosteroid-refractory or corticosteroid-dependent disease should prompt early escalation to infliximab or vedolizumab. Surgery is reserved for refractory or complicated cases. Rechallenge with ICIs may be considered following multidisciplinary evaluation.

DIC represents a diagnostically complex spectrum of disorders that require integrated multidisciplinary evaluation. These ECCO Current Practice Positions provide a practical framework for diagnostic work-up and treatment, particularly for ICI-induced enterocolitis.

💡 Conclusion
AI-enabled digital medicine platforms in Europe are developed within overlapping rules for personal data, medical devices, AI, clinical research, health data access, and health technology assessment. This viewpoint represents the authors' experience and interpretation from the preimplementation phase of INTERCEPT, an Innovative Health Initiative consortium that plans to validate biomarkers and develop a blood-based risk score for early Crohn disease interception. It does not report original empirical research, a systematic regulatory review, a deployed platform, or a validated AI model. Our aim is to explain how compliance-by-design can be translated into an auditable governance architecture and to identify what still requires scientific evaluation. We advance 3 messages. First, governance must begin with intended purpose and boundary mapping: a digital platform may contain research, operational, and medical device software functions, and conformity of one module cannot be inferred for the platform as a whole. Second, overlapping obligations can be operationalized through 5 linked controls: modular responsibility and change control, federated data governance, traceable AI life cycle management, consent and participant rights orchestration, and a common cross-border governance core with local adaptations. Third, compliance is not a surrogate for model validity, clinical benefit, trust, scalability, or market access. Before clinical deployment, planned AI components require prespecified labels and cohorts, leakage prevention, missing data and imbalance strategies, calibration, subgroup assessment, external validation, human factors evaluation, and postdeployment algorithmovigilance. A hypothetical biomarker risk score update illustrates how the 5 controls interact. INTERCEPT is informative because it combines cross-border biomarker research, planned predictive analytics, and heterogeneous institutional settings while key design decisions remain modifiable. Its contribution is nevertheless conceptual: the proposed framework is a testable governance hypothesis, not evidence of implementation success. Prospective studies should compare it with alternative governance models and assess safety, workload, participant experience, evidence generation, and regulatory performance.

Patients with liver disease experience frequent hospitalizations for complications such as hepatic encephalopathy, ascites, infections, and gastrointestinal bleeding, resulting in substantial healthcare utilization and reduced time spent at home. Hospital at Home (HaH) is an increasingly utilized care model that delivers hospital-level acute care in the patient's home through multidisciplinary care teams, remote monitoring, and home-based diagnostic and therapeutic services. This review summarizes the rationale for considering HaH in liver disease and outlines key program components. We review the existing evidence supporting HaH, including the limited hepatology-specific literature as well as broader data from randomized trials and systematic reviews in related populations.
